**Some seeds will not sprout until they have been tricked into thinking winter has passed.**

- **Cold treatment ("stratification"):** echinacea, valerian, many perennials and fruit and nut trees. Mix seed with damp sand or paper towel in a bag and keep in the fridge for **4 to 12 weeks**, then sow.
- **Soaking:** beans, peas, okra, chard and beets sprout faster after an overnight soak.
- **Scratching ("scarification"):** hard-coated seeds such as morning glory and lupins: nick with a file or rub with sandpaper, then soak.
- **Light:** lettuce, celery and snapdragons need light to sprout: press into the surface.
- **Dark:** some seeds, such as onions, like to be covered.
- **Heat:** peppers, eggplant, melons and okra need warmth (80 degrees F) to sprout.

Always read the notes for each crop on the seed packet and in the Crop guides.

Source: University extension home garden guides (Purdue, University of Wisconsin-Madison, Oregon State), paraphrased.

Reference only. Check your local extension office for your area.
