**These are the easiest crops to save from.** They mostly pollinate themselves, so different varieties rarely cross.

**Beans and peas:**
1. Leave some pods on the plant until they are dry and rattle.
2. Pick them, shell them, and dry the seed another week on a plate.

**Tomatoes:**
1. Choose ripe fruit from a healthy plant.
2. Scoop out the seeds and juice into a jar with a little water.
3. Let it sit a few days until a scum forms, then rinse the seed clean.
4. Spread it on a plate to dry for a week.

**Lettuce:** let one plant bolt (grow a flower stalk). Collect the fluffy seed heads when they are dry.

**Peppers:** let the fruit ripen fully, take the seeds, and dry them.

**Always save from the best plants**, not from the weakest. Label the jar with the crop, variety and year.

Source: University extension seed-saving guides (Oregon State, Penn State), paraphrased.

Reference only. Check your local extension office for your area.
