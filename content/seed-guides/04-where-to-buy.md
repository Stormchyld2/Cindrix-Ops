**Good places:** regional seed companies, local garden centres, seed libraries and swaps, and neighbours who grow what thrives near you.

**Check the packet:**
1. A **packed-for year** or a "sell by" date. Fresh seed is better.
2. The **variety name**, not just the crop.
3. Days to harvest and when to plant.

**Be careful with "emergency seed kits".** Some are sold with big promises. They can hold crops that do poorly in your area, a lot of herbs and flowers, and seed that is already several years old. Before you buy one, look at the list of varieties, the count of seeds and the packed year. Often you can build a better vault yourself for less money.

**Keep what you buy dry.** Do not leave seed in a hot car or a damp garage.

Source: University extension guidance on buying garden seed, paraphrased.

Reference only. Check your local extension office for your area.
