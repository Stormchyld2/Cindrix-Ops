**Three enemies: heat, damp and light.** Keep seed cool, dry and dark.

1. **Dry it first.** Seed you saved must be fully dry before you store it. Seed that bends is too damp; it should snap or be hard.
2. **Use airtight containers:** glass jars with a tight lid, or sealed freezer bags in a tin.
3. **Add a drying agent** if you can: a little dry rice or powdered milk in a paper wrapper, or silica gel packets.
4. **Keep it cold if possible.** A fridge works well. The freezer works too, if the seed is very dry and the jar goes in and out of the cold slowly so water does not form on it.
5. **Label everything:** crop, variety and the year.
6. **Pests:** a sealed jar keeps out mice and weevils.

A rule of thumb: the sum of the temperature in degrees Fahrenheit and the percent humidity should be under 100.

Source: Purdue Extension and University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension seed storage guidance, paraphrased.

Reference only. Check your local extension office for your area.
