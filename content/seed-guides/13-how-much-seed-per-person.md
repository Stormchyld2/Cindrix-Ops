**Seed is cheap; running short is not.** Plan for a bad year, for sharing, and for replacing seed that fails.

**Rough packets or amounts for a family of four, to grow most of its vegetables:**
- **Dry beans:** 1 pound of seed plants about 100 feet of row and gives 10 to 20 pounds of beans.
- **Field corn:** 1 pound plants about 1/3 acre; for a garden patch, 1 to 2 packets.
- **Peas:** 1/2 pound for a 100 foot row; sow spring and autumn.
- **Potatoes:** 10 pounds of seed potatoes for about a 100 foot row; 3 to 4 rows for a family.
- **Winter squash and pumpkin:** 1 to 2 packets; each hill gives 3 to 5 fruits.
- **Carrots, beets, onions:** 2 to 3 packets each (carrot seed loses vigour fast).
- **Tomatoes, peppers:** 1 packet of each type: 6 to 8 plants of each is a good start.
- **Greens (kale, chard, lettuce, spinach):** 1 to 2 packets each, resown through the year.
- **Herbs:** 1 packet each.

**Keep a double supply of the crops that matter most** (beans, corn, squash, potatoes, carrots, onions, greens) in case one packet fails. Keep seed from **two or three years** so one bad crop does not end a line.

**Count seeds, not packets:** a packet from a company holds 20 to 1000 seeds depending on the crop. The Seed vault page lets you write the number of seeds per packet.

Source: University extension home garden guides (Purdue, University of Wisconsin-Madison, Oregon State), paraphrased.

Reference only. Check your local extension office for your area.
