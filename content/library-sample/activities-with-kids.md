# Things to do with kids when the power is out
Keep to a routine and tell children, in simple honest words, what is happening and who is looking after them. Give each child a job. Ideas that need no power: read aloud or tell stories, shadow puppets with a flashlight, card and board games, drawing and journaling, cooking helper jobs, building a blanket fort, a scavenger hunt around the house, indoor "camping" with a lantern, singing and simple music, and learning a knot or a card trick.

# Calm-down ideas for scared children
Stay close and keep your voice steady. Let them ask questions and answer simply. Offer a comfort object, a snack and a warm blanket. Slow breathing together, counting breaths or a short walk can help. Keep bedtime familiar. Limit frightening news in front of them.

# Outdoor ideas when it is safe
A nature walk to find leaves, rocks and animal tracks, a "no-trace" picnic, gathering firewood or sticks if it is safe, planting a seed in a cup, cloud watching, and simple games such as tag and I spy. Check for weather, traffic and hazards first, and keep children within sight.

# Learning ideas
Count and sort supplies, measure water with cups, read a map together, practice writing a family emergency plan, learn the family phone number and meeting place, and teach basic safety such as when to call 911.

# Safety with kids during an outage
Keep matches, lighters and candles out of reach and use battery lanterns instead of candles when you can. Never run a generator or grill indoors. Keep medicines and cleaning products locked or high up. Keep children away from downed power lines and floodwater.
