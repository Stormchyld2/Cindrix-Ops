# Keeping morale up in a long outage
Stress, fear and boredom are normal in a long emergency. Keep a daily routine: wake, eat and sleep at regular times. Get daylight and light movement each day. Give everyone a small job so they feel useful. Talk about what is going well as well as what is hard.

# Sleep and rest
Lack of sleep makes everything feel worse. Keep a regular bedtime, keep the sleeping area as dark and quiet as you can, and take turns on night watch or chores so no one is exhausted. Rest is part of the plan.

# Limit the news
Check for important updates at set times, such as morning and evening, rather than all day. Constant news and rumors raise stress. Rely on official sources for safety information.

# Check on each other and your neighbors
Ask how people are doing, listen without fixing everything, and check on neighbors who live alone, are older or are ill. Helping others is good for you too.

# When to get help
Feeling hopeless, being unable to sleep or eat for days, panic that will not settle, or thoughts of hurting yourself or others mean you should reach out for help. In the United States you can call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, and call 911 if someone is in immediate danger. If phones are down, ask a neighbor, a first responder or a shelter staff member.

# Diabetes and stress
Stress, poor sleep and changes in food can change blood sugar. Keep checking as your doctor has told you and follow your doctor's written plan. This app does not give insulin or medicine doses.
