# Emergency toilet when the water is off
Do not use a toilet that has no running water for long. Line a bucket or the toilet bowl with a heavy plastic bag. After each use, cover the waste with a scoop of cat litter, sawdust, ash or dry soil to cut smell and flies. Tie the bag shut when it is about half full and keep it in a lidded container away from where you live and cook.

# Handwashing without a tap
Wash hands with soap and clean water for 20 seconds after using the toilet, before cooking and before eating. If there is no water, use hand sanitizer with at least 60 percent alcohol, but sanitizer does not work well on visibly dirty hands. Keep a small bottle or pump of water and soap by the toilet.

# Digging a latrine
If bags are not possible, dig a trench latrine as far from living space as you can, downhill and at least 100 feet (30 meters) from any well, stream or water you drink. Dig it about 1 to 2 feet (30 to 60 cm) deep, cover each use with soil, and fill it in when the waste is within a few inches of the top. Check local rules and follow any public health instructions.

# Keeping waste away from water
Never put human waste in streams, ponds or storm drains, and never near where you collect drinking water. Flies, rats and rain carry germs from waste to food and water. Keep food and water containers covered.

# Trash and garbage
Keep trash in bags with tight lids, away from the house and out of reach of animals. Burying or burning may be restricted where you live, so follow local guidance. Keep food scraps separate if you can, because they attract pests.

# Signs of sickness from poor sanitation
Diarrhea, vomiting and fever can spread quickly in a household. Drink safe water in small, frequent sips, clean the toilet area with a bleach and water solution made as the label says, and get medical help if someone cannot keep fluids down, is a small child or elderly, has blood in their stool or has diabetes.
