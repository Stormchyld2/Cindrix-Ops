**This is a starter plan for your group to edit. It is not legal advice and not tactical training. Nobody has checked it against your state's laws. Have someone qualified read it, and change anything that does not fit your site.**

## 1. What this plan is for
To keep the people at this site safe by noticing trouble early, giving it a chance to turn away, getting help, and never making a bad situation worse. The aim is **nobody hurt**, not winning an argument or protecting things.

## 2. Ground rules (they come before everything else)
1. People matter more than property. Things can be replaced.
2. **Verify before you react.** A late check-in, an alarm or a noise is a question, not an answer. Find out what it is.
3. **Never go alone to look at something that worries you.** Go in twos, or call the duty lead on the radio first.
4. **Do not chase, corner or pursue anyone.** Let them leave.
5. **Call 911** for any threat to life, any break-in, any weapon, any fire or any serious injury. Do not wait to be sure. In a long-term grid-down emergency, use the contact plan in section 9.
6. **Use only the force the law allows to protect life, and no more.** Know your state's law (West Virginia, Tennessee or wherever you are). Write down who in your group has read it. This plan does not authorise anything beyond it.
7. **No traps.** Never set a trap, a hidden hazard or anything meant to hurt a person. They are illegal, and they hurt children, neighbours and rescuers.
8. Stay calm and speak plainly on the radio. No codes you have not all learned.

## 3. Who does what
- **Duty lead** (one person at all times, named on the watchbill): decides the alert level, makes the calls, writes the log. If the lead is unreachable, the next person on the watchbill becomes lead.
- **Watch stander**: awake, at the post, watching and listening, checks in on the schedule.
- **Runner / pair**: goes to check something only when the lead says so, always in twos, always with a radio.
- **Everyone else**: stays where the lead tells them. Children and anyone who cannot help stay with an adult in the safe place.
- Rule for a camp of four: one stays in camp; people go out in groups of two; not everyone leaves at once. Write the names here: (fill in).

## 4. Layers (from outside in)
1. **Be seen to be ready.** Tidy site, lights where you can power them, a visible watch, no signs of stock you do not want noticed.
2. **Notice.** Watch posts with views of the approaches, low-power no-electricity alarms on the paths and gates (tin-can lines, bells, trip cords that only make noise), and ears. Record each alarm and each inspection in Security > Security.
3. **Delay.** Gates, fences, parked vehicles, thorny plants, rough ground. These buy time to decide. They are not for hurting anyone.
4. **Tell the group.** One signal everybody knows (radio call, whistle pattern or bell). Write it here: (fill in).
5. **Get help or get away.** 911 when it works; the neighbours; the evacuation plan (see Operational Response).

## 5. Alert levels
- **Normal.** The usual watch and inspections. Set by the duty lead.
- **Alert.** Something is not right but nobody is in danger yet: a late check-in, an alarm with no cause, a stranger on the road. Duty lead sets it. Everyone knows where they are and has a radio. Pairs, no singles. Someone is told to watch the approach.
- **Respond.** Someone is on the site who should not be, or a person is at risk. Duty lead sets it. Everyone goes to the safe place. 911 if possible. Use the Operational Response plan.
- Only a person raises or lowers a level. Write the time and who in the log.

## 6. When an alarm goes off or a check-in is missed
1. Duty lead gets on the radio and asks. (No answer is **not** proof of an intruder or an injury.)
2. Try the back-up channel. Ask the pair's last known place.
3. Send **two** people with a radio to the last known place only if the lead says it is safe to. Keep a person watching the approach.
4. If you find a stranger: stay back, speak calmly, ask what they need. Do not argue. Do not touch them. If they are not leaving or are threatening, go to Respond.
5. Write what happened, in order, in the log, including what was checked and found.

## 7. Visitors at the gate
Use the card "Visitor at the gate". Do not open the gate to a stranger in a way that puts the opener in reach. Speak through the gate. Ask what they need. Offer water or directions if you choose, not entry. Note the time, number of people and what they asked for.

## 8. Daily and weekly routine
- Morning: radios, batteries, watchbill, alarms look as set.
- Each watch hand-over: tell the next person what you saw and heard, and where each person is.
- Weekly: walk the perimeter alarms and gates (Security > Security, "Inspect"); post the next watchbill; change the route and the times so they are not predictable.
- Every 90 days: practise the signals and the move to the safe place with everyone, including children.

## 9. Calling for help
- 911 where phones work. Say the address, what is happening, how many people, who is hurt.
- When phones do not work: radio channel plan (Security > Planning), the nearest neighbour, the nearest fire or police post: (fill in places and how to reach each).
- Decide before trouble who makes the call.

## 10. Records
Everything that happens goes in the incident log. Do not edit what was written; add a correction. Keep the exported copy somewhere safe.

## 11. Training
Everyone: radio skills, where the safe place is, the signals, first aid. Duty leads: this plan, the gate card, the missed check-in card. Practise with the lights off and a flat battery once.

## 12. When this plan is finished
The plan stops being in force when the duty lead has set Normal, everyone is accounted for, any injuries are cared for, the log is written and the watchbill is back to normal. Hold a short review within two days: what worked, what did not, what changes.

## 13. Review
Review at least every six months, and after any real incident. Each change is saved as a new version.
