**This is a starter plan for your group to edit. It is not legal or medical advice. Have someone qualified read it, and practise it. A plan nobody has practised will not work on the day.**

## 1. Trigger: when to use this plan
Put it into use (Security > Planning > "Put into use") on an open incident when any of these is true:
- Someone at risk of harm: injury, fire, a person missing too long, a threat.
- A person is on the site who should not be and is not leaving.
- A hazard is coming: flood, wildfire, severe storm, a chemical or gas release nearby.
- The duty lead sets the level to **Respond**.
Activating the plan is always a **person's** decision and is written in the incident log with the time and name. The exact words of this plan, at that moment, are kept with the incident.

## 2. First five minutes (always)
1. **Duty lead announces it** with the group's signal and the words "Operational Response, [what], [where]".
2. **Count heads.** Everyone reports to the muster point: (fill in). Say each person's name aloud. Who is missing?
3. **Call 911** if it works. If not, start the radio plan (section 8).
4. **Choose:** get away, or stay in the safe place (sections 4 and 5).
5. **Write the time** and who decided.

## 3. Roles
- **Duty lead**: decides, talks to outside help, keeps the log.
- **Head counter**: keeps the list of everyone and where each is. Reports missing people to the lead at once.
- **Medic**: first aid (section 6). Uses the first-aid cards. Does not give anyone medicine doses from this plan.
- **Runner pair**: carries messages when radios fail. Always two.
- Name a **back-up** for each role: (fill in).

## 4. Evacuation (leave the site)
Use when staying is more dangerous than going. Use the plan "Evacuation" and the card "Evacuate home".
1. Take the go-bags, medicines, papers, water, radios and chargers. Leave the rest.
2. Everyone goes together, in two vehicles or two groups if possible, with a radio in each.
3. Primary place: (fill in) by the route (fill in). Back-up place: (fill in) by a different route (fill in).
4. Leave a note where the group would look, with the time, who and where to.
5. Tell the person outside the area (section 8) where you are going.
6. On arrival: count heads again, log the time and place.
7. Do not return until the lead decides it is safe, and not before emergency services say so for fire, flood or a crime scene.

## 5. Shelter (stay and protect)
Use when leaving is more dangerous than staying, or while waiting for help.
1. Go to the safe place: (fill in the room or building). Lock or block the door. Lights off if the threat is a person.
2. Children and anyone who cannot help stay with an adult.
3. Phones silent. Radios low. One person keeps contact with the lead.
4. Stay until the lead says it is over, or help arrives and says it is over.
5. For a storm or fire hazard, use the matching card instead of the room plan.

## 6. Medical and fire
- **Fire**: get everyone out first, then call. Do not fight a fire that is bigger than a wastebasket. Never go back inside. Meet at the muster point.
- **Injury**: stop bleeding by pressing hard on it; keep the person warm and still; call 911; use the first-aid cards. Write the time you started and what you did. Do not give or look up medicine doses in this plan. Follow the dispatcher or a medical professional.
- **Every injured person** is seen by a professional as soon as it can be arranged.

## 7. Lawful de-escalation (if a person is the problem)
1. Keep distance and keep calm. Speak low and slow, hands open and visible.
2. Ask what they need. Offer a way to leave. Do not argue or insult.
3. Do not touch them, block their way out or chase them.
4. If they are leaving: let them. Note what they looked like, what they drove and the time.
5. If they are threatening someone, or will not leave: go to shelter, call 911, and let police deal with it.
6. Only if a person is in immediate danger of serious harm and there is no way to get away: protect life using no more force than the law allows, then stop the moment the danger stops and call 911 at once. Know your state's law before this ever happens.

## 8. Communications failure
1. Try the main radio channel, then the back-up channel (Security > Planning > channel plan).
2. Try 911 by phone and by text. Move to a place with a signal if it is safe to.
3. Send a runner pair to the nearest neighbour or the agreed contact: (fill in).
4. Contact outside the area: (name and number). Agree beforehand that if they hear nothing by (time) they will call (fill in).
5. Log every attempt: time, how, result.

## 9. Family accountability
- Keep a written list of everyone on the site, and where each is. The head counter updates it every time someone moves.
- Anyone leaving the muster point tells the head counter first, and goes in a pair.
- Each person knows where to go if they are separated: (fill in).
- A person who is missing is searched for by pairs with radios, starting at the last place seen, with the lead's word. Use the card "Someone is missing".
- The list is closed only when every person is seen, by name, by the head counter.

## 10. Completion criteria
This plan is finished when **all** are true:
- Every person is accounted for, by name.
- Injuries have been cared for and professional help arranged where needed.
- The threat or hazard is over, or the group is somewhere safe from it.
- The duty lead has written the closing note in the log and set the level to Normal.
- A review is booked within two days (what worked, what did not, who needs training, what to change in this plan).

## 11. Training and review
Practise sections 2, 4 and 5 with everyone, including children, every 90 days. Practise the radio failure steps once a year. Review this plan at least every six months and after any real use. Save each change as a new version.
