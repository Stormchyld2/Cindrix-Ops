**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

1. **Rule of threes:** you can last about 3 days without water, so look early.
2. **Water runs downhill.** Follow valleys, gullies and animal trails downhill. Green plants often mean water nearby.
3. **Streams, rivers and lakes** are the best bet. Take water from moving water, not still pools, and from the middle of a stream rather than the edge.
4. **Collect rain** with a tarp, poncho or bowl. Collect dew in the morning by wiping grass with a cloth and wringing it out.
5. **Snow and ice:** melt it first. Eating snow lowers body heat. Use a pot over a fire or stove.
6. **Dry riverbeds:** dig at the outside bend of a curve where water gathers.
7. **Do not drink urine, sea water or alcohol.** They dry you out.
8. **Treat all outdoor water** before drinking (see the water treatment card). Clear water can still make you ill.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased; CDC and Ready.gov water guidance.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
