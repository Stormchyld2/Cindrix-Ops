**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. Stay calm and keep the person still. Do not poke with cotton swabs, tweezers or fingers: this pushes the object deeper.
2. **Nose:** have the person breathe through the mouth. If the object is easy to see and loose, a gentle blow out of the other nostril may move it.
3. **Ear:** tilt the head so the ear points down and shake gently. Do not flush with water if the object is a seed or bean (it swells) or if the eardrum may be hurt.
4. A live insect in the ear: tilt the head and float it out with warm (not hot) water or a little cooking oil, unless there is pain, drainage or a hurt eardrum.
5. If it will not come out easily, stop and see a clinic. Never dig.

**Get care the same day for:** a button battery or magnet (this is an emergency), bleeding, pain, hearing loss, or an object you cannot remove.

Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org) and Mayo Clinic first-aid guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
