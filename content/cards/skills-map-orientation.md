A map is only useful when it matches the ground.

1. **Orient it by compass:** put the compass flat on the map with the direction-of-travel arrow pointing to the top. Turn the map and compass together until the red needle sits in the orienting arrow (adjust for declination as the map says). North on the map now points to north on the ground.
2. **Orient it by features:** turn the map until a road, a river or a ridge on the map lines up with the same thing in front of you.
3. **Orient it by the sun** if you have no compass: the sun is in the east in the morning and the west in the evening.
4. **Hold it flat and keep your thumb on your position** so you can find it again.
5. **Look for catching features** ahead (a road, a river, a ridge) that will tell you if you have gone too far.
6. **Check as you go.** Every time you cross a feature, find it on the map and move your thumb.
7. **Fold the map** so the part you need is on top, and keep it in a waterproof bag.
8. **If you cannot match the map to the ground,** stop. Do not walk further. Use the lost-stop card.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on map orientation, paraphrased

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
