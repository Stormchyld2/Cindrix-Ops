Rough numbers are good enough. Check them against something you can measure when you can.

1. **Lightning and thunder:** count the seconds from flash to bang. About 3 seconds is 1 kilometre; about 5 seconds is 1 mile.
2. **Daylight left:** hold your arm straight out and stack your fingers sideways between the horizon and the sun. Each finger is about 15 minutes and a fist about an hour.
3. **Measure 100 metres** (about 110 yards) once in a walk, then picture that length to judge other distances in units of 100 metres.
4. **Look at the details you can see:** at about 200 metres you see faces and clothing clearly; at 500 metres details are lost; at 1 kilometre people are small shapes.
5. **Use a paced walk** with your pace count (see the pace count card) for accurate distance on the ground.
6. **Expect bias.** Distances look shorter across water, snow, flat ground or in clear air, and longer in rough ground, heat haze or dim light.
7. **On a map,** measure along your route with a string, then hold it against the scale bar.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on estimating range and time, paraphrased

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
