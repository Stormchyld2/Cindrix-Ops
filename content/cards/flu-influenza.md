**Signs:** they come on **suddenly**: fever or chills (not everyone gets a fever), cough, sore throat, runny or stuffy nose, body aches, headache, tiredness. Vomiting and diarrhea are more common in children. A cold comes on slowly and rarely brings a fever or strong aches. A flu fever often lasts 3 to 4 days.

1. **Stay home** and away from others. Rest and drink plenty of fluids.
2. Cover coughs and sneezes. Wash your hands often.
3. Most people get better without special medicine. **Antibiotics do not work on the flu.**
4. **Go back to normal** when, for at least 24 hours, you are getting better overall AND have had no fever without using fever medicine.
5. **Higher risk:** age 65 or older, children under 5 (most of all under 2), pregnancy, asthma, diabetes, heart disease and other long-term illness. Contact a health care provider right away if you are at higher risk. Flu antiviral medicine needs a prescription and works best started within 2 days of the first signs.
6. Do not give aspirin to a child or teen with flu.

**Call 911 or get care now for an adult with:** trouble breathing or shortness of breath, chest or belly pain or pressure that does not go away, dizziness that does not go away, confusion or cannot wake up, seizures, not passing urine, severe muscle pain, severe weakness or unsteadiness, or getting better and then much worse.

**Call 911 or get care now for a child with:** fast or hard breathing, blue lips or face, ribs pulling in with each breath, chest pain, severe muscle pain (refuses to walk), no urine for 8 hours, dry mouth or no tears, not alert, seizures, a fever above 104°F (40°C) that will not come down, or any fever in a baby under 12 weeks old.

Source: CDC, Signs and Symptoms of Flu and Treatment of Flu, checked 2026-10-31.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
