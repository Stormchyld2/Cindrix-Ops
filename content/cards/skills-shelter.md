**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

**Priority:** keep out of the wind and wet, and **off the cold ground.** Heat leaves your body faster into the ground than into the air.

1. **Choose the site:** dry, flat, near water but not in a gully or floodplain, away from dead branches and rock slides, in shelter from wind.
2. **Insulate underneath** first: thick layers of leaves, pine boughs or grass. Aim for a bed at least a hand's width thick, preferably more.
3. **Lean-to:** lay a strong pole between two trees or on forked sticks, lean branches against it at a steep angle, and cover with leaves, bark or a tarp.
4. **Debris hut:** make a ridge pole propped on a stump, lean ribs on both sides, and pile **very thick** leaves or grass over the top (arm's length or more). Make the space just big enough for your body to hold warmth.
5. **Tarp or poncho:** tie a line between two trees, drape the cover over it, peg the corners down and angle it away from the wind.
6. **Snow:** dig a trench or cave, keep a small air hole, and sit on insulation.
7. **Keep it small.** A small shelter is warmer.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
