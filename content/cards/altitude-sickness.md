**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. Signs: headache, nausea, dizziness, poor sleep, feeling very tired, at 8,000 feet (2,400 m) and above.
2. Stop climbing. Rest. Drink water and eat. Do not go higher until the signs are gone.
3. If you do not get better, or get worse, **go down at least 1,000 to 2,000 feet (300 to 600 m)** as soon as you can.
4. Avoid alcohol and heavy exertion in the first days at height.

**Go down and get help for:** a cough with breathlessness at rest, bubbling in the chest (fluid in the lungs), confusion, stumbling, or a headache that will not ease. These can be fatal.

Source: Wilderness Medical Society and CDC altitude guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
