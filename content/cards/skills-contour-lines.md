Contour lines join points of the same height. They tell you how steep the ground is before you walk on it.

1. **Find the contour interval** in the map margin (for example 40 feet). Every line is that much higher or lower than its neighbour.
2. **Bold lines** are index contours and are numbered with their height. Count up or down from them.
3. **Lines close together** mean a steep slope. **Lines far apart** mean gentle ground.
4. **Closed rings** mean a hilltop (heights rise toward the centre) or a pit (small tick marks pointing inward).
5. **A ridge** shows as lines that bend out in a U or V pointing **down** the slope, away from the high ground.
6. **A valley or stream** shows as lines that bend in a V pointing **up** the slope, toward the high ground. Water runs out through the open end of the V.
7. **A saddle** is a low spot between two high points: two sets of rings facing each other with a gap.
8. **Use it to travel:** following a ridge keeps you high and dry, a valley leads to water but is wet and slow, and crossing many close lines means a hard climb.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on terrain association, paraphrased

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
