Signs soon after a food, sting or medicine: hives, swelling of lips, face, tongue or throat, trouble breathing or wheezing, vomiting, dizzy or faint, a feeling something is badly wrong.

1. **Use the person's epinephrine auto-injector right away**, exactly as printed on the device (usually into the outer thigh, through clothing is allowed). Do not wait for it to get worse.
2. **Call 911** even if they seem better. The reaction can come back.
3. Lay the person flat with legs raised. If breathing is hard, let them sit up. If they vomit, turn them on their side.
4. If the signs come back and the person has another device, **follow the instructions on it** or the dispatcher's.
5. Give no pills or drinks if breathing is hard. Stay with them until the ambulance takes over. Start CPR if they stop breathing.

Medicine amounts are not given here. Follow the person's written plan and the label on the device.

Source: Red Cross and CDC first-aid guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
