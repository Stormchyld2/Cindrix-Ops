**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

**You need three things: heat, fuel and air.** Prepare **everything before you light.**

1. **Pick the spot:** sheltered from wind, on bare ground or stones, away from overhanging branches. Clear a circle at least 3 feet (1 m) wide of anything that burns.
2. **Tinder:** very dry, fine stuff that catches a spark, like dry grass, birch bark, cotton fluff, dry pine needles, or fuzz sticks.
3. **Kindling:** pencil-thin sticks, then thumb-thick ones. Gather **much more than you think you need.**
4. **Fuel:** wrist-thick wood and bigger. **Dead wood that snaps** is dry. Dead branches still on the tree are drier than wood on the ground.
5. **Build:** tipi style, with tinder in the middle and kindling leaning over it, or a log cabin. **Leave gaps for air.**
6. **Light the tinder** from the upwind side. Add thin kindling gradually. Do not smother it.
7. **In rain:** split wet wood to use the dry centre, build on a base of sticks or bark, and shield the fire.
8. **Never leave a fire unattended.** Put it out fully with water, stir the ashes and feel that they are cold.
9. **Never burn indoors** or in a tent. Carbon monoxide kills.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased; National Park Service campfire safety guidance.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
