**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. Rest the stomach for 15 to 30 minutes. Then give small sips of clear fluid: water, or an oral rehydration drink.
2. Take a sip every few minutes. Build up slowly. A child can have a teaspoon at a time.
3. Once vomiting stops for several hours, offer plain foods: rice, bread, bananas, toast. Avoid greasy and sugary food at first.
4. Wash hands well and clean surfaces with soap. Keep the sick person's towels and cups separate.
5. Breastfed babies should keep nursing, in small amounts more often.

**Get care for:** blood in vomit, green vomit, severe belly pain, a stiff neck, signs of dehydration (no urine for 8 hours, dry mouth, very sleepy), vomiting after a head injury, or vomiting that lasts more than a day (less for babies). Use the dehydration card too.

Source: CDC and American Academy of Pediatrics guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
