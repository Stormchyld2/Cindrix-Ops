**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. **Do not touch the person** while they may still be in contact with the electricity.
2. **Turn off the power** at the switch or breaker if you can. If you cannot, use a dry object that does not conduct, such as cardboard, plastic or wood, to move the source away.
3. **Stay at least 20 feet (6 metres) away from downed high-voltage wires.** Farther if they are sparking.
4. If the person is not breathing, coughing or moving, **start CPR** (see the CPR cards).
5. Do not move the person unless there is danger, since the neck or back may be hurt.
6. Cover burns with a sterile gauze bandage or a clean cloth. Do not use a blanket or towel, since loose fibres can stick.
7. Keep the person from getting chilled.

**Call 911** for any serious shock: burns, confusion, trouble breathing, a strange heartbeat, seizures, loss of consciousness, or a shock from a high-voltage wire or lightning. **Anyone shocked should be seen by a doctor,** even if there is no mark, because the damage can be inside.

Source: Mayo Clinic, Electrical shock: first aid.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
