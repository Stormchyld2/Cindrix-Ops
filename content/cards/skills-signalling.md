**Rule of three:** three of anything is a call for help. Three whistle blasts, three fires in a triangle, three flashes of light, three shots.

1. **Whistle:** three sharp blasts, a pause, and repeat. It carries farther than your voice and costs no energy.
2. **Mirror or shiny object:** flash it toward aircraft or people, sweeping the beam across them. Practise with the sun behind you.
3. **Fire and smoke:** three fires in a line or a triangle, 100 feet apart if you can. At night, bright flame. In daylight, add green leaves or damp wood for thick white smoke.
4. **Ground signals,** big and in contrast with the ground, at least 10 feet (3 m) long, made of rocks, logs, or trampled snow:
   - **V**: we need help
   - **X**: we need medical help
   - **Arrow**: we went in this direction
   - **SOS**: written large
5. **Bright cloth or clothing** in an open space, hung high.
6. **Phone:** save battery. Text, which often works when calls do not, and turn on low-power mode. Emergency location apps help rescuers find you.
7. **Stay put** and in the open. **Do not waste energy** on a signal that no one can see.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased; US National Park Service and Coast Guard guidance.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
