**Hypothermia** (body temperature under 95°F): shivering, tired, confused, clumsy, slurred speech, drowsy. In a baby: cold bright-red skin and very low energy. When shivering stops and the person is very sleepy, it is serious: **call 911**.

1. Move to a warm, dry place. Handle gently.
2. Take off wet clothing. Wrap in dry blankets, coats, even your own body heat. **Warm the chest, neck and head first**, not the arms and legs.
3. If awake and able to swallow, give warm sweet drinks. **No alcohol.**
4. Do not rub the skin and do not use hot water or a heating pad directly on the skin.

**Frostbite:** skin numb, white, gray-yellow or waxy, hard or stiff.
1. Get warm. Do not walk on frostbitten feet if you can avoid it.
2. Warm the area in warm (not hot) water. Do not rub it. Do not warm it if it could freeze again.
3. Cover it loosely with dry sterile cloth. Get medical care.

Source: CDC (hypothermia, frostbite) and Red Cross guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
