Plan to travel in daylight. If you must move at night, slow down and make it easy for yourself.

1. **Wait for first light** if you can. Night travel is slower and more dangerous.
2. **Let your eyes adjust** for about 30 minutes in the dark. White light ruins it in a second. Use a **red light** if you have one, and shield it.
3. **Look slightly to the side** of what you want to see. Dim things show up better at the edge of your sight than in the middle.
4. **Pick routes with handrails:** a road, a fence line, a stream or a ridge you can follow by feel or sound.
5. **Know what will tell you to stop** (a road, a river, a clear change of slope) so you do not walk past your goal.
6. **Count your paces** on a measured leg and stay in contact with your compass bearing. Distances feel longer at night.
7. **Walk slowly,** feet first on the ground before you put your weight down, hands in front to find branches.
8. **Stop and rest** if you are unsure. Resting and starting again at dawn is better than getting hurt.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on night movement, paraphrased

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
