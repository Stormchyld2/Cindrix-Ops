**Say it plainly. FRS is a shared channel and anyone can hear it. Call 911 if you can reach it and someone is in danger.**

**Red** is danger to life: serious injury, fire, a missing person with urgent worry. **Amber** is a significant problem: failed radio, low power, a late return. **Green** is a routine "task done". Green does not mean the whole place is secure.

1. **Listen first**, then press and pause a moment before you speak.
2. **Say "Emergency, emergency."** Then who you are calling and who you are: "Base, this is Patrol One."
3. **Where you are.**
4. **What is wrong.**
5. **How many people** are affected.
6. **What you need.** End with "Over."
7. **Base repeats the important details back** and sends help. Everyone else stops talking and keeps the channel clear.
8. **After the danger is handled,** write it in the log.

**Example:** "Emergency, emergency. Base, this is Patrol One. Medical emergency near the south trail entrance. One injured adult, conscious and breathing. Request first-aid help. Over."

**Order of traffic:** danger to life, then urgent medical or safety, then lost contact or someone overdue, then weather warnings, then routine reports, then everything else.

**Call 911 for:** any serious injury, fire or threat, when you can reach it.

Source: Cindrix Communications and Watch Operations Plan (COPS-COM-001, section 8), the family's own draft standard. Check it against your actual radios before relying on it.

Reference only, not medical or legal advice. In an emergency, call 911.
