**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. Cut the line off. Wash the area with soap and water.
2. If the hook is only in the skin and the barb is not buried, you may back it out the way it went in.
3. If the barb is under the skin: push the hook point through until the barb shows, snip off the barb with clean cutters, then back the hook out. Skip this if the hook is near an eye, in the face, or deep.
4. Clean the wound again, cover it with a bandage, and watch for infection.
5. Check your tetanus shot status; ask a doctor if it is more than 10 years old.

**See a doctor for:** a hook in the eye, face, a joint, or a tendon; a deep hook you cannot move; or signs of infection (spreading redness, warmth, pus, fever).

Source: Wilderness Medical Society and Red Cross first-aid guidance.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
