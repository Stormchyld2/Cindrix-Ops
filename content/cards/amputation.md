**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

1. **Call 911 right away.**
2. Lay the person down and raise the injured area if you can.
3. **Press firmly and steadily on the wound** with clean cloth for a full 15 minutes. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top. Do not lift the first one. If bleeding is life-threatening and will not stop, use a tourniquet if you have been trained.
4. Cover the wound with a clean dressing and treat for shock: keep the person warm and lying down.
5. **The cut-off part:** rinse off dirt gently with clean water, wrap it in dry sterile gauze or a clean cloth, put it in a plastic bag or waterproof container, and set that on ice. **Never put the part straight into ice or ice water.**
6. Send the part to the hospital with the person.
7. For a **part still attached,** raise it, cover it, press lightly to slow bleeding without cutting off all blood flow, and keep it still with a splint.

**Call 911** for every amputation.

Source: University of Michigan Health and Kaiser Permanente, Emergency care for an accidental amputation (Healthwise).

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
