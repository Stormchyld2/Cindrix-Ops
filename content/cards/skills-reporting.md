When you call for help or tell others what you have seen, give clear facts in the same order every time.

1. **Who:** how many people, and their ages and condition if it matters.
2. **What:** what happened or what you saw, in one sentence.
3. **Where:** street address, nearby landmarks, or a grid reference. Say how you can be found.
4. **When:** the time it happened or when you first saw it.
5. **What you have done:** first aid, shutting off power, leaving the area.
6. **What you need:** an ambulance, fire service, police, a rescue, supplies.
7. **For something you observed,** also add: how many, what they were doing, which way they were moving, what they carried, and how long you watched.
8. **Stick to what you saw.** Say "it looked like" if you are not sure. Do not guess motives.
9. **Write it down** as soon as you can, with the time. Facts fade fast.
10. **Repeat the address** at the end of a call and stay on the line until told to hang up.

Source: Ready.gov and US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) on reports, simplified for civilians

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
