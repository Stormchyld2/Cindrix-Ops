**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

1. **Measure your pace.** On flat ground, lay out 100 metres (about 328 feet). Walk it naturally and count **every second step** (each time your right foot lands). Do it a few times and take the average. Many people get around 60 to 70 double steps per 100 metres.
2. **Write it on a card.** Note that it **changes**: more steps uphill, in sand, snow or mud, with a pack, or when tired.
3. **Count in your head in hundreds,** and move a pebble or knot a bead on a cord every 100 metres.
4. To work out distance: **paces walked divided by your paces per 100 metres,** times 100 metres.
5. **Time works too.** Many people walk about 4 kilometres (2.5 miles) per hour on level ground, about half that in rough country.
6. Keep a **running note** of bearing and distance for each leg of your route.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
