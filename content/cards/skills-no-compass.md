**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

1. **The sun** rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west. At noon in the northern hemisphere, it is in the south.
2. **Shadow stick:** push a straight stick upright into level ground. Mark the tip of its shadow with a stone. Wait 15 minutes and mark the new tip. A line from the first mark through the second runs **west to east**. Stand with the first mark on your left and the second on your right, and you face north.
3. **Watch method (northern hemisphere):** point the hour hand at the sun. Halfway between the hour hand and 12 o'clock is south. (Use 1 o'clock in daylight saving time.)
4. **The night sky:** find the **North Star (Polaris).** Find the Big Dipper and follow the two stars at the front edge of its bowl up, about five times their gap. The North Star is there. It is due north.
5. **Check with two methods** if you can. Moss and tree growth are **not reliable.**
6. If you know the area, use **roads, rivers, power lines and railways** to get your bearings.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
