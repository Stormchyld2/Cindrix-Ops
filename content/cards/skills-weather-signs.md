**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

1. **Falling pressure and rising wind** often mean a storm is coming. A barometer or phone barometer helps.
2. **Dark, towering clouds** and a sudden temperature drop mean thunderstorms. Move to shelter. See the **lightning** card.
3. **Wide grey, low cloud** and steady rain can last for a day or more.
4. **A ring around the moon or sun,** with thin, high clouds, often means rain within a day.
5. **Red sky at night** often means good weather ahead (the old saying works in many mid-latitude places, not everywhere).
6. **Local knowledge beats sayings.** Ask locals, and watch what happens.
7. **Use an official forecast** if you can: a battery or hand-crank NOAA weather radio gives warnings.
8. **Get off ridges and out of open ground** when a storm builds.

Source: National Weather Service weather safety guidance; US Army Ranger Handbook, paraphrased.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
