**DRAFT. Written from general field guidance and not yet checked line by line against its source or by a qualified person.**

1. **Stop.** Sit down. Do not run or keep walking in a panic.
2. **Think.** What do you know? Where did you last know your place? Which way did you come? What do you have?
3. **Observe.** Look around: landmarks, water, hills, roads, the sun, the weather, how much light is left.
4. **Plan.** Decide to **stay** or **go** before you move.

**Stay if:** someone knows where you are and when to look for you, you are hurt, it is getting dark or storming, or you are in the open where you can be seen. Make yourself easy to find (see the signalling card) and keep warm and dry.

**Go only if** you know exactly where you are going, such as a road you can see, a trail you know or a stream you can follow downhill, and you have daylight, water and the strength.

If you move, **leave a note** with your name, the time and your direction, and mark your trail.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76), paraphrased; US Forest Service lost-hiker guidance.

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
