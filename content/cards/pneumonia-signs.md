Pneumonia is an infection of the lungs. It can follow a cold, the flu or COVID-19, and it needs a health care provider.

**Common signs:** cough, fever or chills, shortness of breath, chest pain when breathing or coughing, tiredness, nausea, vomiting or diarrhea, and confusion (most of all in older people).

**Be extra watchful** with older people, young children, people with long-term illness or a weak immune system, and anyone who smokes.

**A cold or flu may be turning into something worse if** the fever or cough gets better and then comes back or gets worse.

1. If you think it could be pneumonia, **see a health care provider promptly.** Do not wait it out.
2. While you wait: rest, sip fluids, and keep away from others.
3. Antibiotics are used when a provider says the cause is bacteria. Do not take leftover ones.
4. Write down the temperature and when signs started, to tell the provider.

**Call 911 for:** trouble breathing, chest pain or pressure that does not go away, new confusion, cannot wake up, or blue, gray or very pale lips or skin.

Source: CDC, About Pneumonia, and CDC, Managing the Common Cold, checked 2026-10-31.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
