When you are close to danger, or cannot be heard, signals let a group move together. These are a suggested set: any signals work if **everyone knows them** and practises.

1. **Stop:** hold a clenched fist up beside your head.
2. **Go / move on:** point forward and swing your arm in a forward arc.
3. **Slow down:** palm down, press the air gently.
4. **Quiet:** finger across the lips.
5. **Danger ahead:** hand flat across the throat, then point toward it.
6. **Look / listen:** point to your eye or ear, then toward what you mean.
7. **Gather here:** circle a raised hand above your head.
8. **Spread out / close up:** arms apart for spread out, hands together for close up.
9. **Agree the replies,** such as a thumb up for "I understand".
10. **Practise in daylight,** then in low light, until nobody needs to ask what a signal means.
11. **Keep the leader and the last person** in sight of each other.

Source: Adapted from the hand-and-arm signals in the US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76); a civilian set, simplified

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
