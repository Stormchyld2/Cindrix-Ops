A six-digit grid reference names a spot on a grid map to within about 100 metres.

1. **Find the grid lines** on your map. They run both ways and are numbered along the edges.
2. **Read right, then up.** First the number on the vertical line just left of the spot (the easting), then the number on the horizontal line just below it (the northing).
3. **Use two digits from the line number**, then estimate **a third digit** for how far across the square the spot is, in tenths (0 to 9). That gives three digits for the right-reading and three for the up-reading.
4. **Example:** vertical line 43, spot about six tenths of the way to line 44: 436. Horizontal line 77, spot about two tenths up: 772. The reference is **436772**.
5. **Say it clearly,** in two groups of three digits: "four three six, seven seven two".
6. **To find a place from a reference,** go along the bottom to the first three digits, then up to the last three, and mark where they meet.
7. **A grid tool helps.** A plotting card or the corner scale of your compass lays out the tenths so you do not have to guess.
8. **Check twice.** Reading the numbers in the wrong order is the most common mistake: right first, then up.

Source: US Army Ranger Handbook (SH 21-76) and FM 3-25.26 on grid coordinates, paraphrased

Reference only. Practise these skills before you need them.
