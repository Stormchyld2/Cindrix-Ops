**Signs:** ear pain, fever, a fussy child, rubbing or tugging at the ear, trouble sleeping. Colds and other infections can cause it.

1. Rest and give extra fluids.
2. Pain and fever medicine can ease the pain but does not cure the infection. **Follow the label, and ask a pharmacist or doctor what is safe for a baby or child.** Babies under 6 months can have only acetaminophen.
3. **Never give aspirin to a child or teen.**
4. Many ear infections clear up on their own in 2 to 3 days. A provider may choose to wait and watch, or may prescribe an antibiotic. A severe infection, or one lasting more than 2 to 3 days, needs an antibiotic right away.
5. Wash hands often to help stop colds and ear infections.

**See a doctor for:** a fever of 102.2°F (39°C) or higher, pus or fluid coming from the ear, pain that gets worse, signs lasting more than 2 to 3 days, or any hearing loss.

**A baby under 3 months old with a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher needs medical care right away.** Call 911 if you cannot reach care.

Source: CDC, Ear Infection Basics, checked 2026-10-31.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
