A cold brings a runny nose, cough and sore throat, and sometimes a mild fever. It comes on slowly and usually gets better on its own. Antibiotics do not work on colds.

1. Rest and drink plenty of fluids.
2. Use salt-water nose drops or spray. For a small child, a rubber suction bulb clears a stuffy nose.
3. Breathe steam from a hot shower, or use a clean cool-mist humidifier.
4. Throat lozenges or cough drops help a sore throat (not for children under 4).
5. Honey soothes a cough in anyone **1 year or older**. **Never give honey to a baby under 1.**
6. Do not give cough and cold medicine to children under 6. It can cause serious harm.
7. Wash your hands often and cover coughs so the cold does not spread.

**Get medical care for:** fast or hard breathing, signs of dehydration, a fever longer than 4 days, signs that last more than 10 days without getting better, a fever or cough that gets better and then comes back worse, or a long-term illness that is getting worse. Contact a provider right away if you are at higher risk from COVID-19 or the flu, even if you feel only a little sick.

**Call 911 for:** trouble breathing, blue or gray lips, new confusion, or cannot be woken.

Source: CDC, Managing the Common Cold, checked 2026-10-31.

Reference only, not medical advice. In an emergency, call 911.
