---
title: 72-hour power outage
minutes: 25
where: at home
summary: Light, water, food, phones, medications and a plan for the first three days.
status: draft
---
Walk through the house and actually look, do not just tick.
- Find flashlights, lanterns and spare batteries. Test each one.
- Count drinking water for everyone, for three days (about 1 gallon per person per day)
- Charge phones and battery banks, then switch phones to low-power mode
- Gather medications and glucose supplies in one place, and a small cooler for any that must stay cool
- Keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed; note the time the power went out
- Pick a safe warm or cool room, and never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors
- Write down who to call and where to meet if you are separated
- Check food that needs no cooking and a way to open and heat it safely

## Pass mark
- Every light works, three days of water is counted, and phones are charged
- Everyone can say where the medicines, the radio and the meeting place are
- Done in under 25 minutes with nothing left on a "to buy" list you cannot fill this week
## Common mistakes
- Lights found but not tested, so the batteries are dead when needed
- Counting bottled water but forgetting pets and cooking water
- Opening the fridge to check
## Make it harder
- Do it at night with the power really off at the breaker for two hours
- Add a one-week version: count food, fuel and water for seven days
## With less gear
- No lanterns: a headlamp or phone light and a jar of water under it spreads light
- No stored water: fill tubs, pots and bottles at once and treat them later
