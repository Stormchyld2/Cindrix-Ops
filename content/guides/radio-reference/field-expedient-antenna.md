**A simple wire dipole can be made from a few feet of wire, two supports and a cable, and it works well for HF, VHF and UHF.**

**Length of a half-wave dipole:** total length in feet = **468 divided by the frequency in MHz.** Cut it in two equal halves.

**Examples:**
- 7.1 MHz (40 metres): 468 / 7.1 = about 66 feet total, 33 feet each side.
- 14.2 MHz (20 metres): about 33 feet total, 16.5 feet each side.
- 146 MHz (2 metres): about 3.2 feet total, 19 inches each side.
- 462.7 MHz (GMRS): about 1 foot total, 6 inches each side.

**Building one:**
1. Cut two equal lengths of insulated wire for the "arms".
2. Connect the centre conductor of the coaxial cable to one arm and the braid (outer shield) to the other. Insulate the connection, and tie a strain relief so the cable does not pull on it.
3. Hang the arms in a straight line (or a shallow "V" shape) with rope at the ends. Insulators at the ends can be plastic, dry wood or a plastic bottle cap.
4. Raise it as high as possible. Tie the ends in trees, on a pole, or on a building.
5. Keep the cable running away from the antenna at a right angle, if possible, for the first few feet.
6. Test with an SWR meter if you have one. Trim a little at a time if needed (cut shorter to raise the frequency, longer to lower it). Wire stretches and trees change things.

**Alternative:** a long wire fed with a tuner (an "end-fed") is useful for several HF bands. Make it at least a half-wavelength long and keep it away from metal.

**Safety:** keep wire antennas away from power lines. Do not touch an antenna while transmitting. Keep the antenna above head height, so nobody walks into it.

Source: ARRL and standard amateur radio practice (half-wave dipole formula 468 divided by frequency in MHz). Not yet reviewed.
