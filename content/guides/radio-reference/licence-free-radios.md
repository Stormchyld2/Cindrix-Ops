**Which radio, and do you need a licence?** This is a short summary of the United States rules. Rules differ in other countries and can change, so check the official FCC pages for anything important.

- **FRS (Family Radio Service):** the small "bubble pack" radios. No licence. 22 channels. Short range, usually about a mile or two between people on foot. Some channels are limited to very low power.
- **GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service):** the same channels as FRS plus more power, and radios that can use repeaters. It needs an **FCC licence** (there is no test). One licence covers your whole family. Check fcc.gov for the current fee and rules.
- **MURS (Multi-Use Radio Service):** 5 channels. No licence. Often used with outdoor sensors and small business radios.
- **CB (Citizens Band):** 40 channels. No licence. Longer range with a good antenna, and a mixed crowd of listeners. Channel 9 is for emergencies.
- **Amateur ("ham") radio:** a test and a licence, but far more power, range and choice. The best choice if you are serious about radio.

**Range is nearly always shorter than the box says.** Hills, buildings and trees block these radios. Test yours before you need them.

**Keep the radios charged, and keep spare batteries.** Practise with them. See the card on a family radio plan.

Source: US Federal Communications Commission (FCC), personal radio services, 47 CFR Part 95 (fcc.gov). Check fcc.gov for the current rules.
