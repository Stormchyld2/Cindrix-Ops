**These simple shapes are understood by pilots and rescue crews. Make them large, high-contrast and straight.**

**The code (make each symbol at least 10 feet long, preferably 20 or more):**
- **V:** we require assistance.
- **X:** we require medical assistance.
- **N:** no, negative.
- **Y:** yes, affirmative.
- **An arrow:** we are going in the direction it points.
- **SOS** or **three lines/fires:** distress.
- **Box with X or a large "I":** serious injury, need doctor.

**How to make them:**
1. Choose an **open area** with a clear view of the sky, not under trees. Beaches, snow, fields and rock ledges are good.
2. Make the symbol with **contrasting material:** rocks, logs, branches, tarps, clothing, or by stamping or digging in snow or sand.
3. **Make it tall:** shadows help. Dark on snow, light on dark ground. Piles of material give shadows.
4. **Keep the lines straight and the corners sharp.** They show it is man-made.
5. **Use as many materials as you can** to make it as large as you can.

**When you hear an aircraft:**
1. Stand in the open and wave both arms above your head in a "Y" shape.
2. Use a mirror, a flashlight, or a bright cloth on a stick.
3. Light a signal fire if safe.
4. **A pilot who rocks the wings** is acknowledging that they've seen you. **A pilot who flies a circle or figure eight** may be directing someone to your position.

**Stay near your signal** and your shelter.

**Be careful** that your signal does not set a fire. Clear the ground, have water to put it out, and check the wind.

Source: International ground-to-air emergency code, as taught by wilderness rescue organisations and the US military and Coast Guard. Not yet reviewed.
