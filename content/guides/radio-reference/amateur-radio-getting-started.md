**Ham radio is the most powerful and flexible radio you can use, and it has a long record in disasters.** It needs a licence, which means a short exam.

**The licences (United States):**
1. **Technician:** the starting licence. 35 multiple-choice questions from a public pool. Gives all VHF and UHF privileges (for local and regional radio) and some HF.
2. **General:** adds large parts of the HF bands, which reach around the world.
3. **Amateur Extra:** all privileges.

**How to study:** the question pools are public. Free study sites and apps, library books, and local clubs help. Many people pass after a few weeks of study. Volunteer examiners run exams in most areas (some online). Ask a local ham club.

**Your first radio:** a small handheld for the 2-metre (144 MHz) and 70-centimetre (430 MHz) bands. Inexpensive models exist. Learn to use it before you need it.

**Where to listen and talk:**
- **146.520 MHz** is the national 2-metre simplex calling frequency (radio-to-radio without a repeater). **446.000 MHz** is the 70 cm one.
- **Repeaters** extend range to 25 to 50 miles. Your local club has a list.
- **Nets:** regular scheduled meetings on the radio, including weekly emergency nets run by clubs and ARES/RACES groups.

**What you give up:** a call sign is public and rules apply. You may not use it for business, and you may not use codes to hide the meaning of messages.

**Emergency role:** hams run radio links for shelters, hospitals and county emergency offices when phones fail. Even if you never expect to do that, a ham radio lets you hear and be heard far beyond family radios.

**For a family plan:** every licensed member should keep their call sign card and a radio ready. An unlicensed family member can use a ham radio only in a real emergency to save life and property.

Source: FCC rules (47 CFR Parts 95 and 97) and FCC consumer guides, paraphrased, as understood in the United States. Rules change: check fcc.gov before you rely on a number. Not yet reviewed. ARRL (American Radio Relay League) beginner guidance.
