**NOAA Weather Radio broadcasts weather, flood, fire and civil emergency alerts, 24 hours a day, from the National Weather Service.** It works without the internet or a phone signal.

**How it works:**
- It transmits on **seven VHF frequencies: 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525 and 162.550 MHz.**
- A **"Public Alert" or "SAME" radio** can be set to your county (a six-digit code) so that it sounds a tone only for warnings that affect you. It can wake you at night.
- Range is about 40 miles from a transmitter, less in hills.

**What to buy:** a radio marked **"Public Alert"** or "NOAA Weather Radio with SAME". Look for battery power (and a hand crank or solar for backup), and a battery backup if mains-powered.

**Setting it up:**
1. Find the SAME code for your county at weather.gov/nwr/counties.
2. Choose the strongest channel.
3. Set it to alert you only for the warning types you want, and keep **tornado, flash flood and severe thunderstorm warnings** on.
4. Test it: the weekly test is usually on Wednesday around midday, and your radio's test button works any time.

**Also alerts for:** wildfire, chemical or radiological emergencies, earthquakes, tsunami, and AMBER alerts.

**Tip:** keep one on the bedside table and one in the car or go-bag. Check the batteries on the 1st of the month.

**Not a replacement** for a plan: a warning is useful only if you know where to go. See the plans in the Plan screen.
