**One way of communicating will fail. A PACE plan gives you four, in order.**

- **P: Primary.** The way you normally use. Example: a mobile phone call.
- **A: Alternate.** A second way that doesn't depend on the first. Example: a text message, or a call using a different carrier or a landline.
- **C: Contingency.** The way you use when the usual infrastructure is gone. Example: licence-free radios (FRS/GMRS) on an agreed channel at agreed times.
- **E: Emergency.** The last resort. Example: a visible signal at a known meeting place, a written note, or a ham radio on a net.

**Write one for each situation**, such as "family members in different places" and "the house is evacuated". Include:
1. **Who:** the people who need to be reached.
2. **How:** the method at each level, with numbers, channels, and call signs written down.
3. **When:** check-in times, for example every day at 6pm for 5 minutes when a radio plan is in force.
4. **Where:** a place to go when no one can be reached (see your rendezvous points on the Map).
5. **A phone number outside your area**, because after local disasters, out-of-state calls often connect when local ones fail.

**Rules:**
- Each level must work without the one before it.
- Practise at least twice a year, as a drill, and write down what failed.
- Put a printed copy in every bag and car. Do not rely on a phone to hold the plan.
- Keep the plan simple enough for a child to follow.

Use the **Comms plan** screen to record your family's plan.

Source: Military and emergency-management communications planning practice (Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency), widely taught by FEMA and emergency-management programs. Not yet reviewed.
