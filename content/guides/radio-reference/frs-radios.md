**FRS (Family Radio Service) radios need no licence and no test.** They are the blister-pack walkie-talkies sold in any store. They are fine for staying in touch at a campsite, a store, or a short walk away.

**What to expect:**
- **22 channels** at 462 and 467 MHz. Channels 8 to 14 are low power (about half a watt); the rest allow up to 2 watts.
- **Real range:** the packages say "up to 30 miles". Real range is **a quarter mile to one mile** in woods or town, and 1 to 2 miles in open ground. Buildings, hills and trees cut it sharply.
- **Fixed antennas:** the antenna cannot be changed, so power and range stay limited.
- **Privacy codes** (CTCSS/DCS "sub-channels") only mute other people's chatter. **They are not private or secret.** Anyone on the channel can still hear you.

**Tips:**
1. Set everyone's radios to the same channel and code before you need them.
2. Hold the radio upright, away from your body, and as high as possible.
3. Keep batteries fresh: use lithium or rechargeable batteries and carry spares.
4. Say "over" when you finish; wait a second before you talk.
5. Test every few months. Radios that sit unused in a drawer often have flat batteries and corroded contacts.

**Limits:** not good for long distance, hills, or from inside a building. If you want more range, look at GMRS.

**Emergency use:** FRS can be used in an emergency, including channel 9 (462.6125 MHz) or any channel you agree on. Listen first so you do not talk over someone else.

Source: FCC rules (47 CFR Parts 95 and 97) and FCC consumer guides, paraphrased, as understood in the United States. Rules change: check fcc.gov before you rely on a number. Not yet reviewed.
