**Print this page. These are the numbers to know when the phones are gone. (United States.)**

**Family and short range (no exam):** **FRS/GMRS** channels 1 to 22 around 462 and 467 MHz. **MURS** 151.820, 151.880, 151.940, 154.570, 154.600 MHz. **CB** channels 1 to 40 around 27 MHz.

**Emergency and calling:** **CB channel 9** (27.065 MHz) is the emergency channel. **CB channel 19** (27.185 MHz) is the highway channel. **Marine VHF channel 16** (156.800 MHz) is distress and calling. **Aircraft emergency** 121.500 MHz. **Amateur 2 metre calling** 146.520 MHz and **70 cm calling** 446.000 MHz.

**Weather:** **NOAA Weather Radio** 162.400, 162.425, 162.450, 162.475, 162.500, 162.525 and 162.550 MHz. A weather radio picks the nearest.

**Amateur radio (licence needed to transmit):** 2 metres (144 to 148 MHz), 70 cm (420 to 450 MHz), 6 m (50 to 54 MHz). HF nets: Hurricane Watch Net 14.325 and 7.268 MHz; Maritime Mobile Service Net 14.300 MHz. Check current net lists.

**Time signals:** WWV and WWVH on 2.5, 5, 10, 15 and 20 MHz; WWVB 60 kHz for clocks.

**Satellite beacons:** Personal locator beacons transmit on 406 MHz to the satellite system.

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
