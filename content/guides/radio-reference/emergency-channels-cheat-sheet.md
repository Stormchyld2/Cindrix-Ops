**Print this page and keep it with your radios. Only use a service you are allowed to use.**

**Licence-free (anyone can use):**
- **FRS / GMRS channel 1-22 (462 / 467 MHz):** use agreed family channels. **Channel 20 (462.675 MHz)** is commonly used for emergency and traveller calls (a GMRS licence is needed above FRS power).
- **FRS channel 9 (462.6125 MHz):** often watched for emergencies in some areas.
- **MURS (151.820, 151.880, 151.940, 154.570, 154.600 MHz).**
- **CB channel 9 (27.065 MHz):** emergency and travellers; **channel 19 (27.185 MHz):** trucks and road information.

**Weather (listen only):**
- **NOAA Weather Radio:** 162.400 to 162.550 MHz.

**Amateur radio (licence needed):**
- **146.520 MHz:** 2 m national simplex calling.
- **446.000 MHz:** 70 cm national simplex calling.
- **Local repeaters:** from your club's list.
- **HF emergency nets:** often 3.9 MHz and 7.2 MHz at night, 14.3 MHz by day (check local nets).

**Time (listen only):**
- **WWV:** 2.5, 5, 10, 15 and 20 MHz.

**Distress:**
- **406 MHz:** personal locator beacons (satellite).
- **121.5 MHz:** aircraft emergency, monitored less than it used to be.
- **Marine VHF channel 16 (156.800 MHz):** boaters' distress and calling channel.

**Reminders:**
1. **Call for help in plain words:** "Help, emergency, this is [name], at [place]..." Repeat it slowly. See the calling for help page.
2. **Listen before you talk.**
3. **Do not transmit** on frequencies where you have no licence unless you must save life and have no other way.
4. **Write the local numbers** (sheriff dispatch, county emergency office, hospital) on this sheet.

Source: FCC allocations, NOAA, amateur radio calling frequencies and CB channel use. Frequencies and local practice can change: confirm for your area. Not yet reviewed.
