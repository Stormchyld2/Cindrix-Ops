**HF (shortwave, 3 to 30 MHz) bounces off the ionosphere and goes hundreds or thousands of miles.**

**Day and night:** Higher bands (20 m, 15 m, 10 m) are best in daylight. Lower bands (80 m, 40 m) are better at night. 40 m works day and night over a few hundred miles.

**NVIS:** For local and regional emergency use (0 to about 300 miles), use 40 m or 80 m with a low horizontal antenna (a dipole 10 to 20 feet up). The signal goes up and comes straight back down, filling the gap that line-of-sight radios leave.

**Sunspots:** At high sunspot years, 10 m and 15 m carry long distances. At low sunspot years, 20 m and 40 m do most of the work.

**Check:** WWV broadcasts propagation conditions at 18 minutes past the hour (a voice report on 5, 10, 15 and 20 MHz).

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
