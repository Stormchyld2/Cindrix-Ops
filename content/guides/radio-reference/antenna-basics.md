**A better antenna often helps more than more power.** Height, clear space, and the right length matter most.

**The rules of thumb:**
1. **Higher is better.** For VHF and UHF (handhelds, GMRS, MURS), the radio signal travels mostly in a straight line. Height is range. Moving a handheld from ground level to a roof or a hilltop helps more than doubling the power.
2. **Clear of obstacles.** Metal roofs, trees, hills and walls block signals. Get outside, up, and away from large metal objects.
3. **Right length.** An antenna works best when it is about a quarter or half of the radio wave's length. The "rubber duck" on a handheld is a compromise: a longer whip antenna or a roof antenna gives a clearer signal.
4. **Good cable.** Use low-loss coaxial cable for runs over a few feet. The thin cable in cheap kits loses much of the signal at VHF/UHF.
5. **Matching.** An antenna must be matched to the radio (50 ohms). A mismatched antenna reflects power back and can damage a radio. An SWR meter checks it.
6. **Vertical for local, horizontal for long distance on HF.**

**Practical upgrades:**
- Swap a handheld's rubber antenna for a longer whip (check it is made for your band).
- Use a small mag-mount antenna on a vehicle roof, or a mast on a house.
- Connect the handheld to a mobile antenna with an adapter cable.
- A simple outdoor antenna on a pole, connected through a cable, extends a base station.

**Safety:**
- **Look up before you raise a mast.** Contact with power lines kills. Keep masts and wires at least the length of the mast, plus 10 feet, from power lines.
- Do not mount antennas during thunderstorms. Disconnect the antenna cable during storms, or use a lightning arrestor and ground.
- Never transmit with the antenna disconnected: it can damage the radio.

Source: ARRL Antenna Book and standard amateur radio practice, simplified. Not yet reviewed.
