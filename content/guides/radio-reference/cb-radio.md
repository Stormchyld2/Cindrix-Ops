**CB (Citizens Band) is a licence-free radio service at about 27 MHz with 40 channels.** It was made for trucks and families and is still found in trucks, farm vehicles, and in rural areas.

**Basics:**
- **Power:** up to 4 watts on AM (12 watts on single-sideband).
- **Range:** a vehicle radio on a good antenna may reach 3 to 10 miles on the ground. Handheld CBs reach less. In some conditions, long-distance signals "skip" for hundreds of miles. This is unreliable and unpredictable.
- **Antenna:** CB wavelength is about 36 feet, so a good antenna is long (a 3 to 5 foot whip, or a full-length one) and should be tuned with an SWR meter.

**Important channels:**
- **Channel 9:** designated for emergency communications and travellers' assistance. It is often monitored by volunteers.
- **Channel 19:** the long-distance trucker channel, with road and weather reports.
- Other channels are used locally. Agree on one for your group.

**Rules:** you do not need a licence. Do not interfere, and do not use obscenities. Radios must be FCC-approved. Do not use linear amplifiers (illegal on CB).

**Interference:** CB shares the band with noisy electrical equipment, and the signal can be rough with engine noise.

**Good for:** keeping a vehicle convoy together, roadside help, and listening to the situation on the road. A CB is a cheap backup, but not a first choice for a family plan where everyone has to carry the radio.

Source: FCC rules (47 CFR Parts 95 and 97) and FCC consumer guides, paraphrased, as understood in the United States. Rules change: check fcc.gov before you rely on a number. Not yet reviewed.
