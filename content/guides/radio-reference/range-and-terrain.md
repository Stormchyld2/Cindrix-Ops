**Radio range is mostly decided by what is between the two radios, not by the number on the package.**

**VHF and UHF (FRS, GMRS, MURS, ham 2 m / 70 cm) travel in nearly straight lines.** Anything solid in the way, such as hills, buildings and dense trees, blocks or weakens them.

**Rough real-world ranges for handhelds (2 to 5 watts):**
- Dense woods or town: **a quarter mile to one mile.**
- Suburbs: **one to two miles.**
- Open fields, from high ground: **three to ten miles.**
- Hilltop to hilltop: **ten miles or more.**
- Inside a building or vehicle: much less.

**How to get more:**
1. **Go up.** A second storey, a roof or a hilltop makes a larger difference than more power.
2. **Move a few steps.** A signal that fails in one spot may work 20 feet away (reflections and shadows).
3. **Face the other station,** and hold the radio vertical, away from your body.
4. **Use a better antenna,** and low-loss cable.
5. **Use a repeater** (ham or GMRS) when you must cover a wide area.
6. **Use simplex relays:** a station at high ground relays messages.

**HF radio** (3 to 30 MHz) bounces off the ionosphere and can reach hundreds or thousands of miles. Which band works depends on the time of day and the season: lower bands (80 and 40 metres) by night, higher bands (20, 15 and 10 metres) by day.

**Plan around the weakest link:** test the real range between your home and the places you expect to go (work, school, relatives), and write the results into the Comms plan.

Source: Standard VHF/UHF propagation practice (line of sight) and ARRL guidance; real ranges vary widely. Not yet reviewed.
