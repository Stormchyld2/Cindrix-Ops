**GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service) is the step up from the blister-pack walkie-talkie.** It uses the same 462 and 467 MHz channels as FRS but allows more power, bigger antennas, and repeaters, so it reaches much farther.

**You need a licence, but not a test.** In the United States you apply to the FCC and pay a fee (check fcc.gov for the amount and the term). **One licence covers your whole immediate family**, so the children, spouse and in-laws living with you may all use it. The licensee gives the call sign.

**What you get:**
- **Power:** handhelds are typically 2 to 5 watts; mobile radios up to 50 watts.
- **Range:** handheld to handheld, about 1 to 3 miles in towns, further in open country. Vehicle radios on a roof antenna often 5 to 10 miles. **Repeaters** (a tall antenna that re-sends your signal) can give 20 miles or more.
- **Channels:** the same 462 MHz channels as FRS, plus more GMRS-only channels at 462 MHz and repeater channels near 467 MHz. **462.675 MHz** is widely used for roadside assistance and emergency calls.

**Say your call sign** at least every 15 minutes during a conversation and at the end. Do not use it for business. Do not use profane language.

**Good to know:**
- FRS-only radios can talk to GMRS radios on the shared channels.
- Many cheap radios sold "for both" are labelled GMRS: if they transmit above 2 watts, you need the licence.
- A radio with removable antenna and higher power is the best buy for a vehicle.

**Plan:** put GMRS channel and "privacy code" settings in the Comms plan so every radio in the family uses the same ones.

Source: FCC rules (47 CFR Parts 95 and 97) and FCC consumer guides, paraphrased, as understood in the United States. Rules change: check fcc.gov before you rely on a number. Not yet reviewed.
