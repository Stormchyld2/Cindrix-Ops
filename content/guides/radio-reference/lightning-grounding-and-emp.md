**Radio equipment is damaged most often by lightning, not by exotic events.**

**Lightning:** Disconnect antennas from radios in a storm and move the cable outside the building. Install a lightning arrestor and a grounding rod at the entry point. Do not use a radio outdoors in a thunderstorm.

**Grounding:** Connect radio cases and the antenna system to a ground rod (8 feet in the earth), with short, thick wire.

**EMP and solar storms:** A big solar storm or a nuclear blast high in the sky could damage electronics. Evidence is limited. A cheap protection is to keep a **spare** handheld radio, spare batteries, and a receiver inside a sealed metal container (a metal ammo can or trash can with a lid, with the contents insulated from the metal with cardboard).

**Spares:** The best EMP plan is a second set of the cheapest radios, unpowered and stored away.

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
