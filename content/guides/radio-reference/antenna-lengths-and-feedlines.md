**The right length matters more than power.**

**Half-wave dipole:** Total length in feet = **468 divided by the frequency in MHz**. For 7.1 MHz (40 metres) that is about 66 feet, cut in the centre with feed cable attached; each leg 33 feet.

**Quarter-wave vertical:** Length in feet = **234 divided by the frequency in MHz**. For 146 MHz (2 metres) about 19 inches.

**2 metre / 70 cm J-pole:** Easy to build from copper pipe or twin-lead, about 19 inches per quarter wavelength on 2 metres.

**Height:** Higher is better. Get an antenna above roofs and trees. For VHF and UHF, height is worth more than power.

**Cable:** Use good coax (RG-8X or LMR-400 for long runs; RG-58 for short). Long cheap coax loses much of the signal at VHF and UHF.

**SWR:** A meter that reads "standing wave ratio" shows if the antenna matches the radio. Aim for 1.5 or below, under 2.0 at worst. A high reading means a fault or the wrong length.

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
