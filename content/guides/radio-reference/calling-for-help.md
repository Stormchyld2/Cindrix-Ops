**Only for a real emergency: someone's life or safety is in danger.**

1. Say **"MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY"** (spoken for danger to life; it comes from the French "m'aidez", help me).
2. Say **where you are** and **who you are**. Give landmarks, a road, or a position.
3. Say **what is wrong**, how many people need help, and what you need.
4. Say **"Over"** and listen. If nobody answers, repeat the call. Keep the radio on.

**"PAN-PAN"** (said three times) is for an urgent problem when nobody is in immediate danger.

**Where to call:**
- **CB radio, channel 9:** the emergency channel in the United States. Some volunteers and drivers may be listening, but nobody is guaranteed to be.
- **Marine VHF, channel 16:** the international distress, safety and calling channel at sea.
- **Family radios (FRS) and the others** have no emergency channel that is always watched. Try the call anyway, and keep trying while you also try other ways of getting help.
- **Always try 911 first** if a phone works.

Do not use these calls as a test. Misuse can be punished.

Source: International Telecommunication Union (ITU) radio regulations for distress calls; US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) rules for CB radio (47 CFR Part 95) and US Coast Guard guidance on VHF channel 16.
