**If you travel where phones do not work, a beacon can bring rescue.**

**Personal locator beacon (PLB):**
- Transmits a distress signal on **406 MHz** that is picked up by satellites and sent to rescue coordination centres.
- **No subscription.** You register it once with NOAA (free) so rescuers know who you are.
- Battery lasts about 5 to 6 years standby and about 24 hours of use. The signal includes your **GPS position** if the beacon has GPS.
- It is for **life-threatening emergencies only.** False alarms waste rescue resources and may bring a fine.

**How to use one:**
1. Pull out the antenna, point it at the sky, and press the button.
2. **Stay put** with a clear view of the sky.
3. Keep it on until rescued. Do not turn it off. Keep warm and dry and keep the beacon above ground in the open.

**Satellite messengers** (a different product) allow two-way text messages and "check-in" messages, tracking, and an SOS button, but **need a paid subscription.** They work in remote areas and give peace of mind to those at home. They depend on a company's service.

**Vehicle emergency beacons** and ship (EPIRB) beacons are the same idea for cars and boats.

**Testing:** most beacons have a self-test button. Test it as directed by the maker, usually once a month. Never run the "activate" test.

**Plan:** tell someone where you are going and when you will be back. A beacon does not replace that.

Source: COSPAS-SARSAT and NOAA guidance on 406 MHz beacons; manufacturer documentation. Not yet reviewed.
