**A scanner is a radio that listens to many channels in turn.** It lets you hear police, fire, ambulance, weather and road crews in your area, and it can be the fastest way to know what is going on in a local emergency.

**Is it legal?** In most of the United States it is legal to own a scanner and listen to public safety, aviation, marine and amateur radio. Listening to cell and cordless phone calls is illegal. **Some states restrict scanners in vehicles,** and using what you hear to commit a crime or evade police is illegal. Check your state's law.

**Types:**
- **Analog scanners** are cheap but cannot hear systems that have gone digital.
- **Digital (P25) and trunked scanners** can follow modern public safety systems. In many places they are needed.
- **Encrypted channels** cannot be heard at all by any scanner.

**Getting started:**
1. Find the frequencies or system for your county in the **RadioReference** database (radioreference.com, online) *before* an emergency, and save them to a printed list or the scanner's memory.
2. Program fire, EMS, sheriff, state police, highway, and local weather channels.
3. Add NOAA weather, the national simplex calling frequencies, and the local ham repeater.

**Using one in an emergency:**
- Keep it low to avoid confusion and so you can talk to the family.
- Do not repeat rumours. Traffic may be wrong, incomplete or old.
- **Never interfere** with emergency traffic or transmit on public safety channels.

**Batteries:** handheld scanners use AA batteries or a rechargeable pack. Keep spares.

Source: FCC and state law summaries; the Electronic Communications Privacy Act. State and local rules differ, so check yours. Not yet reviewed.
