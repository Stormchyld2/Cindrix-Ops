**Writing a message in a set order stops mistakes, and lets others pass it on.**

**Radiogram order:** Number, precedence (Routine, Welfare, Priority, Emergency), station of origin, check (number of words in the message), place of origin, time and date filed; then the address (name, street, town, phone); then the text (25 words or fewer); then the signature.

**Example:** "1  W  KA1ABC  12  Boston MA  1400Z  Oct 24 / Anna Smith / 14 Oak St / Concord NH / Safe and well. Phone down. Will call when able. Love, Ben."

**Incident report:** Who, what happened, where, when, how many hurt, what help is needed, who is on scene, and the contact. Keep it short.

**Read back:** Always read back names, numbers and addresses. Use the phonetic alphabet.

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
