**Small, cheap LoRa radios can send short text messages between devices over several miles, with no licence, no cell tower and no internet.** Each radio can also pass on other people's messages, so they form a "mesh".

**What they do well:**
- **Short text messages and locations,** sent from a phone connected to the radio by Bluetooth.
- **Low power:** a small radio can run for days on a battery and charge from a small solar panel.
- **Range:** often **1 to 5 miles** between radios in the woods, and **10 miles or more** from high ground. Each radio in the middle adds range.
- **Free to use** in the United States, in the 902-928 MHz band, at low power.
- **Encryption** is available for messages between members of a group.

**What they do poorly:**
- **No voice.** Messages are short, and slow.
- **They depend on enough radios** in the right places. A mesh of two or three is only as good as the hills between them.
- **Everyone in range can see the traffic** unless you set a key.
- **They need a charged phone** and the app to use them in the usual way.

**For a family or street:**
1. Give each person a radio and set it up with a **common channel and key** before you need it.
2. Leave one or two radios on high ground (a roof or hill) as fixed relay points.
3. Agree on what messages mean ("OK" "need help" "meet at X").
4. Keep batteries charged.

**The Cindrix phone map** can be loaded on the same phone, so the same device shows where you are and sends a short note.

Source: Meshtastic project documentation and FCC rules for the 902-928 MHz ISM band (47 CFR Part 15). Rules, devices and range vary. Not yet reviewed.
