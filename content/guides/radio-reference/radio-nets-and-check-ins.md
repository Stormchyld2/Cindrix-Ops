**A net is a scheduled radio meeting with one person in charge.** A check-in schedule works the same way for a family or neighbourhood.

**Why scheduled times work:**
- Radios stay off to save batteries, and everyone switches on at the same time.
- A short, regular schedule means silence has a meaning: if someone does not check in, others know to look for them.
- It prevents the channel from being filled with idle talk.

**A simple family check-in plan:**
1. **Time:** for example, at 7:00 and 19:00 every day, for **five minutes only**.
2. **Channel:** the agreed channel. Write it down, with a second channel in case the first is busy.
3. **Order:** the net control (a regular, reliable person) calls each station by name. Each answers with their name, their status in one sentence, and any need.
4. **If nobody answers** in two calls, move on and try again at the end.
5. **If conditions change** (danger, a storm), switch to hourly checks.

**Net control duties:**
- Start on time, say what the net is for, and list who has checked in.
- Take emergency traffic first.
- Keep the net short.
- Log every check-in.
- Close the net and say when the next one is.

**Neighbourhood plan:** a street or group of farms can each hold a radio on the same channel. Choose a person for each block who checks on neighbours who have no radio.

**A common mistake:** everyone waiting for someone else to call. Agree who calls first and who calls when they don't.

**Practise** monthly with a short test net.

Source: Common practice in amateur radio emergency nets and community preparedness groups. Not yet reviewed.
