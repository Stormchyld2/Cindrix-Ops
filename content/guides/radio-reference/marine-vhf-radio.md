**Boats and coastal areas use VHF marine radios, and these are monitored by the Coast Guard.**

**Channel 16 (156.800 MHz):** Distress, safety and calling. Monitor it. Use for emergencies only; set up a conversation on another channel.

**Other channels:** 9 (156.450) is a recreational calling channel. 22A (157.100) is used by the Coast Guard for working and safety broadcasts. 13 is for bridge-to-bridge navigation. WX1 to WX7 are weather.

**Distress call:** "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday. This is (boat name) (three times). Mayday (boat name). Position, nature of distress, number of people, description of the boat." Then "Over."

**Urgent but not life-threatening:** "Pan-pan" three times. Safety broadcasts use "Securite".

**On land:** Using marine radio on land is for a real emergency only, and needs a licence for other use. Handheld units are common.

Source: Published United States radio rules and guidance (FCC Parts 95 and 97, NOAA, U.S. Coast Guard, FAA, ARRL). Frequencies and rules change and differ by country: check a current FCC or ARRL chart before relying on a number. Not yet checked line by line.
