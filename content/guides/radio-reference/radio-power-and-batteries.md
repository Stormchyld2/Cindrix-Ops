**A radio with a dead battery is a brick. Power is the weakest link in most plans.**

**Battery types:**
- **Alkaline (AA/AAA):** easy to find, 5-10 year shelf life, but weak in the cold and leak when old.
- **Lithium primary (AA):** best for storage: 10-15 years, work in cold. More expensive.
- **NiMH rechargeable (AA/AAA):** can be recharged hundreds of times, but lose charge in storage (low-self-discharge types such as "eneloop" hold it much better).
- **Lithium-ion packs** (in most handheld ham and GMRS radios): good capacity, but ageing sets in after 2-3 years, and they can be damaged when stored fully charged or fully empty. Store at roughly half charge in a cool place if not used for months.

**How much do you need?** A handheld listening most of the time and transmitting a little uses little power. **A good rule: transmit less than 10% of the time.** Typical handheld batteries last a day or more of scheduled check-ins.

**Charging without the grid:**
1. **Solar panel** with a controller and a USB or 12-volt output. A 10 to 20 watt panel charges a handheld battery in a day. A foldable panel suits a bag.
2. **12-volt car or deep-cycle battery** with a power cable for the radio.
3. **Power banks** for USB-charging radios. Keep them charged and top up every few months.
4. **Hand-crank radios** are good for weather and AM/FM reception, not for transmitting.

**Habits:**
- Keep **spare charged batteries** for every radio, labelled with the date.
- Turn radios **off** when not in use. Turn the volume and screen brightness down.
- Use **low power** when the other station is close.
- Test and rotate batteries twice a year when you do your drills.
- **Do not mix** old and new batteries or different brands in one radio.
- Keep batteries away from heat and metal objects (coins in a pocket can short them).

Source: Standard battery guidance (NiMH, lithium) and radio operating practice; manufacturer manuals. Not yet reviewed.
