**MURS (Multi-Use Radio Service) uses five VHF channels near 151 and 154 MHz. No licence is needed.**

- **Power:** up to 2 watts. Antennas may be changed or raised, which is where the range comes from.
- **Range:** a handheld reaches roughly 1 to 3 miles. A mast antenna on a house or hill can reach 5 miles or more. VHF travels better through trees than the 460 MHz radios do.
- **Channels:** five fixed channels at 151.820, 151.880, 151.940 MHz and 154.570 and 154.600 MHz (the last two are the "blue dot" and "green dot" channels used for years by shops).
- **Uses:** farms and property, hunting parties, home to barn or to a neighbour, and motion sensors and alarms.

**Rules:** no repeaters, no connecting to the telephone system, no use from aircraft, and the radio must be an approved MURS model.

**Why use it:** fewer people use it than FRS and CB, so the channels are less crowded. Many farms and small businesses have kept using them.

**Weakness:** fewer family-use models are sold, and your neighbours may not own one. Pick the same radio for everybody.

Source: FCC rules (47 CFR Parts 95 and 97) and FCC consumer guides, paraphrased, as understood in the United States. Rules change: check fcc.gov before you rely on a number. Not yet reviewed.
