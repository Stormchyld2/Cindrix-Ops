**The radio is poor for long speech. Write the message first, then send it.** The same method works for a note to a neighbour or a report to a shelter.

**Write it down before you transmit,** in this order:
1. **To:** who the message is for.
2. **From:** who it is from.
3. **Date and time.**
4. **Message,** short and complete: what, where, how many, and what you need. Aim for **25 words or fewer**.
5. **Reply wanted?** Say so.

**Send it:**
1. Call the other station and wait for "go ahead".
2. Read the message **slowly,** in short phrases of 5 to 7 words, pausing between. Spell names and unusual words with the phonetic alphabet.
3. At the end, say "that is all" and ask them to **read it back**.
4. If the readback is wrong, send the wrong part again.
5. Log the time you sent it and who received it.

**Receive it:**
1. Write while the other person talks. Do not trust memory.
2. Read it back.
3. Say "received" when it is right.

**An example:**
"To: Dad. From: Anna. Time 1430. Road closed at the creek bridge. We are at the church hall. Seven people, one needs a doctor. Please bring water. Reply wanted."

**A formal radiogram** adds a message number, a precedence (routine, priority, emergency) and a word count so that errors can be spotted. Hams use this to send messages across a region through several stations.

**Emergency traffic first.** Someone who needs urgent help always goes before routine chat.

**Keep a log** of what was sent and received, with times. It is useful for an after-action review.

Source: Message-handling practice from amateur radio (ARRL radiogram format) and ICS forms, simplified. Not yet reviewed.
