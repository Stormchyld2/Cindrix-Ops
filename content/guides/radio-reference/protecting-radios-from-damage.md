**Radios last for decades if they are kept dry, clean and protected from surges.**

**Lightning and surges:**
- **Disconnect** outdoor antenna cables from the radio during thunderstorms, and keep them away from the equipment.
- A **lightning arrestor** on the coaxial cable where it enters the house, connected to a ground rod, helps. Follow proper wiring advice, and use a professional for tall masts.
- Plug mains-powered radios into a **surge protector**, or unplug them when a storm is near.
- Handhelds with their own batteries and short antennas are much less exposed.

**Storage:**
1. Take the **batteries out** of stored radios, or use lithium primary cells that do not leak.
2. Store them in a **dry, cool place,** in a sealed container with a little desiccant (silica gel).
3. Keep the radio, charger, cable and spare antenna together in the same box, labelled.
4. Check once or twice a year: power on, check the channel settings, and recharge the battery.

**EMP and solar storms:** nobody knows exactly how radios would be affected by a rare, strong electromagnetic pulse or solar storm. A common suggestion is to keep **one spare radio and its battery in a sealed metal container** (such as a steel ammo can, with the contents wrapped in cardboard or a bag so they do not touch the metal). It costs little and might help; it is not proven. Do not rely on it as your only plan.

**Water and dust:** radios with a "weatherproof" rating handle rain; most are not waterproof. Keep them in a zip-lock bag in heavy rain, and dry them gently, with the battery out, if they get wet.

**Physical protection:** use a case or pouch. Replace antennas that are bent or damaged. Keep a **spare** of the cheapest radio you rely on.

Source: NOAA lightning safety, ARRL guidance on grounding and surge protection, and manufacturer advice. Claims about electromagnetic pulses (EMP) are uncertain and are not covered by proof here. Not yet reviewed.
