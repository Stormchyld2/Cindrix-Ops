**Canning is safe only when you follow a tested method exactly.** Done wrong, jars can grow the bacteria that cause **botulism**, a rare but deadly food poisoning. You cannot see, smell or taste it.

**The rules that matter most:**
1. **Low-acid foods** (vegetables, meat, fish, beans, soup) **must be pressure canned.** A boiling-water canner is not hot enough.
2. **High-acid foods** (most fruit, pickles, tomatoes with added acid) can be processed in a boiling-water canner.
3. **Use only recipes from a tested source.** Do not change the amounts of acid, the jar size, or the thickness of the food.
4. **Times and pressures depend on your altitude and the jar size.** This program does not list them, because a wrong number is dangerous. Copy the official guide onto your drive.
5. **Do not can by oven, dishwasher or open kettle.** Do not reuse flat lids.
6. **Before eating:** throw away any jar that is bulging, leaking, spurting liquid, or smells off. Do not taste it.

**The official guide:** the *USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning* is free (public domain) and is kept up to date by the National Center for Home Food Preservation (nchfp.uga.edu). Put a copy in the **library** folder of your drive, so it shows up on the Shelf.

Source: USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning, and the National Center for Home Food Preservation (nchfp.uga.edu).
