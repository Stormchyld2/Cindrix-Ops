**Many crops keep for months with no power if you can offer the right cold and damp. You need a place that stays between about 32 and 50 degrees F.**

**Where:** an unheated basement corner, a cellar, a dug pit or clamp, an insulated outbuilding, a garage against an inside wall, or a buried barrel or cooler. Keep it above freezing but below 50 degrees F, and shield it from light.

**Four conditions:**
1. **Temperature:** 32 to 40 degrees F for most roots; 50 to 60 for winter squash, sweet potatoes, and pumpkins.
2. **Humidity:** roots like it damp (85 to 95%). Onions, garlic and squash want it dry (60 to 70%).
3. **Air:** a little fresh air stops mould and removes the gas that ripening fruit gives off. Vent in cold weather.
4. **Darkness:** light turns potatoes green and sprouts onions.

**By crop (approximate):**
- **Potatoes:** 38 to 45 degrees F, dark, humid; 4 to 8 months. Cure 1 to 2 weeks first.
- **Carrots, beets, parsnips, turnips:** 32 to 40 degrees F, in damp sand or sawdust; 3 to 6 months.
- **Cabbage:** 32 to 40 degrees F, humid; hang by the root or wrap; 3 to 5 months.
- **Onions and garlic:** cure, then 32 to 40 degrees F, dry; 4 to 8 months.
- **Winter squash and pumpkin:** 50 to 55 degrees F, dry; 3 to 6 months.
- **Apples:** 30 to 35 degrees F, humid, wrapped separately.

**Rules:**
- **Keep apples and pears away from potatoes, carrots and cabbage.** Ripening fruit gives off ethylene gas, which spoils vegetables and makes them sprout.
- **Handle gently.** A bruised vegetable rots and spreads rot.
- **Check every week or two** and remove anything soft or mouldy at once.
- Do not wash before storing; brush off loose dirt.
- Use pest-proof boxes against mice and rats.

Source: the National Center for Home Food Preservation (nchfp.uga.edu) and USDA guidance, paraphrased; state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: check against the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning and the Shelf manuals.
