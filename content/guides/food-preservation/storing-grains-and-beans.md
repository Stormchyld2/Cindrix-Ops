**Dry staples are the cheapest long-term food and the easiest to store, if they stay dry, cool, dark and sealed.**

**What keeps longest (sealed, cool, dry):**
- **White rice:** 20+ years. (Brown rice has oils and keeps only about 6 to 12 months.)
- **Dry beans, lentils, split peas:** 10+ years. Old beans get harder and take longer to cook, but stay safe.
- **Hard wheat berries, field corn, oats (rolled or whole):** 10 to 30 years if properly stored; ground flour keeps only 6 to 12 months (whole grain flour goes rancid sooner).
- **Pasta:** 10+ years; **salt, sugar:** indefinitely; **honey:** indefinitely.
- **Powdered milk, dried potatoes:** 10 to 20 years if cold and sealed; shorter in warmth.

**Containers:**
1. **Food-grade 5-gallon buckets** (HDPE, marked with a "2" and a cup-and-fork symbol) with gasket lids.
2. **Mylar bags** inside buckets, with an oxygen absorber sized to the bag.
3. **Glass jars** and **PETE (#1) soda bottles**, kept dark.
4. Metal cans for small items. Avoid thin plastic bags, paper, and anything that held non-food products.

**Oxygen absorbers** remove oxygen so insects can't live in the container. Use them for grains and beans, **not for sugar, salt, flour with moisture, or high-oil foods** like nuts. Packets are used once opened (seal bags quickly).

**Bugs:** most grain has weevil eggs in it already. Freeze small amounts for a few days, or use absorbers or dry ice to stop them.

**Rules that matter:**
- Keep below 70 degrees F where you can; every 10 degrees F cooler roughly doubles shelf life.
- Keep off bare concrete floors (put on a pallet or shelf).
- Label with **contents and date**; rotate: first in, first out.
- Don't store anything that smells musty or shows moisture.
- **Never store dry beans or grain that is not completely dry**; mould and botulism risk rise with moisture.

Source: general food-storage guidance from Utah State and Brigham Young University Extension and USDA, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed.
