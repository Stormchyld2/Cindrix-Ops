**Pickling in vinegar keeps vegetables for months. It works because of the acid, so the recipe must keep enough.**

**Rules:**
1. Use vinegar with **5 percent acidity** (check the label). Do not dilute it more than the recipe says.
2. Use a **tested recipe** for the ratio of vinegar to water to vegetables. Do not reduce the vinegar.
3. Use **fresh, firm vegetables** and clean jars. Trim the blossom end of cucumbers.
4. Use pickling or kosher salt with no additives.
5. For shelf storage, **process in a water bath** for the time in the recipe (adjusted for altitude). For short storage, **refrigerator pickles** need no processing but must stay cold and be eaten in a few weeks.

**Quick steps:**
- Pack the vegetables into hot jars with spices.
- Pour on hot brine, leaving the headspace in the recipe.
- Remove air bubbles, wipe rims, add lids.
- Process or refrigerate.

**Wait:** let pickles sit 2 to 4 weeks before eating for the best flavor.

**Not safe:** pickling low-acid foods like garlic in oil, or fermenting beans in plain water and storing at room temperature.

**Throw away:** a bulging lid, a bad smell, cloudiness that is not from spices, or slime.

Source: National Center for Home Food Preservation and USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning, paraphrased.
