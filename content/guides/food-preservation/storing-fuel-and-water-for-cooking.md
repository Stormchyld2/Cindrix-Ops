**Food that needs no cooking, or very little, saves fuel in a long emergency.**

**Plan:**
- **Count fuel in meals:** one small canister or a little wood cooks a few meals. Write down how long a pot of beans or rice takes on your stove, then multiply.
- **Cook once, hold hot:** wrap a hot pot in blankets or in a box with hay or towels (a "hay box"). Rice and beans keep cooking.
- **Soak first:** beans, rice and grains soaked overnight cook faster and use less fuel.
- **Use a lid** and a pot that fits the flame.
- **Dried and ready foods** (jerky, hard cheese, nuts, dried fruit, crackers) need no fuel at all.
- **Plan for wood:** a stove or fire needs seasoned wood. Cut and stack it a year ahead.

**Stoves:** rocket stove, camp stove, charcoal grill, wood stove. **Never burn indoors** unless the stove is made for it and vented.

**Safety:** keep a fire extinguisher or water and a shovel near. See "Carbon monoxide" and "Using a generator safely".

Source: Ready.gov and extension service cooking and fuel guidance, paraphrased.
