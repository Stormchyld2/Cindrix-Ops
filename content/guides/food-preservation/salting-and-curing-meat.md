**Salt pulls water from meat and stops bacteria. It is one of the oldest ways to keep meat when there is no power.**

**Always:**
- Start with **fresh, cold, clean meat** from an animal handled well. See "Jerky and meat safety".
- Work at **cool temperatures:** 36 to 40 degrees F, never above 50. Winter and a cellar are ideal.
- Use **non-iodized salt** (pickling or kosher). Weigh it, do not guess.
- **Keep it clean:** sanitize knives, boards and containers.

**Dry salting (packing in salt):**
1. Cut meat into pieces no thicker than 4 to 6 inches.
2. Rub salt well into every surface. Pack into a clean container with salt between the layers.
3. Keep cool. Drain liquid. Re-salt after a day or two.
4. Leave for about 1 to 2 days per inch of thickness, then **wash, dry and hang or wrap** in cloth in a cool, dry, airy place.

**Brining (wet cure):**
1. Use a **tested recipe** with the right salt percentage. Meat must stay under the brine, weighted down.
2. Keep cold. Turn and check every few days.
3. **Cure time** depends on the thickness. Cook cured meats well.

**Curing salt (nitrite)** protects against botulism in long cures like bacon and ham. Use it **only** as a tested recipe says and measure carefully. Too much is poisonous.

**Signs of spoilage:** slime, a bad smell, discoloration, or soft spots. Throw it out.

**Cooking:** cured meat should be cooked to a safe temperature before eating.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service and National Center for Home Food Preservation guidance, paraphrased.
