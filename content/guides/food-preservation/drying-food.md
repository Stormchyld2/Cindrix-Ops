**Drying is one of the oldest, cheapest and lightest ways to store food, and needs no power if the weather is dry and warm.**

**Methods:**
1. **Electric dehydrator:** steady and reliable. Best if you have power.
2. **Oven:** set as low as it goes (about 140 degrees F), door propped open for air, food on racks. Slow, but works.
3. **Sun:** needs several days of hot (over 85 degrees F), dry, low-humidity weather. Use screens raised off the ground, covered with cheesecloth, and bring in at night so dew doesn't wet it.
4. **Air drying:** herbs, hot peppers, and green beans (as "leather britches") hung in a warm, dry, airy room out of sunlight.

**Prepare:**
- Wash, trim, and cut into **uniform thin slices** (1/8 to 1/4 inch): uneven pieces dry unevenly.
- **Blanch most vegetables** before drying (about 2 to 4 minutes steam). It keeps colour and speeds drying. Onions, peppers, garlic, herbs and mushrooms do not need it.
- For pale fruit (apples, pears, bananas), dip briefly in lemon juice mixed with water to slow browning.

**How dry is dry:**
- **Vegetables:** brittle or crisp. Drop one: it clicks.
- **Fruit:** leathery and pliable, no moist spots when torn open.
- **Herbs:** crumble between your fingers.
- Let dried food **cool**, then test: put some in a closed jar for a few days and check for condensation. Mist inside means it needs more drying.

**Storing:** airtight glass jars or sealed bags, out of light, in a cool place. Dried vegetables keep about **6 to 12 months**; fruit about **6 to 12 months**; herbs about a year. Check jars after the first week, and again now and then, for moisture or mould. Throw away anything mouldy.

**Using:** soak vegetables in water for 30 minutes to a few hours, or add dry to soups and stews. Use the soaking water.

**Meat jerky is different** and carries a risk (see the jerky page).
