**Salt-fermenting uses salt and time, not a stove or canner. Good bacteria sour the food and make it hard for bad ones to grow.** Done correctly it is a long-standing and generally safe method.

**Sauerkraut method:**
1. Finely shred cabbage. Weigh it.
2. Mix in salt: about **2 to 2.5% of the cabbage's weight** (about 3 tablespoons of canning salt to 5 pounds). Use salt with no additives (canning or pickling salt, not iodised table salt with anticaking agents).
3. Squeeze and pack hard into a clean crock or jar until juice rises above the cabbage. Press down.
4. Keep the cabbage **under the brine** with a weight (a clean plate with a jar of water on top). Cover loosely with cloth or a lid with an airlock.
5. Ferment at about **65 to 75 degrees F** for **1 to 4 weeks**, longer in cool rooms. Taste it. When sour enough, move to the fridge or a cool cellar.

**Whole vegetables (pickles, carrots, green beans):** pack them in a jar and cover with **brine of about 3 to 5% salt** (about 2 to 3 tablespoons of salt per quart of water). Weight them under the brine.

**What is normal:** cloudy brine, small bubbles, a sour smell, a soft white scum of yeast that can be skimmed off.

**Throw it out if:** it smells rotten (not sour), is slimy, or shows **fuzzy mould** (blue, green, black, pink) on the vegetable itself. Never taste to test.

**Keep it under the brine.** Anything above the brine can rot.

**Storing:** fridge or cold cellar for months. Refrigerated or very cold, ferments keep a long time.

**Safety note:** home-fermented pickles that are then **canned** must follow a tested canning recipe. See the canning safety page.

Source: the National Center for Home Food Preservation (nchfp.uga.edu) and USDA guidance, paraphrased; state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: check against the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning and the Shelf manuals.
