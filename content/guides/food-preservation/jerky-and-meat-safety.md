**Drying meat removes water, but drying alone does not kill the bacteria that cause food poisoning. Heat does.**

**Jerky, safe method (USDA advice):**
1. Use **lean** meat (fat goes rancid). Cut with the grain about 1/4 inch thick. Freezing slightly first makes slicing easier.
2. **Marinate in the fridge**, never on the counter. Throw out used marinade.
3. **Heat the strips first:** bring the meat to **160 degrees F (beef, venison, game) or 165 degrees F (poultry)** before or after the drying step, in a steamer, a pot of simmering marinade, or a hot oven. Use a **meat thermometer**.
4. Dry at **130 to 140 degrees F** in a dehydrator or oven (door propped), until the jerky bends and cracks but doesn't snap.
5. Cool, then pack. Store in a sealed bag in a cool place for **1 to 2 months**, or in the fridge or freezer for longer.

**Wild game:** can carry parasites and disease, and freezing does not reliably kill all of them (some parasites in bear and other wildlife survive freezing). Cook to the temperatures above. Wear gloves when field dressing, and do not eat an animal that looked sick.

**Curing and smoking:**
- Salt, sugar, **nitrite or nitrate curing salt** and smoke are what safely preserve ham, bacon and sausage. **Curing salt (such as Prague powder / pink salt)** must be measured exactly: too much is poisonous, too little is unsafe.
- Learn from a tested recipe from a university Extension service. Do not guess.
- Smoking for flavour is not the same as smoke-curing for storage.

**Never:**
- Dry meat in the open sun.
- Leave raw meat out for more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90 degrees F).
- Eat canned meat or low-acid food from a bulging, spurting or off-smelling jar. Do not taste to check.

**Safe temperatures:** keep cold food at **40 degrees F or below** and hot food at **140 degrees F or above**. Between is the "danger zone" where bacteria multiply fast.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) guidance on jerky and food safety, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed; curing and smoking need training: learn from a tested source before relying on them.
