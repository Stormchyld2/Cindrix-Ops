**Freezing is the easiest preservation method if you have steady power.** Without power it fails (see the power-cut page), so treat it as a short-term store.

**Blanch first.** Most vegetables must be briefly boiled, then chilled, before freezing. Blanching stops the enzymes that spoil flavour, colour and texture.

1. Wash and cut vegetables to even sizes.
2. Boil water in a large pot (1 gallon per pound of vegetables). Add vegetables, return to the boil, and start timing.
3. Blanch times are short, roughly: **peas 1.5 minutes, green beans 3, broccoli florets 3, corn on the cob 7 to 11, greens 2, carrot slices 2, cubed squash 3.** Times change with size.
4. **Chill at once** in ice water for the same time as the blanch, then drain well.
5. Pack in freezer bags or tubs, squeeze out the air, leave 1/2 inch headspace for liquids, label with the **name and date**.
6. Freeze flat on a tray first for loose, pourable vegetables.

**No blanching needed:** peppers, onions (chopped), herbs, tomatoes (cooked or raw whole), and most fruit.

**Keeping time:** best quality within **8 to 12 months** at 0 degrees F. Food stays safe past that, but dries and loses flavour ("freezer burn" is a quality problem, not a safety problem).

**Freezer rules:**
- Keep the freezer at **0 degrees F or lower**. Use a thermometer.
- Keep it full; empty space warms fast. Fill gaps with jugs of water.
- A **full freezer** keeps food frozen about 48 hours without power if you leave the door shut; a half-full one about 24.
- **Never refreeze** food that has fully thawed and warmed above 40 degrees F for two hours or more. Food that still has ice crystals or is at 40 degrees F or below can be refrozen.

Source: the National Center for Home Food Preservation (nchfp.uga.edu) and USDA guidance, paraphrased; state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: check against the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning and the Shelf manuals.
