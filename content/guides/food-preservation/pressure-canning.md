**Low-acid foods must be pressure canned.** A boiling-water bath is not hot enough to kill the spores that cause botulism. The **only safe method** for vegetables, meat, poultry, fish, beans and soups is a pressure canner, with a **tested recipe.**

**What you need:** a **pressure canner** (not a pressure cooker) with a rack, a gauge that is **tested every year,** jars, new lids, and a tested recipe.

**Steps:**
1. **Follow a tested recipe** from the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning. The time and pressure depend on the food, jar size and altitude.
2. Put the rack and a few inches of hot water in the canner. Use the amount in the maker's book.
3. Fill hot jars, leaving the headspace the recipe says. Remove air bubbles. Wipe rims. Put on lids and bands fingertip tight.
4. Place jars in the canner. Lock the lid and **vent steam for 10 minutes** before closing the vent.
5. Bring to the pressure in the recipe. **Start timing only when it reaches that pressure** and keep it steady. Adjust the heat so it does not drop.
6. When the time is up, turn off the heat and let it cool on its own. Do not force-cool. Open only when the pressure is zero.
7. Take jars out and let them sit 12 to 24 hours. Check seals. Label and store.

**Safety:**
- Do not use an old gauge or one that has never been tested. Ask a local extension office to test it.
- **Do not can** things the recipe does not give: pumpkin puree, mashed vegetables, dairy, flour, grease and thickened soups are not safe at home.
- **Boil home-canned low-acid food for 10 minutes** before eating, to be safe (20 minutes for corn and spinach). This is for extra safety against botulism.
- **Throw away without tasting:** bulging lids, leaks, spurting, odor, mold, cloudy liquid.

**Botulism signs:** double vision, drooping eyelids, slurred speech, trouble swallowing, muscle weakness. This is an emergency. If phones work, call 911.

Source: USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning and National Center for Home Food Preservation, paraphrased.
