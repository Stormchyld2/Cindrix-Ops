**Eggs, butter and hard cheese last longer than most people expect when handled well.**

**Eggs:**
- **Unwashed fresh eggs** with their natural coating can sit on a counter for a few weeks. In a cool cellar they last for months.
- **Washed eggs** (the usual store eggs in the US) must be kept cold in the fridge. Without power, eat them quickly.
- **Water glassing:** use pickling lime in cooled boiled water. It keeps unwashed, clean eggs for many months. Follow a tested recipe.
- **Freezing:** crack and beat eggs, and freeze in small containers.
- **Test freshness:** a fresh egg sinks and lies flat in water. An old egg floats upright. A floater should not be eaten.

**Butter:**
- Keep in a cool cellar, in a crock of cool water changed each day, or well sealed.
- **Salted butter** keeps longer than sweet.
- **Ghee (clarified butter)** keeps for months without a fridge.

**Cheese:**
- **Hard cheeses** (cheddar, parmesan) keep for months. Wax, waxed cloth or oil on the rind keeps them from drying.
- **Soft cheese** keeps only a few days without cold.

**Milk:** raw or pasteurized, milk spoils quickly without cold. Turn it into cheese, yogurt or butter.

**Throw away:** anything that smells off, has slime, or has mold on soft products.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service and National Center for Home Food Preservation, paraphrased.
