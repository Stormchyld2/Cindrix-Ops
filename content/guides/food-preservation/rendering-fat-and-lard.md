**Fat is energy, and in a long emergency it is precious. Rendered fat keeps for a long time without power if it is clean and dry.**

**Making lard (from pork) or tallow (from beef or deer):**
1. Use **clean, cold, firm fat** (leaf fat from around the kidneys is best). Cut it into small pieces or grind it.
2. Put the pieces in a heavy pot over **very low heat** with a little water to keep it from scorching.
3. Stir now and then. The fat melts and the bits shrink and brown. This takes a few hours.
4. **Strain** the liquid fat through a clean cloth into clean jars.
5. Let it cool, then **cover tightly** and label.

**Storing:**
- **In a cool, dark place** it lasts many months. In a fridge or cellar, a year or more.
- Water in the fat makes it spoil. Heat gently until all the bubbling stops to drive water out.
- Rancid fat smells sharp and bad. Throw it out.

**Uses:** cooking, frying, making soap, lighting a lamp, protecting leather and tools from rust.

**Safety:** hot fat burns badly and fat fires cannot be put out with water. Cover the pot with a lid and turn off the heat. See "Burns".

**Game:** deer fat does not taste as good as pig fat but can be used for soap and candles.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service and extension service guidance, paraphrased.
