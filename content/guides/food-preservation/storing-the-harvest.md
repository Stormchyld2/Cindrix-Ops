**Many crops keep for months if they are cured, kept at the right cold, and checked often.**

- **Potatoes:** dark, cool (about 40 to 45 degrees F), a little humid. Do not store near apples.
- **Carrots, beets and turnips:** cold (just above freezing) and very humid. Pack them in damp sand or sawdust.
- **Onions and garlic:** cure first, then keep them cool, dry and airy. Hang them in braids or nets.
- **Winter squash:** cure, then keep dry at about 50 to 55 degrees F.
- **Cabbage:** cold and humid, just above freezing.
- **Apples:** cold and humid. One rotten apple spoils the others, so check often.
- **Dry beans and grain:** fully dry, in closed containers, cool and dark, away from mice.

**Check everything every week or two,** and use what is starting to spoil first. Keep a list of what you have.

Source: general food storage practice from state Extension services and the USDA. Not yet checked against one named page.
