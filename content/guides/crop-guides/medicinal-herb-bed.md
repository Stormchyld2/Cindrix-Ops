**A bed of easy herbs can supply teas and simple home remedies. They are not a substitute for care for serious illness.**

**Timing:** Chamomile, calendula, borage: sow in spring as soon as the soil can be worked. Echinacea, yarrow, lemon balm, comfrey, feverfew and valerian: sow indoors in late winter (echinacea and valerian need cold treatment first) or buy divisions in spring.

**Sowing:** Sow shallow and keep damp; most sprout in 1 to 3 weeks (echinacea and yarrow slower).

**Care:** Perennials need 2 years to settle. Chamomile and calendula self-sow readily. Comfrey and lemon balm spread: give them a corner.

**Harvest:** Pick flowers (chamomile, calendula, echinacea, yarrow) when just open. Harvest leaves of lemon balm before flowering, and roots (echinacea, valerian) in autumn of the third year.

**Storing:** Dry flowers and leaves on screens out of the sun until crisp; store in dark glass jars for up to a year. See the Home remedies section for how each herb is used and any cautions.

**Seed saving:** Let flowers go to seed in late summer to keep a supply.

**Know each plant before you use it.** Many look alike (wild carrot and poison hemlock look similar). Some herbs are unsafe in pregnancy or with medicines a person takes.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
