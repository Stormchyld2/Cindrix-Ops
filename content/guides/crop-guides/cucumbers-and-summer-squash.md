**Very productive warm-season vines that need plenty of water.**

**Timing:** sow **1 to 2 weeks after the last frost**, when the soil is at least 65 degrees F. Seed can also be started indoors in pots 3 to 4 weeks ahead (they dislike root disturbance, so use pots that can be planted). Cucumbers take 50 to 70 days; summer squash and zucchini 45 to 60.

**Planting:**
- Sow in "hills" (low mounds): 4 to 5 seeds per hill, thin to the 2 or 3 strongest. Hills 3 to 4 feet apart for bush types, 4 to 6 feet for vines.
- **Cucumbers do best on a trellis**: straight fruit, less disease, less space used.
- Feed lightly: well-composted soil is enough.

**Care:**
- **Water deeply** every few days in dry weather. Cucumbers turn bitter and misshapen with drought.
- Mulch to hold moisture and keep fruit clean.
- Squash and cucumbers are **pollinated by bees**. If flowers drop without fruit, try pollinating by hand: dab the pollen of a male flower (thin stem) onto the centre of a female flower (a tiny fruit behind the blossom).
- **Pests:** cucumber beetles and squash bugs and vine borers. Check undersides of leaves; squash eggs and bugs by hand into soapy water. Row cover keeps pests out until flowering, then must be removed for pollination.

**Harvest:** pick **often**. Zucchini is best at 6 to 8 inches. A forgotten giant tells the plant to stop producing. Cucumbers for slicing 6 to 8 inches, for pickles 2 to 4 inches.

**Preserving:** pickle (tested recipes only), ferment, or dry zucchini slices. Squash does not freeze well as slices but grated zucchini freezes for bread and soup.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
