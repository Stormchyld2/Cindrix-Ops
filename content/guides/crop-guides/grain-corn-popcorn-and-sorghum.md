**Grains feed a family and animals. Corn is the easiest grain for a home garden.**

**Timing:** Corn: sow **1 to 2 weeks after the last frost** (soil 60 degrees F); dry corn needs 100 to 120 days. Sorghum: sow when soil is 65 degrees F and nights are warm; 100 to 120 days.

**Sowing:** Corn: 1 to 2 inches deep, 8 to 12 inches apart, in **blocks of at least 4 rows** (not one long row) so the wind pollinates well. Sorghum: 1/2 inch deep, thin to 6 inches. Plant beans and squash with corn in the "three sisters" way.

**Care:** Corn is hungry for nitrogen: compost or a legume cover crop before. Keep weeds down until plants are knee high. Water at tasselling and silking. Sorghum is far more drought-tolerant.

**Harvest:** Leave corn on the stalk until the husks are dry and brown and the kernels are hard (a thumbnail will not dent them). Husk, and dry ears further in a dry airy place for 3 to 4 weeks. Cut sorghum heads when seed is hard.

**Storing:** Shell fully dry kernels and keep them in a sealed container; grind as needed. Dry grain below about 13 percent moisture keeps for years. Freeze a week first to kill weevils.

**Seed saving:** Isolate from other varieties of corn by 250 yards or more, or stagger planting dates 2 weeks. Save the best 100 ears for seed to avoid inbreeding.

Treat corn that is eaten as the main food with care: whole corn needs "nixtamalisation" (soaking in lime or wood-ash water) to give its niacin, otherwise pellagra can result. See the grain pages in the food stores guides.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
