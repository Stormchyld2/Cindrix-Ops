**Sweet, thirsty vines that need a long hot summer and plenty of room.**

**Timing:** Sow direct **2 to 3 weeks after the last frost** when soil is above 70 degrees F, or start indoors 3 to 4 weeks earlier in pots and transplant carefully (roots dislike disturbance). Ready 75 to 100 days after sowing. In short-summer areas, choose early varieties and use black plastic or cloches.

**Sowing:** Plant on mounds, 3 seeds a mound 1 inch deep, thin to 2; mounds 4 to 6 feet apart.

**Care:** Deep watering once a week, then **stop heavy watering as fruit ripens** for sweeter melons. Mulch and place a board or straw under fruit to keep it off wet soil. Fertilise lightly: too much nitrogen gives leaves and no fruit.

**Harvest:** Cantaloupe "slips" (separates) from the vine with a gentle tug when ripe and smells sweet. Watermelon: the tendril nearest the stem turns brown and dry, the underside spot turns creamy yellow, and a hollow thump.

**Storing:** Cantaloupe keeps about a week after ripening; watermelon 2 to 3 weeks cool. Cut fruit can be dried or made into preserves.

**Seed saving:** Melons cross with other melons (not with cucumbers or squash). Take seed from ripe fruit, wash and dry.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
