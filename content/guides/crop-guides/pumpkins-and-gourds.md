**Pumpkins are a winter squash by another name: they feed you through winter and give seed you can eat.**

**Timing:** Sow **1 to 2 weeks after the last frost**; they need 90 to 120 days, so count back from the first fall frost.

**Sowing:** Hills 4 to 6 feet apart, 3 seeds each, 1 inch deep, thin to 2. Bush types fit smaller gardens.

**Care:** Heavy feeders: put compost in each hill. Water at the base. Squash vine borers hollow the stem near the soil: wrap the base in foil or tape and watch for sawdust-like frass. Plant a second sowing in midsummer as insurance.

**Harvest:** Harvest before a hard frost when the rind is hard (a thumbnail will not dent it) and the stem is dry. Cut with 3 to 4 inches of stem; never carry by the stem.

**Storing:** "Cure" in a warm (80 degrees F) dry place for 10 days, then store at 50 to 55 degrees F and dry. Most keep 3 to 6 months; butternut up to 6. Seeds can be washed, dried and roasted.

**Seed saving:** Squash species cross within a species (see the seed saving guides). Take seed only from fully ripe fruit.

See also the Winter squash page for the different species.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
