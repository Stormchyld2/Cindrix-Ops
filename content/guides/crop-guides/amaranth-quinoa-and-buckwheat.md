**Seed crops that grow in a garden and give protein and oil without a field.**

**Timing:** Sow **after the last frost**: amaranth and buckwheat when soil is 60 degrees F; quinoa prefers cool nights and is best in a cool summer or sown very early. Sunflowers after the last frost. Buckwheat is ready in 10 to 12 weeks, so a second sowing in midsummer is possible.

**Sowing:** Amaranth and quinoa seed is tiny: sow shallow, thin to 12 inches. Buckwheat can be broadcast thickly. Sunflowers: 1 inch deep, 6 to 12 inches apart.

**Care:** Amaranth thrives in heat and poor soil. Buckwheat grows fast and shades out weeds; it is also a bee crop and a cover crop. Sunflowers need staking in wind. Birds eat the seed: cover heads with a paper bag or net.

**Harvest:** Cut seed heads when seed shakes loose. Sunflowers: when the back of the head turns brown. Dry for 1 to 2 weeks and rub out the seed.

**Storing:** **Quinoa seed has a bitter coating (saponin): rinse well before cooking.** Store dry seed in sealed jars for a year or more. Sunflower seed keeps better in the shell.

**Seed saving:** Amaranth and quinoa are easy to save but cross with relatives; sunflowers cross with each other.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
