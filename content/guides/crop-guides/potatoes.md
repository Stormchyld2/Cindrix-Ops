**A forgiving crop that stores well.** Plant **seed potatoes** (sold for planting), not grocery potatoes, which may be treated to stop them sprouting.

1. **When:** 2 to 4 weeks before your last spring frost, once the soil can be worked and is no longer soggy.
2. **Prepare:** cut large seed potatoes into pieces about the size of an egg, each with 1 or 2 "eyes". Let the cut sides dry for a day.
3. **Plant:** 4 inches deep, 12 inches apart, in rows about 3 feet apart.
4. **Hill:** as the plants grow, pull soil up around the stems. This protects the new potatoes from light.
5. **Harvest:** when the vines have died back. Dig carefully so you do not cut them.
6. **Cure and store:** keep them for 1 to 2 weeks somewhere cool and dark to toughen the skins, then store in the dark in a cool place (about 40 to 45 degrees F), with some humidity.

**Green skin or sprouts:** potatoes that have turned green in the light contain a bitter poison. Cut the green parts away, and throw away a potato that is very green or bitter.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet checked against one named page: check planting times with your own Extension office.
