**Cool-season cabbage-family crops: they hate summer heat, so time them to ripen in cool weather.**

**Timing:** Start indoors **6 to 8 weeks before the last spring frost** and transplant 2 to 4 weeks before it (they take light frost). For autumn, start in midsummer and transplant **10 to 12 weeks before the first fall frost**. Brussels sprouts need a long season: 90 to 110 days from transplant, planted early summer for a late-autumn crop.

**Sowing:** Seed 1/4 inch deep. Set transplants deeper than they grew, up to the first leaves. Space 18 inches apart (24 for Brussels sprouts), rows 30 inches apart.

**Care:** Steady water, about 1 inch a week; mulch to keep roots cool. They are heavy feeders: work in compost first. Cover with fine netting from planting to stop cabbage moth caterpillars. Cauliflower heads stay white if you tie the leaves over them when the head is the size of an egg ("blanching"). Rotate: do not follow another cabbage-family crop for 3 years (clubroot, disease).

**Harvest:** Cut broccoli when the buds are tight and dark green, before any yellow shows; side shoots keep coming for weeks. Cauliflower heads are ready when firm and 6 to 8 inches across. Brussels sprouts ripen from the bottom up; pinch off the top of the plant about 4 weeks before you want to finish so the sprouts size up. A hard frost sweetens them.

**Storing:** Fresh heads keep 1 to 2 weeks in a cold fridge. Blanch and freeze, or ferment cabbage-family crops. Brussels sprouts on the stalk keep a few weeks in a cold cellar.

**Seed saving:** Biennial (Brussels sprouts, cauliflower) or needs cold to flower; broccoli can set seed in the first year. They cross freely with cabbage, kale and each other, so save seed from one type at a time.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
