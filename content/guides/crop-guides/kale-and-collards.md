**The cabbage family's easiest members. They give a lot of food and take frost well.**

**Timing:**
- **Kale and collards:** sow indoors 4 to 6 weeks before last frost or direct sow 2 to 4 weeks before. Sow again in midsummer for a fall crop that **sweetens after frost** and can last through winter in many areas.
- **Broccoli:** start indoors 6 to 8 weeks before the last frost; set out 2 to 3 weeks before. Or start in summer for autumn. It bolts in heat.

**Planting:** space 18 to 24 inches apart. Rich soil with plenty of compost. Full sun, but afternoon shade helps in hot regions.

**Care:**
- Even water: about 1 inch a week.
- Mulch.
- **Cabbage worms** (small green caterpillars) chew holes. Pick them off, or cover with row cover from planting. Rotate with non-cabbage-family crops each year to cut clubroot and other diseases.

**Harvest:**
- **Kale and collards:** pick outer leaves, leaving the growing centre. The plant keeps producing for months.
- **Broccoli:** cut the main head while buds are tight and green, before yellow flowers show. Side shoots follow for weeks.

**Preserving:** blanch and freeze, ferment, or dry kale into chips.

**Winter note:** under a row cover or cold frame, kale and collards often last well past the first hard frost.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
