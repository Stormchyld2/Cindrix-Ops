**One of the first things in the ground each spring.** Peas are a **cool-season** crop: they stop producing when hot weather arrives, usually by early summer.

**Timing:** sow as soon as the soil can be worked, **4 to 6 weeks before the last frost**; seed germinates in soil as cool as 45 degrees F. In mild-winter areas, sow in autumn for a winter crop. Most take **55 to 75 days**.

**Types:**
- **Shelling (garden) peas:** you eat the peas, not the pod.
- **Snap and snow peas:** eat the pod too.
- **Field peas (dry peas):** left to dry on the vine for soup and storage, like dry beans.

**Planting:**
1. Sow seed **1 to 2 inches deep**, 2 inches apart, in a double row 6 inches apart with a trellis between.
2. Most peas climb: give them a fence, netting or brush **at planting**. Dwarf types need little support.
3. Do not overwater seed in cold wet soil: it rots. Do not feed with much nitrogen: peas make their own from the air.
4. Dusting seed with a pea-and-bean **inoculant** (a harmless soil bacteria powder) improves the crop in new beds.

**Harvest:** pick every day or two once pods fill. Frequent picking keeps the plant producing. Pick shelling peas when pods are round and full but still bright green. Sugar fades within hours, so eat or freeze soon.

**Drying for storage:** leave pods on the vine until brown and rattling, pull the plants, finish drying in the pods indoors, then shell. Heat a few minutes, or freeze for a few days, to kill any weevil eggs, then store in a sealed jar.

**After the crop:** cut the plants at the soil line and leave the roots; the nitrogen they hold feeds the next crop. Plant fall greens or carrots in the same bed.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
