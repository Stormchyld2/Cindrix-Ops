**The quickest vegetables in the garden, plus two roots that store through winter.**

**Timing:** Radishes: sow from **4 weeks before the last frost** until early summer, and again in autumn; ready in 25 to 35 days. Turnips: sow in early spring and **8 to 10 weeks before the first fall frost**; ready in 40 to 60 days. Rutabagas need 90 to 110 days: sow in midsummer.

**Sowing:** Seed 1/2 inch deep in rows 12 inches apart. Thin radishes to 1 inch, turnips to 3 to 4 inches, rutabagas to 6 inches. Sow radishes every 10 days for a steady supply.

**Care:** Cool weather, even water and no fresh manure. Hot weather or dry spells make radishes woody and hot-tasting. Flea beetles pepper the leaves: cover with fine netting.

**Harvest:** Pull radishes as soon as they are the size of a large marble. Turnips at 2 to 3 inches (larger ones turn woody); rutabagas at 3 to 5 inches. A light frost sweetens turnips and rutabagas. The green tops of turnips are good food.

**Storing:** Radishes keep 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge. Turnips and rutabagas keep for months in damp sand in a cold cellar, just above freezing. Leave in the ground under heavy straw in mild winters.

**Seed saving:** Radishes flower the first year; turnips and rutabagas are biennial. They cross with other members of their own kind.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
