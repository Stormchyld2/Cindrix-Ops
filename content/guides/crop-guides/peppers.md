**Warm-season, slow to start.** Sweet and hot peppers grow the same way.

**Timing:** start seed indoors **8 to 10 weeks before the last frost** (they are slow: 1 to 2 weeks to sprout, warm soil helps). Plant out **2 to 3 weeks after the last frost**, when nights stay above 55 degrees F. Cold nights stunt them. Most take **60 to 90 days** after transplanting; hot varieties take longer.

**Planting:** space **18 inches** apart, rows 24 to 30 inches apart. Give them full sun. Stake tall plants.

**Care:**
- Even moisture: about 1 inch of water a week.
- Moderate feeding. Too much nitrogen makes leaves, not fruit.
- Mulch after the soil warms.
- Fruit set drops if daytime heat goes above about **90 degrees F** or nights below 55.

**Harvest:** sweet peppers can be picked green or left to ripen to red, yellow or orange (sweeter, higher in vitamins). Cut, do not pull, so branches do not break.

**Handling hot peppers:** wear gloves, do not touch eyes or face, and wash hands well. Peppers hold their heat more in the seeds and white ribs.

**Preserving:** drying is the easiest (string and hang in dry air, or use a dehydrator). Freezing works: no blanching needed for sweet peppers. Peppers (plain, low-acid) must be pressure canned or pickled using a tested recipe; use the USDA guide.

**Seed saving:** peppers cross easily between types in the same species if close together. Isolate by 50 feet or more, or bag blossoms. Save seed from fully ripe fruit.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
