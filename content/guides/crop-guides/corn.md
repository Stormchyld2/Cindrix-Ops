**A hungry, sun-loving, warm-season grass.** Sweet corn is eaten fresh or frozen. **Field (dent) corn** is dried and ground into meal; flour corn and popcorn are grown the same way.

**Timing:** plant **1 to 2 weeks after your last spring frost**, when the soil is at least 60 degrees F. Seed rots in cold, wet soil. Sweet corn takes **65 to 95 days**; field corn **100 to 120 days**.

**Plant in blocks, not one long row.** Corn is **wind-pollinated**. Pollen falls from the tassels on top onto the silks below. Plant at least **4 short rows side by side** (a block), not a single row, or the ears will be gappy.

1. Sow seeds **1 to 1.5 inches deep**, 8 to 12 inches apart, in rows 30 inches apart. Thin to one plant per hole.
2. Corn uses a lot of **nitrogen**. Work in compost or well-rotted manure before planting. Side-dress with more when plants are knee-high.
3. Water **well when the tassels and silks appear**. Dry weather then means poor ears.
4. Keep weeds down while plants are small; once tall, corn shades most weeds out.

**Do not let different types cross:** sweet corn grown near field corn or popcorn will turn starchy. Separate them by about **250 feet**, or plant them so they flower at least 2 weeks apart.

**Harvest sweet corn** about **3 weeks after the first silks show**, when silks are brown and dry and a kernel squirts milky juice when pressed with a thumbnail. Cook or freeze the same day: the sugar turns to starch within hours.

**Harvest field corn** when the husks are dry and brown and the kernels are hard and dented. Leave it on the stalk as long as the weather allows. Finish drying in a dry, airy place until a kernel cannot be dented by a fingernail, then store in a sealed container. Moisture is what spoils stored corn.

**Companion planting:** the "three sisters" method plants corn, pole beans (climbing the stalks) and squash (shading the soil) together. It works best once the corn is knee-high before beans are sown.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
