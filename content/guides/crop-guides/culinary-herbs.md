**A small herb bed turns plain stores into food people want to eat, and many herbs are also gentle remedies.**

**Timing:** Annuals (basil, dill, cilantro, parsley): sow after the last frost, or start basil indoors 6 weeks ahead. Perennials (thyme, sage, oregano, chives, mint, lavender, rosemary): plant in spring from seed, cuttings or divisions; rosemary is tender in cold winters.

**Sowing:** Seed is tiny: press into the surface and barely cover. Dill and cilantro hate transplanting: sow where they will grow, and every 3 weeks for a steady supply.

**Care:** Most herbs like full sun and **lean, well-drained soil**; too much fertiliser dulls the flavour. **Mint spreads everywhere: grow it in a pot sunk in the ground.** Pinch basil flowers off to keep leaf coming.

**Harvest:** Cut in the morning after the dew dries, just before flowering when oils are strongest. Cut no more than a third of a plant at a time.

**Storing:** Hang small bunches upside down in a dry, shaded, airy place, then crumble into jars. Basil keeps best frozen in oil or pesto. Dried herbs keep a year or two.

**Seed saving:** Let a few plants of dill, cilantro (coriander seed) and parsley go to seed. Perennials are divided.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
