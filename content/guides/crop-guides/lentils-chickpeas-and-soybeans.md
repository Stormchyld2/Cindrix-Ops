**More protein crops: dry pulses store for years and fix their own nitrogen.**

**Timing:** Lentils, chickpeas and fava beans: sow **as soon as the soil can be worked in spring** (they like cool weather, favas are frost-hardy); ready in 80 to 110 days. Soybeans: sow **2 weeks after the last frost** when soil is 60 degrees F; 90 to 120 days.

**Sowing:** Seed 1 to 2 inches deep (favas 2 inches), 2 to 4 inches apart in rows 18 to 24 inches apart. Inoculate seed with the right nitrogen-fixing bacteria if the soil has not grown the crop before (garden suppliers sell it for each type).

**Care:** Little feeding needed, since they make their own nitrogen. Keep weeds down while small. Lentils and chickpeas hate wet feet; favas tolerate cold wet spring.

**Harvest:** Let pods dry on the plant until rattling, then pull the plants and finish drying under cover. Soybeans can be picked green (edamame) after boiling. Thresh by stamping in a bag or beating on a tarp, then winnow.

**Storing:** Dry seed fully, freeze a week to kill weevils, then store in sealed jars. Keeps for years. **Soybeans and favas must be cooked well**; raw favas are dangerous for people with a certain inherited enzyme deficiency (favism).

**Seed saving:** Pulses self-pollinate and stay true. Keep the best-looking seed.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
