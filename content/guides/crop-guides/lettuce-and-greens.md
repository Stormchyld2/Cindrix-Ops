**Quick, cool-season crops that can feed you within a month.**

**Timing:** sow as soon as the soil can be worked in spring, and again in **late summer** for autumn. In hot weather they **bolt** (shoot up a flower stalk and turn bitter). Sow every 2 weeks for a steady supply. In hot regions, grow in partial shade or in the cool months only.

**Types:**
- **Leaf lettuce and mesclun:** cut-and-come-again; ready in 30 to 45 days.
- **Spinach:** very cold-hardy; sow early and in late summer. Fast-bolting in long days.
- **Chard:** tolerates heat and cold both. Cut outer leaves and it keeps growing for months.
- **Kale and collards:** hardy through winter in much of the country; taste sweeter after frost.
- **Arugula, mustard, and other greens:** ready in 3 to 5 weeks.

**Planting:** seeds are small. Press them 1/4 inch deep or just cover with fine soil. Keep damp. Thin leaf lettuce to 4 inches, heads to 8 to 12.

**Care:** shallow roots, so water often and lightly. Mulch lightly to hold moisture. Shade cloth or taller crops on the sunny side help in warm weather.

**Harvest:** cut outer leaves, or cut the whole plant 1 inch above the ground; it often regrows. Harvest in the morning for crispness.

**Winter growing:** a cold frame, low tunnel or row cover (see the season extension page) lets spinach, kale and lettuce keep going well into winter in many areas.

**Safety:** wash leaves well before eating, and use clean water. Do not use untreated manure on leafy greens within 120 days of harvest.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
