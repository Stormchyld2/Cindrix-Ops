**A way to turn waste wood and straw into protein, in shade and with little space.**

**Timing:** Inoculate logs in **late winter to spring**; fruiting starts 6 to 12 months later and continues for 3 to 5 years. Oyster mushrooms on straw are ready in 4 to 8 weeks.

**Sowing:** Shiitake: use fresh-cut hardwood logs (oak is best) 3 to 4 inches thick and 3 feet long, cut within a few weeks of felling. Drill holes, push in plug spawn or sawdust spawn, and seal with wax. Oysters: pasteurise straw in hot water, mix with spawn and pack in bags or buckets with holes.

**Care:** Stack logs in a shady spot off the ground. Keep moist in dry spells. Soak logs 24 hours in cold water to start a flush of mushrooms.

**Harvest:** Pick when caps have opened but the edges are still curled slightly under.

**Storing:** Eat fresh within days, or dry slices until brittle and store in jars.

**Seed saving:** Buy new spawn each time, or grow your own from clean cultures (an advanced skill).

**Never eat a wild mushroom you cannot identify with certainty.** Grown mushrooms from spawn are safe because you know what you planted.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
