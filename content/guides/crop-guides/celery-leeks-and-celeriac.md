**Slow, cool-season crops that reward patience; leeks are the easiest and the hardiest.**

**Timing:** Leeks: start indoors **10 to 12 weeks before the last frost**, transplant after 8 weeks; 100 to 120 days to harvest. Celery and celeriac: start indoors **10 to 12 weeks before the last frost** and transplant after the last frost; 100 to 120 days.

**Sowing:** Leek seed is thin like grass; sow in a tray or shallow row. Transplant leeks into a 6-inch trench or dibbed holes and fill gradually as they grow, to blanch a long white shaft. Celery needs rich, moist soil, 8 inches apart.

**Care:** Constant moisture is the rule for celery (it is mostly water). Leeks tolerate cold; hill soil around the stems. Mulch heavily.

**Harvest:** Dig leeks from autumn until the ground freezes. Celeriac is ready when the root is a baseball size. Cut celery stalks from the outside.

**Storing:** Leeks overwinter in the ground under mulch. Celeriac keeps in a cold cellar for months; celery keeps several weeks.

**Seed saving:** Biennial: they flower in year two. Leeks save well; celery seed is also a spice.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
