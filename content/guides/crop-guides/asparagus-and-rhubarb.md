**Plant once; harvest for ten to twenty years. A good use of a back corner of the garden.**

**Timing:** Plant crowns in **early spring** as the soil can be worked. Do not harvest asparagus for 2 years, or rhubarb for the first year, so the roots build strength.

**Sowing:** Asparagus: dig a trench 8 inches deep, set crowns 12 to 18 inches apart, cover with 2 inches of soil and add more as shoots grow. Rhubarb: plant crowns with buds 1 to 2 inches below the soil surface, 3 feet apart.

**Care:** Weed carefully (the roots are shallow), mulch heavily and feed with compost each spring and after harvest. Let asparagus ferns grow all summer to feed the roots, and cut them down in late autumn.

**Harvest:** Asparagus: snap spears at 6 to 8 inches for 4 to 6 weeks each spring. Rhubarb: pull stalks (do not cut) from the second spring on. **Rhubarb leaves are poisonous: never eat them.** Stop harvest in midsummer.

**Storing:** Asparagus: eat fresh, freeze or pickle. Rhubarb stalks: freeze or make into sauce and jam.

**Seed saving:** Propagate by dividing rhubarb crowns every 5 to 10 years in early spring.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
