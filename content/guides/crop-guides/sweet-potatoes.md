**A heat-loving, high-calorie crop that stores for months, and the leaves are edible too.** Not related to potatoes.

**Timing:** they need **a long warm season** (90 to 120 frost-free days) and warm soil. Plant "slips" (rooted shoots) **2 to 3 weeks after the last frost**, when the soil is at least 65 degrees F. Buy slips, or grow your own by setting a sweet potato half-submerged in water or moist soil in a warm room for 4 to 6 weeks.

**Soil:** loose, well-drained, not too rich. Too much nitrogen gives lots of vine and few roots. Plant on raised **ridges 8 inches high** to help drainage and warmth. Heavy clay gives small, misshapen roots.

**Planting:** set slips deep, leaving 2 or 3 leaves above ground, **12 to 18 inches apart**, in rows 3 to 4 feet apart. Water in well, and shade for a few days.

**Care:** water weekly for the first month, then reduce. Stop watering 2 to 3 weeks before harvest. Vines spread widely: lift and move them off the row now and then.

**Harvest:** before the first frost, when leaves start to yellow. **Frost-damaged vines can rot the roots**, so cut vines off and dig right away. Dig gently with a fork: the skin is thin and bruises easily.

**Cure (essential):** keep roots at about **80 to 85 degrees F and high humidity for 5 to 10 days** (a warm closet with a pan of water or a covered box near a heater), then store at **55 to 60 degrees F**. Do not refrigerate. Cured roots turn sweeter and keep **4 to 6 months** or more.

**Eating the greens:** young leaves cook like spinach.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
