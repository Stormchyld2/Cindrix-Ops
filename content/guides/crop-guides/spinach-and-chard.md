**Fast, cool-season leaves. Spinach bolts (goes to seed) in heat and long days; chard keeps going all summer.**

**Timing:** Spinach: sow **4 to 6 weeks before the last spring frost** and again **6 to 8 weeks before the first fall frost**; fall sowings do best. Chard: sow around the last frost date; it survives summer and winter in mild climates. Both are ready in 40 to 60 days.

**Sowing:** Seed 1/2 inch deep, 1 inch apart; thin spinach to 3 to 4 inches, chard to 8 to 12 inches (chard seed is a cluster, so always thin). Soak chard seed overnight to speed sprouting. Spinach will not sprout in soil above about 75 degrees F: sow in shade in late summer.

**Care:** Even moisture and a nitrogen-rich bed (compost). Shade cloth stretches spinach into early summer. Leaf miners leave tunnels: pick off affected leaves.

**Harvest:** Cut outer leaves of chard and "cut and come again" spinach when 4 to 6 inches long; leave the centre to regrow. Pull the whole plant when spinach starts to bolt.

**Storing:** Wash, blanch 2 minutes and freeze, or dry. Fresh leaves keep about a week in the fridge. Chard stems can be pickled.

**Seed saving:** Spinach is male or female plants and cross-pollinates by wind; chard and beets cross with each other. Chard is biennial: it seeds in its second year.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
