**Crops that give a lot of food from very little care, useful as backups.**

**Timing:** Jerusalem artichoke (sunchoke): plant tubers in **early spring or late autumn**. Peanuts: sow after the last frost when soil is 65 degrees F; they need about 120 to 140 days of warmth. Both are in the ground for the whole season.

**Sowing:** Jerusalem artichoke tubers 4 inches deep, 18 inches apart. Peanuts: shell the seed just before planting, 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart. (Raw "seed" peanuts, not roasted.)

**Care:** Sunchokes grow 8 to 10 feet and spread: put them where they can stay, they are hard to remove. Peanuts need loose, sandy soil; hill the soil up around the plants when they flower so the "pegs" can push down.

**Harvest:** Sunchokes: dig after the first frost; any left sprout again. Peanuts: dig when the leaves yellow, shake off the soil, and let the plants dry in the sun for 1 to 2 weeks.

**Storing:** Sunchokes keep best left in the ground, or in damp sand in a cold place for a few weeks. Dry peanuts are kept in the shell in a cool dry place.

**Seed saving:** Both are grown from tubers or seed nuts you keep back each year.

Sunchokes are mostly inulin, not starch, and can cause gas for some people: eat small amounts first.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
