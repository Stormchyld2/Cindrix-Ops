**Root crops: cool-season, long-storing, and they stay in the ground till you need them.**

**Timing:** sow **2 to 3 weeks before the last spring frost**, and again in **midsummer for an autumn crop** (the sweetest ones). Carrots take 60 to 80 days, beets 50 to 65, parsnips 100 to 130.

**The one big rule: loose, stone-free soil.** Rocks, clods or compacted ground make forked, stunted roots. Dig the bed 10 to 12 inches deep and rake it fine. Do not add fresh manure: it makes roots hairy and split.

**Sowing:**
1. Seed is tiny. Sow **thinly**, about 1/4 to 1/2 inch deep, in rows 12 inches apart.
2. Carrot and parsnip seed is slow to sprout (**1 to 3 weeks**). Keep the surface **constantly damp**: lay a board or damp burlap over the row until the first green shows, then remove it.
3. **Thin** when seedlings are 2 inches tall: carrots to 2 to 3 inches apart, beets to 3 to 4 inches, parsnips to 4 inches. Skipping this gives a crop of thin roots.
4. Beet "seeds" are really clusters holding several seeds, so they always need thinning. Beet leaves are edible, like chard.

**Care:** about 1 inch of water a week. Uneven watering causes cracking. Keep the shoulders of roots covered with soil so they do not turn green and bitter.

**Harvest:** carrots when about 3/4 inch across at the top; beets at 1.5 to 3 inches (larger ones turn woody). Parsnips are sweetest **after several frosts**.

**Storing:**
- **In the ground:** in cold climates, cover the row with 12 inches of straw or leaves after the soil starts to freeze, and dig as needed.
- **Cellar:** twist off the tops (leave 1/2 inch), pack unwashed roots in damp sand or sawdust in a box, and keep near freezing and humid. See the root cellaring page.

**Seed saving:** biennial: they flower in year two. Carrots will cross with wild Queen Anne's lace.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
