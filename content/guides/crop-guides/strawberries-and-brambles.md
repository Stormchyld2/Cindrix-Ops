**Berries that come back every year. Strawberries spread; brambles send up new canes.**

**Timing:** Plant bare-root plants in **early spring** as soon as the soil can be worked. Strawberries give a light crop the first year and full crops after.

**Sowing:** Strawberries: 18 inches apart, with the crown (the middle bud) level with the soil. Raspberries and blackberries: 2 to 3 feet apart in a row, with a wire or post to tie canes to.

**Care:** Mulch with straw. Pinch off strawberry flowers the first year for stronger plants and renew beds every 3 to 4 years. Raspberries: cut canes that have fruited to the ground after harvest (summer-bearing) or all canes in late winter (autumn-bearing). Birds love berries: net them.

**Harvest:** Pick strawberries fully red, every day or two in season. Brambles: pick when berries come off the plant with a gentle pull.

**Storing:** Freeze whole on a tray, then bag; dry; make jam and juice (see the jam page).

**Seed saving:** Propagate with runners (strawberries) or root suckers (raspberries); no seed needed.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
