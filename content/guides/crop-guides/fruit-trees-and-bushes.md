**Fruit trees take years to bear, so the best time to plant them was five years ago and the second best is this autumn.**

**Timing:** Plant bare-root trees in **early spring or late autumn** while dormant. Dwarf trees bear in 2 to 4 years; standard trees in 5 to 8. Grapes 3 years. Blueberries need 3 years to give a real crop.

**Sowing:** Dig a hole twice as wide as the roots and no deeper than they were grown. Do not put fertiliser in the hole. Most apples, pears and plums need **two different varieties** nearby for pollination; peaches, sour cherries and many grapes are self-fertile. Choose varieties rated for your cold hardiness zone and your chill hours.

**Care:** Water deeply once a week the first year. Mulch in a ring, not touching the trunk. Prune in late winter: remove dead, crossing and inward-growing branches and keep an open centre so light and air reach the fruit. Blueberries need acid soil (pH 4.5 to 5.5).

**Harvest:** Apples and pears are ripe when they come off with a gentle lift and twist and the seeds are brown. Pears are best picked a little firm and ripened indoors. Berries and stone fruit: when fully coloured and slightly soft.

**Storing:** Late apples keep in a cold cellar for months, wrapped singly in paper and kept off each other. Dry, can or ferment the rest into cider or vinegar.

**Seed saving:** Fruit trees are grafted: seed does not come true. Buy or make grafted trees; learn grafting from your Extension office.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
