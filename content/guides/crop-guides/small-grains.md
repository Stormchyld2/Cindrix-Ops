**Small grains are grown by broadcast sowing and harvested with a sickle or scythe: well within reach of a family plot of 1/10 acre or more.**

**Timing:** Winter wheat, barley and rye: sow **4 to 6 weeks before the soil freezes**, harvest the next summer. Spring wheat, oats and barley: sow as soon as the soil can be worked in spring; harvest in midsummer to late summer. Winter types give a bigger yield where winters allow.

**Sowing:** Broadcast 3 to 4 pounds of seed per 1000 square feet and rake in 1 inch deep. Rye is the most winter-hardy and tolerates poor soil; oats are the most tolerant of acidic or wet ground; barley is the fastest.

**Care:** Little care: weed early, keep birds off new seed with netting, and avoid very wet seasons which spread mould. Grains are cheap protection against soil erosion and the straw is useful for bedding and mulch.

**Harvest:** Cut when the stalks have turned golden and the grain is hard (a kernel snaps when bitten). Bundle into shocks to dry in the field for 1 to 2 weeks.

**Storing:** **Thresh** (knock grain loose by beating on a tarp or in a bag), **winnow** (pour from one container to another in a breeze to blow off chaff). Store dry in sealed containers. Whole grain keeps for years; ground flour keeps months.

**Seed saving:** Grains self-pollinate and mostly stay true. Save from the best, healthiest stands.
