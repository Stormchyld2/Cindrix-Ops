**An easy, fast, warm-season crop that fixes its own nitrogen.**

**Types:**
- **Bush beans:** ready in 50 to 60 days; most of the crop comes in over 2 to 3 weeks. Sow every 2 to 3 weeks for steady picking.
- **Pole beans:** climb a trellis; take 60 to 90 days but bear for weeks. More beans per square foot.
- **Dry beans** are covered in their own page.

**Timing:** sow **1 to 2 weeks after your last spring frost**, when the soil is at least 60 degrees F. Cold, wet soil rots the seed. Sow the last crop **about 8 to 10 weeks before the first autumn frost**.

**Planting:** 1 inch deep, **2 to 4 inches apart** (bush) in rows 18 to 24 inches apart. For pole beans, sow 4 to 6 seeds around the base of each 6 to 8 foot pole, or 4 inches apart along a trellis. Do not feed with much nitrogen: you will get leaves instead of beans.

**Care:** about 1 inch of water a week; **do not work or pick among wet plants** because it spreads rust and bacterial blight. Mulch to hold moisture.

**Harvest:** pick often, before the seeds bulge in the pods. Pods should snap cleanly. Regular picking keeps plants producing.

**Preserving:** blanch and freeze (see the freezing page), dry as "leather britches" (strung and hung in a dry, airy place), or pressure can. **Green beans must be pressure canned, never boiled-water canned.** Use the USDA guide.

**Seed saving:** very easy and mostly self-pollinating. Leave a few pods on the plant until fully dry and rattling. Keep dry.

Source: general home-gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services (university garden guides). Not yet checked against one named page: confirm planting times and varieties with your own county Extension office.
