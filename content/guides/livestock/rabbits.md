**Rabbits are quiet, small, fast-growing, and need little space.** A doe can raise many young a year on a small amount of feed. Rabbit meat is very lean: eat it with fat or oil, as a diet of only lean meat is unhealthy over time ("rabbit starvation").

**Housing:**
- Wire cages 30 x 36 inches for a doe and litter, raised off the ground, with a solid resting board. Wire floors keep them clean; they need a place to rest the feet.
- **Shade and airflow matter most.** Rabbits die above about **85 to 90 degrees F**. Give shade, cool tiles or frozen water bottles in heat. They handle cold well if dry and out of draughts.
- Predator-proof (raccoons, dogs, hawks, snakes).

**Feeding:** pellets plus unlimited **grass hay** (timothy or similar), and clean water at all times. Add garden greens slowly. Avoid sudden changes (they cause deadly gut upsets). Never feed lawn clippings that have fermented, or any wilted or mouldy food.

**Breeding:** does can breed from about 5 to 6 months (medium breeds). Pregnancy is about **31 days**. Provide a nest box and fur or straw. Kits are born naked and blind; do not disturb them early. Weaned at 6 to 8 weeks. Ready for the table at about **8 to 12 weeks** (4 to 5 pounds, depending on breed).

**Good meat breeds:** New Zealand, Californian, Champagne d'Argent.

**Health signs to act on:** not eating, runny droppings, a head tilt, wet nose or eyes, or sore hocks. Separate sick animals right away. Hygiene is the best medicine: clean cages, clean water.

**Slaughter and butchering:** see the butchering safety page.

**Check local rules** on keeping animals; town and housing rules vary.
