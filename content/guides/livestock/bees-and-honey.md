**Bees give honey, wax, and pollination for your garden and orchard. They take time and learning.**

**Before you start:**
- Join a local beekeeping club or learn from a keeper. Bees are easy to lose in the first year without help.
- Check you are not allergic to stings (a severe reaction is an emergency: see the first aid cards). Keep an epinephrine kit if a doctor has given you one.
- Check local rules, and tell your neighbours.

**Equipment:** hive boxes and frames (a Langstroth hive is the common type), a veil, gloves, a smoker, and a hive tool. A beginner's first year is a lot of equipment: allow for it.

**Bees:** buy a package or a "nuc" (small starter hive) in spring.

**The year:**
- **Spring:** build up; watch for swarming (a lot of bees leaving with the old queen). Give room by adding boxes.
- **Summer:** main honey flow. Leave honey for the bees; take only the surplus.
- **Autumn:** check food stores. A hive needs about **60 pounds of honey** in colder climates to survive winter. Feed sugar syrup (2 parts sugar to 1 part water) in autumn if short.
- **Winter:** leave them alone; protect from wind, damp and mice.

**Biggest threats:** the **varroa mite** (check and control; ask a keeper or Extension office), moisture, starvation, and robbing. Healthy hives are the best defence.

**Honey:** keep sealed; it does not spoil. **Never give honey to infants under 1 year old.**

**Smoke** calms bees by hiding alarm signals. Work on warm, sunny days, calmly, and slowly.

Source: general small-farm practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: ask a local vet or Extension office. This page gives no medicine amounts.
