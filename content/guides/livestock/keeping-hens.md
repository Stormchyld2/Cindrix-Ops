**Hens are the easiest first animal. They turn scraps and bugs into eggs.**

1. **Space:** about 3 to 4 square feet per bird inside the coop, and about 10 square feet per bird in the outside run.
2. **Coop:** dry, with no draughts, and **safe from foxes, raccoons, dogs and hawks**. Close it every night. Use strong wire, not chicken wire, on any opening a predator could reach.
3. **Nest boxes:** one for every 4 or 5 hens, in a quiet dark corner, with clean straw.
4. **Roost:** a bar higher than the nest boxes, about 8 to 10 inches of space for each bird.
5. **Food:** layer feed, and some grit and calcium (crushed oyster shell). Scraps and bugs are a bonus, not a full diet. Keep feed in a closed bin, away from rats.
6. **Water:** clean, **always**. In freezing weather check it twice a day.
7. **Eggs:** a hen starts laying at about 5 months. A good hen lays most days at first. Laying slows in winter and a little more each year.

**If a bird is ill:** keep her apart, and ask a vet or an experienced keeper. This program gives no medicine amounts.

Source: general backyard-poultry practice from state Extension services. Not yet checked against one named page.
