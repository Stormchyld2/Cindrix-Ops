**Keeping a flock or herd going year after year means breeding. Done carelessly, it weakens the animals; done well, it keeps them healthy and productive.**

**Gestation and incubation (typical):**
- **Chickens:** eggs hatch in 21 days. **Ducks:** 28 days (Muscovy 35).
- **Rabbits:** 31 days.
- **Goats and sheep:** about 150 days (five months).
- **Pigs:** about 114 days ("3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days").
- **Cattle:** about 283 days.
- Heat (oestrus) comes about every 3 weeks in goats, 17 days in sheep, 21 in cattle and pigs.

**Inbreeding** (mating close relatives) increases the chance that two copies of a harmful gene meet: weak chicks, small litters, deformities, poor growth. A little is manageable. A lot, generation after generation, builds up.

**Rules of thumb:**
1. **Don't breed parent to child or brother to sister** for more than one generation. Keep records of who is related.
2. **Bring in new blood** every 2 to 3 years: a male from another herd or flock.
3. **Keep several unrelated males** or lines if you can, and swap them between groups.
4. **Keep your best animals** for breeding: healthy, fast-growing, good mothers, easy-going. Cull weak or sick ones.
5. **Don't keep too few.** A closed group of under about 20 to 25 animals will become inbred in a few generations. For chickens, a **rooster for every 8 to 12 hens**.
6. **Record:** dates of breeding, births, litter sizes, losses, and weights.

**Heritage breeds** raise their own young, forage well and breed true. **Hybrids** (such as Cornish Cross chickens) don't breed true; the offspring are not like the parents.

**Rabbits:** a doe can have 4 to 8 litters a year; rest her between litters, and do not breed her before about 5 to 6 months.

**Birthing:** be present if you can. Have clean towels, a heat source for newborns, and a plan if the mother rejects them. Watch for trouble: a mother straining for more than an hour with no progress needs help from someone experienced or a vet.

**Don't breed** animals that are sick, too young, too thin, or older than their productive life.
