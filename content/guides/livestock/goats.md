**Goats give milk, meat, and eat brush and weeds, but they are escape artists and need real fencing.**

**Before you buy:** goats are herd animals: **never keep just one**. Check local zoning. Plan on a strong fence (woven wire or electric, 4 feet or more), a dry three-sided shelter, and a plan for kidding and milking.

**Breeds:**
- **Dairy:** Nubian, Alpine, Saanen, Nigerian Dwarf (small, rich milk).
- **Meat:** Boer, Kiko, Spanish.

**Feeding:**
- **Hay and browse** make up most of the diet. Goats prefer brush, leaves and weeds to grass.
- Fresh clean water daily.
- A loose **goat mineral** made for goats. Sheep minerals can hold too much copper for goats; your Extension office can say what is right for your area.
- Grain only for milking does and growing kids, in small amounts. Too much grain causes bloat and urinary stones in bucks and wethers.
- **Poisonous plants:** rhododendron, azalea, laurel, cherry leaves (wilted), yew, oleander, and others. Walk your pasture first and fence them off.

**Housing:** dry, draught-free, with bedding. Goats hate rain and wet ground.

**Parasites:** worms are the biggest problem for goats on wet pasture. Rotate grazing, don't overstock, and learn the **FAMACHA** eye-colour check and a worm egg count with a vet. Treatment choices and amounts are for a vet or Extension office to give you.

**Milking:** a doe must kid to give milk, then milks for 8 to 10 months. Milk twice a day at the same times. Clean udder, clean pail, then **cool milk fast** to 40 degrees F or below. Raw milk can carry disease: heat to **145 degrees F for 30 minutes** or **161 degrees F for 15 seconds** to pasteurise, then cool.

**Hooves** need trimming every 6 to 8 weeks.

Source: general small-farm practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: ask a local vet or Extension office. This page gives no medicine amounts.
