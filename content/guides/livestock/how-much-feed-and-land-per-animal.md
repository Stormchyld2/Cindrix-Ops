**The numbers behind every decision about what to keep and how many.**

**Daily dry feed (rough):** Cow 20 to 30 pounds hay. Horse or mule 15 to 20. Sheep or goat 3 to 5. Pig 4 to 8 pounds grain-equivalent. Adult chicken about 1/4 pound. Duck about 1/3 pound. Rabbit about 1/4 pound pellets or hay plus greens.

**Water per day:** Cow 8 to 12 gallons (20 or more in milk and heat). Horse 5 to 10. Sheep and goat 1 to 3. Pig 3 to 5. Chicken about a pint. Rabbit about a pint. Double in hot weather.

**Land:** 1 cow or horse per 1.5 to 3 acres of good pasture. 5 to 6 sheep or goats per acre. 6 to 10 pigs per acre on rotation. Poultry: 10 square feet of outdoor run per chicken, more is better.

**Eggs and meat from small flocks:** A hen lays about 200 to 250 eggs a year when young, dropping each year. A dozen hens feed a family of four with eggs. A doe rabbit raises 30 or more kits a year, or about 100 pounds of meat. A sheep or goat gives a lamb or kid or two a year.

**Reality check:** Feed stored for the animals competes with feed for people in a long emergency. Count the calories you need to keep an animal alive through winter against what it gives back. Chickens, rabbits, ducks and goats are the most efficient for small plots.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
