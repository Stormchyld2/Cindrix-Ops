**Fish are a good protein per pound of feed, and a pond is also a water store. Success depends on local rules, water quality and the right species.**

**Species:** Cold water: trout (needs clean flowing cold water). Warm water: **tilapia** (needs 70 degrees F or warmer, indoors in cold climates), **channel catfish**, bluegill and largemouth bass in ponds, carp where allowed. Many places forbid some species: ask your fish and wildlife agency.

**Pond basics:** A pond for fish should be at least 1/4 to 1/2 acre and 6 to 8 feet deep in part, so fish survive winter and hot spells. Fill from a reliable spring or stream and a clay lining or soil that holds water. Stock a **balanced mix**; your Extension service gives stocking rates for your region (often 100 bass and 500 bluegill per acre).

**Tank or tub fish:** Tilapia in tubs or tanks need aeration, filtration and a warm room. Fish waste water makes excellent garden fertiliser.

**Feeding:** Feed only what the fish eat in 5 minutes. Fish can also eat insects, duckweed, azolla, worms and kitchen scraps.

**Water quality:** Fish die from low oxygen (hot nights, algae die-off, ice cover), ammonia and sudden temperature changes. Never let a pond be fed by runoff from fields that were sprayed.

**Harvest and safety:** Clean and cook or smoke fish soon after catching. Never eat fish from water you are unsure of (industrial or sewage pollution).

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
