**Meat birds and rabbits can be processed in about 15 to 20 minutes each with a sharp knife, hot water and a clean place to work.**

**Before:** Take feed away 8 to 12 hours (water is fine). Sharpen knives. Set up a cold water tub (ice if possible), hot water at **145 to 150 degrees F** for scalding, and clean surfaces. Wear an apron.

**Chicken or duck, step by step:** 1) Hold the bird calmly, head down. 2) Make a swift clean cut across the main blood vessels in the neck with a very sharp knife (or use a killing cone); let it bleed out. 3) Dip in hot water for 30 to 60 seconds (ducks up to 2 minutes), and pluck by hand. 4) Remove the head and feet. 5) Cut around the vent and open the belly up to the breastbone, being careful not to cut the intestines. 6) Remove the entrails together, keeping the heart, liver and gizzard if you like (trim off the gall bladder, which is green). 7) Rinse inside and out. 8) **Chill at once** in ice water, then rest the meat in the fridge for 24 hours before freezing or cooking.

**Rabbit:** 1) Quick humane kill (a blow to the back of the head or neck stretch). 2) Hang by the hind legs and cut off the head and feet. 3) Cut around the hind legs and pull the skin down like a sock. 4) Open the belly and take out the organs, avoiding the bladder and gut. 5) Wash, chill, and rest 12 to 24 hours.

**Clean up:** Compost or bury offal and feathers at least 3 feet down, away from wells. Wash tools and surfaces with hot soapy water and then a bleach rinse (1 tablespoon of unscented bleach to a gallon of water) and rinse again.

**Safety:** Cook poultry to **165 degrees F** inside and rabbit to 160. Wash hands after handling raw meat. Never eat an animal that was sick, had a fever or acted strangely when it died.

Source: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service guidance on poultry and meat handling, and home-processing guides from state Extension services. Not yet checked line by line: confirm with your Extension office or an experienced neighbour.
