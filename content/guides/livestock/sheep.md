**Sheep are small, quiet and easy to handle, and make good use of rough pasture. They are prey animals and need good fencing and predator protection.**

**Before you buy:** Never keep one sheep alone: they are flock animals. Start with 2 to 4 ewes. Check zoning and predators first (dogs, coyotes).

**Breeds:** Wool and meat: Corriedale, Romney, Shetland, Katahdin (hair sheep that do not need shearing, hardy and good for beginners). Dairy: East Friesian, Lacaune. Meat: Suffolk, Dorper.

**Feeding:** Grass and hay are most of the diet: about **3 to 5 pounds of hay a day** per adult in winter. Give a **sheep mineral and salt** (never goat or cattle mineral: sheep are very sensitive to copper). Ewes carrying twins and nursing need grain in the last 6 weeks of pregnancy and in early milk.

**Water:** 1 to 3 gallons a day, more in hot weather and milk.

**Shelter and fencing:** A three-sided dry shed. Woven wire or 5-strand electric netting 36 to 42 inches high. Keep them off wet ground.

**Lambing:** Gestation is about **147 days** (5 months). Ewes usually lamb unaided, often in the early morning. Have iodine for navels and a clean towel ready. Lambs should stand and nurse within an hour. Cold wet lambs die fast: dry and warm them.

**Shearing:** Once a year in spring, before warm weather. Hand shears or a mechanical set; wool must be dry. Learn from someone first: shearing is a skill.

**Parasites:** Worms are the chief problem on wet pasture. Rotate grazing, do not overstock, and learn the eye-colour check (FAMACHA) and egg counts with your vet. Treatment choices and amounts are for a vet to give you.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
