**Other poultry for meat, eggs and weeding, each with a special use.**

**Turkeys:** Heritage breeds can breed naturally (broad-breasted turkeys cannot). Poults need a **higher-protein starter** and a warm brooder for 6 to 8 weeks; they are delicate for the first weeks. Ready for the table at 5 to 7 months (heritage 6 to 8). Incubation is **28 days**. Keep them away from chickens if blackhead disease has been in your flock.

**Geese:** Hardy, long-lived and very good guards. They graze grass and weed gardens. Incubation **28 to 35 days**. A goose lays in spring only. They need only a simple shelter and water to wash in. Meat geese are ready at 20 to 28 weeks.

**Quail:** Tiny and fast: they lay at 6 to 8 weeks, and need only about 1 square foot each in a cage. Quail eggs and meat are an efficient use of small space and feed. Incubation **17 to 18 days**. They need warmth and a higher-protein feed.

**Guinea fowl:** Excellent watchdogs and tick and insect eaters. Loud. They roost in trees and may lay away from the coop. Incubation **26 to 28 days**. Keep chicks warm and dry.

**Muscovy and other ducks:** See the ducks and meat birds page.

**Feeding all poultry:** Layer or grower feed with the right protein, grit, clean water, and kitchen scraps and garden greens. Give oyster shell to laying birds for strong shells. Never feed chocolate, avocado, raw dry beans or mouldy food.

**Predator proofing:** Close birds in a secure house every night. Dig fencing in 12 inches or put it in an apron on the ground; use hardware cloth (not chicken wire) on windows.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
