**Animals depend on you for water first, then feed, then shelter. Plan for a hard winter and a power cut.**

**Water:** the most urgent need.
- **Chickens and rabbits:** a pint or two per bird or rabbit a day, more in heat.
- **Goats and sheep:** 1 to 3 gallons a day.
- **Cattle:** 10 to 20 gallons a day.
- In freezing weather, check twice a day, break ice, or use a heated bucket. Without power, use black rubber buckets (they warm in sun) and carry warm water.

**Feed stores:**
- Keep at least a month's feed ahead; more is better. Store in metal bins with tight lids to keep out rodents, damp and mould.
- **Hay** needs to be kept dry and off the ground; damp hay rots and can heat and catch fire.
- **Grain and pellets** last about 3 to 6 months, less in warm weather.
- Keep **minerals and salt** available.

**Plan feed for no shop trips:** pasture, hay, garden surplus, and kitchen scraps. **Don't feed** chocolate, avocado, raw potato peel or green potato, rhubarb leaves, onions to most animals, or mouldy food.

**Winter shelter:**
- **Dry and out of the wind** matter more than warm. Wet animals lose heat fast.
- Deep dry bedding (straw) is warm.
- Chickens need **ventilation** high up: damp air causes frostbite. Don't close them in tightly.
- **Calories** are the winter fuel: animals eat more when it is cold.

**Power-cut plan:** hand-carried water, spare feed, a way to light the barn safely (no open flame near hay), and a check-in routine with a neighbour.

**Keep records**, and have the number of a vet. Learn the normal look and behaviour of each animal so you spot illness early.

Source: general small-farm practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: ask a local vet or Extension office. This page gives no medicine amounts.
