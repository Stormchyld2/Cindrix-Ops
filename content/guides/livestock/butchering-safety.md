**Meat is safe when it is clean, kept cold, and cooked enough.** Illness comes from the gut contents, dirty tools, warm meat, and undercooking.

**Before:**
- Don't slaughter an animal that looks sick, is limping, has diarrhoea, or has lumps. Do not feed or give any drug close to slaughter without checking the **withdrawal time** on the label with a vet.
- Withhold feed for 12 to 24 hours (leave water) to empty the gut.
- Have a clean place, a sharp knife, clean water, cold water or ice, and containers ready first.

**During:**
1. Work calmly: stress spoils meat.
2. **Do not cut the intestines or bladder.** If you do, rinse that area clean at once.
3. Remove the organs with care; check the liver and lungs. **Spots, pus, or lumps mean you should throw the organ out**, and if widespread, do not eat the animal.
4. Wash hands and tools with soap and hot water. Use separate knives for dirty and clean work.

**After:**
1. **Chill the meat quickly** to **40 degrees F or below**. Poultry in ice water for an hour or two. Large animals hang cool (35 to 40 degrees F) for a few days to age and firm up; in warm weather skip ageing and chill at once.
2. Never leave meat out for more than 2 hours (1 hour over 90 degrees F).
3. Cook: **poultry 165 degrees F; ground meat 160; whole cuts of beef, pork and game 145** with a 3-minute rest (use a thermometer in the thickest part).
4. Freeze or preserve what you won't eat in 2 to 3 days (see the food preservation pages).

**Wild game:** wear gloves. Don't eat an animal that looked sick or behaved oddly; if the liver or lungs are spotted, discard.

**Keep a record** of animals, dates and any treatments.

**Disposal:** bury offal deep (several feet), away from wells, or compost it hot. Don't leave it for scavengers near your animals.

Source: USDA FSIS guidance on safe handling and temperatures, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed: learn butchering in person from an experienced person if you can.
