**Most farm animals did fine before electricity, but some need a plan: water, milk, heat and fencing.**

**Water:** Hand pump, gravity from a spring or tank on high ground, rain barrels, or a windmill. Water by hand is heavy: 8 to 12 gallons per cow per day. Plan the storage: a full tank can last several days.

**Fencing:** Solar electric fence chargers run on a small panel and battery. Keep a spare charger and wire. Where possible use woven wire and strong posts that need no power.

**Milking and cooling:** Hand milking needs no power. Cool milk in spring water, a cellar or a wet cloth evaporation cooler; use it fast or turn it into butter, yoghurt and cheese.

**Heat for young animals:** Heat lamps need power. Use deep dry bedding, a warm corner under a wooden hood, hot water bottles for chicks, and the body heat of a close-packed brood. Put brooders away from anything that can burn.

**Hot weather:** Shade, extra water, wet floors and airflow. Pigs and rabbits suffer most.

**Fuel:** Without tractors, a pair of oxen, mules or horses plus a plough, mower and harrow can work 5 to 10 acres. Hand tools work smaller plots.

**Feed:** Without feed shops, grow, store and ration. Be ready to cull the herd down to what you can feed through winter before autumn, not after.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
