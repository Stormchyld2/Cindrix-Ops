**When fuel is gone, animal power moves loads, ploughs fields and carries people. These animals live 25 to 40 years, so the commitment is long.**

**Which animal:** **Donkeys** are tough, quiet, cheap to feed, and good guards and pack animals. **Mules** (donkey sire, horse mare) are strong, sure-footed, long-lived and very good workers. **Draft horses** (Percheron, Belgian, Clydesdale) pull ploughs and wagons. **Riding horses** carry people. **Oxen** (a trained steer) pull slowly but strongly and eat only grass and hay; they can also be eaten at the end of their working life.

**Feeding:** A horse or mule needs about **1.5 to 2 percent of its body weight in hay a day** (15 to 20 pounds for a 1000 pound animal), more when working hard, plus salt and clean water (5 to 10 gallons a day). Donkeys do well on **less rich feed**, and fat donkeys founder (a painful hoof disease): feed straw and hay, not much grain.

**Hooves and tack:** Hooves must be trimmed every 6 to 8 weeks. Shoes or boots are needed on stony ground or hard work. Learn to trim and to fit a harness and collar. Keep leather oiled.

**Training:** Work animals must be trained. Start young with a patient person, and learn from someone who has done it. A badly trained draft animal is dangerous.

**Health:** Colic (belly pain: pawing, rolling, looking at the flank) is an emergency: walk the animal and call a vet. Founder, strangles (a nasal discharge and swollen glands) and tetanus are common problems; ask a vet about vaccines and care.

**Gestation:** Horse about **340 days**; donkey **365 days**.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
