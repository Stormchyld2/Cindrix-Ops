**Grass is the cheapest feed, and resting it is how you keep it growing.**

**The rule:** Graze it down to about **3 to 4 inches** and then move the animals off for 3 to 6 weeks (more in dry weather) so the grass can recover. Never graze to bare soil.

**Paddocks:** Divide the pasture into 4 to 8 sections with electric fence. Move animals every 1 to 3 days, or whenever grass is grazed to the target height.

**Mix of plants:** A good pasture has grasses (fescue, orchardgrass, ryegrass, timothy) and legumes (clovers, alfalfa, bird's-foot trefoil), which add nitrogen. Overseed thin spots in early spring or late summer.

**Winter feed:** In cold climates, plan hay or stockpiled grass for the whole winter. Count the number of grazing days you have and the days you must feed hay.

**Poisonous plants:** Learn those in your area (for example hemlock, nightshade, milkweed, bracken, wilted wild cherry, buttercup, ragwort, yew). Animals usually avoid them on good pasture but eat them when hungry or in hay.

**Animals per acre:** Roughly: **1 cow or horse per 1.5 to 3 acres** of good pasture; **5 to 6 sheep or goats per acre**; **6 to 10 pigs per acre** on rotational pasture. Dry country needs far more land per animal.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
