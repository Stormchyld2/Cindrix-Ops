**A notebook turns guesses into knowledge, and shows you the signs of a problem early.**

**For each animal:** Name or number, date of birth, parents, breeding dates, due dates, births, weights, illness and treatment (what, when, and the withdrawal time), and what happened to it.

**For the herd:** Feed bought and made, hay and grain stores, milk or egg counts, losses and why, and the dates you moved animals to new pasture.

**Where:** A paper notebook or a card per animal in the barn. Keep a copy in the house.

**Use it:** Compare year to year. Cull animals that do not produce. Spot a pattern (a barn that always has sick lambs, a pasture with worm trouble).

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
