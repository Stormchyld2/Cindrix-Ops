**Start small and learn. Each new animal adds work and feed.**

**First year:** **Chickens** (eggs and meat, simple and quick to learn) and **rabbits** (meat, tiny space, quiet). Then consider **ducks** (hardy, good in wet).

**Second year:** **Goats** (milk and meat from little land) or **sheep** (easy care, wool). Add **bees** (honey, and pollination) if you want sweetness and have the patience.

**Later:** A **pig** for a season (fast meat and lard). A **cow** if you have land and can milk twice a day for years. A **donkey or horse** for work and guarding.

**Count the cost:** Each animal needs water daily, a roof, fence, feed through winter, and a plan for when it is sick or old. Do not buy more animals than your land and your time can carry.

**What to learn:** Learn first from a neighbour or a short course. Read the guide for each animal before you buy it.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
