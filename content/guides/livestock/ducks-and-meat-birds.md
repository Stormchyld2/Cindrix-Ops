**Ducks** are hardy, lay well in the cold, handle wet weather, eat slugs, and are less prone to disease than chickens. They need **water to dip their heads** (a bucket is enough) but not a pond.

- **Breeds:** Khaki Campbell and Indian Runner (eggs), Pekin and Muscovy (meat), Cayuga (dual).
- **Feed:** a duck or waterfowl ration; **niacin** needs are higher than chickens'. Ducklings need niacin-rich feed or brewer's yeast. Never medicated chick starter without checking the label.
- **Shelter:** a dry, secure shed for night; they will use it if you feed them there.
- **Eggs:** layers lay 150 to 300 eggs a year; they lay in the early morning, so shut them in until mid-morning.

**Meat chickens (broilers)**
- **Cornish Cross** grow fast, to 5 to 6 pounds in **6 to 8 weeks**. They eat a lot and are weak: heat, overfeeding and leg problems kill them. Keep feed limited at night, and give shade.
- **Heritage or dual-purpose breeds** (Plymouth Rock, Orpington, Rhode Island Red) take **16 to 20 weeks** but forage and can breed true.
- Chickens bred to be dual-purpose are better for a long-term plan because they reproduce.

**Brooding chicks and ducklings:**
1. Brooder: draught-free box with a heat source. 95 degrees F in the first week, reduce by 5 degrees each week until they feather out.
2. Water and starter feed at all times; keep bedding dry (ducklings are messy).
3. Watch the chicks: huddling means cold, spreading to the edges means hot.

**Predators:** raccoons, foxes, hawks, dogs, rats and snakes. Close the coop at night; use hardware cloth (not chicken wire) on windows.

**Disease:** sick birds should be separated at once. Wash hands after handling birds: they can carry salmonella.

Source: general small-farm practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet reviewed: ask a local vet or Extension office. This page gives no medicine amounts.
