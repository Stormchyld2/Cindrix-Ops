**Most fruits, many vegetables, and nuts need insects to carry pollen. No pollination, no crop.**

**Who does the work:**
- **Honey bees** (kept in hives).
- **Native bees:** bumble bees, mason bees, sweat bees, squash bees. Many are better pollinators than honey bees, are gentle, and need no management.
- **Others:** flies, wasps, beetles, butterflies, moths, and hummingbirds.

**Crops that need insects:** apples, pears, cherries, plums, berries, squash, cucumbers, melons, pumpkins, many beans on poor sets. **Wind-pollinated** crops (corn, wheat, rye) and **self-pollinating** ones (peas, beans, lettuce, tomatoes mostly) do not.

**Helping wild pollinators:**
1. **Flowers all season:** early spring (willow, crocus, fruit blossom), summer, and autumn (asters, goldenrod). Single-flowered kinds are best.
2. **Leave some bare ground** and dead stems and logs for nests.
3. **Mason bee houses:** bundles of hollow reeds or drilled blocks of untreated wood, 3 to 5/16 inch holes, 3 to 5 inches deep, facing east or south-east.
4. **Don't spray** insecticide on blooms. If you must, do it at night.
5. **Water:** a shallow dish with stones.
6. **Mow less.** Clover and dandelions feed early bees.

**Orchards:** most apples, pears and many plums and cherries need a **different variety** nearby for cross-pollination (within about 50 to 100 feet). Check each tree. Bees need to be flying: bloom in cold, wet weather means a poor crop.

**Hand pollinating (for squash, cucumbers, melons, and fruit in low-bee areas):**
1. In the morning, find a male flower (thin stem, no tiny fruit) and a female flower (a small swelling behind the bloom).
2. Pick the male flower and peel the petals back, or use a small soft brush.
3. Dab pollen on the centre of the female flower.
4. Do it for several flowers a day while blooming.

**Fruit trees too early to bloom?** A late frost during bloom kills the crop. Plant on slopes, not in low spots, and choose later-blooming varieties.

Source: general gardening and orchard practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet reviewed.
