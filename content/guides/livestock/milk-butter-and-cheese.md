**Milk spoils in a day or two without a fridge. Turning it into butter, yoghurt and cheese lets you keep the food for weeks or months.**

**Clean milk first:** Wash udder and hands, milk into a clean pail, strain and chill fast. Everything the milk touches must be washed in hot soapy water and rinsed.

**Safe milk:** Heat to **145 degrees F for 30 minutes** (or **161 degrees F for 15 seconds**) and cool. Heated milk is safer for children, older people, pregnant women and anyone ill.

**Butter:** Let cream rise (24 hours), skim, and churn it in a jar or churn until the butter forms and buttermilk separates. Wash the butter in cold water, work out water, salt to taste. Keeps 1 to 2 weeks cool; **salted butter keeps longer; ghee (clarified butter) keeps months** at room temperature.

**Yoghurt:** Heat milk to 180 degrees F, cool to about 110 degrees F, stir in a spoon of live yoghurt, and keep warm (110 degrees F) for 6 to 8 hours in a thermos or warm pan. Keep some to start the next batch.

**Soft cheese:** Add lemon juice or vinegar to hot milk (about 180 degrees F), strain through cloth: you get paneer or ricotta-style cheese. Add salt. Eat within days or press and dry.

**Hard cheese:** Needs rennet, a starter culture, a press and a cool cellar (50 to 55 degrees F) for aging 2 to 12 months. Salting heavily and waxing helps it keep. Learn from a cheesemaker.

**Whey:** Feed to pigs and chickens, or use in baking and soups.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
