**A cow gives milk, meat, manure and work, and turns grass you cannot eat into food, but she needs land, daily care and a plan for every calf.**

**Before you buy:** A single milk cow needs about **1.5 to 3 acres of good pasture** (more in dry country), strong fencing, a dry shelter, and **twice-a-day milking** at the same times for most of the year. If you cannot be there, do not buy a dairy cow. Beef cattle are less work but need the same land.

**Breeds:** Small dairy breeds suit a family: Jersey and Guernsey (rich milk), Dexter and Miniature Jersey (small, hardy). Larger: Holstein, Brown Swiss. Beef: Angus, Hereford, Highland, Galloway. A "dual-purpose" breed such as Shorthorn or Dexter gives both.

**Feeding:** A cow eats about **2 to 3 percent of her body weight a day in dry feed** (about 20 to 30 pounds of hay for a 1000 pound cow in winter). Good pasture and hay are most of the diet. A milking cow needs extra grain or good legume hay. Give a **cattle mineral** and plain salt free choice.

**Water:** 8 to 12 gallons a day, more (20 or more) in hot weather or in milk. A cow that does not drink does not milk.

**Housing and fencing:** Three-sided shelter or barn, bedding, a dry yard in mud season. Fence with 2 or 3 strands of electric wire on a good charger and a few strong posts, or woven wire. A bull is dangerous: use artificial insemination (a vet or breeder can supply it) or hire a bull.

**Calving:** Gestation is about **283 days** (9.5 months). Most cows calve on their own. Watch for the cow to isolate herself, restless, tail raised, then pushing. Call a vet if she strains for more than an hour without progress, or if you see only one foot, a tail first, or no feet. Make sure the calf stands and nurses in the first hours: the first milk (colostrum) is vital.

**Milking:** Wash the udder with warm water, dry it, and milk with clean, dry hands into a clean steel pail. Strip a few squirts first. Strain at once and **cool to 40 degrees F quickly**. Milk the same hours every day. Dip teats after milking. Heat raw milk to **145 degrees F for 30 minutes** (or 161 degrees F for 15 seconds) if you are unsure of the cow's health or for children, then cool.

**Meat:** A steer is usually ready at 18 to 24 months on grass. Butchering a large animal needs a hook or hoist, cold hanging for 7 to 14 days, and skill: learn from someone who has done it, or use a local custom butcher while they exist. See the butchering safety page.

**Health:** Daily: look at each cow for eating, chewing the cud, walking, dung. See the daily health check page. **Bloat** (swollen left side) from wet clover or alfalfa is an emergency: call a vet. Treatments and amounts of any medicine are for a vet to give you.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
