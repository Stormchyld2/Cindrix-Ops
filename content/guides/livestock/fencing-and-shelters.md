**Most animal losses and neighbour problems begin with a weak fence or a wet, draughty shelter.**

**Electric fence:** A good **charger**, a ground rod, and one or two strands of wire keep most animals in once they have been trained (the shock teaches them). Use solar chargers where there is no power. Test the fence with a tester or a stalk of wet grass.

**Woven wire and posts:** Strong corner posts braced with a diagonal rail and wire are the whole fence. Line posts 8 to 12 feet apart. Use field fence with small openings for sheep, goats, hogs; horse-safe mesh for horses.

**Fence heights:** Cattle 4 feet; sheep and goats 4 feet (goats may climb); pigs 3 feet of strong mesh with electric wire at the bottom; poultry 6 feet and roofed against predators.

**Shelters:** A dry, draught-free, **ventilated** shelter. Condensation and ammonia cause lung disease. Open the south side in summer for air. Bedding deep and dry, cleaned out before it gets soggy. Allow enough space: sheep and goats 15 to 20 square feet each, a cow 80 to 100, a pig 8 to 10.

**Mud and manure:** Put shelters on high ground that drains. Move feed and water around so the ground does not become a bog. Compost manure and bedding before use in the garden.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
