**A dog is an alarm, a guard and a worker; a cat keeps rats and mice from your stored grain. Both need food and care, which must be counted in your stores.**

**Working dogs:** A dog barking at night warns the household of a person or predator. Herding dogs (border collie, Australian shepherd) move livestock. Guardian dogs (Great Pyrenees, Anatolian) live with the flock. Hunting dogs find game and flush rabbits.

**Feeding:** A medium dog eats about **1 to 2 cups of dry food a day**, a large dog 2 to 4. In a long emergency you can feed cooked rice, potatoes, eggs, meat scraps and bones that do not splinter. **Never give chocolate, onions, garlic in quantity, grapes or raisins, or sweeteners such as xylitol.**

**Cats:** A farm cat needs little: water, shelter and a little food. They keep grain stores free of rats. Keep a few. Neutering prevents unwanted kittens.

**Health:** Keep vaccines (rabies in particular) current while a vet is available. Worm and flea control keep dogs well. Watch for rabies: a dog that is aggressive, drooling, or paralysed without cause. Do not handle it.

**Stores:** Count dog and cat food in your supply plans: dry food keeps 6 to 12 months in a sealed container in a cool dry place.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
