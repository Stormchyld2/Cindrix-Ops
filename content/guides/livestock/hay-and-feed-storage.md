**Without stored feed, winter and drought end a herd. Plan for it before you buy the animals.**

**How much hay:** Count a winter of **150 to 200 days**. Rough hay per day: cow 25 to 30 pounds; horse 15 to 20; sheep or goat 3 to 5; rabbit 1/4 pound. Add 10 to 20 percent for waste.

**Making hay:** Cut when the plants are just before flower (best food value) on a stretch of dry weather. Dry in the sun until it is brittle and has no green juice when twisted. Bale or stack when below about **18 percent moisture**: damp hay overheats and can catch fire by itself.

**Storing hay:** Keep dry and off the ground on pallets or poles under a roof, with air passing. Stack loosely at first; check for heat in the first weeks (use a hay probe or a rod pushed in and felt for warmth).

**Grain and feed:** Store grain in sealed metal or thick plastic bins that rats cannot chew, in a cool dry place. Whole grain keeps longer than ground. Feed oldest first. Mouldy feed can make animals very sick: throw it away.

**Silage and other options:** Sprouted barley fodder, root crops (mangels, turnips, pumpkins), tree leaves and branches in autumn can stretch feed. Dry leafy branches from safe trees (willow, poplar, maple, elm, ash) for goats and sheep.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
