**Foxes, coyotes, raccoons, hawks, stray dogs and snakes take more poultry and lambs than disease does.**

**Locking up:** The best protection is a **secure house at night**: solid walls, hardware cloth over every opening, latches that raccoons cannot open (they use their hands), and a floor or buried skirt so nothing digs in.

**Fences:** Electric fence (a strand at 6 and 12 inches) keeps out many diggers. Cover runs with netting or wire against hawks and owls.

**Guard animals:** **Livestock guardian dogs** (Great Pyrenees, Anatolian, Maremma) live with the flock from puppyhood and are very effective, but are big, loud and need training. **Llamas and donkeys** guard sheep against single dogs and coyotes. **Geese and guinea fowl** raise the alarm.

**Disease risk:** Predators and rodents carry disease. Clean up spilled feed, which attracts rats and wild birds.

**Wild animal bites:** An animal that attacks without fear or acts strangely may have rabies. Do not handle it. Wash any bite or scratch well with soap and water for 15 minutes and get medical care.

Source: general livestock practice as taught by state Extension services and the USDA (for example Penn State, Oregon State and Cornell small-farm guides).
