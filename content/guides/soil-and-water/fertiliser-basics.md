**Plants need three main nutrients, shown on a fertiliser bag as three numbers (N-P-K).**
- **N (nitrogen):** leafy growth. Greens, corn and cabbage take a lot.
- **P (phosphorus):** roots, flowers and fruit.
- **K (potassium):** overall health and storage quality.

**Free and local sources:**
- **Compost:** feeds the soil slowly and improves its structure. The best single thing to add.
- **Aged manure** (chicken, rabbit, cow, horse, goat): rabbit is mild; **chicken is strong** and can burn plants. **Age it for 6 months** or compost it hot before use. Do not put fresh manure on crops you will eat within 120 days.
- **Wood ash:** potassium and lime; use sparingly (a handful per square yard a year), never near potatoes or blueberries, and only if soil is not already alkaline. Never use coal ash.
- **Bone meal (P), blood meal (N), fish scraps, seaweed (K and trace elements)**.
- **Leaves, grass clippings, straw** as mulch that rots into the soil.
- **Legumes** (beans, peas, clover, vetch) add nitrogen through bacteria on their roots.

**Signs of trouble:**
- Pale, yellowing older leaves: low nitrogen.
- Purple tinge on young plants: often cold soil, or low phosphorus.
- Brown, scorched leaf edges: low potassium or salt/drought stress.
- Too much nitrogen: lots of dark leaves and few flowers or fruit.

**Soil pH** (see the soil test page) decides whether plants can take nutrients up at all. Most vegetables like 6.0 to 7.0.

**Rule:** feed the soil, not just the plant. Add 1 to 2 inches of compost to beds each year.

**Safety:** wash your hands after handling manure; wash all produce.

Source: general gardening and water practice, as taught by state Extension services and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
