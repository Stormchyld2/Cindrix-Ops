**Soil acidity decides whether plants can reach their food. Most vegetables grow best at a pH of 6.0 to 7.0.**

**Test:** Buy a home kit, use a meter, or send a sample to the Extension office. Test in autumn so you have time to correct it.

**Too acid (low pH, below 6):** Add **ground limestone**. A rough amount to raise pH by one point: **5 pounds per 100 square feet** for loam, less for sand and more for clay. Work it in and wait weeks to months. Wood ash also raises pH but use it sparingly and not on potatoes.

**Too alkaline (high pH, above 7.5):** Add elemental **sulfur** or lots of organic matter, or use pine needles and peat for acid-loving plants. Change slowly; retest in a year.

**Plants that like acid soil:** Blueberries (4.5 to 5.5), potatoes (5.0 to 6.0), strawberries and raspberries (5.5 to 6.5). Most vegetables, beans and brassicas like 6.0 to 7.0.

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
