**Floodwater carries sewage, animal waste, fuel and chemicals. Treat anything it touched as unsafe until you know otherwise.**

**After a flood:**
1. **Throw out** any crop whose edible part touched floodwater: leafy greens, lettuce, berries, tomatoes and other fruit near the ground, and anything eaten raw. Washing does not make it safe.
2. **Root crops** (carrots, potatoes, beets) were in the soil that was flooded. Many Extension offices advise not eating them. Follow your local guidance.
3. **Wait before planting and picking.** Many Extension offices advise waiting at least 60 days before harvesting anything from flooded ground, longer for crops that touch the soil, and after a flood from a source with chemicals, until the soil is tested. Check with your county office.
4. **Tree fruit and nuts** that were above the water are generally safe if washed, but if the water reached the fruit, discard it.
5. **Canned food and jars** that were covered by floodwater should be thrown away unless the jar seal and rim can be washed and sanitised, with no dents or rust. When in doubt, throw it out.
6. Wear gloves and boots, and wash well after work in flooded soil. Keep children out.

**Other kinds of contaminated ground:**
- **Heavy metals** (lead, arsenic, cadmium) stay in soil for decades. They get into leafy greens and root crops, and into children who play in the dirt. Test the soil. If metals are high, use **raised beds with clean soil lined from the ground**, grow fruiting crops (tomatoes, beans) rather than roots and leaves, keep soil damp to cut dust, and wash and peel.
- **Fuel, oil, solvents or unknown chemicals:** don't grow food there. Ask for advice.
- **Irrigation water:** don't use water from ponds, ditches or streams that run past farms, factories or towns on vegetables you eat raw unless treated or tested. Well water should be tested for bacteria.
- **Fresh manure or sewage sludge** on fields: wait the advised time (see the sanitation page).

**How to test:** many state Extension offices and university labs test soil for lead and other metals for a small fee. A kit from a hardware store is a rough check only.

**Safe approach:** if you can't be sure, grow in containers and raised beds with imported soil, and drink and wash with treated water only.

Source: FDA, USDA and state Extension guidance on flooded gardens, and EPA guidance on contaminated soil, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed; rules differ by state, so follow your county Extension office.
