**Water used well doubles a harvest from the same supply.**

**When to water:** Deep and less often is better: **1 inch a week** from rain or hose, in the early morning. Check by digging 2 inches down: if it is dry, water.

**Drip lines:** Hoses with holes or emitters laid along the row put water at the roots and waste little. Gravity can feed a drip system from a barrel raised 2 to 3 feet.

**Ollas:** A buried unglazed clay pot filled with water. The water seeps out slowly into the soil as plants take it. Fill every few days. Works well for tomatoes, squash and fruit.

**Plastic bottles:** Poke small holes in the cap of a bottle, bury the neck near a plant and fill. A simple drip.

**Mulch and shade:** Mulch cuts water use by half. Shade cloth in hot afternoons helps leafy crops.

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
