**Prevention works better than cure. Healthy soil, the right plant, and watching every day.**

**Habits that prevent most problems:**
1. **Rotate** crop families.
2. **Water at the soil**, in the morning, so leaves dry.
3. **Space plants** for airflow.
4. **Remove sick leaves and plants** quickly, and throw them out (don't compost diseased tomato or potato plants).
5. **Clean up** old plants in autumn.
6. **Use resistant varieties** where you can.
7. **Mix plants:** flowers like marigold, alyssum and dill bring in beneficial insects.

**Look every day or two. Common problems:**
- **Aphids:** knock off with a spray of water, or wipe by hand. Ladybugs eat them.
- **Cabbage worms and tomato hornworms:** pick off by hand.
- **Slugs:** beer traps, boards to collect them in the morning, copper tape, clean mulch.
- **Squash bugs, cucumber beetles:** hand-pick, row cover, remove hiding places.
- **Colorado potato beetles:** hand-pick adults, eggs (on the leaf undersides), larvae.
- **Deer and rabbits:** a fence is the only answer that lasts.
- **Birds:** netting over berries.
- **Powdery mildew** (white dust): space plants, water at the soil, remove the worst leaves.
- **Late blight** (tomatoes, potatoes: dark, wet patches, white fuzz underneath): remove at once and bag; do not compost.
- **Blossom-end rot:** a water problem, not a disease.

**Barriers:** row cover, collars of cardboard around seedlings against cutworms, wire cages.

**Do not use** household chemicals or mixes you are unsure of: they can poison you, soil, or pollinators. If using any product, read and follow the label exactly. A mild **soap spray** (a few drops of plain soap per quart of water) helps against soft-bodied insects, but test on a few leaves first.

Source: general gardening and water practice, as taught by state Extension services and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
