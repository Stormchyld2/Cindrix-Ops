**Check the land before you put weeks of work into it.** An afternoon of tests can save a failed season.

**1. Sun.** Vegetables want 6 to 8 hours of direct sun. Watch the spot at 9am, noon and 4pm, and remember that trees will leaf out and shade it in summer.

**2. Slope and cold air.** Cold air runs downhill and pools in low spots ("frost pockets"), so frost comes earlier and later there. A gentle south-facing slope is warmest. Steep slopes erode: plant across the slope, not up and down it.

**3. Drainage test.** Dig a hole a foot deep and wide. Fill it with water and let it drain. Fill it again and time the second draining. Good garden soil drains **1 to 6 inches an hour**. If water is still standing after a day, use raised beds or choose another site.

**4. Soil texture (the jar test).**
1. Fill a straight-sided jar one-third full of soil with the stones removed. Add water nearly to the top and a spoonful of dish soap.
2. Shake hard for a minute and set it down.
3. **Sand** settles in about a minute, **silt** in a few hours, **clay** in a day or two. Measure the layers. A good garden soil is a mix, with no layer much more than half.

**5. Depth.** Push a spade or a rod down. If it stops at 6 inches on rock or hardpan, use raised beds.

**6. Acid or sweet (pH) and nutrients.** See the soil test page. Most vegetables like pH 6.0 to 7.0.

**7. History of the site.**
- Old buildings, orchards or roadsides can leave **lead or arsenic** in the soil. Test before growing food, or use raised beds with clean soil.
- Former dumps, fuel storage, or industrial land: do not grow food there without testing.
- Land that flooded: see the flood page.

**8. Water.** Is there a well, a stream, or a roof to collect from? Water you can carry in buckets limits the size of your garden.

**9. Wind, fences and animals.** Wind dries soil and breaks plants. Deer, rabbits and neighbours' dogs are real issues.

**10. Access.** Can you reach it every day? Can a cart get to it?

**Score it:** a site that fails on sun, drainage or contamination should be fixed or avoided. Weeds are a clue: dock and moss mean wet or acid soil; clover and plantain mean compact soil.

Source: general gardening and farm practice, as taught by state Extension services, USDA and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
