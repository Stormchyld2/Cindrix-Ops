**Where and how to lay out a garden matters as much as what you grow.**

**Choose the site:**
- **Sun:** 6 to 8 hours of direct sun a day for vegetables (fewer for leafy crops).
- **Water** within reach, and drainage: avoid low spots where water pools.
- **Close to the house**, so you see it every day.
- **Away from big tree roots** and from walnut trees (they poison tomatoes and some other plants).
- **Test for lead** if the site is next to an old painted building or road: lead in soil is dangerous, especially for children. Use raised beds with clean soil if in doubt. Wash and peel root crops.

**Beds:**
- **In-ground rows** are cheap and good for big crops (potatoes, corn, squash).
- **Raised beds** (8 to 12 inches high, 3 to 4 feet wide so you can reach the middle, any length) warm sooner in spring, drain well, and let you improve small areas of soil. Fill with a mix of topsoil and compost.
- Paths 18 to 24 inches wide.
- **Don't walk on the beds:** compaction harms roots.

**Layout tips:**
- Put tall crops (corn, trellised beans) on the north side so they don't shade the rest.
- Put the thing you pick every day (salad greens, herbs) nearest the door.
- Group by water needs and by family for rotation.
- Plant in blocks rather than long rows: more food per square foot.

**How much to grow (rough, per adult for a year):** many people plan about 200 square feet per person for fresh eating, and 4 to 5 times that to feed a person most of the year, including storage crops. Calorie crops (potatoes, dry beans, corn, winter squash, sweet potatoes) matter most.

**Fence** against deer, rabbits, and groundhogs from the start.

Source: general gardening and water practice, as taught by state Extension services and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
