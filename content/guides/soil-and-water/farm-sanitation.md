**Animals and their droppings carry germs (salmonella, E. coli, campylobacter, leptospirosis, and worms) that can make people seriously ill. A few habits prevent most of it.**

**Hands:** wash with soap and clean water after touching animals, feed, bedding, manure, eggs, or soil, and before eating or drinking. Keep a hand-wash station at the barn door. Don't eat or drink while doing chores. Keep small children from putting hands in their mouths during chores. Keep chore boots and clothes outside the house.

**Manure:**
- **Keep it at least 100 feet** from wells, springs and streams, and downhill from them.
- **Compost it hot.** Pile it to hold heat: keep it at **131 to 170 degrees F for at least 15 days with 5 turns** (or 3 days in an enclosed or aerated bin). Use a compost thermometer. Then let it cure for months.
- **Raw manure on food crops:** apply it at least **120 days before harvesting anything whose edible part touches the soil** (carrots, lettuce, strawberries), and 90 days for crops that don't touch it (sweet corn, tree fruit). Don't put it on anything eaten raw close to harvest.
- Never use **human, dog or cat waste** on food gardens. Pet waste can carry parasites. Don't use manure from animals that ate treated hay without checking (some herbicides pass through and harm garden plants).

**Washing produce:**
1. Wash hands first.
2. Rinse vegetables and fruit under **running clean water**, rubbing or scrubbing firm ones with a brush. No soap or bleach.
3. Peel or discard outer leaves of cabbage and lettuce.
4. Dry with a clean cloth.
5. Use separate boards and knives for raw meat.

**Water for washing and drinking animals:** keep troughs clean and free of droppings. Use treated water if the source is uncertain.

**Eggs:** collect often, don't wash eggs that you'll store at room temperature (it removes the protective coating). Wash dirty ones in water warmer than the egg, then refrigerate. Cook until yolks are firm in a bad-sanitation emergency.

**Sick animals:** isolate, wear gloves, wash up after, and do not eat a sick animal. Report unusual die-offs to a vet.

**Pests:** rodents spread disease and spoil feed. Store feed in metal bins, keep food scraps out of reach, and clean up spilled grain.

**Dead animals:** bury deep (several feet), burn or compost hot, away from water sources.

Source: FDA produce safety guidance, USDA National Organic Program composting standards, and CDC advice on animals and disease, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed.
