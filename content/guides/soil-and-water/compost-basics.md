**Compost turns kitchen and garden waste into food for the soil.**

1. **Mix two kinds:** "greens" (kitchen scraps, grass clippings, fresh weeds) and "browns" (dry leaves, straw, torn cardboard). Use about 2 or 3 parts brown to 1 part green.
2. **Keep it damp,** like a wrung-out sponge.
3. **Build a heap** about 3 feet wide, 3 feet deep and 3 feet high. A big heap gets hot, which breaks the material down faster and kills many weed seeds.
4. **Turn it** every week or two so the middle gets air.
5. **It is ready** in a few months, when it is dark, crumbly and smells like soil.

**Leave out:** meat, fish, bones, dairy, oily food, and **dog and cat droppings**. They attract pests and can carry disease.

Source: general composting practice from state Extension services and the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).
