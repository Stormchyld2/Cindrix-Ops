**Farm work injuries are common and severe, and help may be a long way off in an emergency. Prevent them.**

**Heat:**
- Work in the early morning and evening. Rest in shade. Drink water often (before you are thirsty). Wear a hat and light, loose clothing.
- **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, fast pulse. Stop, cool the body with shade, water and wet cloths, and sip water.
- **Heat stroke is an emergency:** confusion, hot dry skin or no sweat, collapse. Cool the person fast (wet cloths, fan, ice at neck, armpits and groin) and get help. See the first aid card on heat illness.

**Tools and machines:**
- Keep blades sharp (dull ones slip). Cut away from your body.
- **Chainsaws:** wear eye, ear, leg and head protection; keep both hands on it; never cut above shoulder height; stay clear of the kickback zone.
- **Power takeoffs, belts, and moving parts:** never wear loose clothing or hang jewellery; shut the engine off before clearing a jam or working near moving parts.
- **Tractors and carts:** most deaths are rollovers. Avoid slopes, wear a seat belt if there is a rollbar, and don't carry children.
- Store tools safely, out of children's reach.

**Chemicals:** read labels. Store in original containers, locked, away from food and children. Never mix products. Wear gloves and eye protection. If swallowed or splashed, call **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222** and follow the label.

**Confined spaces (silos, grain bins, manure pits, wells, tanks) can kill within minutes:**
- Manure pits give off gases that kill quickly. Grain bins can trap and bury a person. **Never enter alone, and never go in to rescue someone without ventilation and help.**
- If you must enter: air tested, ventilation running, a person outside, a rope and harness.

**Animals:** most injuries are kicks, bites, crushing and gores. Bulls, stallions, boars and mothers with young are dangerous. Learn calm handling, keep an escape route, and don't turn your back.

**Fire:** hay stored damp can heat and catch fire. Keep extinguishers and water near. Don't smoke around hay and fuel.

**Lifting and strain:** bend your knees; use carts, not your back. Take breaks. Sprains and back injuries stop the whole farm.

**Never work alone far from help without telling someone** where you are and when you'll be back.

Source: OSHA, NIOSH and state Extension farm-safety guidance, paraphrased. Not yet reviewed. See the first aid cards for treatment.
