**Good soil is the real long-term asset. These habits build it year after year.**

1. **Add organic matter every year:** compost, leaves, manure, cover crops. 1 to 2 inches a year.
2. **Never leave soil bare** in winter: cover crop or mulch.
3. **Disturb it less:** over-digging breaks soil structure and harms soil life. Loosen with a fork rather than flipping.
4. **Rotate crops** each year.
5. **Test** pH and nutrients every 2 to 3 years (see the soil test page).
6. **Keep records:** what was grown, what was added, how it did.
7. **Make compost continuously** from kitchen scraps, garden waste, leaves and manure (see the compost page).
8. **Avoid compaction:** use paths, don't work wet soil.

**If your soil is:**
- **Heavy clay** (sticky, slow to drain): add lots of compost, use raised beds, never work it wet.
- **Sandy** (fast draining, poor): add compost and mulch; water little and often at first.
- **Stony:** pick out the big stones and use raised beds for carrots.
- **Acid** (below pH 6): add ground limestone as the test advises.
- **Alkaline** (above 7.5): add compost; sulphur only if a test recommends it.

**Worms and soil life** are signs of health. Dig a spadeful in spring: it should crumble, smell earthy and show worms.

**Plan for a long time without shops:** keep a compost system running, save seed, and record which crops did best on your soil.

Source: general gardening and water practice, as taught by state Extension services and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
