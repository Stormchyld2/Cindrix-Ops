**A reliable water source on your own land matters more than almost anything else.**

**Springs:** Enclose a spring with a box or tank, with a cover and a drain, fence out animals, and direct overflow away. Test water before drinking.

**Shallow wells:** Hand-dug or driven wells are shallow and easily contaminated. Locate away from septic fields and animals (uphill and at least 100 feet), line with a concrete cap, and cover the top.

**Hand pumps:** A hand pump can replace or sit beside an electric pump on a drilled well. Keep spare leathers or seals and learn how to repair it.

**Rainwater:** See the rainwater page for catchment and treating it.

**Testing:** Test for bacteria and nitrates at least yearly. If unsure, boil or treat water before drinking (see the Water emergency plan).

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
