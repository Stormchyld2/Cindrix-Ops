**Plants show what they lack in their colour and shape.**

**Nitrogen low:** Whole plant pale green or yellow, starting with the **older leaves**; slow growth. Add compost, manure, or grow beans and peas ahead of the crop.

**Phosphorus low:** Dark green or purplish leaves, stunted plants, especially in cold soil. Add bone meal or compost.

**Potassium low:** Yellow or brown scorched leaf **edges** on older leaves. Add wood ash (lightly), compost or kelp.

**Calcium low:** Blossom-end rot (black patch at the bottom of tomatoes and peppers): usually uneven watering, not missing lime. Water evenly and mulch.

**Magnesium low:** Yellow between the veins of older leaves. Epsom salts in small amounts, or dolomitic lime.

**Not a deficiency:** Holes and chewed leaves are pests; wilting in the day that recovers at night is heat; curling in tomatoes can be herbicide drift or heat.

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
