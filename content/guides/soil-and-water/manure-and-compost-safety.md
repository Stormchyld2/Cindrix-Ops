**Manure feeds the garden but can carry germs. Handle it with care and wait before harvest.**

**Which manure:** Chicken, rabbit, horse, cow, sheep and goat manure all work. **Never use dog, cat or human manure** on food crops. Chicken manure is "hot" (strong): compost it first or it burns plants.

**Raw manure rule:** Apply raw manure at least **120 days before harvest** for crops that touch the soil (carrots, lettuce, strawberries) and 90 days for those that do not. In a long emergency, compost it hot.

**Hot composting:** A pile 3 feet across and 3 feet high, with a mix of green (manure, kitchen waste) and brown (leaves, straw), kept damp as a wrung-out sponge and turned when it cools, reaches **131 to 160 degrees F** and kills most germs. Keep it at 131 or above for 3 days, turned, and let it finish for a month.

**Hygiene:** Wear gloves and wash hands. Keep children away from the pile. Wash vegetables well.

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
