**Bare soil loses fertility to wind, sun and rain. Cover it.**

**Mulch** is a layer on top of the soil.
- Use straw, shredded leaves, wood chips (for paths and around trees), or grass clippings (not from sprayed lawns), 2 to 4 inches deep.
- Benefits: holds water, blocks weeds, keeps soil cool, feeds soil life as it rots.
- Keep it **a few inches away from plant stems** to prevent rot and hiding slugs.
- Cardboard or newspaper under mulch kills grass and weeds.
- Do not use hay with seeds in it, or sawdust from treated wood.

**Cover crops** are grown to improve the soil, not for food.
- **Winter rye:** sow in autumn; suppresses weeds, holds soil. Cut or turn under in spring 3 to 4 weeks before planting.
- **Crimson clover, hairy vetch, field peas:** add nitrogen. Good before heavy feeders like corn and tomatoes.
- **Buckwheat:** fast, 30 to 40 days; smothers weeds in summer and feeds bees.
- **Oats:** sown in late summer, killed by winter cold, leaving a mulch.

**How to use:**
1. Sow thickly in empty beds after harvest.
2. Cut before it sets seed, then dig it in, or cut and lay it as mulch ("chop and drop").
3. Wait a few weeks before sowing small seeds in the bed (rotting plants can hold back seedlings).

**No-dig beds:** layer cardboard, then compost, then mulch on top of grass. Plant into it the same season. It takes less labour, and soil life stays unbroken.

Source: general gardening and water practice, as taught by state Extension services and the EPA. Not yet reviewed: confirm with your county Extension office.
