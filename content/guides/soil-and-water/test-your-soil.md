**A soil test costs little and tells you what your garden needs, so you do not guess.**

1. **Where:** your state or county **Extension office** sells soil tests, usually for a small fee.
2. **How to sample:** dig 6 inches deep in 5 or 6 places in the same bed. Mix the soil in a clean bucket, let it dry, and send about a cupful.
3. **What it tells you:** the **pH** (how acid the soil is), and whether it is short of the main plant foods.
4. **Aim for:** most vegetables grow best at a pH of about **6.0 to 7.0**. The test report says how much lime (to raise it) or sulphur (to lower it) to add.
5. **Test again** every 2 or 3 years.

**Without a test:** add compost every year. It improves almost any soil.

Source: general soil-testing practice from state Extension services and the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS).
