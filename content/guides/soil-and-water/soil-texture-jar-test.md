**Know whether your soil is sand, silt or clay. It tells you how it drains, how it holds food and water, and how to improve it.**

**What you need:** A tall straight-sided jar with a lid, a cup of soil dug from 4 to 6 inches down (remove stones and roots), water and a spoon of dish soap.

**Do it:** Fill the jar one-third with soil, add water to nearly full and a squirt of soap, shake hard for 2 minutes, then leave on a flat surface. Mark the level of the layers: **sand settles in 1 minute**, **silt in 2 hours**, **clay in 1 to 2 days**.

**Read it:** Measure each layer and divide by the total depth. **Loam** (the best garden soil) is about 40 percent sand, 40 silt, 20 clay. Mostly sand drains fast and needs compost and mulch; mostly clay holds water, is heavy, and needs organic matter and raised beds.

**Improving it:** Add compost, leaf mould and well-rotted manure every year: they improve every kind of soil. Do not dig clay when wet.

Source: general soil and water guidance from state Extension services, the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) and the EPA. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm with your county Extension office.
