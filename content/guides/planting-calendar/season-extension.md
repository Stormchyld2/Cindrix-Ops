**Simple covers add weeks at both ends of the season, and let hardy greens and roots keep going through winter.**

**Row cover (floating cover, frost cloth):** a thin fabric laid right over plants or on hoops. A light type gives 2 to 4 degrees F of protection and keeps out insects; heavy types give more. Weigh down the edges with soil or boards.

**Low tunnel:** hoops of wire, conduit or plastic pipe, 2 to 3 feet high, with clear plastic or row cover over them. On sunny days **open the ends**: a closed plastic tunnel can overheat above 100 degrees F even when it is cold outside.

**Cold frame:** a bottomless box (wood, bales of straw, or bricks) with a hinged clear lid (an old window works), facing south, with the back higher than the front. Prop the lid open on sunny days and close it at night. It is excellent for hardening off seedlings and for lettuce, spinach, kale and carrots in winter.

**What to grow under cover in cold weather:**
- Spinach, mache, kale, claytonia and lettuce: tough down to the low teens F when covered.
- Carrots, beets and leeks: protected from deep freezes and still dug as needed.

**Rules:**
1. Plants stop growing when the day is shorter than about 10 hours (roughly November to February in the north). They **hold** what they have: you are storing food alive in the garden, so plant for winter in late summer.
2. Water less: little evaporates in winter, and wet leaves rot.
3. Ventilate on sunny days even in cold weather.
4. Add a second layer (cover inside a cover) for much colder nights.
