**Winter is for planning, repairs, and the cold-hardy crops that keep growing under cover.**

**Plan (December to February):**
- Count your seed. Test old seed: put 10 seeds in a damp paper towel inside a bag, keep warm, and count how many sprout in a week or two. Under 50% sprouting: sow thicker or replace it.
- Draw the beds on paper. Rotate crop families (tomato family, bean family, cabbage family, squash family, roots) so each family moves to a new bed each year.
- Work out how much to grow: a rough guide is 10 to 15 plants of each main crop per person.
- Order seed early; popular varieties sell out.

**Repair and prepare:** sharpen and oil tools, mend fences, build trellises and cold frames, clean pots, sort the shed.

**Grow in winter:**
- A **cold frame** or **low tunnel** (hoops with plastic or row cover) extends the season in many areas. See the season extension page.
- Indoors: sprouts and microgreens in jars or trays give fresh food in any weather in about 5 to 10 days.
- Start onions, leeks and celery seed late in winter (about 10 to 12 weeks before last frost).

**Check your stored food** every week or two: onions, potatoes, squash and apples. Remove anything soft or sprouting before it spoils its neighbours.

Source: general gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm dates with your own county Extension office.
