**Use your two frost dates and the "days to maturity" on the seed packet.**

**Cool-season crops** (cabbage, peas, potatoes, onions, garlic, lettuce, spinach, carrots): they tolerate a light frost. Plant them **2 to 4 weeks before your last spring frost**, or in late summer for an autumn crop.

**Warm-season crops** (beans, squash, tomatoes, corn, peppers): they are killed by frost. Plant them **after your last spring frost**, once the soil is warm.

**Check that the crop will finish:**
1. Take the date of your **first autumn frost**.
2. Count back the **days to maturity** on the packet. Then add **2 weeks**, because growth slows in the cooler days of autumn.
3. That is the **last day you can plant**. If it has already passed, choose a faster crop or a faster type.

**Example:** your first autumn frost is October 15 and the beans take 90 days. October 15 minus 90 days is July 17. Subtract 2 weeks and the last safe planting day is about **July 3**.

Source: the method taught by state Extension services for working out planting dates; the example dates are made up.
