**Almost every planting time is counted from two dates:** your average **last spring frost** and your average **first autumn frost**. They are different in every town.

**Where to find them:**
- Your state **Extension office** (ask for the "average last and first frost dates" for your town).
- The **National Weather Service** and **NOAA** publish them from long weather records.
- A neighbour who has gardened for years: write down what they say, and when they plant.

**Two things to remember:**
- An average date is a guess, not a promise. In an average year there is still about a one in two chance of a frost *after* the spring date. Wait a week or two for tender crops, or be ready to cover them.
- The **plant hardiness zone** on the USDA map tells you how cold your winters get. It is useful for fruit trees and perennials. It is **not** the same as frost dates.

Write your two dates here and keep them with your seeds:

- Last spring frost: ______
- First autumn frost: ______

Source: USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (public domain); NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information climate normals (public domain). Frost dates for each town will be added to this program later; until then ask your Extension office.
