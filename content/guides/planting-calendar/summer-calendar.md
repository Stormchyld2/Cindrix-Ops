**Summer is for watering, mulching, picking, and planting the fall garden.**

- **Water:** about 1 inch a week; deep and less often is better than a daily sprinkle. Water in the morning.
- **Mulch** 2 to 4 inches of straw, leaves or grass clippings (no clippings from sprayed lawns). It holds moisture and shuts weeds out.
- **Weed** while weeds are small. A hoe passed lightly through the top inch is quicker than pulling.
- **Pick often.** Beans, squash, cucumbers and peas stop producing if allowed to go to seed.
- **Succession sow:** every 2 to 3 weeks, sow more bush beans, carrots, beets and lettuce.

**Midsummer (about 10 to 14 weeks before the first autumn frost) is the time to sow the autumn garden:**
- Broccoli, cabbage and cauliflower seedlings.
- Carrots, beets, turnips, and kale from seed.
- Bush beans for one last crop (needs time before frost).
- Lettuce and spinach once nights cool.

**Watch for:** blossom-end rot (uneven water), squash bugs and borers, tomato hornworms (large green caterpillars; pick off), blight on tomatoes and potatoes (remove sick leaves, do not compost), and leaf-spot in wet weather.

**Preserve as it comes:** a little each day is easier than a flood in one weekend. Dry herbs, freeze beans and corn, and can tomatoes using tested recipes only.

**Hot-weather note:** above about 90 degrees F, tomatoes and peppers drop blossoms. Shade cloth (30 to 40%) helps in the hottest afternoons. Lettuce and spinach bolt: eat early and sow shade-grown replacements later.
