**Start small, and learn one thing at a time. A beginner with a big garden often quits by July.**

**Year one: a 10 by 10 foot bed, or two raised beds (4 by 8 feet).**
1. Pick a sunny, well-drained spot near the house.
2. Grow **forgiving crops:** bush beans, potatoes, kale, lettuce and radishes, zucchini (one plant), and tomatoes (2 or 3 plants).
3. Add compost. Water deeply once or twice a week. Mulch.
4. Keep a notebook: dates sown, what came up, what was eaten, problems.
5. Eat what you grow. Preserve a little (freeze beans, dry herbs).

**Year two: double it.** Add one new bed with onions, carrots, cabbage, and garlic, and start saving seed from beans and lettuce. Make your own compost.

**Year three: add the calorie crops.** Winter squash, dry beans, sweet potatoes, and a larger potato patch. Start preserving more: root cellar, drying, canning from a tested recipe.

**Year four on:** try grain, fruit trees, chickens or rabbits. Build a pantry plan.

**Learn from people:**
- A neighbour who gardens will know your soil and weather.
- **County Extension offices** and Master Gardener programs answer questions, often free.
- Seed swaps and garden clubs.

**Skills to practise before you need them:** starting seeds, composting, canning, drying, saving seed, sharpening tools, and fixing a fence. Don't wait for an emergency to try for the first time.

**Common beginner mistakes:**
- Too big a plot for the time available.
- Planting too early, before the frost date.
- Overwatering in small amounts instead of watering deeply.
- Crowding plants.
- Skipping the soil: poor soil gives poor crops whatever you do.
- Not writing it down.

**Use this program:** the Academy has courses, the Seed vault holds your seed inventory, and the Plan pages can include growing and preserving.

Source: general gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services and Master Gardener programs. Not yet reviewed.
