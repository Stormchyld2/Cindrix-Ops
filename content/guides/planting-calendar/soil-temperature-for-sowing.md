**Seed sprouts when the soil is warm enough, not when the calendar says so.** Push a kitchen or soil thermometer 2 inches into the ground in the morning, and sow when it reads at least the number below.

- **40 to 45 degrees F:** peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes, kale, broccoli, cabbage, onions, favas, lentils, parsnips.
- **50 degrees F:** beets, carrots, chard, turnips, potatoes (planting), garlic planting in autumn, small grains.
- **60 degrees F:** snap beans, corn, sunflowers, soybeans.
- **65 degrees F:** cucumbers, squash, pumpkins, cowpeas, sweet potatoes (slips), amaranth, buckwheat.
- **70 degrees F:** melons, watermelons, okra, peanuts, sorghum.
- **Plant tomatoes, peppers and eggplant outside** when the soil is at least 60 degrees F and the night air stays above 50 degrees F.

**Too hot is a problem too:** lettuce and spinach will not sprout above about 80 degrees F, and peas and cabbage-family crops sulk when soil is above 85. Sow those in shade and keep the soil damp.

**Quick check without a thermometer:** if the soil is cold and wet in your hand it is not ready. Wait for it to crumble.

Source: Soil temperature guides from state Extension services (for example the University of Maryland Extension and Oregon State). Not yet checked against one named table: confirm with your county Extension office.
