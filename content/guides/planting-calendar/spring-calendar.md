**Work in weeks from your last spring frost** (see the frost dates page). "-8" means 8 weeks before it; "+2" means 2 weeks after.

- **-10 to -8 weeks:** start indoors: onions, leeks, celery, peppers, eggplant. Check last year's seed (see the seed vault pages). Order seed and sets.
- **-8 to -6:** start indoors: broccoli, cabbage, kale, early tomatoes. Prune fruit trees if still dormant. Get the soil tested.
- **-6 to -4:** direct sow as soon as the soil can be worked: peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes. Plant onion sets and potatoes (-4 to -2).
- **-4 to -2:** carrots, beets, kale, chard, turnips outdoors. Harden off cabbage-family seedlings. Cold frames help.
- **-2 to 0:** plant potatoes if you haven't. Set out cabbage, broccoli seedlings. Start cucumbers and squash indoors. Prepare beds for warm crops.
- **0 to +1:** the frost risk is falling, but keep covers handy. Plant sweet corn, bush and pole beans once soil is 60 degrees F.
- **+1 to +2:** set out tomatoes, peppers, eggplant (nights above 50 degrees F). Sow cucumbers, squash, melons, sweet potato slips (+2 to +3).
- **+3 to +4:** second sowing of beans. Mulch everything. Thin and weed.

**Rule of thumb for soil:** a handful of soil that crumbles when squeezed is ready to work. If it stays in a sticky ball, wait: digging wet soil ruins its structure.

**Late frost plan:** keep sheets, buckets, row cover or straw near the garden. Cover tender plants before dark when frost is forecast; remove in the morning.

Source: general gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm dates with your own county Extension office.
