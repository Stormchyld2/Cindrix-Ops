**Starting seeds indoors gives a head start on crops with a long season: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, onions, broccoli.**

**You need:** containers with drain holes, **seed-starting mix** (light and sterile; not garden soil), light, and warmth.

1. **Light is what matters most.** A sunny south window is rarely enough: seedlings get tall and weak ("leggy"). A shop light or LED grow light 2 to 4 inches above the seedlings, on for 14 to 16 hours a day, works well.
2. **Warmth:** most seeds sprout best at 70 to 80 degrees F. After sprouting, 60 to 70 is fine.
3. **Sow** at the depth on the packet (a rule: 2 to 3 times the seed's thickness). Keep damp, not soaked. Cover with a clear lid until sprouting.
4. **Thin** to one per cell when the first true leaves appear: snip the extras, don't pull.
5. **Air movement:** a small fan on low for part of the day makes stronger stems and prevents "damping off" (seedlings rotting at the soil line).
6. **Feed** with weak liquid fertiliser once true leaves show.
7. **Pot on** tomatoes and peppers into bigger pots if they outgrow the first ones.

**Hardening off (do not skip):** seedlings raised indoors are tender. Over **7 to 10 days**, set them outdoors in the shade for a few hours, then more each day, then in sun, then leave them out overnight. Transplant on a cloudy day or in the evening, and water in well. Skipping this step can scorch or kill them.

**Timing:** count back from your last frost date using the numbers on the spring page.
