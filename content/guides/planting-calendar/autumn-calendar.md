**Autumn decides how well you eat through winter.**

**Before the first frost (watch the forecast):**
- Pick tomatoes, peppers, squash and beans if frost is coming. Cover with sheets if only one cold night is forecast; the warm days after often allow more harvest.
- Bring in green tomatoes that have started to colour.
- Harvest winter squash and pumpkins before hard frost, with 2 inches of stem. Cure them in a warm place for 10 days and store cool and dry.
- Pull sweet potatoes and cure them (see the sweet potatoes page).

**After light frosts:** kale, collards, carrots, parsnips and Brussels sprouts get sweeter. Leave them and pick as needed, with mulch over carrots and beets.

**Plant:**
- **Garlic:** 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes, which is usually October in the north. Mulch with straw.
- **Spinach and winter lettuce** under a cold frame or row cover.
- **Cover crops** (rye, clover, vetch) on empty beds (see the soil pages).

**Clean up:**
- Pull and remove diseased plants. Do not compost them.
- Add compost and leaves to beds. Leave the roots of peas and beans in the soil.
- Save seed from your best plants (see the seed vault).
- Drain hoses and store them. Empty and turn over barrels and buckets.

**Write it down:** what grew well, what failed, planting dates, harvest weights. A notebook is the best garden tool you will ever own.

Source: general gardening practice, as taught by state Extension services. Not yet checked against one named page: confirm dates with your own county Extension office.
