**Thorough handwashing with soap and warm water (especially after toilet use and before eating), daily morning bathing with soap and water, daily underwear changes, short and clean fingernails, and laundering of bedding and pajamas in hot water are the key household self-care measures for managing pinworm infection.**

**What people do:** Bathe after waking up each morning and wash hands regularly, especially after using the bathroom. Change underwear daily, wash pajamas and bed sheets often, and avoid nail biting to prevent reinfection.

**What the evidence says:** CDC identifies handwashing as 'the most important way to prevent the spread of pinworms.' MedlinePlus (NIH/NIAID) lists bathe after waking up, wash pajamas and bed sheets often, wash hands regularly, change underwear every day, and avoid nail biting and scratching the anal area as the core preventive hygiene steps.

**Be careful:** Hygiene alone typically cannot eliminate an active pinworm infection — over-the-counter antiparasitic medication (pyrantel pamoate) is generally needed, and all household members should be treated simultaneously. Medication is typically repeated after 2 weeks because it kills worms but not eggs; the second dose treats worms that hatched after the first dose.

**Get medical help** if it is for a child under 1, someone is pregnant or on regular medicine, or the problem is severe, lasts more than a few days, or gets worse. In an emergency, call 911.

Source: MedlinePlus (U.S. National Library of Medicine), https://medlineplus.gov/pinworms.html
