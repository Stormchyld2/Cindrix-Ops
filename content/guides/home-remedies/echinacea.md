**DRAFT. Not yet reviewed by a qualified person.**

**Echinacea is primarily marketed for the common cold and upper respiratory tract infections, based on the idea that it may support immune system function.**

**What the evidence says:** Studies indicate that taking echinacea may slightly reduce the chances of catching a cold, though evidence regarding whether it shortens cold duration is inconclusive. Evidence for other conditions, including eczema, is unclear.

**Be careful:** E. purpurea extracts appear likely safe for short periods in adults; allergic reactions can occur, with digestive symptoms most common. Children may experience rashes potentially linked to allergic reactions. Theoretical interactions with immunosuppressants and certain other drugs; consult a healthcare provider before use with medications. Limited data on safety in early pregnancy.

**Get medical help** if it is for a child under 1, someone is pregnant or on regular medicine, or the problem is severe, lasts more than a few days, or gets worse. In an emergency, call 911.

Source: NCCIH (U.S. National Institutes of Health), https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/echinacea
