**Peppermint oil is promoted for irritable bowel syndrome, indigestion, headaches, muscle tension, and nausea. Topical application is used for tension headaches, and inhaled peppermint oil is used for nausea.**

**What people do:** For IBS, peppermint oil is taken as enteric-coated capsules (which reduce heartburn compared to plain capsules). For tension headaches, apply peppermint oil topically to the skin. For nausea, peppermint oil is inhaled as aromatherapy.

**What the evidence says:** A 2022 review found peppermint oil was better than placebo at improving overall IBS symptoms and abdominal pain. A 2024 review concluded that inhaling peppermint oil was particularly effective at reducing nausea and vomiting in patients with cancer. Evidence suggests potential benefit for tension headaches when applied topically, though peppermint oil taken alone may worsen indigestion in some people.

**Be careful:** Oral side effects may include heartburn, nausea, abdominal pain, and dry mouth; enteric-coated capsules reduce heartburn risk. Never apply topically to infants' or young children's faces due to menthol's respiratory effects. Skin irritation is possible with topical use. Consult a healthcare provider before use with medications, as interactions may occur.

**Get medical help** if it is for a child under 1, someone is pregnant or on regular medicine, or the problem is severe, lasts more than a few days, or gets worse. In an emergency, call 911.

Source: NCCIH (U.S. National Institutes of Health), https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/peppermint-oil
