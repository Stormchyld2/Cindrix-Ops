## Know your number
check: water
go: Inventory
button: Open Inventory
The Red Cross asks for **1 gallon of water per person per day**, for drinking, cooking and washing. Keep a **3-day supply** to leave with and a **2-week supply** for home.
- Count the people and pets in your home.
- Add more for hot weather, illness or pregnancy.
- Put what you have in the Inventory so the program can count the days for you.

## Store it so it stays good
- Use clean, food-grade containers with tight lids. Do not reuse milk jugs, which are hard to clean.
- Keep water in a cool, dark place, off bare concrete if you can, and away from fuel, paint and chemicals.
- Write the date on every container.
- Spread it out: some in the kitchen, some in the garage, some in each car.

## Keep it fresh
every: 180
go: Inventory
button: Open Inventory
Store-bought sealed water lasts until the date on it. Water you filled yourself should be replaced about every six months. Use the old water on plants or for cleaning and refill.

## Find a second source
If the tap stops, there is more water than you think:
- The water heater tank (30 to 50 gallons) and the toilet **tank** (not the bowl) hold clean water. Learn where the shut-off and the drain valve are.
- Rain, a creek, a pond or a pool can be treated for drinking, or used as is for washing and flushing.
- Write down the closest safe place to fetch water on foot.

## Be ready to make it safe
go: Cards
button: Open the emergency cards
Have **two** ways, so one failing does not matter: a filter, plain unscented bleach, a pot and a way to boil, and purification tablets. See the card on making water safe.
- Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 feet).
- Bleach with 5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite: 8 drops a gallon, wait 30 minutes, double if cloudy.
- A filter removes germs but not viruses, so add bleach after it.

## Practise once
every: 365
go: Drills
button: Open Drills
Make a gallon of 'found' water safe with your own kit, from start to finish, and time it. Fix whatever was missing.

## Plan for hauling
Water weighs about 8 pounds a gallon. Check you can carry or wheel what you need: a cart, a wagon, handles on the containers, and a route.
