Some wounds are deep, and pressing on the surface does not reach the bleeding. Packing fills the wound and puts pressure from the inside.

## When to pack
- **Deep wounds** where blood wells up from inside.
- **Junctional areas:** where limbs join the body, such as the **groin, armpit, shoulder, and the base of the neck.** A tourniquet will not fit there.
- **Large open wounds,** where a cloth alone will not reach.

## What to use
- **Hemostatic gauze:** gauze treated with an agent that helps blood clot. It is the best choice, such as the kinds that trauma programs recommend.
- **Plain gauze** (rolled) is the next best choice.
- **Clean cloth,** a shirt or other clean fabric, if you have nothing else.

## How to pack
1. **Put on gloves,** if you have them.
2. **Expose the wound.** Clear away clothing.
3. **Find the source.** Look for where the blood is coming from deep in the wound. Wipe away pooled blood if needed. Do not spend long on this.
4. **Push the gauze into the wound** with your fingers, **directly onto the source,** and pack it in firmly, packing more in as you go. Work it all the way to the bottom of the wound. Keep feeding gauze until the wound is full.
5. **Press hard with both hands** over the packed wound. Use your body weight. Hold for **at least three minutes** without letting up. Hemostatic products may need longer, so follow the packaging.
6. **Bandage** over it with a pressure bandage or wrap. Secure firmly.
7. **Do not remove** the packing. Leave it for the hospital.
8. **Keep watching** for soaking, and reapply pressure.

## Cautions
- **In the neck,** pack only if bleeding is life-threatening, take care not to block the airway, and keep pressure only on the wound.
- **In the chest or belly,** do not pack deep wounds. Cover and call 911. Packing is for wounds you can reach.
- **Pain** will be severe. Warn the person and keep going.
- **Do not use powder** or loose products unless trained.
- **Gauze that comes in a sealed packet** should be opened only when needed. Learn how to open it with gloves on.

## Practice
- Practice on a training model. Many classes have wound simulators with fake blood.
- Practice with several kinds of gauze.
- Time yourself.

## Checklist
- Know when to pack
- Know to pack to the bottom of the wound and press for at least three minutes
- Hemostatic gauze in each kit

> This lesson is education, not a replacement for a hands-on Stop the Bleed class. Find one at stopthebleed.org. Call 911 for any life-threatening bleeding.
