A tourniquet saves lives. Modern research and military experience show that tourniquets applied early are safe and effective, and the risk of leaving severe limb bleeding uncontrolled is far greater than the risk of a tourniquet.

## When to use
- **Life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg:** spurting, pouring, pooling, or a wound where pressure is not working.
- **Amputations** of a limb.
- **Multiple victims** or **an unsafe scene,** where you cannot stay holding pressure.
- **You are the victim** and cannot hold pressure.

**Do not use** a tourniquet on the neck, chest, belly, or head. Do not put it directly over a joint (knee or elbow).

## Types
- **Commercial tourniquets** (C-A-T, SOF-TT and other recommended models) have a strap, buckle and windlass rod. They are strong and fast. **Use these.**
- **Improvised tourniquets** (belts, cloth, rope) often fail, can cut skin, and are hard to tighten enough. Use only if nothing else is available.

## How to apply a commercial tourniquet to a limb
1. **Place it high** on the limb, **2 to 3 inches above the wound,** toward the heart. If you cannot find the wound or are in a hurry, put it **"high and tight"** at the top of the arm or leg. **Never on a joint.**
2. **Pull the strap tight.** Pull the free end through the buckle and cinch it as tight as you can.
3. **Twist the windlass rod** until the bleeding stops. This is the key step. It will hurt.
4. **Lock the rod** in its clip so it cannot unwind.
5. **Secure** with the strap or time tab.
6. **Check:** is bleeding stopped? Is there still a pulse below? (In a leg, the foot pulse should be gone.)
7. **If bleeding continues,** tighten more. If it still continues, put a **second tourniquet** just above the first. You can also pack the wound.
8. **Write the time** on the tourniquet or the person's forehead.
9. **Do not remove or loosen** it. Leave that for hospital staff.
10. **Do not cover it.** Make it visible for responders.

## Self-application
Practice putting a tourniquet on your own arm and leg. Use one hand, and your teeth or a knee if needed. Know how to get it out of the pouch in the dark.

## Common mistakes
- **Not tight enough:** the most common. The bleeding must stop.
- **Too low,** or over a joint or bulky pocket.
- **Waiting too long.** Use it early for life-threatening bleeding.
- **Taking it off.**
- **Forgetting the time.**
- **Using a flat strip, a thin cord,** or a wire that cuts.
- **Stopping at one** when bleeding continues. Add a second.

## Pain and safety
- Tourniquets **hurt.** Tell the person it is working.
- **Safe for two hours,** and often longer, when applied correctly. Hospitals manage the rest. Limb loss from tourniquets is rare, and is much less likely than death from bleeding.
- **Get the person to a hospital** as soon as possible.

## Improvised tourniquet (last resort)
1. Use a **wide** band (at least 2 inches, a folded triangular bandage or strip of cloth).
2. Wrap it around the limb **2 to 3 inches above the wound** (or high).
3. Tie a half-knot, place a **rigid rod** (a strong stick or pen) on it, and tie a full knot over it.
4. **Twist** the rod until bleeding stops.
5. **Secure** the rod with a strip of cloth.
6. **Note** the time.

## Buy the right one
- Buy only **recommended** models from **reputable** sellers.
- Counterfeit tourniquets are sold online and can break.
- Carry **more than one** and put them where you can reach them.
- **Practice with a trainer,** not your real one.

## Checklist
- Know when and where to apply
- Practiced application in under a minute, and one-handed
- Reliable tourniquets in kits, in the car and on you
- Know to note the time and not remove it
