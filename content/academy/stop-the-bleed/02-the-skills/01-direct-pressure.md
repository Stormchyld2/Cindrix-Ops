Direct pressure stops most bleeding and is the first thing to try if you have no tourniquet or the wound is not on a limb.

## How to do it
1. **Cover the wound** with a clean cloth, gauze, a clean shirt or the cleanest thing you have. If there is nothing, press with your hands.
2. **Push down hard.** Use both hands, lock your elbows and lean in with your body weight. It should be firm, even painful. Do not be gentle.
3. **Press directly on the source,** not just near it.
4. **Keep pressing without lifting.** Peeking breaks clots.
5. **If blood soaks through,** add more cloth on top and press harder. Do not remove the first layer.
6. **Keep going for at least five minutes,** or until help arrives.
7. **Bandage firmly** if you must free your hands, wrapping a pressure bandage or an elastic wrap, snug but not so tight that you cut off blood below it. Check fingers and toes for color, warmth and feeling.
8. **Keep watching.** If bleeding restarts, press again, or go to the next method.

## Tips
- **Kneel or sit** to get your body weight behind your hands.
- **Two people** can take turns when your arms tire.
- **A knee on a thigh wound** can help in a pinch, if you have training.
- **Object in the wound:** do not pull it out. Press **around** it, and pad it so it does not move.
- **Head wounds** bleed a lot. Press firmly, but not on a depressed skull fracture. If the skull feels soft, press gently around the edges.
- **Nosebleed:** sit up, lean forward, and pinch the nose for 10 to 15 minutes.
- **Elevation** of a limb above the heart may help but does not replace pressure.

## When direct pressure is not enough
- **The wound is deep** and the source cannot be reached: go to **packing.**
- **The limb is bleeding heavily** and pressure is not working, or you must leave or care for others: use a **tourniquet.**
- **You are alone** with more than one victim: treat the worst bleeding with a tourniquet to free your hands.

## Practice
- Practice on a training pad or a foam block.
- Practice pressing for a full minute to feel how tiring it is.
- Practice wrapping a pressure bandage.

## Checklist
- Know to press hard and not lift
- Know how to add layers, not remove
- Know when to move to packing or a tourniquet

> This lesson is education, not a replacement for a hands-on Stop the Bleed class. Find one at stopthebleed.org. Call 911 for any life-threatening bleeding.
