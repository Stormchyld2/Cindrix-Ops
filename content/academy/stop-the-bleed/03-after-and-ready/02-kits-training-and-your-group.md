Bleeding control works when the tools and skills are close to the person who needs them.

## What to carry
A basic **bleeding control kit:**
- **Two tourniquets** (a recommended model)
- **Hemostatic gauze**
- **Plain compressed gauze**
- **A pressure bandage** (Israeli bandage or similar)
- **Gloves**
- **Trauma shears**
- **A permanent marker**
- **An emergency blanket**
- **Two vented chest seals** (if trained)
- **A card** with the steps

Keep it in a bag, vacuum pack or pouch. Put the **tourniquet** where you can reach it with either hand.

## Where to put kits
- **Home:** kitchen, workshop, garage and near bedrooms.
- **Car and truck.**
- **Boat, RV and farm vehicles.**
- **Workplace,** school and place of worship, with an AED if possible.
- **On your belt or in your pack** if you work or play in a risky setting.
- **Public places:** many have wall-mounted bleeding control kits. Learn where they are.

## Check your kits
- **When the clocks change,** open and inspect.
- Replace anything that is damaged, **expired,** or used.
- Keep out of the heat and sun.
- **Practice** with a trainer, not your real tourniquet.

## Get trained
- **Find a class** at stopthebleed.org.
- **Many hospitals,** fire departments and community groups run **free classes** of about an hour.
- **Ask your employer** and your school to host one.
- **Take CPR** and first aid too.
- **Practice** every few months.
- **Teach others.** Lead a short session for your group. Many people become Stop the Bleed instructors.

## Your group
- **Make sure** everyone knows where kits are.
- **Hold a drill:** a fake injury with a training tourniquet and gauze.
- **Time the response.**
- **Track training** on the Academy sign-off page.

## Legal notes
Most states have **Good Samaritan laws** that protect people who give reasonable emergency help in good faith. Check yours.

## Checklist
- A bleeding control kit at home, in the car and on you
- Everyone trained and practicing
- Kit checked when the clocks change
- A group drill planned

> This lesson is education, not a replacement for a hands-on Stop the Bleed class. Find one at stopthebleed.org. Call 911 for any life-threatening bleeding.
