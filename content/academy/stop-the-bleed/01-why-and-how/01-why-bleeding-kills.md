Severe bleeding is the leading preventable cause of death after injury. A person can bleed to death in as little as three to five minutes, often before help can arrive.

## The numbers that matter
- An adult has about **five liters** of blood (roughly 10 or 11 pints). Losing **a third or more** can lead to shock and death.
- **Emergency response times** are measured in minutes, not seconds. Even in cities, ambulances often take longer than five minutes.
- The person nearest the victim, **you,** is the first and best responder.

## Why bystanders save lives
The **Hartford Consensus,** a group of trauma surgeons, emergency physicians, military leaders and law enforcement experts that met after the 2012 Sandy Hook shooting, called for the public to be trained in simple bleeding control, as the public is trained in CPR. That effort became **Stop the Bleed.**

Bleeding control is useful for far more than shootings:
- Car crashes and workplace accidents
- Falls and farm or shop accidents
- Kitchen and home injuries with knives and glass
- Chainsaw, power tool and lawn equipment injuries
- Natural disasters with debris, such as earthquakes and tornadoes
- Stabbings and gunshots
- Explosions

## Types of bleeding
- **Capillary:** oozing from tiny vessels, as in a scrape. Stops on its own or with a bandage.
- **Venous:** dark red blood flowing steadily from a vein. Can be serious. Usually controlled with pressure.
- **Arterial:** bright red blood **spurting** with the heartbeat, or flowing fast. Life-threatening within minutes.

You do not need to identify the type. Treat **any bleeding that is pooling, soaking clothing, spurting or will not stop** as life-threatening.

## Signs of life-threatening bleeding
- Blood **spurting** or **pouring** from a wound.
- Blood **pooling** on the ground.
- **Clothing soaked** with blood.
- A bandage that is **soaked through.**
- **Loss of limb** (amputation).
- The person is **confused, pale, weak, or unresponsive** (signs of shock).

## What stops bleeding
Blood stops when it clots, and clots form when blood slows. Everything in this course is a way to **slow or stop the flow long enough for clots to form,** or to stop it mechanically:
1. **Direct pressure** squeezes the vessels closed from outside.
2. **Packing** fills a deep wound and presses from inside.
3. **A tourniquet** squeezes the entire limb to stop the arterial flow.

## Be ready
- **Training** matters. Take a class.
- **Gear** helps. Carry a tourniquet and gauze.
- **Confidence** helps. You can do this.

## Checklist
- Know why bleeding is deadly
- Know the signs of life-threatening bleeding
- Know the three ways to control it
- Find a Stop the Bleed class

> This lesson is education, not a replacement for a hands-on Stop the Bleed class. Find one at stopthebleed.org. Call 911 for any life-threatening bleeding.
