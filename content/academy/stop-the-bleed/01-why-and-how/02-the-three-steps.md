Stop the Bleed teaches a simple order. Memorize it.

## Step 1: Call 911 and make sure the scene is safe
- **Check the scene:** is it safe? Are there hazards such as traffic, fire, a shooter, falling debris? If it is not safe, get yourself and the person to safety if you can, or wait for responders.
- **Call 911** or tell someone to call. Say where you are and that someone is bleeding badly.
- **Protect yourself:** wear **gloves** if you have them. If you do not, use a plastic bag, or a cloth, but do not wait. Wash your hands afterward.

## Step 2: Find the bleeding
- **Look for where the blood is coming from.** Bleeding may be hidden under clothes.
- **Expose the wound.** Cut or tear clothing if needed.
- **Check the whole body,** including the **back, the neck, the armpits and the groin.** Run your hands over the person, and check them for blood.
- **Decide how bad it is.** Life-threatening bleeding: spurting, pooling, soaking, or from an amputation.

## Step 3: Stop the bleeding
Choose the method:
- **Wound on the arm or leg and life-threatening:** apply a **tourniquet** high and tight, between the wound and the heart.
- **Wound on the neck, shoulder, armpit, groin or torso:** **pack** the wound with gauze and apply **direct pressure.**
- **Any other wound:** **direct pressure** with a cloth or dressing. If it keeps bleeding, add more.

## While you wait
- **Stay with the person.**
- **Keep them warm** and lying down.
- **Reassure them.**
- **Check them** for other injuries and for shock.
- **Keep applying pressure** until help takes over.
- **Tell responders** what you did, and the time a tourniquet was applied.

## Practice makes it automatic
- Walk through the steps **out loud.**
- **Practice** with a partner and a training tourniquet.
- **Do it quickly:** time yourself. A good goal is under a minute for a tourniquet.
- **Practice one-handed** on yourself. You may be the one hurt.

## What not to do
- Do not waste time looking for the perfect supplies. **Use what you have.**
- Do not lift the cloth to peek.
- Do not remove embedded objects.
- Do not apply a tourniquet to the neck, chest, or abdomen.
- Do not give food or drink.
- Do not leave the person alone.

## Checklist
- Know the order: safe, call, find, stop
- Know which method fits which wound
- Practiced one-handed tourniquet application

> This lesson is education, not a replacement for a hands-on Stop the Bleed class. Find one at stopthebleed.org. Call 911 for any life-threatening bleeding.
