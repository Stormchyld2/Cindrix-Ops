When there are more injured people than helpers, you help the **most people** by treating those you can save first. This is called **triage**.

## The START method (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)
Go from person to person. Spend under a minute on each.
1. **Can they walk?** Ask everyone who can walk to move to a safe place. They are **minor (green)**. Check them later.
2. **Are they breathing?** If not, open the airway. If still not breathing, they are **expectant (black)**. Move on. If breathing starts, they are **immediate (red)**.
3. **Breathing too fast (more than 30 breaths a minute)?** They are **immediate (red)**.
4. **Is the pulse at the wrist missing, or does the nail bed take more than two seconds to turn pink again?** **Immediate (red)**. Stop the bleeding if there is any.
5. **Can they follow a simple command, such as "squeeze my hand"?** If not, **immediate (red)**. If yes, **delayed (yellow)**.

## What each colour means
- **Red (immediate):** needs help now.
- **Yellow (delayed):** serious but can wait.
- **Green (minor):** walking wounded.
- **Black (expectant):** dead or not likely to survive with the help you have.

## Do two things on the way
While you triage, stop severe bleeding and open airways. Do not stop for anything longer.

## Re-check
People change. Go round again and move people between colours if they get better or worse.

> Triage is hard. You will not help everyone. Doing the most good for the most people is the right aim.
