Two or three people working together help far more than one. A little planning beforehand makes it smooth.

## Roles
- **Leader.** Looks at the whole scene, makes decisions, keeps people calm. Does not stay on one task.
- **Treater.** Does the hands-on care: stops bleeding, checks the airway.
- **Helper.** Fetches the kit, holds light, writes down times, keeps the casualty warm, calls for help.
- **Watcher.** Keeps an eye on the surroundings for danger.
In a small family, one person may do two jobs. Decide who does what beforehand.

## Clear calls
Short, plain words work best. Repeat back what you hear.
- "Tourniquet on, left leg, 14:32."
- "Airway open, breathing."
- "Need more gauze."

## Handover
When help arrives, give a short report. A handy order is **MIST**:
- **M**echanism: what happened.
- **I**njuries: what you found.
- **S**igns: how they are now, such as breathing and awareness.
- **T**reatment: what you did and when.
Write the same facts on a card or a note and send it with the person.

## Keep records
Note the time of each action and any change. A phone with the clock on screen works.

> Stay calm and speak slowly. A steady voice spreads calm. A panicked voice spreads panic.

## Check yourself
? What does the M in MIST stand for?
+ Mechanism (what happened)
- Medicine
- Movement
- Message
> Mechanism tells the receiving team how the injury happened.
? What is the leader's job?
+ Look at the whole scene and make decisions
- Do all the bandaging
- Stay on one wound
- Wait for orders
> The leader keeps the big picture.
? Why repeat back what you hear?
+ It avoids misunderstandings under stress
- It sounds military
- It wastes time
- It is a legal need
> Repeating confirms the message was heard right.
