A kit should be small enough to carry and contain what actually stops people dying from injury. It does not need medicines for this course.

## Core items
- **Two tourniquets** of a tested design (the kind approved for military use). Beware of cheap copies.
- **Gauze for packing** (plain or hemostatic), two or three rolls.
- **Pressure bandages**, two.
- **Chest seals**, a pair, one for each side.
- **Gloves** (several pairs), **trauma shears** to cut clothes.
- A **space blanket** or two, and a **marker** to write the time on a tourniquet.
- Tape, a few ordinary bandages, a small notepad.

## Where to put it
- One kit in the home, easy to find, **not locked away**.
- One in each car.
- One small kit on each adult, for your pocket or belt, with a tourniquet and a pack of gauze.

## Check and replace
- Look at the kit every six months. Replace items that are damaged or past their date.
- Learn where everything is. At a bad moment you will not read the labels.

## What this kit leaves out
Medicines are not part of this course. Ask your doctor or pharmacist about what to keep, and follow the label.

> A tourniquet in a drawer at home does not help the person bleeding in the garden. Spread the kits around and know where they are.

## Going deeper: a kit built for trauma
Most store-bought kits are full of small bandages. A kit that saves lives in a crisis needs the heavy-bleeding items first.

## Trauma-first items
- 2 commercial tourniquets
- Hemostatic gauze and rolls of plain gauze for packing
- Pressure bandages (elastic wrap with pad)
- A pair of vented chest seals
- Trauma shears
- Nitrile gloves (several pairs) and eye protection
- A mylar blanket and a space blanket or wool blanket
- Triangular bandages and a splint (a SAM-type or rolled padding)
- Medical tape, a marker and a notebook to record times
- A CPR face shield

## Everyday items
Adhesive bandages, antiseptic wipes, burn dressings, eyewash, tweezers, a thermometer, and a small first aid guide. Add any prescriptions and personal items. Carry medicines only as your doctor or the label directs, and do not guess at amounts.

## Match the kit to the person
Pack for your family: infant items, an epinephrine auto-injector for people with serious allergies and an inhaler for people with asthma, kept as prescribed.

## Where
Keep a kit at home, one in each car, one in the go-bag and one where you work. Make sure every adult knows where it is and how to use each item. Keep tourniquets reachable, not buried.

## Maintain
Check twice a year. Replace expired items, and restock whatever you use. Train with it, because a kit you cannot use is just weight.
