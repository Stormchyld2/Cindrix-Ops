Reading is not enough. You will do these tasks badly the first time, so do them for the first time in your living room.

## Safety rules for practice
- Use a **training tourniquet** or put a real one on a **padded training limb or a rolled towel**. Never leave a real tourniquet tight on a person for more than a few seconds.
- Never practice wound packing on a real wound.
- Use a **timer** and keep it fun, not scary.

## Drill 1: tourniquet on yourself
Put a tourniquet on your own arm or leg, high and tight, **one-handed**, in under a minute. Repeat with the other limb.

## Drill 2: tourniquet on a partner
Put one on a practice limb or partner's thigh for a few seconds (no longer). Say the time out loud and write it down.

## Drill 3: packing
Make a training wound from a thick towel, with a pocket in it. Pack it with gauze and hold pressure for the full three minutes. Practice with a shirt you can cut.

## Drill 4: the full sequence
One person plays the injured. The other does: safety check, find bleeding, stop it, check airway and breathing, check for shock, cover to keep warm, call for help and write a handover. Time it.

## Repeat
Do the drills every few months, with everyone in the house, including teenagers. Skills fade without practice.

> A drill you can do half-asleep in the dark is a drill you can do under stress. Practice until it feels boring.
