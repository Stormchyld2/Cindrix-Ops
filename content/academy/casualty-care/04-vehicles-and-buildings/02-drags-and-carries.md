Moving a person is hard on them and on you. Move them only when you must, and use a method that protects their spine and your own back.

## When to move
- Fire, smoke, gas, rising water, traffic, falling walls, or any danger you cannot remove.
- When the person must be carried to reach help that cannot reach you.
Otherwise, stay and give care.

## One-person moves
- **Clothes drag.** Grip the collar of the jacket behind the head and drag backwards, supporting the head with your forearms.
- **Blanket drag.** Roll the person onto a blanket or a coat, then pull the cloth along the floor. Good for long distances on smooth ground.
- **Shoulder (fireman's) carry.** Only for someone who is not badly injured, and only if you are strong enough.

## Two-person moves
- **Seat carry.** Each rescuer holds the other's wrists to make a seat and carries the person sitting up. Only for a conscious person who can hold on.
- **Chair carry.** Tie or hold a person on a sturdy chair and carry it down stairs.
- **Stretcher.** A door, a board, or a blanket held at the edges by several people.

## Protect your back
Bend the knees, keep your back straight, and lift with your legs. Get help if the person is heavy.

> Think before you lift. A badly done carry can injure both of you and takes longer than a good plan.

## Check yourself
? When should you move an injured person?
+ When danger makes staying unsafe
- As soon as you reach them
- Never
- When they are comfortable
> Move only to avoid immediate danger.
? What does the blanket drag use?
+ A blanket or coat under the person, pulled along the floor
- A rope round the neck
- Dragging by the arm
- A chair
> The cloth takes the weight along the floor.
? How should you lift to protect your own back?
+ Bend the knees and lift with your legs
- Bend at the waist
- Twist as you lift
- Jerk quickly
> Lifting with the legs protects your spine.
