Crashes are among the most common causes of serious injury. They also put the rescuer at risk from traffic, fire and fuel.

## Before you touch anything
1. **Park well back** and turn on your hazard lights if you are on a road. Put out warning triangles or a light if you have them.
2. **Look for hazards.** Fuel on the ground, smoke, sparks, power lines, a car on a slope, other traffic.
3. **Call for help.** Say exactly where you are.
4. Turn the ignition **off** if you can reach it safely.

## Reaching the person
- Talk to them through the window. Tell them not to move their head or neck.
- Try the doors. If locked, a broken **side** or rear window is usually the safest way in. Cover your hands and look away when you break glass.
- A person who is conscious and talking can usually wait. Treat bleeding and airway first.

## Should you move them?
**Do not move them** unless:
- There is fire, smoke or a real risk of fire.
- The vehicle is in danger of falling or being hit.
- You must move them to give CPR or to reach life-threatening bleeding.
If you must, move them as one unit, keeping the head, neck and back in a line, and pull them along the line of the spine in the direction of the head.

## Seat belts
Support the person's weight before you cut the belt. Cut it away from them. A small belt cutter belongs in every car.

> Most crashed-vehicle injuries need time more than speed. Make the scene safe first, then work.

## Going deeper: dangers at a crash
- **Traffic** kills more helpers than the crash does. Park well back, turn on hazard lights, place warning triangles or flares far enough back for approaching drivers to react, and wear a bright vest if you have one.
- **Fire:** if you smell fuel or see smoke, get people away from the car, at least 100 feet upwind if possible.
- **Electricity:** if wires are down on a car, tell people inside to stay put and call 911. Do not touch the car. If fire forces them out, tell them to jump clear, landing with feet together and shuffling away, without touching the car and the ground together.
- **Airbags** that have not deployed can still go off. Keep clear of the airbag zone.
- **Hybrid and electric cars** have high-voltage cables, usually marked orange. Do not cut them. Turn the car off and leave it to responders.

## Reaching the person
Approach from the front or side where the person can see you, if the neck may be injured, to avoid turning the head. Turn off the ignition and set the brake. Talk to them, ask them not to move and hold the head steady until help arrives.

## Do not move them unless
- There is fire or an immediate danger.
- You cannot reach life-threatening injuries, such as to the airway, any other way.
If you must move them, keep the head, neck and back in line and use many hands.

## Helmets
Leave a motorcycle helmet on unless the airway is blocked and you cannot clear it, and then remove it with two people, one steadying the head.

## Seat belts
Support the person's weight before you cut or release the belt. Cut away from the neck.
