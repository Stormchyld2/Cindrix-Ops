After a storm, an explosion, an earthquake or a fire, buildings can hurt rescuers as much as the original event.

## Check before you go in
- Look for cracks, leaning walls, a sagging roof, or broken stairs.
- Smell for **gas**. If you smell it, do not go in, do not switch anything on or off, and get away.
- Switch off the power and gas at the mains if you can do so safely **from outside**.
- Look for fire, smoke and water.

## Going in
1. Tell someone where you are going.
2. Wear sturdy shoes, a hat or helmet, gloves and a mask. Carry a **torch** (not matches).
3. Move **slowly**, testing the floor in front of you.
4. Mark each room you have checked with a note on the door or chalk.
5. Stay low in smoke. The cleanest air is near the floor.

## What to avoid
- Do not use lifts.
- Do not remove walls or beams that hold up the roof.
- Do not stay in a building with smoke alarms going and a heavy smell of smoke.

## Smoke and carbon monoxide
Smoke and carbon monoxide poison silently. A person who is drowsy, with a headache, who has been in smoke, needs fresh air at once and emergency care.

> If the building feels unsafe, trust the feeling. No one is helped by a second casualty.

## Going deeper: why buildings are dangerous after a disaster
After an earthquake, storm, fire or explosion, a building can collapse without warning. Aftershocks, weakened walls and unstable floors can kill rescuers.

## Check before you go in
- Look for cracks, leaning walls, sagging roofs, missing supports and broken glass.
- Smell for gas. If you smell gas or hear hissing, leave at once and call from outside.
- Look for downed power lines and flooded basements.
- Do not enter if flames, heavy smoke or a damaged structure threaten you. Call 911.
- Have another person stay outside, watching, and agree on a signal to get out.

## If you go in
Wear sturdy shoes, gloves, a hard hat and a dust mask. Move slowly and test floors. Keep to exits you can retreat through. Check one room at a time, and mark the doors you have searched. Do not move heavy debris, which can shift and collapse. Call and listen for voices and tapping.

## Do not
- Use matches or lighters, or flip switches, near gas.
- Use elevators.
- Enter a smoke-filled space without breathing protection.
- Pull on beams or loose rubble that is holding up something else.

## Smoke and carbon monoxide
Smoke carries poison gases. Stay low where the air is better. Feel the door with the back of your hand before opening it. If it is hot, do not open it. CO poisoning causes headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness and confusion, and kills silently. Get everyone outside to fresh air and call 911. People rescued from smoke need medical checks, even if they seem well.

+ A torch
- A candle
- A match
- A lighter
> A torch cannot ignite leaking gas.
? Why mark each room you have checked?
+ So no one searches it twice and no one is missed
- For decoration
- To scare people
- To block the door
