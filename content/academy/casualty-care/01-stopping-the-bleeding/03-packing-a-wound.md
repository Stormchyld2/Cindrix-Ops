Some wounds are too deep or in the wrong place for a pad on top to work, for example the groin, the armpit, the neck, or a deep gash. Here you **pack** the wound.

## What you need
Plain gauze that unrolls, or hemostatic gauze (gauze treated to help blood clot), or in an emergency a strip of clean cloth.

## Steps
1. Open the wound with your fingers and find where the blood is coming from.
2. **Push the gauze into the wound** with your fingers, right down to the source of bleeding. Keep feeding it in until the wound is full.
3. Press **hard** on the packed wound with both hands for **at least three minutes** without letting up.
4. Hold the packing in place with a pressure bandage.
5. Do not remove the packing. Medical staff will take it out.

## Notes
- It hurts. Tell the person it is needed and that it will be over soon.
- Pack down to the bottom of the wound. Gauze laid on the surface does not stop deep bleeding.
- A wound in the neck: pack and press, but never press hard enough to block breathing.
- A tourniquet cannot be used on the groin, armpit or neck, which is why packing matters.

> Count the three minutes on a watch or phone. It feels much longer than it is, and people stop too soon.

## Check yourself
? Where should the gauze go when you pack a wound?
+ Right down into the wound to the source of bleeding
- Only on the surface
- Beside the wound
- Over the person's clothes
> Deep bleeding is stopped by gauze that reaches the source.
? How long should you press on a packed wound?
+ At least three minutes without letting up
- Ten seconds
- Until it hurts the person
- Until the gauze is damp
> Pressure needs time for a clot to form.
? Why is packing important for wounds in the groin or armpit?
+ A tourniquet cannot be used there
- Packing is cheaper
- It is faster than pressure
- It needs no gauze
> Those places are too high on the limb for a tourniquet to work.
