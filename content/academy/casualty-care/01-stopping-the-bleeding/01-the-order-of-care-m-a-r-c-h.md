> This course is basic awareness for people who cannot reach help right away. It does not replace a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can.

People who die from injuries that could have been survived most often die from **bleeding**, then from a blocked airway, then from a chest injury. The order of care follows that order. A simple memory aid is **M-A-R-C-H**.

## The five steps
1. **M: Massive bleeding.** Find and stop life-threatening bleeding first. It can kill in a few minutes.
2. **A: Airway.** Make sure the person can breathe through an open airway.
3. **R: Respiration.** Look at the breathing and the chest. Seal open chest wounds.
4. **C: Circulation.** Check for shock and for bleeding you missed. Treat shock.
5. **H: Hypothermia and head injury.** Keep the person warm. Watch for signs of a head injury.

## Why this order
Stopping bleeding comes before checking breathing because blood loss is fastest. A person who is talking to you has an open airway for now, so you can stop the bleeding first.

## Your first moves
- Make sure the scene is safe for you. A second injured person does not help anyone.
- Put on gloves if you have them. Use a bag or cloth if you do not.
- Look for **spurting or pooling blood**, soaked clothing, or blood that will not stop.

> Say what you are doing out loud, in a calm voice. "I am going to stop the bleeding." It keeps you steady and helps the injured person.

## Check yourself
? In the M-A-R-C-H order, what comes first?
+ Stopping massive bleeding
- Checking the pulse
- Giving water
- Moving the person
> Severe bleeding can kill in minutes, so it is dealt with first.
? What does the R in M-A-R-C-H stand for?
+ Respiration (breathing and the chest)
- Rest
- Rescue
- Recovery
> R is for respiration: look at the breathing and seal open chest wounds.
? Before you start helping, what should you do?
+ Make sure the scene is safe for you
- Move the person at once
- Give food and drink
- Leave them alone
> A second casualty helps nobody. Safety comes first.
