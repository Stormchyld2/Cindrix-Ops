Most bleeding stops with firm pressure. Start here for any wound that is bleeding heavily.

## How to do it
1. Expose the wound so you can see where the blood is coming from.
2. Press **hard** on the wound with a thick pad of clean cloth or gauze, using both hands and your body weight.
3. Keep pressing without peeking. Lifting the pad to look lets the clot break away.
4. If blood soaks through, **add more cloth on top** and keep pressing. Do not remove the first pad.
5. After the bleeding slows, hold the pad in place with a firm wrap such as a pressure bandage.

## Pressure bandages
A pressure bandage is a pad with an elastic wrap and a bar or clip. Put the pad over the wound, wrap round the limb, pass the wrap through the bar, turn back and wrap again so the bar presses down on the pad. It should be tight enough that the pad does not move.

## What can go wrong
- Pressing too lightly. Bleeding keeps going.
- Letting go to look. The clot is lost.
- Using a bandage that is too loose. It does nothing.

> If direct pressure does not control bleeding on an arm or leg, move on to a tourniquet. Do not keep holding a wound that keeps pouring.

## Check yourself
? Blood soaks through the pad. What do you do?
+ Add more cloth on top and keep pressing
- Remove the soaked pad and start over
- Stop pressing
- Pour water on it
> Taking the pad off breaks the clot. Add layers and keep pressure on.
? Why should you avoid lifting the pad to look?
+ It breaks the clot that is forming
- It hurts you
- It makes the pad dirty
- It wastes time only
> A clot takes time to form. Lifting the pad undoes it.
? A pressure bandage should be
+ Tight enough that the pad does not move
- Loose so blood can flow
- Left off when there is a pad
- Wrapped only once without a bar
> The bar and wrap press the pad onto the wound.
