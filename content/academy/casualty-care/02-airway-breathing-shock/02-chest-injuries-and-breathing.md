A wound in the chest can let air into the space around the lung, and a lung can collapse. This is why the chest is checked early.

## Look and listen
- Is the chest rising on **both** sides?
- Is there a hole, a sucking noise, or bubbling blood? This is an **open chest wound**.
- Is the person struggling harder and harder to breathe, with blue lips?

## Treating an open chest wound
1. Wipe the wound if you can, and ask the person to breathe out.
2. Cover the hole with a **chest seal** (a sticky plastic dressing with a valve), pressed on firmly. If you do not have one, use a piece of plastic and tape it down on **three sides**, leaving one side free so air can escape.
3. Let the person sit up if they are more comfortable, or lie on the injured side.
4. Check them often. If they keep getting worse, a build-up of air in the chest may be pressing on the lung. This needs a medical professional. Take the seal off for a moment to let the air out only if you have been taught how.

## Other chest injuries
- Do not remove anything stuck in the chest. Pad round it.
- A rib injury can be very painful. Support the chest with an arm or a cushion.

> A person who finds it harder and harder to breathe is in danger. Call for help and keep them calm.

## Check yourself
? How do you tape a makeshift chest seal?
+ On three sides, leaving one free
- On all four sides
- On one side only
- Without tape
> The open side acts as a valve that lets air out but not in.
? What should you do about an object stuck in the chest?
+ Leave it in and pad around it
- Pull it out
- Push it in further
- Cover it with plastic only
> Removing it can cause heavy bleeding.
? Which is a sign of a worsening chest injury?
+ Breathing getting harder and harder with blue lips
- A slow calm pulse
- Warm hands
- A loud voice
> Increasing breathing trouble is an emergency.
