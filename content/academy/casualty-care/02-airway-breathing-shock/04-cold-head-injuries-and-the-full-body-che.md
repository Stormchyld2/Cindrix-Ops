Once the big problems are dealt with, do a careful check from head to toe and protect the person from cold.

## Cold
Blood loss and shock make a person cold, even in warm weather. A cold body cannot clot blood.
- Take off wet clothing and cover the person with blankets, coats, or a space blanket. Put something **under** them as well.
- Cover the head. Keep them out of the wind and off cold ground.

## Head injury
Watch for these signs after a blow to the head:
- Loss of consciousness, even briefly.
- Getting more drowsy or confused.
- Repeated vomiting, a severe headache, or a fit.
- One pupil bigger than the other, or weakness on one side.
If you see these, call for help. Keep the person still and keep checking.

## The blood sweep
1. Run your hands over the person from head to toe, including the back, armpits and groin, looking for blood on your gloves.
2. Check for **exit wounds**. A wound can look small on one side and be large on the other.
3. Check the neck, the back of the head and under the clothes.
4. Cut clothing away if you have to. It is better than missing a wound.

## Splinting
Support a broken limb in the position you find it, using padding and a rigid support. Check the fingers or toes below for colour and feeling.

> The blood sweep takes under a minute. Do it every time. The wound you miss is the one that kills.

## Going deeper: hypothermia
Cold kills quietly. Injured people lose heat fast, and shock makes it worse, even in mild weather. The "hypothermia prevention" step in MARCH matters.
- Get the person off the ground and out of wind and wet. Remove wet clothing if you can replace it.
- Insulate under and around them, including the head and neck.
- Wrap them in a sleeping bag, blankets or a reflective blanket, and add a vapor barrier such as a plastic sheet over the top.
- Give warm, sweet drinks only if they are alert and can swallow.
- Handle severely cold people gently, since rough movement can disturb the heart.
- Warm the center of the body first, not hands and feet. Do not use hot water or rub the limbs.

## Head injury
Danger signs include loss of consciousness, repeated vomiting, a severe headache, confusion, unequal pupils, weakness, clear fluid from the nose or ears, a seizure, and drowsiness that grows. These need urgent medical care. Keep the person still, protect the neck if a spine injury is possible, watch the airway and note changes over time.

## The head-to-toe check
After the major threats are handled, run a calm, full check, hands only, from head to feet and then the back. Feel for blood, deformity, swelling, pain and wounds, and look at the skin. Cut clothing if needed. Roll the person as one unit if you suspect a spine injury, with help.

## Splinting
Splint a broken limb in the position found. Pad it, immobilize the joints above and below and check circulation beyond the splint. Do not try to straighten a fracture, unless there is no pulse, and then only a trained person should attempt it.

## Record
Write down findings, times and care so the medical team has the facts.

## Check yourself
? Why is keeping a bleeding person warm important?
+ A cold body cannot clot blood properly
- It saves blankets
- It stops pain
- It keeps them awake
> Cold makes bleeding worse.
? Why check for exit wounds?
+ A wound can look small on one side and be large on the other
- It helps with paperwork
- They are easy to see
- They never bleed
> A thorough check finds hidden bleeding.
? Which is a warning sign after a head injury?
+ Getting more drowsy or confused
- Feeling hungry
- Sneezing
- A mild itch
> Worsening drowsiness or confusion points to serious injury.
