Shock means the body is not getting enough blood to its organs. It is usually caused by heavy bleeding and it gets worse without treatment.

## Signs
- Pale, cold, clammy skin.
- Fast, weak pulse and fast breathing.
- Anxiety, restlessness, confusion, or drowsiness.
- Extreme thirst, and feeling faint.

## What to do
1. **Stop the bleeding.** This is the most important treatment.
2. Lay the person **flat**. If there is no head, chest or neck injury, you can raise the legs a little.
3. **Keep them warm.** Cover them from below as well as above.
4. Reassure them and keep them still.
5. Give **nothing to eat or drink**, even if they are thirsty. They may need an operation.
6. Check breathing and pulse every few minutes and write down what you see.

## Why warmth matters
A person who is bleeding loses heat quickly, and cold stops blood from clotting. Keeping them warm is part of treating bleeding.

> Shock can look mild at first and then collapse quickly. Treat the signs even if the person says they feel all right.

## Going deeper: what shock is
Shock is when the body's organs do not get enough blood flow. In trauma, the most common cause is blood loss, but it can follow burns, heart trouble, severe infection or a serious allergic reaction.

## Signs to watch
- Pale, cool, clammy skin, and a gray or blue tint to lips.
- A fast, weak pulse, and fast, shallow breathing.
- Thirst, nausea, dizziness, weakness or fainting.
- Anxiety, confusion or drowsiness. A person who was alert becoming restless or sleepy is a warning.
- Slow capillary refill: press a fingernail and see color return in more than two seconds.

## What to do
1. Stop the bleeding first. Nothing else helps until you do.
2. Lay the person flat, unless they have a head, chest or breathing problem, or are vomiting. Do not prop the head up for shock.
3. Raise the legs a little only if there is no spinal, leg or pelvis injury, and it causes no pain.
4. Keep them **warm.** Put a blanket under and over them, cover the head, and insulate from the ground.
5. Give nothing to eat or drink if they might need surgery or are not fully alert.
6. Calm them, and stay with them.
7. Call for help, and monitor breathing and pulse every few minutes.

## A related emergency
Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) can cause shock with swelling, hives and trouble breathing. Use the person's epinephrine auto-injector as its label and plan direct, and call 911.

## Do not delay
Shock can kill even when the injuries look small. Treat it as urgent and get the person to medical care.
