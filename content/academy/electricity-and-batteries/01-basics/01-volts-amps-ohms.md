Think of electricity as water in a pipe.

- **Voltage (volts, V)** is the push, the difference in electrical pressure between two points.
- **Current (amps, A)** is the flow of charge.
- **Resistance (ohms, Ω)** is the opposition to flow. Thin, long or hot wire has more resistance than thick, short, cool wire.

## Ohm's law
**Current = Voltage ÷ Resistance**, written I = E ÷ R. Rearranged: E = I × R and R = E ÷ I.

*Example:* 1.5 volts across 5 ohms gives 0.3 amps. Double the voltage and the current doubles. Double the resistance and it halves.

## Power
**Watts = Volts × Amps.** A 12-volt device that draws 2 amps uses 24 watts. Over one hour that is 24 watt-hours.

## Why it matters
Wire that is too thin for the current gets hot. That is how fires start.
