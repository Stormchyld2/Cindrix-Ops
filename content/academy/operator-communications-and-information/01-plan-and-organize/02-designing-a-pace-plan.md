PACE is a method from military and emergency planners for making sure you always have a way to communicate. You list four methods, in order, for each important link.

## The four levels
- **Primary:** the normal method used day to day.
- **Alternate:** the next best, used if the primary fails.
- **Contingency:** a more rugged backup that works when the first two are gone.
- **Emergency:** a last resort, often slow or manual.

## Choose different failure modes
Each method should fail for a **different reason.** If both your primary and alternate depend on the cell network, one outage kills both.

## An example for a family
- **Primary:** cell calls and texts.
- **Alternate:** messages by internet (Wi-Fi), such as a secure chat app.
- **Contingency:** GMRS or FRS handheld radios on agreed channels. Later: ham radio.
- **Emergency:** meeting at a set place and time (a note left at the rally point, signal flags, whistle codes).

## An example for a group or neighborhood
- **Primary:** group chat.
- **Alternate:** phone tree by voice.
- **Contingency:** GMRS repeater or simplex channels, with a net at set times.
- **Emergency:** runners and a message board at the rally point.

## Build the plan
1. **List the links:** household to household, home to family out of town, group to authorities, leader to team.
2. **For each link, assign P, A, C and E methods.**
3. **Set triggers:** when does a method count as failed? ("If texts do not go through in 10 minutes, go to the next level.")
4. **Assign owners:** who is responsible for each method.
5. **Schedule checks:** a weekly or monthly radio check-in.
6. **Write it on one page** and keep copies with each person.
7. **Test every level,** especially the contingency and emergency.

## Power for each method
List what powers each: batteries, charging, solar, car, hand crank. Count hours.

## Information to carry
- Phone numbers on paper
- Radio channel list and call signs
- Meeting places and times
- Check-in schedule
- Code words in plain language, such as "Red" for "come home now" (not for secrecy but speed)

## The Cindrix PACE page
The Settings screen includes a PACE plan page with questions that build a one-page plan in a few minutes. Fill it in with your group.

## Review
Review after each test or event. Move methods up or down as you learn.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.

## Check yourself
? What should each PACE method have in common with the others?
- The same battery
+ Nothing: they should fail for different reasons
- The same tower
- The same app
> Avoid shared weak points.

? What is a trigger in a PACE plan?
- A weapon
- A button
- A cable
+ A condition for moving to the next method, such as no connection after ten minutes
> It removes guessing.

? What is a typical emergency-level method?
+ Meeting at a set place and time
- A text
- A cell call
- A video call
> Low-tech methods work when all else fails.
