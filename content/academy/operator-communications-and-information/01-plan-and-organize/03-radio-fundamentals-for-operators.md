Operators need to know why a radio works or fails. A few basics explain most problems.

## Frequency, wavelength and bands
A radio signal is a wave. **Frequency** is how often it repeats, in hertz. Higher frequencies have shorter wavelengths.
- **HF (3 to 30 MHz):** bounces off the ionosphere and can travel hundreds or thousands of miles. Needs a license (ham), good antennas, and knowledge of propagation.
- **VHF (30 to 300 MHz):** FRS, GMRS (462 to 467 MHz is UHF), 2-meter ham (144 to 148 MHz), marine and many public safety bands. Mostly **line of sight.**
- **UHF (300 MHz to 3 GHz):** GMRS, FRS, 70-cm ham. Better through buildings than VHF, but shorter range.

## Line of sight
VHF and UHF signals travel in nearly straight lines. Hills, buildings and the horizon block them. Height is power. A handheld at head height may reach a mile or two. At a hilltop it may reach 10 or 20 miles. A repeater on a tall tower can reach much farther.

## Power and range
Doubling power does not double range. A change from 5 watts to 50 watts adds only a bit. A better antenna, more height and clear line of sight do more.

## Simplex, duplex and repeaters
- **Simplex:** two radios talk directly on one frequency. No infrastructure needed.
- **Repeater:** a high-site station that receives on one frequency and retransmits on another, extending range. Needs power and may fail in a disaster, unless it has backup power.
- **Duplex:** talking and listening at the same time on two frequencies (what repeaters do).

## Modes
- **FM voice:** common on VHF and UHF.
- **SSB voice:** common on HF, efficient for long distance.
- **CW (Morse):** the most efficient, works when others fail.
- **Digital modes:** data, text and images, such as Winlink email over radio and FT8.

## Antennas
- The antenna matters more than the radio. A bad antenna wastes power.
- **Stock rubber duck** antennas on handhelds are compromised. A longer whip, or a roll-up J-pole, helps a lot.
- **Vertical** antennas cover all directions. **Directional** antennas (Yagi) focus on one.
- **Place it high, in the clear,** away from metal.
- **Coax cable** loses signal. Keep short, use good quality.
- **Never** transmit without an antenna, and keep clear of power lines.

## Power sources
Batteries, car, solar panels with a charge controller, and small generators. Keep spares. Know how long each radio lasts on a charge.

## Licensing
- **FRS:** no license, low power.
- **GMRS:** license for a family, up to 50 watts and repeaters.
- **Amateur (ham):** an exam, with more bands, power and privileges.
- **CB:** no license, short range.
- Know your radio's legal limits.

## Test and map
Test range in your area, with the radios in your plan. Map the dead spots.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
