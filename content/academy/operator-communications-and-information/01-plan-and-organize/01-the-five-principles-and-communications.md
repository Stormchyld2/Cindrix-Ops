When power, cell towers and the internet fail, communication becomes a lifeline and a leadership tool. People who cannot reach each other panic, duplicate effort and make poor decisions.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- How could communication fail? Power cut, tower damage, network congestion, internet outage, cyber attack, jamming, a lost or broken phone, dead batteries.
- Who must you reach: household, neighbors, the group, an out-of-area contact, emergency services, authorities?
- What do you need to send: an alert, a check-in, a medical request, a location, a plan change?

## 2. Preparedness
- A **PACE plan:** Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency methods.
- Radios, batteries, antennas, and trained operators with licenses.
- Written procedures, call signs, channel lists, schedules.
- Practice nets and drills.

## 3. Response
- Send the right message, to the right person, by the right method, in the right format.
- Keep a log. Follow net discipline.
- Move to the next method when one fails.

## 4. Mitigation
- Redundancy: many methods and many people who can operate them.
- Protect equipment from surge, weather and theft.
- Reduce exposure: do not reveal sensitive data over open channels.

## 5. Recovery
- Restore networks, replace gear, review logs, hold an after-action review.

## The communications cycle
Message created, passed, received, confirmed, acted on, and recorded.

## What makes good communication
- **Clear:** short, simple, complete.
- **Correct:** facts, not rumors.
- **Concise:** time and battery are limited.
- **Confirmed:** read back to be sure.
- **Calm:** voice and tone steady.
- **Coded where legal:** use pre-agreed plain-language words, not secret codes.

## The Tier 1 job
An operator **designs and runs** the communications system for the group, teaches others, and keeps it working under stress.

> A radio in a drawer is not a communications plan. A plan that is practiced is.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
