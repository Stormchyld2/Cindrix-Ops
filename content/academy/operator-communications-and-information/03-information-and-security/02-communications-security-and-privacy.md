What you say can be heard by anyone with a receiver, and what you type can be read by anyone with access. Some caution is just good sense.

## Know the rules
- **Amateur and GMRS radio:** **no encryption** and no secret codes meant to hide meaning. Transmissions are public. Plain language is the law.
- **FRS and CB:** public.
- Some other services allow encryption. Check the rules for your license.
- Cell and internet apps can be encrypted legally.

## Assume radio is public
Anyone can listen on a scanner. Do not broadcast:
- Addresses, schedules or when homes are empty.
- Details of supplies, stores or caches.
- Names of children, medical details or financial information.
- Plans that could hurt you if known.
Use general terms, a rally point name instead of a street address, and speak in time-neutral terms ("at the usual place at the usual time").

## What you can do legally
- **Pre-arranged meaning words** for speed and brevity (not to hide meaning): "Blue" means "all clear."
- **Brevity:** keep it short to cut exposure.
- **Directional antennas and low power** reduce who can hear you.
- **Change channels** if needed.
- **Use landlines, phones and apps** for private conversations. Use encrypted messaging (Signal and similar) for private text when networks work.

## Digital hygiene
- **Strong, unique passwords** and a password manager.
- **Two-factor authentication.**
- **Updates** on phones and computers.
- **Backups** of important data.
- **Phishing awareness:** do not click unknown links or open attachments.
- **Public Wi-Fi** is risky.
- **Location services:** turn off when not needed.
- **Metadata:** photos and files carry location. Strip it before sharing.

## Offline devices
- Keep an offline copy of key documents on an encrypted USB or a drive.
- Keep paper copies in a safe place.
- Protect devices with a passcode.

## Jamming and interference
Radio can be disrupted by interference or deliberate jamming. Keep alternates and **know the channels** you can move to. Report deliberate interference to the FCC.

## Surveillance and privacy
Ordinary people have a right to privacy. Be aware that phones, cars, cameras and social media can track you. Decide what you share. Teach children.

## Lawful use
Use radios and networks only for lawful, safe purposes. Do not interfere with emergency services.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
