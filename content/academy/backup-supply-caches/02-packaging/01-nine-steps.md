Poor packaging ruins a cache. The Army's rule: **design the packing to last at least as long as the shelf life of the contents.** The steps, in civilian terms:

1. **Inspect**: everything complete, working and free of corrosion.
2. **Clean** metal items thoroughly. Handle them with clean gloves, because sweat rusts steel.
3. **Dry**: every trace of moisture off. Wipe, warm gently, or use a desiccant such as silica gel. Do not let a desiccant touch bare metal.
4. **Coat** bare metal tools with a light film of oil.
5. **Wrap** each item **separately** so one leak does not ruin everything. Seal the folds.
6. **Pack** into the container with padding so nothing rattles or scrapes.
7. **Include instructions**: simple words and pictures for whoever opens it.
8. **Seal** the container.
9. **Test the seal**: submerge it and watch for bubbles. Warm water shows leaks that cold water hides.

## Weight
If one person must carry it, keep each container to **about what you can carry easily**, such as a small suitcase's worth. Split large stores into several packages.

## Check yourself
? Why wrap each item separately?
+ So one leak or tear does not expose everything
- It looks tidier
- It saves wrap
- It adds weight
> Separate wrapping limits any failure.
? How do you test a container's seal?
+ Submerge it and watch for escaping bubbles
- Shake it
- Smell it
- Weigh it
> Bubbles show where air, and therefore water, can get in.
? Why handle metal items with clean gloves?
+ Sweat causes rust
- Gloves look nice
- Fingerprints add weight
- To keep them warm
> Even small amounts of sweat corrode metal.
