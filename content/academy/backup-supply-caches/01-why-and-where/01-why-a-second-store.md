Your main stash can be lost all at once: house fire, flood, burglary, a road closed by an emergency, or simply being away from home when trouble starts. A **backup cache** is a small, protected store kept somewhere else.

## What belongs in it
Keep it small and valuable, the things that are hardest to replace quickly:
- Copies of papers, a list of contacts, and cash in small bills
- A water filter or purification supplies
- Basic first aid supplies, spare glasses, and any needed prescriptions rotated on schedule
- Fire starters, a knife, a light and spare batteries
- Seeds, sealed
- A printed map and your plan

## Plan like the Army did
The manual boils planning down to a few questions: **What is it for? What could damage it? Who could stumble on it? Can I get to it, and can I carry what I take out?** Security always comes first, but you must also be able to **reach** it when you need it.

> Fewer, smaller caches with a clear purpose beat one giant hoard nobody can move.

## Check yourself
? What makes a backup cache different from your main stash?
+ It is kept somewhere else so one disaster does not take both
- It is larger
- It holds only food
- It is hidden from you
> Separate locations protect against a single event.
? What should a cache contain?
+ Small, valuable, hard-to-replace items you can carry
- Everything you own
- Only heavy items
- Perishable food
> Small portable items give the best value.
? Which question matters most when planning a cache?
+ Can I reach it safely when I need it?
- Is it exciting?
- Is it near a road sign?
- Is it huge?
> A cache you can't reach is useless.
