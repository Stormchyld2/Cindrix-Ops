## Good sites
- **A place you own or have permission to use**: a relative's house, a storage unit, a locked shed, your own land. Do not bury anything on someone else's land or public land without permission.
- Dry, above any flood line, easy to reach in winter and summer.
- Not likely to change: avoid spots near new construction, road work or places the owner might sell.
- Away from key infrastructure that might be blocked off in an emergency.

## Before you dig
In the US, call **811** a few days ahead so buried gas, power and water lines are marked. Never dig blind.

## Few people should know
Everyone who helps place or recover the cache knows where it is. Keep that circle **small and trusted**, and write the location in a form only you can read, such as a map note kept apart from the cache.

## Concealment versus burial
- **Concealed indoors** (a wall cavity, a false-bottom box, a trusted friend's attic): quick to reach and easy to check, but easier to find by accident.
- **Buried**: very secure over time but slow to place and recover, and needs a very good container.
- **Submerged**: rarely a sound choice for ordinary people.

## Check yourself
? What must you do before digging a cache hole?
+ Have permission and call 811 to mark buried utility lines
- Dig at night with no checks
- Ask a neighbor to guess
- Nothing
> Hitting a line can kill you and break the law.
? What is a drawback of burying a cache?
+ It is slow and hard to place and recover
- It is easy to find
- It never needs a container
- It can't be hidden
> Burial is secure but laborious and demands excellent packaging.
? Who should know where a cache is?
+ As few trusted people as possible
- The whole neighborhood
- Anyone who asks
- Nobody, including you
> Each extra person raises the risk.
