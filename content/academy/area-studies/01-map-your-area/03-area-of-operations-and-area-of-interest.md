The first step in any area study is to decide **where** you are studying. The military uses two terms:

- **Area of Operations (AO).** The ground you directly work in and can influence. For a family: your home and the neighborhood around it, the place you would spend your time if cars did not exist.
- **Area of Interest (AI).** A wider area that affects you, even though you do not control it. For a family: the shops, reservoir, hospital, highway, power station and other places you depend on or that could affect you.

## Mark your AO
1. **Get a map.** A printed street map or topographic map of your area. You can also use the Maps screen in this program.
2. **Mark your home.** Put a clear dot on it.
3. **Draw the boundary.** Use a 15-to-20-minute walk from home as a start. That is roughly one mile.
4. **Use natural boundaries.** A river, a ridge, a railway or a major road makes a good edge.
5. **Note the crossing points.** Where do roads cross the boundary? Where are the bridges and underpasses?
6. **Mark the key places.** Fire station, shops, schools, medical clinic, houses of friends and family, parks.

## Mark your AI
The AI extends farther. Include things that could affect you or that you may need:

- The nearest hospital and urgent care.
- A main grocery store and a hardware store.
- A fuel station.
- Rivers, dams and reservoirs upstream of you.
- Major highways, rail lines and airports.
- Power substations and major utility facilities.
- Large employers and warehouses that generate traffic.
- Industrial sites that store chemicals.
- Public agencies such as police and fire headquarters and emergency management.

## Choke points
A **choke point** is a place where many routes funnel into one: a bridge, a tunnel, an underpass, a highway on-ramp, a narrow valley road.

- Note the choke points on your routes. They are where **traffic jams** build first, where **floods and fires** cut roads first, and where **crashes** block everyone.
- Do not plan to block public roads. Roadblocks on public roads are unlawful without authority. Use choke points to **choose your own routes**, and to understand where trouble may build.

## Ask good questions about the map
- If this road is blocked, how do I get out?
- If this bridge is closed, what is the nearest crossing?
- What is the closest place I can get water?
- Where would a flood go? Where would smoke settle?
- Which direction is the nearest safe, high ground?

## A paper map matters
Phones and GPS may not work. Keep at least one paper map of your AO and AI. Mark it with a pencil and keep it in your go-bag and vehicle. Learn to read it. (See the Land Navigation course.)

## Checklist
- Get a printed map of your area.
- Draw your AO boundary with a pencil.
- Mark the crossing points and choke points.
- Draw your AI and mark the key places in it.
- Put a copy in the go-bag and one in each vehicle.
- Date the map.

> The AO is where you can act. The AI is what can affect you. You need to know both.
