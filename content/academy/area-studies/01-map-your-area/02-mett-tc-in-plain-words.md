In the military, **METT-TC** is the checklist of mission variables that a planner thinks through before making a plan. It stands for:

- **M**ission
- **E**nemy
- **T**errain and weather
- **T**roops and support available
- **T**ime available
- **C**ivil considerations

It is a way to make sure you have looked at every important part of the problem before you decide. You can use the same checklist for a family or a preparedness group, with plain-language versions of each word.

## The civilian version
- **Mission: What is our purpose?** Keep everyone safe, fed, warm and in touch for at least [your target: three days, three weeks, three months]. Be clear and write it down.
- **Threats: What could go wrong?** The hazards that fit your location: flood, fire, storm, earthquake, power cut, chemical release, illness, crime.
- **Terrain and weather: What does the ground and climate do to us?** Hills, rivers, flood plains, tree cover, heat, cold, wind, snow and the seasons.
- **People and resources: What do we have?** Who is in the group and what they can do, plus your supplies, tools, vehicles, skills and money.
- **Time: How long do we have?** Warning time before the event, how long it will last, and how long you must be self-reliant.
- **Community: What is around us?** The neighborhood, local services, businesses, agencies and the way the community behaves under stress.

## How to use it
1. Write the six headings on one page.
2. Fill in each from what you know today.
3. Mark the places where you do not know. Those are your **information gaps**.
4. Go find the answers. The rest of this course shows you how.

## An example
**Mission:** Our household of four stays at home for up to two weeks after a winter storm, warm and fed.

**Threats:** Ice storm. Power out for a week. Roads closed for three days. Burst pipes.

**Terrain and weather:** Hillside street that ices over. Heavy snow once or twice a year. The house is on well water that needs a pump.

**People and resources:** Two adults, one teen, one toddler. One wood stove, a month of firewood, two weeks of food, two days of stored water.

**Time:** Storms are forecast two to three days ahead. Outages in the past have lasted up to five days.

**Community:** A fire station two miles away. A neighbor with a generator. A church hall that opens as a warming center.

From this you can see the gaps right away: more stored water, a way to run the well pump, and a plan for the baby's supplies.

## Common mistakes
- Making plans for a vague threat instead of the real hazards where you live.
- Listing only equipment and forgetting skills and time.
- Forgetting the community. Your neighbors and local services are part of the picture.
- Treating the page as finished. Update it as you learn.

## Checklist
- Write the six headings on a page for your household.
- Fill in what you know.
- Mark the gaps.
- Turn each gap into a task on your To-do list.

> A plan built without the facts is just a wish.
