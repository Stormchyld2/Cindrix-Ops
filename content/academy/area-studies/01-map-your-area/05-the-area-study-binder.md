Your area study needs a home. A **physical binder** is the best choice, because paper works with no power. Keep a copy in this program's Binder screen as well, and a second copy off site.

## What you need
- A 3-ring binder, at least 2 inches.
- Clear sheet protectors for maps and key pages.
- Tabbed dividers, simple and labeled.
- A hole punch, a pencil, a ruler, highlighters and sticky notes.
- Printed maps at three scales: your street, your neighborhood and your region.
- A zip bag or waterproof case to protect it.

**Date everything.** Put the date on every page. Old information is dangerous when it looks current.

## The sections
Suggested sections follow the structure used in the *Area Intelligence Handbook*. Adjust them to suit you.

**Section 1: Title page.** The name of your area, who it is for, the version number and the date. Add the contact details for the person who owns the binder.

**Section 2: Area of Operations overview.** The AO is the area immediately around your home, your neighborhood, the place where you would spend your time if cars did not exist. Include boundaries and route maps.

**Section 3: Area of Interest overview.** Your AI extends outward to include nearby important features: a shopping center, reservoir, police or fire station, hardware store. Include boundaries and route maps.

**Section 4: Hazard and threat overview.** The hazards in your area and what they could do. For example: "Large apartment complex near the river, flood plain." "Freeway off-ramp close to my AO, traffic jams and spills." Add analysis. For example: "If ATMs stop working and stores are closed, crowds may gather at the supermarket. Plan to shop before the crowd and avoid that area for the first days."

**Section 5: Operating environment.** Physical terrain and weather; human terrain (the community); critical infrastructure; local government; security and safety; economy and finance.

**Section 6: Resources.** Water, food, fuel, medical help, shelters, tools and skills nearby, with locations and hours.

**Section 7: Routes and rally points.** Primary and alternate routes out, and your rally points, with written directions.

**Section 8: Contacts.** Names, numbers and roles: your out-of-area contact, local emergency management, utilities, neighbors, and nearby friends and family.

**Section 9: Plans.** Your Plan A, Plan B and Plan C for each hazard.

**Section 10: Logs and updates.** A change log with the date, what changed and who changed it.

## Safe handling
- The binder may include your address and your resources. **Keep it private.** Do not leave it where visitors can open it.
- Keep a scanned copy on a protected drive.
- Keep a second copy at a trusted person's home outside your area.
- Do not post it online.

## Using the Binder screen
This program has a Binder screen. Use it for your digital copy, and print from it when you update.

## Keeping it fresh
- Review one section a month.
- Update the whole binder every quarter.
- Update immediately after any significant event or change.

## Checklist
- Buy or set up the binder and tabs.
- Add the title page and dates.
- Print three scales of map and mark your AO and AI.
- Add your hazard list and your first resource list.
- Store a second copy off site.
- Add a quarterly review to the Calendar.

> The binder is only useful if it is current. Date it and update it.
