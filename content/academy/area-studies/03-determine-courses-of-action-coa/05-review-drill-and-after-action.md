An area study is a living document. Keep it alive with regular **reviews**, **drills** and **after-action reviews (AARs)**.

## A yearly rhythm
- **Monthly:** One section of the binder reviewed. A radio check. A smoke alarm test.
- **Quarterly:** A full review. A family drill. Rotate water and food. Check batteries.
- **Twice a year:** A full PACE exercise. A go-bag check. A route drive.
- **Yearly:** Rewrite the whole area study. Update maps. Walk your AO. Refresh training.
- **After every event:** An AAR within one week while it is fresh.

## Types of drill
1. **Talk-through (tabletop).** Gather and talk through a scenario: "A flood warning has been issued at 3 a.m. What do we do?" Cheap and quick, good for finding gaps.
2. **Walk-through.** Walk the real route to the rally point or the real path to the shelter.
3. **Functional drill.** Practice one function, such as the radio check, fire escape or go-bag grab.
4. **Full exercise.** Run a whole plan from trigger to finish.

Start simple and build up. Add stress little by little.

## The after-action review (AAR)
The Army's AAR is a simple and powerful method that anyone can use. Gather the people who took part, in a calm place, and ask four questions:

1. **What did we plan to happen?**
2. **What actually happened?**
3. **Why was there a difference?**
4. **What will we do differently next time?**

Rules for a good AAR:
- Focus on **what** happened, not **who** to blame.
- Everyone speaks, from the youngest to the oldest.
- Write it down.
- Name **sustains** (keep doing) and **improves** (fix).
- Give each fix to a named person with a date.

## A simple AAR form
- Date and event.
- Who took part.
- What we planned.
- What happened.
- What went well (sustain).
- What did not go well (improve).
- Changes: what, who, by when.

## Close the loop
An AAR that sits in a drawer is wasted. Put each change on the To-do list in this program, with an owner and a due date. At the next review, check that each change was made.

## Update the study
After each event or drill:
1. Correct maps, contacts and resources.
2. Update the plans.
3. Add what you learned.
4. Record the changes in the change log, with the date.

## Checklist
- Put the monthly, quarterly and yearly reviews on the Calendar.
- Run a talk-through drill this month.
- Run a full drill this quarter.
- Hold an AAR after every drill and every real event.
- Add each fix to the To-do list with an owner and date.
- Update the binder after every AAR.

> The goal is not to be perfect. It is to get a little better each time.
