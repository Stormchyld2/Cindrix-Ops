When your phones fail, the plan you wrote ahead of time is the one that works. Two parts of that plan are **where you meet** and **how you get there**.

## Rally points
A **rally point** is a place family members or team members agree to go to when they cannot reach each other.

Choose at least **two**:

1. **Near rally point.** A place close to home, such as the neighbor's house, the end of the street or a nearby landmark. Use it for a home fire or a local emergency.
2. **Far rally point.** A place outside your immediate area, such as a relative's house or a library in the next town, for use if you cannot return home.

A good rally point is:

- **Easy to find.** Everyone knows where it is, including children.
- **Safe.** Not in a flood zone, next to a hazard or on a dangerous road.
- **Reachable on foot** if cars cannot move.
- **Open or accessible** at all hours.
- **Not announced publicly.**

For each rally point, write:
- Name and address.
- A description a child could follow.
- A map with the route marked.
- A backup if it is not available.
- The time rule: when to go, and how long to wait.

## Routes
For each route, document:

1. **Primary route.** The fastest way.
2. **Alternate route.** A different road that avoids the same bridges and choke points.
3. **Third route.** On foot, by bike or off-road, if roads are closed.
4. **Time and distance.** Measure them in normal conditions, and note that emergencies will make them longer.
5. **Hazards on the route.** Low spots that flood, bridges, narrow roads, railroad crossings, steep hills.
6. **Fuel and water stops.** Where you can refuel and rest.
7. **Written directions.** Street names and turns, in case your phone does not work.

## Drive or walk each route once
A route on paper is a guess. A route you have driven or walked is knowledge.

1. Go out with the family on a calm day.
2. Time each route.
3. Note landmarks, hazards and good places to stop.
4. Fix the written directions.
5. Ask the children to lead you.

## Vehicle readiness
- Keep your tank at least half full.
- Keep a vehicle kit: water, food, blanket, first aid kit, flashlight, tools, jumper cables, maps, a charged power bank and a radio.
- Keep tires, oil and fluids in good order.
- Have a spare key hidden at home.

## Tell people your plan
- Tell your out-of-area contact where you are going.
- Leave a note if you leave home.
- Tell the school and workplace where family will go.

## Checklist
- Choose two rally points and write down directions.
- Map three routes to each rally point.
- Drive or walk every route once.
- Put the maps and directions in each go-bag.
- Fill the vehicle kit.
- Review quarterly.

> The best rally point is one every member can find in the dark, in the rain, without a phone.
