Now document the things around you that **support your survival**. These are the places and services you would rely on, and the ones you must plan around if they fail.

## The list
Use this as a start, and add to it. Do not limit yourself to the examples.

**Safety and security**
- Fire station and police station, with their locations and phone numbers.
- Emergency management office.
- Hospital, urgent care and pharmacy, with hours.
- Poison control: 1-800-222-1222 in the United States.

**Water**
- Where your drinking water comes from and who runs the system.
- Natural sources: rivers, lakes, ponds, springs, rain.
- Wells and pumps, including community ones.
- Public water points, fountains and pools.
- Any water treatment or storage.

**Food**
- Grocery stores, markets, farms and farm stands.
- Food banks and pantries.
- Community gardens and orchards.
- Hunting and fishing opportunities and the rules.

**Fuel and power**
- Gas stations and fuel depots.
- Propane suppliers.
- Firewood sources.
- Who has solar panels or generators.

**Shelter and warmth**
- Official shelters and warming or cooling centers.
- Churches and halls that open in emergencies.
- Hotels and campgrounds.
- Friends and relatives within reach.

**Medical and care**
- Clinics, dentists, vets.
- Neighbors with medical training.
- Medical supply and equipment sources.

**Tools, supplies and skills**
- Hardware, farm and building supply stores.
- People with trades: electricians, plumbers, mechanics, builders.
- Boat, tractor and truck owners who may help.

**Communications**
- Local radio and TV stations, with frequencies.
- Local radio clubs and repeater locations.
- Places with Wi-Fi.
- Official alert sources for your county.

**Transport**
- Main roads, side roads and trails.
- Bus, rail and ferry routes.
- Bridges, with their load limits and height.

## For each item, write down
1. Name and what it is.
2. Address and a spot on your map.
3. Phone number or contact.
4. Hours, if it has them.
5. Whether you can reach it **on foot**, **by bike** or **by car**.
6. Distance and time from your home.
7. Whether it depends on **power**, **water** or **internet**.
8. The date you checked it.

## Rally points and escape routes
- **Rally points.** Choose two, one close and one farther away, where family or team members can meet. Write directions, and test them.
- **Two escape routes.** At least two ways out of your area, in different directions. Drive or walk both once.
- **A route to each important place.** Write step-by-step directions in case your phone does not work.

## How to collect it
1. **Walk your area** with a notebook and take photos.
2. **Call** the important places and ask about hours, emergency plans and backup power.
3. **Search** official sites for shelters, evacuation routes and alert systems.
4. **Talk** to neighbors about what they know and can share.
5. **Put it on the map.** Use symbols and a key.

## Plan for failure
For each resource, ask: what happens if it is closed, damaged or out of power? Where is the next best one? A hospital that depends on a bridge is not reachable when the bridge is closed.

## Checklist
- Fire, police, hospital, urgent care and pharmacy mapped
- Water sources mapped, including backups
- Fuel locations mapped
- Food sources mapped
- Shelter options mapped
- Two rally points chosen and written down
- Two escape routes chosen and tested
- Each item dated

> A good resource list is one you have visited, tested and written down with directions.

? What should you write for each resource?
- Only the color of the building
- Only the price
+ Name, address, contact, hours, access by foot or car, power dependence and date checked
- Only the name
> Complete entries make the list useful in a hurry.

? Why note whether a resource depends on power or water?
- It looks neater
+ It may fail in the same event that affects you
- It is required by law
- It sets the price
> A pump station with no backup is not reachable when the power fails.

? What should you do with a list once you have written it?
+ Visit places, test routes, and date the entries
- Leave it unchanged forever
- Put it online
- Hide it
> Information you have tested is information you can trust.
