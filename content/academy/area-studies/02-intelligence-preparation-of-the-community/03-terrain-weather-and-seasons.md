The land and the weather shape every plan. The military studies terrain with five factors, known as **OAKOC**. They work well for a family.

## OAKOC for civilians
**Observation and fields of fire.** What can you see from where you are, and what can see you? High ground gives you a view and helps radios. Open fields give you long lines of sight.

**Avenues of approach.** The routes that people, water, fire and smoke are likely to take. Roads, valleys, streams, drainage ditches, fire breaks and railways.

**Key terrain.** Places that give an advantage to whoever uses them: a hill with a good view and radio coverage, a bridge, a spring, a crossroads, a high building.

**Obstacles.** Things that slow or block movement: rivers, steep slopes, fences, dense woods, closed roads, rail lines, floodwater, downed trees.

**Cover and concealment.** Cover protects from weather, wind, flying debris and danger. Concealment hides you from view. A stone wall is cover. A hedge is only concealment.

## Describe your terrain
Use a topographic map and walk the ground:

1. **High ground and low ground.** Where does water run? Where would it pool? Where is the high ground, and how do you reach it?
2. **Water.** Rivers, streams, lakes, springs. How high can they rise? What bridges cross them?
3. **Vegetation.** Forest, grass or crops. How easily does it burn? How much cover does it give?
4. **Soil and slope.** Landslide risk, mud, steep hills.
5. **Built environment.** Dense or spread out? Old or new buildings? High-rise or houses?
6. **Choke points and bottlenecks.** Where routes narrow.
7. **Radio coverage.** Where are dead spots? Where is the high ground you can use?

## Weather and climate
Find out the normal pattern and the extremes for your area.

- **Seasons.** Which months are storm, flood, fire, cold or heat seasons?
- **Temperature extremes.** Record highs and lows. What does the worst week look like?
- **Wind.** Which way does it usually blow? Wildfire smoke and chemical plumes move with the wind.
- **Rain and snow.** Yearly totals and the heaviest events.
- **Daylight.** Hours of light in winter and summer. Darkness changes movement and safety.
- **Forecast sources.** National Weather Service forecast, local radio, NOAA Weather Radio. Learn how to get alerts without a phone.

## Describe the effects
For each important piece of terrain and each season, write one line saying what it does to you.

- "Our street ices over in winter and the hill is too steep to drive. We stay put."
- "The creek floods to the lower road in heavy rain. The north exit closes."
- "Summer winds blow from the south. A fire in the south valley would push smoke to us."
- "The hilltop park has good radio coverage. We use it for the Contingency radio window."

## Use the Calendar
Add the seasons and the hazard windows to the Calendar in this program so reminders appear before the season starts: storm preparation in the fall, fire preparation before summer, water checks before a heat wave.

## Checklist
- Get a topographic map of your area.
- Walk or drive your high ground, low ground and water.
- Write the OAKOC notes for your AO.
- List your climate extremes and seasonal hazards.
- Find where you get alerts without a phone (NOAA Weather Radio, local radio).
- Add the hazard seasons to the Calendar.

> The land and the weather do not change their minds. Learn them and plan around them.
