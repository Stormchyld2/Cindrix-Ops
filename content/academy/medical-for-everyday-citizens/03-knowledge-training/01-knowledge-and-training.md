A structured training path from everyday life-saving skills to advanced preparedness.

The world of medicine is a vast ocean. Doctors often spend 11 to 15 years in school and residency training. The goal of this course is not to turn you into a doctor. It is to help you become **medically capable, confident and useful** during emergencies.

## The training path
**Tier 3: everyday life-saving skills**
- CPR and AED certification
- Basic first aid
- Stop the Bleed training
- (This course)

**Tier 2: intermediate emergency care**
- Wilderness First Aid (about 16 to 20 hours)
- Emergency trauma care
- CERT training
- Basic patient assessment

**Tier 1: advanced preparedness**
- Wilderness First Responder (about 70 to 80 hours)
- EMT coursework
- Austere medicine
- Long-term care in disaster settings

Work from Tier 3 up. Each level builds on the one before.

## Why understanding matters
Training that teaches **why** a treatment works helps you adapt. For example:
- **Direct pressure** works because it slows blood flow long enough for clots to form.
- **Keeping people warm** matters because cold makes blood clot poorly.
- **Tourniquets** work by compressing arteries, so they hurt.
- **Early CPR** keeps the brain alive until the heart can be restarted.
- **Epinephrine** relaxes airways and raises blood pressure in anaphylaxis.
- **Splints** reduce pain and bleeding by stopping movement.

When the textbook situation does not match reality, understanding lets you decide.

## Know your limits
- **Scope of practice:** you are a first responder, not a doctor. Provide first aid, call for help and hand off.
- **Do no harm.** If you are unsure, do the simplest safe thing.
- **Consent:** ask a conscious adult for permission. Consent is implied for unconscious people in an emergency.
- **Good Samaritan laws** in most states protect people who give reasonable help in good faith. Learn your state's rule.
- **Privacy:** keep patient information confidential.

## Self-study and skill sustainment
Training does not stop after a class. Good habits include:
- **Practice tourniquet application** regularly.
- **Review CPR steps** every few months.
- **Repack and inspect** medical kits.
- **Run family emergency scenarios.**
- **Watch reputable medical instruction videos** from the Red Cross, AHA, wilderness schools and Stop the Bleed.
- **Read first aid manuals.**
- **Train with friends** or your preparedness group.

Skills fade without repetition. Even a short refresher every few months can maintain confidence and effectiveness.

## Build a personal training plan
1. **Take CPR and AED.**
2. **Take Basic First Aid.**
3. **Take Stop the Bleed.**
4. **Join CERT.**
5. **Take Wilderness First Aid.**
6. **Practice** with your kit monthly.
7. **Teach** a family member.
8. **Consider** advanced courses, such as Wilderness First Responder, EMT or paramedic, and volunteer work with a rescue squad.

## Checklist
- Coordinate a medical training session with your preparedness group
- Personal training plan written
- Recertification dates on the calendar

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
