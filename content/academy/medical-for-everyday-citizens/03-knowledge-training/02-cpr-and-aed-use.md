These classes are widely available in most communities.

## Why take a class
You can read the steps in this course, but a class gives you **hands-on practice on a manikin,** feedback on depth and rate, and a certification that employers, schools and groups often require.

## Where to find one
- **American Red Cross** (redcross.org/take-a-class)
- **American Heart Association** (cpr.heart.org)
- **Local fire departments** and rescue squads
- **Hospitals** and community health programs
- **Community colleges** and recreation departments
- **Employers** and workplace safety programs
- **CERT** and church or community groups

## What the course should cover
- **Adult, child and infant CPR**
- **AED use**
- **Choking relief**
- **Hands-only CPR**
- Often **basic first aid,** including bleeding, burns, fractures and allergic reactions

Check that the course includes **hands-on skills practice,** not only an online video.

## Online, blended and in person
- **Fully in person:** all instruction and practice in a classroom.
- **Blended:** online lessons, then a short hands-on skills session. Efficient for busy people.
- **Online only:** may not meet workplace requirements, and has no hands-on practice. Better than nothing, but not ideal.

## Cost and time
Courses vary by provider. Many take a few hours to a day. Some fire departments and community programs offer free or low-cost classes. Ask your employer, who may pay.

## Certification and renewal
Certification is typically valid for **two years.** Put the renewal date on the calendar. Refresh before it lapses. Guidelines are updated regularly.

## Equipment to own
- A CPR face shield or pocket mask
- Gloves
- Consider an AED for your home, group, church or business, plus practice with it.

## Make it a group event
Book an instructor for your group. Many instructors will come to you for a group rate. Invite neighbors and friends.

## Also take
- **Stop the Bleed** (about an hour; free or low cost): stopthebleed.org
- **Basic first aid**
- **CERT** (free from your local emergency management office)

## Checklist
- Take a CPR and first aid class
- Know how an AED works
- Attend a Stop the Bleed course
- Renewal date on the calendar
- Book a group class

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
