Even a minor cut treated correctly means quick healing, and one treated poorly can turn into a life-threatening infection. In a disaster, when medical care is far away, wound care is one of the skills you will use the most.

## Hands first
Dirty hands increase the risk of infection. **Wash with soap and clean water** for 20 seconds, or use hand sanitizer if you have no water, and **put on gloves** if you can.

## After bleeding is controlled
Minor bleeding stops with a few minutes of gentle pressure. For serious bleeding, see the bleeding lesson first.

## Clean the wound
1. **Rinse with clean running water** for several minutes. Safe drinking water is best. Flowing water carries dirt out.
2. **Wash around the wound** with mild soap, keeping soap out of the wound if possible.
3. **Remove visible dirt** and small debris with tweezers cleaned with alcohol.
4. **Do not scrub** a deep wound or use hydrogen peroxide or strong antiseptics on the wound itself, which can damage tissue. Use them on the skin around it if needed.
5. **Irrigate** dirty wounds with a lot of clean water. A syringe or a clean plastic bottle with a small hole gives pressure.
6. **Pat dry** around the wound.

## Dress the wound
**Dressing** = what goes directly on the wound (gauze, non-stick pad). **Bandage** = what holds it in place (roller gauze, tape, elastic wrap).
1. **Apply a thin layer of antibiotic ointment,** if you have it and no allergy. It can help keep the surface moist and clean.
2. **Cover with a sterile or clean dressing.**
3. **Secure with a bandage** or tape. Snug, but not tight enough to cut circulation. Check fingers or toes for color, warmth and feeling.
4. **Change the dressing** daily, or sooner if wet or dirty.
5. **Keep it dry** and clean.

## Types of wounds
- **Abrasion (scrape):** clean, and cover.
- **Laceration (cut):** clean, and close the edges with tape strips (Steri-Strips) if it is shallow and clean. Deep, gaping or dirty cuts need a medical professional.
- **Puncture:** small hole, deep. Higher risk for infection and tetanus. Clean well. Seek care.
- **Avulsion:** skin torn away. Control bleeding, cover with moist gauze and seek care.
- **Bite:** wash well with soap and water for several minutes. Seek care for any bite that breaks the skin, because of infection and rabies risks.
- **Blister:** do not pop unless painful. Cover with a bandage.
- **Splinter:** remove with clean tweezers.

## When to get medical help
- Bleeding that will not stop.
- A deep cut, a wound that gapes, is longer than about half an inch, or is on the face or over a joint.
- Dirt or debris you cannot remove.
- An animal or human bite.
- A puncture, especially from a rusty object or in a dirty setting.
- A wound near the eye.
- Numbness, or loss of movement.
- Signs of infection.

## Signs of infection
- Increasing redness, swelling, warmth or pain after the first day or two.
- **Red streaks** spreading from the wound.
- Pus or a bad smell.
- Fever.
- Swollen glands.

If you see these, **seek medical care.** Infection can spread to the blood. In a long emergency, if you cannot reach a doctor, ask a medical professional by radio or phone about options.

## Tetanus
Tetanus is a serious bacterial illness that enters through breaks in the skin, including puncture wounds and dirty wounds. **Keep tetanus shots up to date.** Adults generally need a booster every ten years, and sooner for a dirty wound. Check your records and ask your doctor.

## Don'ts
- Do not use butter, oil, toothpaste or home remedies on wounds.
- Do not close a dirty wound tightly. Trapped dirt causes infection.
- Do not pull out embedded objects.
- Do not use tight bandages that cut circulation.
- Do not ignore wounds.

## Checklist
- Wash hands and wear gloves before wound care
- Know how to rinse, dress and bandage a wound
- Know the signs of infection
- Know when to seek care
- Tetanus shot up to date

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
