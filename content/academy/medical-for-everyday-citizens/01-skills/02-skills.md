The hands-on abilities that can mean the difference between life and death.

The ability to perform basic first aid skills can mean the difference between life and death. Even treating a minor laceration correctly could mean the difference between quick healing and a life-threatening infection.

In high-stress emergencies, people do not rise to the occasion — they fall back on their level of training. Repetition and familiarity are critical.

## To be prepared, you must learn:

## Checklist
- How to perform CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)
- How to use an AED (Automated External Defibrillator)
- Basic Wound Care
- Bleeding Control
- Chest Seal
- Applying a Tourniquet (TQ)
- Splinting
- Administering Narcan
- Epinephrine Administration

Where to Learn Tier 3 Medical Skills

Medical skills are perishable. Reading about them is helpful, but true competence comes from hands-on training, repetition, and practice under stress.

CPR & AED Training — Most classes can be completed in a single day. Certifications are typically valid for two years.
• American Red Cross
• American Heart Association
• Local fire departments
• Community colleges
• Hospitals
• Employers or workplace safety programs

Basic First Aid Courses — Topics typically include wound care, bleeding control, burns, fractures and sprains, allergic reactions, medical emergencies, and patient assessment.
• American Red Cross
• American Heart Association
• CERT programs (Community Emergency Response Team)
• Outdoor schools
• Wilderness medicine organizations
• Community preparedness groups

Other Resources
• Stop The Bleed

## Checklist
- Attend and complete a Basic First Aid class
- Attend and complete a CPR class

The average citizen does not need to become a doctor to make a difference in an emergency. A person with basic training, a calm mindset, and simple medical equipment can save lives before professional responders arrive.
