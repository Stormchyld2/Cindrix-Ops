Before you treat anything, you need to know what you are dealing with. This lesson is the framework every other skill hangs on.

## Check, call, care
The American Red Cross teaches three steps:
1. **Check** the scene and the person.
2. **Call** 911 (or your local emergency number).
3. **Care** for the person until help takes over.

## Step 1: Check the scene
- **Is it safe for you?** Look for fire, traffic, downed power lines, unstable structures, gas, water, violence, animals.
- **If it is not safe,** do not go in. Call for help. You cannot help anyone if you become a patient.
- **What happened?** How many people are hurt? What caused it?
- **Wear gloves** and, if you have it, eye protection and a mask.
- **Do not move** an injured person unless they are in danger (fire, traffic, collapse), or you must to give life-saving care. If you suspect a neck or back injury, avoid moving them.

## Step 2: Check the person
### Responsive?
Tap the shoulders and shout, "Are you okay?"
- **If they respond,** talk to them. Ask what happened, where it hurts, and about allergies, medicines and medical conditions.
- **If they do not respond,** shout for help, **call 911,** send someone for an AED and the first aid kit, and check breathing.

### The primary survey: look for life threats in order
Many courses use letters, such as **MARCH** (used in tactical and trauma care) or **ABC** (used in basic first aid). The idea is the same: find and fix what kills first.

- **M: Massive bleeding.** Look for blood pooling or soaking clothes. Stop it first. (See the bleeding lesson.)
- **A: Airway.** Is the airway open? Is the person choking? Tilt the head and lift the chin if there is no spine injury, or use a jaw thrust.
- **R: Respiration (breathing).** Is the person breathing normally? Look, listen and feel for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is **not** normal breathing and counts as not breathing. Look for chest wounds.
- **C: Circulation.** Check for signs of shock: pale, cool, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, confusion. Check for bleeding again.
- **H: Hypothermia and head injury.** Keep the patient warm. Check for head injury and altered mental state.

If the person is **not breathing normally and unresponsive,** start CPR and use an AED (see the CPR lesson). In a trauma case with severe bleeding, bleeding control comes first.

### The secondary survey
When life threats are handled and the person is stable:
1. **Head to toe check,** gently, looking and feeling for wounds, deformity, swelling, bruising, tenderness.
2. **Ask the SAMPLE questions:**
   - **S:** Signs and symptoms (what hurts, what is wrong?)
   - **A:** Allergies
   - **M:** Medications
   - **P:** Past medical history
   - **L:** Last oral intake (when did they eat or drink?)
   - **E:** Events leading up to it
3. **Check vital signs,** if you have the tools: breathing rate, pulse, skin color and temperature, level of alertness, and if possible oxygen saturation and temperature.
4. **Write it down:** times, findings, what you did.

### Level of alertness: AVPU
- **A:** Alert
- **V:** responds to Voice
- **P:** responds to Pain
- **U:** Unresponsive

Record changes over time.

## Step 3: Call 911 well
When you call:
1. **Give the exact location** (address, cross streets, landmarks).
2. **Say what happened** and how many people are hurt.
3. **Describe the person's condition** (conscious, breathing, bleeding).
4. **Say what you are doing.**
5. **Stay on the line** and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Do not hang up until told.
6. **Send someone** to meet the ambulance.

If phones are down, use your communications plan (see the Communications course): radios, a runner, or get the patient to help.

## Triage when there are many patients
In a disaster, you may find more than one person hurt. Use the CERT START idea: sort quickly into those who need help **now** (severe bleeding, breathing problems), those who can **wait,** those who are **walking wounded,** and those who are **beyond help.** Treat the most savable first. The Casualty Care course covers this in more depth.

## Keep the patient calm and warm
- Talk to them. Tell them what you are doing.
- Keep them still and comfortable.
- **Prevent heat loss:** cover them with a blanket or coat, and put something between them and the ground.
- **Do not give food or drink** to a seriously injured person.
- **Stay with them** until help arrives.

## Hand off
When professionals arrive, give a short report:
- Who the patient is (age, sex, name if known).
- What happened and when.
- What you found (injuries, vital signs).
- What you did (tourniquet and time, CPR, medicines given).
- Allergies, medicines and medical history.

## Checklist
- Know Check, Call, Care
- Know the MARCH or ABC order
- Know the SAMPLE questions
- Know how to call 911 clearly
- Practiced a head-to-toe check on a partner

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
