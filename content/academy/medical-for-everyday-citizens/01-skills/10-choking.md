Choking is a blocked airway. A person can lose consciousness in minutes. Quick action saves lives.

## Mild versus severe
**Mild (partial) obstruction:** the person can cough, speak or make noise and is moving air.
- **Stay with them,** keep them calm and **encourage coughing.** Do not slap their back or give water.
- Call 911 if they do not clear it or get worse.

**Severe (complete) obstruction:** the person cannot cough, speak or breathe, or has a weak, silent cough. They may clutch the throat (the universal choking sign) and turn blue.
- **Act now,** and have someone call 911.

## Adults and children (over 1 year)
Use **five back blows and five abdominal thrusts,** as taught by the Red Cross, repeated until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.

**Back blows**
1. Stand to the side and slightly behind.
2. Support their chest with one hand and lean them forward so the object can fall out.
3. Give **5 firm back blows** between the shoulder blades with the heel of your other hand.

**Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver)**
1. Stand behind them and wrap your arms around the waist.
2. Make a fist and place the thumb side **just above the navel,** well below the breastbone.
3. Grasp the fist with your other hand.
4. Give **5 quick, upward and inward thrusts.**
5. Repeat the cycle: **5 back blows, 5 abdominal thrusts.**

**Pregnant or very large people:** use **chest thrusts** instead of abdominal thrusts, with the hands at the center of the breastbone.

**If you are alone and choking:** lean over a firm object (a chair back or a counter) and thrust your abdomen against it, or use your own fist.

## Infants (under 1 year)
1. **Sit** and hold the infant face down on your forearm, **head lower than the body,** supporting the jaw. Rest your arm on your thigh.
2. Give **5 back blows** with the heel of your hand between the shoulder blades.
3. **Turn** the infant face up, supporting the head, on your other forearm.
4. Give **5 chest thrusts** with two fingers in the center of the chest, just below the nipple line.
5. Repeat until the object comes out or the infant becomes unresponsive.
6. **Never** use abdominal thrusts on an infant.

## If the person becomes unresponsive
1. **Lower them to the ground.**
2. **Call 911** (or make sure someone has) and get an AED.
3. **Begin CPR** starting with chest compressions (see the CPR lesson).
4. **Each time you open the airway to give breaths, look in the mouth.** If you see the object, remove it. **Never do a blind finger sweep.**
5. Continue CPR until help arrives or the person recovers.

## After choking
Anyone who needed abdominal or chest thrusts should see a doctor, because thrusts can cause internal injury, and a piece of the object may remain.

## Prevention
- Cut food into small pieces, especially for children and older adults.
- Eat slowly and sit down to eat.
- Do not give young children whole nuts, hard candy, popcorn, whole grapes or hot dog rounds.
- Keep small objects out of reach of young children.
- Do not talk or laugh with food in your mouth.
- Be careful with alcohol and eating.

## Practice
Practice the movements on a partner, with gentle pressure, or on a manikin, in a class.

## Checklist
- Know the difference between mild and severe choking
- Know 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts
- Know the infant technique
- Know what to do if the person becomes unresponsive
