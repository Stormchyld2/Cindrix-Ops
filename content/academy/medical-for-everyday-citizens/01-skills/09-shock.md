Shock is a life-threatening condition in which the body is not getting enough blood flow to its organs. It can follow severe bleeding, burns, serious injury, severe infection, heart problems, allergic reactions or dehydration.

## Signs
- **Pale, cool, clammy** skin.
- **Fast, weak pulse** and **fast breathing.**
- **Dizziness,** faintness or weakness.
- **Confusion,** anxiety or restlessness, then drowsiness.
- **Thirst,** nausea or vomiting.
- **Blue or gray lips and fingertips.**
- **Low blood pressure,** if you can measure it.

Shock gets worse with time. Early signs may be subtle: a person who looks anxious and pale after an injury may be in shock.

## What to do
1. **Call 911.**
2. **Stop the cause:** control bleeding first. Do all the life-saving steps: airway, breathing, CPR if needed.
3. **Lay the person down** on their back, flat, on a blanket or mat, away from the cold ground. Handle gently.
4. **Raise the legs** about 12 inches **only if** there is no head, neck, back, leg or pelvic injury and it does not cause pain or breathing trouble. If in doubt, keep them flat.
5. **Keep them warm.** Cover with a blanket or coat, put something under them, and shield them from wind and rain. Preventing heat loss matters a lot.
6. **Loosen tight clothing.**
7. **Give nothing by mouth.** No food or drink, because surgery may be needed and vomiting is a risk. Wet their lips if they are thirsty.
8. **If they vomit or bleed from the mouth,** turn them on their side (recovery position), keeping the head and neck in line if you suspect a spinal injury.
9. **If breathing is hard,** let them sit up in whatever position is easiest, unless there is another reason to lie flat.
10. **Stay with them,** talk to them calmly and **monitor** breathing, pulse and alertness. Write down times and changes.
11. **Be ready to start CPR** if they stop breathing and have no pulse.

## Do not
- Do not give food or drink.
- Do not leave them alone.
- Do not let them get cold.
- Do not raise the legs if it may hurt or if a spinal injury is possible.
- Do not move them unnecessarily.

## Types of shock you may hear about
- **Hemorrhagic:** from blood loss.
- **Anaphylactic:** from a severe allergic reaction. (Epinephrine is the treatment. See the allergy lesson.)
- **Septic:** from severe infection.
- **Cardiogenic:** the heart cannot pump enough.
- **Neurogenic:** from spinal injury.

The first aid steps are mostly the same: call 911, treat the cause, lay them down, keep them warm.

## In a disaster
Shock is a leading cause of death when care is delayed. Early bleeding control, keeping people warm and quick transport save lives. Watch everyone who was hurt, even if they say they feel fine.

## Checklist
- Know the signs of shock
- Know the positioning and warming steps
- Blankets and emergency blankets in the kit
- Know not to give food or drink

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.

## Check yourself
? What are signs of shock?
+ Pale, cool, clammy skin, a fast weak pulse and confusion
- A loud cough
- Warm red skin and a slow pulse
- A mild headache only
> Shock needs urgent care.

? How should a person in shock be positioned?
+ Lying down and kept warm, legs raised only if safe
- Sitting in the cold
- Standing
- Walking
> Flat and warm helps blood reach vital organs.

? Should you give food or drink to a person in shock?
+ No
- Only coffee
- Yes
- Only water at once
> They may need surgery and may vomit.

? What is the most important thing to prevent?
- Sleep
- Daylight
- Talking
+ Heat loss
> Keep them warm.
