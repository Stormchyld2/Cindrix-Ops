When the danger has passed, careful recovery protects you from lasting harm.

## Returning home
- **Wait for the official all-clear.**
- Follow the route officials give.
- Be alert for damage: structural, gas, electrical, broken glass, debris.
- **Do not** return to a flooded or damaged house until checked.
- Look for notices about water, food and health.

## Water, food and air
- **Check** whether tap water is safe. Use bottled or stored water until told otherwise.
- **Discard** open food that was exposed to dust or gas. Sealed cans and containers are fine after washing.
- **Wash** fresh produce, or follow official advice.
- **Ventilate** the house once outside air is safe.

## Cleaning
- Use wet methods and disposable cloths.
- Wear gloves, mask and old clothes while cleaning.
- Wash yourself after.
- Keep children away from cleanup.

## Health follow-up
- See a doctor for symptoms, exposure concerns or ongoing worries.
- Register with health departments' exposure registries if offered.
- Keep notes of where you were and symptoms.
- Pay attention to emotional health. Fear, anger, grief and sleep trouble are normal. If they last, ask for help.

## Pets and livestock
Check with vets and agricultural officials.

## Paperwork and money
- Photograph damage and keep receipts.
- File insurance and aid claims through FEMA and your insurer.
- Keep records of cleanup.

## After-action review
Hold a family or group review: What did we know? What did we do? What worked? What do we change?

## Your CBRN binder
- Local hazard map and official alert sources
- Shelter-in-place plan and supply list
- Evacuation routes and destinations
- Contacts: poison control, health department, utilities
- Medical information for each person
- Masks, filters and protective gear list with expiry dates
- Decontamination checklist
- Records of drills and reviews

## Drills
Practice at least yearly: seal a room, put on masks, do a bag-and-wash drill, and use the radio to find instructions.

## Mindset
Most people who follow simple guidance (get inside, stay inside, stay tuned; get clean; get care) come through events well.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
