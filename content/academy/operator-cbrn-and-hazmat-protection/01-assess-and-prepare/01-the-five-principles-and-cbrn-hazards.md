CBRN stands for chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear. Hazmat means hazardous materials such as fuels, gases, acids and industrial chemicals. Most real events are **accidents,** such as a tanker spill, a pipeline leak, a plant fire or an outbreak of illness. Some are deliberate. The protective steps are very similar.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- Learn what hazards are near you: chemical plants, rail lines, highways with tankers, pipelines, nuclear plants, labs, ports, and storage sites.
- Read your county hazard plan and the EPA's facility information.
- Know the wind direction and typical weather patterns.
- Store household chemicals safely.

## 2. Preparedness
- A shelter-in-place room and supplies: plastic sheeting, duct tape, towels, water, radio.
- Evacuation routes and a go-bag.
- Alerts: weather radio and local emergency alerts.
- Masks and basic protective gear where appropriate.
- Training and drills.

## 3. Response
- **Get information** from official alerts.
- **Decide:** shelter in place, or evacuate, as instructed.
- **Reduce exposure:** distance, shielding, time and barriers.
- **Decontaminate** when safe.
- **Get medical care** when needed.

## 4. Mitigation
- Reduce exposure routes: sealed shelter, filtered air, clean water and food.
- Keep contamination out of the shelter.
- Limit time outside.

## 5. Recovery
- Safe return, cleaning, testing, health follow-up, and rebuilding with official guidance.

## Three core ideas
1. **Time:** the less time exposed, the less dose.
2. **Distance:** the farther away, the safer.
3. **Shielding:** put solid material (walls, earth) between you and the hazard.
For chemicals and biological agents, add **barriers:** sealed rooms, clothing, masks.

## Getting official help
Call 911. Tune to local radio, NOAA Weather Radio and official alerts. Follow instructions from emergency managers.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
