Biological hazards are germs and toxins that cause disease. Most are natural outbreaks, such as flu, COVID-19, measles or food-borne illness. A few are deliberate. The protective habits are the same.

## How germs spread
- **Droplets and aerosols** in the air, from coughs, sneezes and talking.
- **Contact** with surfaces or people.
- **Food and water.**
- **Insects and animals.**
- **Body fluids.**

## Layers of defense
1. **Vaccines** where available and recommended.
2. **Hand washing** with soap for 20 seconds, or sanitizer with at least 60 percent alcohol.
3. **Masks:** a well-fitting N95 or similar respirator offers the best protection from airborne spread.
4. **Ventilation:** open windows, use HEPA filters.
5. **Distance** from sick people and avoiding crowds in outbreaks.
6. **Stay home when sick.**
7. **Clean** high-touch surfaces.
8. **Safe food and water** (see the Water and sanitation course).
9. **Insect and animal safety.**

## Prepare for a pandemic or outbreak
- **Supplies for weeks:** food, water, medicines, household items.
- **Health supplies:** thermometer, masks, gloves, soap, sanitizer, cleaning supplies, oral rehydration salts, and a pulse oximeter if wanted.
- **A sick room and a bathroom,** if possible, for a patient.
- **A plan for work, school, childcare and care of elders.**
- **Information:** CDC, WHO and local health departments.

## If someone is sick at home
1. **Separate** them in a room with a window, with their own bathroom if possible.
2. **One caregiver,** ideally not at high risk, with a mask and hand washing.
3. **Fluids and rest.**
4. **Watch for warning signs:** trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, bluish lips, very high fever, inability to drink, worsening symptoms. Get medical care urgently.
5. **Clean** shared surfaces and wash laundry on hot.
6. **Isolate** for the time public health officials advise.
7. **Tell** a doctor or the health department.

## Unusual illness events
If many people become sick, or officials announce a biological incident:
- Listen to health authorities.
- Follow instructions on masks, distancing and places to get medicine or vaccines.
- Shelter at home if told.
- Do not use unproven remedies.

## Mental health and rumors
Outbreaks cause fear. Rely on official information, and check on others by phone.

## Mass care
In large events, health systems may be overwhelmed. Plan for home care of mild illness and know when to go to hospital.

## Waste
Bag soiled tissues and masks, tie the bag and keep it away from pets and children.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
