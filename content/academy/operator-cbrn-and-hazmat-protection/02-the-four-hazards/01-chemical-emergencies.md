Chemicals can be released in industrial accidents, transport crashes, fires, pipeline leaks, or deliberately.

## Types, in plain language
- **Choking gases** harm the lungs (for example chlorine).
- **Blood and nerve agents** harm the body's systems. They can act quickly.
- **Blistering agents** burn skin, eyes and lungs.
- **Irritants** such as tear gas cause burning eyes and skin.
- **Toxic industrial chemicals** such as ammonia, fuels, solvents and pesticides.

## Warning signs
- Many people suddenly ill with the same symptoms.
- Unusual smells, odd clouds or mist, dead birds, insects or plants.
- Strong chemical odors, a hissing noise, or a spill.
- Burning eyes, nose and throat, coughing, trouble breathing, tight chest, headache, dizziness, nausea, watering eyes, blurred vision, skin burns.

## What to do
1. **Get away from the source.** Move upwind and uphill if outside, or into a building and shelter in place if instructed.
2. **Avoid the area.** Do not try to rescue others by walking into the cloud.
3. **Call 911.**
4. **Protect breathing:** cover nose and mouth with a damp cloth if nothing else is available. This is a limited measure.
5. **Remove contaminated clothing** quickly (see decontamination) and wash skin and hair.
6. **Flush eyes** with plenty of water for 15 minutes. Remove contact lenses.
7. **Seek medical care.** Poison control (1-800-222-1222 in the United States) can advise.

## Heavy and light gases
Some gases are heavier than air and settle low, so go **up high.** Others rise, so staying low may help. Follow official guidance, which depends on the substance.

## Do not
- Do not rely on home remedies.
- Do not mix cleaners to fight a spill.
- Do not use water on unknown materials that may react.
- Do not touch unknown liquids or powders.

## At home
- Keep chemicals in labelled containers.
- Use carbon monoxide detectors: CO is a common deadly gas from heaters, generators and engines. Never run a generator inside or near an opening.
- Install smoke alarms and test them.

## Medicines
There are special treatments for some chemical exposures. They are given by medical teams. Do not use medicines from stockpiles unless a doctor or poison control directs you.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
