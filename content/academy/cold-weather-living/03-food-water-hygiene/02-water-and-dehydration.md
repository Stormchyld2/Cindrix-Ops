In the cold you still sweat, and dry cold air takes water from your breath. But layers hide the sweat, so you may not notice you are losing water.

## Signs of dehydration
Dry mouth, headache, weakness, dizziness, cramps, dark urine and poor focus. It can lead to a quick slide into cold injury.

## Prevent it
- Drink often, with meals and between them. **Hot drinks** warm you at the same time.
- Do not rely on thirst.
- Work at a pace that does not soak your clothes.
- Rest is part of the cure.

## Melting snow
Melt snow slowly in a pot with a little water already in it. Never eat snow when you are cold; it costs heat. Boil or treat melted water if its source is uncertain.

## Treat gently
Keep the person warm, loosen tight clothing, give fluids a little at a time, and let them rest. If they do not improve, get medical help.

## Check yourself
? Why can you get dehydrated in the cold without noticing?
+ Sweat soaks into layers and dry air takes moisture, so thirst is easy to miss
- The cold adds water
- You never sweat in winter
- Snow replaces water
> Drink on schedule, not just when thirsty.
? How should you melt snow for water?
+ Slowly, in a pot with a little water in the bottom, then treat the water if unsure
- Eat it to save fuel
- Melt it dry and burn the pot
- Never boil it
> A little water stops the pot scorching and melts the snow faster.
? Should you eat snow when cold?
+ No, it costs body heat
- Yes, as much as you like
- Yes, to save fuel
- Only at night
> Melt it first; eating it chills you.
