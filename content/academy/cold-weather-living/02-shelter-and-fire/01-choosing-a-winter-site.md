A good winter camp, or a good room to shelter in at home, comes down to the same ideas.

- **Wind protection first.** Use trees, a ridge, a building, or a wall of snow. Wind steals heat faster than cold air alone.
- **Hard, dry, level ground**, with water and firewood nearby.
- **Hazards:** avoid the foot of steep slopes where snow can slide, dead trees that can fall, and ice you cannot test.
- **Evergreens** break the wind, give boughs for insulation and burn well. Pines and spruce usually stand on well-drained ground.
- **On wet or swampy ground** build a floor of logs or brush so you are not lying in the cold.
- For a longer stay, **drain the site**: dig a ditch around the shelter so melt and rain run away.

## Track discipline
Footprints show in snow and mud. Walk the same few paths in and out. This also keeps trampled snow where you want it and keeps the camp looking less busy from outside.

## Check yourself
? What is the first thing to look for in a winter site?
+ Protection from the wind
- A view of the whole valley
- The tallest hilltop
- Soft snow
> Wind chill removes heat quickly.
? Why walk the same few paths around camp?
+ It keeps tracks few and makes the site less obvious
- It melts the snow
- It warms the ground
- It makes the camp bigger
> Fewer, repeated paths mean less trace and less trampled snow.
? What can you build on swampy ground to stay off the cold wet earth?
+ A floor of logs or brush
- A bigger fire
- A deeper hole
- Nothing
> A raised floor insulates from the ground.
