A shelter must shed weather, hold heat, allow ventilation, and give a way to dry things out.

## Lean-to (forest)
Lash a crosspiece between two sturdy trees at about chest height. Lean stringers against it every hand's width, then cover with boughs, a tarp or a poncho, laid like roof shingles from bottom to top. Put a log or snow wall at the back and keep the open side facing away from the wind. A long fire along the open side warms the whole length.

## Double lean-to
Two lean-tos face each other about two arm-spans apart with a long fire between them. Leave the middle open at the top so smoke escapes.

## Tarp or poncho tent
Make a ridge line, drape the tarp over it, and stake or weigh the edges. Seal the windward side with snow, sod or boughs.

## Snow shelters
In deep, well-packed snow you can dig a cave, trench or hole. Keep a **vent**, keep your gear dry, and never dig where the snow can slide.

## Insulate below you
The ground steals more heat than the air. Lay boughs, dry grass, or a pad **under** you before anything else.

> Fires inside any shelter need great care. See the next lesson on carbon monoxide.

- A sealed door
- A fire inside
- A flat ceiling
