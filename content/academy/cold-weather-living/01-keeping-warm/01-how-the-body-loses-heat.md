Your body makes heat and loses heat all the time. In the cold, the loss can outrun the making. Shivering is your warning: it burns energy to make heat, and it means **act now** (add a layer, move, eat, get out of the wind).

## Why layers work
Warmth comes from **trapped still air**, not from thickness. Wool, fleece and fur hold thousands of tiny air pockets. Several medium layers hold more warmth than one thick garment, because each layer traps its own blanket of air, and you can add or remove layers as you work.

- **Inner layer:** soft, wicking, airy. Traps warm air next to skin.
- **Middle layers:** wool or fleece. The main insulation.
- **Outer layer:** windproof and water-resistant, so wind cannot push out the warm air.

## Ventilation: treat clothing like a house
Neck, waist, wrists and ankles are the windows. Open them to dump heat when you work; close them when you stop. Opening the neck and waist together lets warm air out without a cold draft over your whole body.

> In freezing weather, taking a layer off before you sweat is as important as putting one on.

- The weight of the cloth
- Tight fit
- Smooth, shiny fabric
> Insulation is trapped air; tight or crushed clothing holds less.
? Shivering means what?

> Shivering is the early warning to add warmth.
