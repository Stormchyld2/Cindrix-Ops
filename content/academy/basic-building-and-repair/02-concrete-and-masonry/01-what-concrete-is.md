Concrete is **cement + water + sand + gravel**. The cement and water make a paste that glues the sand and gravel into artificial stone.

- A general mix is the **1:2:3 method**: 1 part cement, 2 parts sand, 3 parts gravel by volume, plus clean water. Always test a small batch first.
- **Too much water weakens concrete.** Use just enough to make it workable.
- Use clean sand and gravel. Dirt, clay and organic matter keep the paste from sticking.
- Mix until all the stones are coated and the color is even.

## Reinforcement
Concrete is strong when squeezed and weak when pulled or bent. **Steel bars or wire mesh** inside carry the pulling forces. Keep bars off the ground with spacers so concrete surrounds them.

## Forms
Forms hold wet concrete in shape. They must be strong, tight and well braced, because wet concrete is heavy and pushes outward.

## Check yourself
? What happens if you add too much water?
+ The concrete gets weaker
- It gets stronger
- It cures faster
- Nothing
> Extra water leaves voids when it evaporates.
? Why use steel in concrete?
+ Concrete is weak when pulled or bent
- It is decoration
- To make it lighter
- To speed curing
> Steel carries tension.
? What does the 1:2:3 mix mean?
+ 1 part cement, 2 sand, 3 gravel by volume
- 1 cement, 2 water, 3 sand
- 1 gravel, 2 cement, 3 sand
- 1 hour, 2 days, 3 weeks
> It is a general starting recipe; test a small batch.
