## Nail sizes
Nail length is given in **pennies**, written **d**. A 6d nail is 2 inches long; a 10d nail is 3 inches. The rule: a nail should be **at least three times as long as the thickness of the board it holds**, so about two-thirds goes into the second piece.

## Nail types
- **Common**: rough framing.
- **Box**: lighter work and toenailing.
- **Casing and finish**: trim, set below the surface.
- **Roofing**: large heads, sized by length.
- **Ring-shank or spiral**: extra grip for flooring and panels.
- **Galvanized or coated**: for outdoors and damp places.

## Rules that save wood
- Drive nails **slightly toward each other** for more holding power.
- Nails driven **across** the grain hold better than with it.
- **A few well-placed nails beat many crowded together**, which split the board.
- Bend over protruding points so nobody gets hurt.

## Screws
Screws cost more but hold stronger and can be backed out. A pilot hole stops splitting.

? What helps a nail hold better?

- Driving with the grain
