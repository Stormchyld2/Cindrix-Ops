Without a compass you can still find direction, and watching the sky can warn you of bad weather.

## Direction from the sun
- The sun **rises in the east** and **sets in the west** (roughly). At midday in the northern hemisphere it is **south** of you.
- **Shadow stick method:** push a straight stick upright into level ground. Mark the shadow tip. After 15 to 30 minutes mark again. A line drawn through the two marks runs **west to east** (first mark is west).
- **Watch method:** in the northern hemisphere, point the hour hand at the sun. South lies halfway between the hour hand and 12 o'clock. Adjust for daylight saving time.

## Direction from the stars
- **Polaris (the North Star)** is almost directly north in the northern hemisphere. Find it by drawing a line through the two end stars of the Big Dipper's bowl, then extending it about five times the distance between them.
- **In the southern hemisphere,** use the Southern Cross to find south.

## Be careful with folk rules
"Moss grows on the north side of trees" is **not reliable.** Use the sun and stars, and a map.

## Using landmarks and terrain
- **Rivers** flow downhill, toward larger rivers, the sea and often people.
- **Ridges and valleys** guide travel.
- **Roads, power lines and fences** lead to people.
- **Slopes:** snow melts first on south-facing slopes in the northern hemisphere.

## Weather signs
- **Cumulus clouds,** puffy and white: fair weather.
- **Cumulonimbus,** tall, dark, anvil-topped: thunderstorms, lightning and hail.
- **Cirrus** wisps followed by thickening clouds: rain or snow in a day or so.
- **A falling barometer** (or a phone's pressure sensor) means worsening weather.
- **A sudden drop in temperature or a shift in wind** means a front is coming.
- **A ring around the moon** may mean rain within a day.
- **Red sky at night, sailor's delight; red sky in morning, sailors take warning** is a rough guide.
- **Smell of rain,** darkening sky, and a quiet before a storm.

## Lightning safety
- **When thunder roars, go indoors.** A hard-topped vehicle or a sturdy building is safe.
- **If you are caught outside:** get off peaks and ridges, away from open fields, isolated trees, water and metal objects. **Crouch low** on the balls of your feet with feet together, in a low spot. Spread out groups. **Do not lie flat.**
- **Wait 30 minutes** after the last thunder before going back out.

## Flash flood safety
- **Never camp** in a dry wash.
- **If rain falls upstream,** move to high ground.
- **Never cross** moving water deeper than your knees. Six inches of fast water can knock you down.

## Heat and cold
Check forecasts and dress for what could happen, not what is happening.

## Checklist
- Know how to find direction from the sun and stars
- Know the weather warning signs
- Know lightning and flash-flood rules

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.
