A map and compass work without batteries or signal. Learn them before you need them.

## The compass
- The needle points to **magnetic north**. In many places this differs from true north by a number of degrees called **declination**. It is printed on good maps.
- To follow a direction, turn the compass housing to the bearing, hold it level, and turn your body until the needle sits inside the orienting arrow ("red in the shed"). Walk toward the direction-of-travel arrow.

## The map
- Brown contour lines show hills. Close lines mean steep ground. Rings mean a peak or a pit.
- The scale tells you how far a distance on paper is on the ground.
- Match features you can see (a stream, a road, a peak) to the map to find your place.

## Handrails and backstops
- A **handrail** is a long feature to follow, like a stream or ridge.
- A **backstop** is a feature that tells you that you have gone too far.

## Measuring distance
Learn how many of your paces equal 100 meters by walking a measured distance, and count paces as you travel.

> Practice in a park first. A skill you have never used will not work when you are stressed.

## Check yourself
? What do close-together contour lines mean?
+ Steep ground
- A road
- Flat ground
> The closer the lines, the faster the land rises or falls.
? What is declination?
+ The difference between magnetic north and true north
- The slope of a hill
- A compass brand
> Good maps print it. Adjust for it when you take a bearing.
? What is a handrail in navigation?
+ A long feature, such as a stream or ridge, that you follow
- A rail on a bridge
- The edge of the map
> Following a handrail keeps you on track without exact compass work.
