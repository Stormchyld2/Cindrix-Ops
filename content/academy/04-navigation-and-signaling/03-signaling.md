If you are stuck, make it easy to be found. Signals work best when they are **big, contrasting, unusual and repeated.**

## The rule of three
Three of anything is the **international distress signal:** three whistle blasts, three shots, three fires in a triangle, three flashes of light. Three is easy to recognize. If searchers hear it, they know it is not a random sound.

## Sound
- **Whistle:** carries much farther than a shout and takes less energy. Three blasts, pause, repeat. Keep one on your zipper.
- **Shout** only when you hear a likely rescuer.
- **Horns and alarms:** a car horn in sets of three.
- **Banging** on metal or hollow objects.

## Light
- **Signal mirror:** the most powerful. A mirror flash can be seen for miles, even from aircraft. Hold the mirror near your eye, make a "V" with two fingers, and aim the flash at the target by sweeping. Practice.
- **Flashlight:** three flashes at night. Strobe lights on a headlamp.
- **Fire:** three fires in a triangle or a line, 100 feet apart, or one fire kept ready. A **smoky fire:** add green leaves, damp moss or rubber (use caution) in daylight. At night, a bright flame.
- **Glow sticks** and chemical lights.

## Color and shape
- **Bright cloth** (orange, red, yellow) hung high.
- **A space blanket,** shiny side out, flashes in the sun.
- **Ground-to-air signals:** make large symbols at least **10 feet** high, in an open area. Use contrasting materials (rocks, logs, clothing, snow trampled in lines):
  - **V:** need assistance
  - **X:** need medical help
  - **N** or an arrow: indicate direction traveled
  - **SOS** or a big **X** can be seen from the air
- **Use straight lines** and right angles, because nature has few.
- **Put signals in the open,** where aircraft can see.

## Electronic
- **Cell phone:** try to call or text 911 even with weak coverage. Texts need less signal. Go to high ground. Save the battery by using airplane mode in between and low brightness. Share your location (the phone's map app, or "emergency SOS").
- **Personal locator beacon (PLB) or satellite messenger:** the most reliable for remote travel. Register it. Follow instructions.
- **Radio:** emergency channels (see the Radio Licenses course).
- **Emergency SOS via satellite** on some phones.

## When aircraft or searchers approach
- **Stand in the open,** wave both arms overhead (a "Y" shape means yes, I need help).
- **Use your mirror** and bright items.
- **Stay near** your signals.
- **Be ready,** with a fire or signal ready to light at the sound of a plane.
- **Do not run** toward helicopter rotors. Wait for the crew to signal.

## Make a plan to be found
- **Leave a trip plan** with someone: where, route, when back, car description.
- **Stay near your car** or the last known point if you can.
- **Leave notes** and signs if you must move.
- **Mark** your shelter and route with brightly colored items.

> Big, bright, unusual, repeated, and in threes.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.
