[video] Trip plans, phones and GPS

Phones and GPS units are powerful, but they fail. Use them, and carry backups.

## A trip plan
Before you leave, tell a responsible person:
- **Where** you are going and your route.
- **When** you will be back, and when to call for help if you are not.
- **Who** is with you.
- **Vehicle** description and where it is parked.
- **Gear and clothing** you are carrying.
- **Medical conditions** and medicines.
- **Phone numbers.**
Leave the plan in writing, and **tell them when you return.**

## Phone navigation
- **Download offline maps** before you go. Coverage drops quickly.
- **Charge fully** and carry a **power bank** and cable.
- **Use airplane mode** to save the battery. GPS still works.
- **Keep the phone warm** (inside a pocket) in the cold. Batteries drain fast when cold.
- **Use a waterproof case.**
- **Set a "turnaround time"** and keep to it.
- **Share your live location** with someone you trust.
- **Emergency SOS:** know how it works on your phone, including satellite messaging on newer models.

## Dedicated GPS and satellite devices
- **Handheld GPS:** long battery life, rugged, and designed for outdoors.
- **Satellite messengers and PLBs:** send SOS and messages from anywhere. Register and test them.
- **Carry spare batteries.**

## Backups
- **A paper map and a compass.**
- **Know your route** and landmarks. Do not rely only on a blue dot.
- **Check your position** often.

## Navigation tips
- **Look at the map** before you start, and note big features (rivers, ridges, roads) that tell you when you are off course.
- **Aim off** (aim to one side of a target on a line feature, so you know which way to turn).
- **Count time and pace.**
- **Turn around** if unsure.
- **Stop** and recheck when the terrain does not match.

## Hiking in a group
- **Keep together,** and keep the slowest in front.
- **Use a sweep** (a person at the back).
- **Stop at junctions** to regroup.
- **Count** heads at every stop.

## The ten essentials
1. Navigation (map, compass, GPS)
2. Headlamp, with spare batteries
3. Sun protection
4. First aid kit
5. Knife and repair kit
6. Fire starter
7. Shelter (emergency bivy or space blanket)
8. Extra food
9. Extra water and a way to treat it
10. Extra clothes

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.

## The plan is your best rescue tool
The most effective safety device is a person who knows where you are. Write a trip plan, give it to someone responsible and agree on when they should call for help.

## What a good plan holds
- Names and phone numbers of everyone on the trip.
- Vehicle make, color and license plate and where it is parked.
- Route, campsites and expected times.
- Gear and the medical needs of the group.
- A check-in time and what to do if you miss it.
- Emergency contacts, and the local agency to call.

## Phone tips
- Download offline maps before you go.
- Keep the phone in airplane mode to save battery, turning it on to check position.
- Carry a power bank and a short cable.
- Learn how to share your location and how to drop a pin.
- Do not rely on signal. Mountains and canyons kill it.

## The ten essentials
Navigation (map and compass), sun protection, insulation, illumination, first aid, fire, repair tools, nutrition, hydration and emergency shelter. Pack them every time, even for a short day hike.

## Group rules
Keep the group together, set the pace by the slowest member, count heads at junctions and agree on turnaround times. Agree on signals and what to do if separated.
