When the shaking starts, you have seconds. This is the one action that protects you.

## The three actions
1. **DROP** to your hands and knees. This keeps you from being knocked down, and lets you crawl to cover.
2. **COVER** your head and neck with one arm and hand. If a sturdy table or desk is nearby, crawl under it. If not, crawl next to an interior wall away from windows. Stay low and cover your head and neck with your arms.
3. **HOLD ON** to your shelter with one hand, and be ready to move with it if it shifts. If you have no shelter, hold your head and neck with both hands.

Stay there **until the shaking stops.**

## Do not
- **Do not run outside.** Falling debris, glass and facades kill people at exits.
- **Do not stand in a doorway.** It is not stronger than other parts of a modern building, and it gives no protection from flying objects.
- **Do not move more than 5 to 7 feet** before you get down.
- **Do not use elevators.**
- **Do not stand near windows, mirrors, tall furniture, hanging objects, exterior walls, or heavy cabinets.**
- **Do not "triangle of life."** The idea of lying beside furniture to be crushed into a void is not supported by research and is not recommended by emergency agencies.

## Where you are
### In bed
Stay there. **Lie face down** and cover your head and neck with a pillow. Hold on. Do not get out of bed during shaking.

### Seated and unable to get down
If you cannot reach the floor, **bend forward,** cover your head with your arms and hold your neck with your hands.

### Wheelchair, walker or cane
**Lock your wheels,** if you use a wheelchair. **Bend forward** to protect your head and neck, and hold on. If you use a walker or cane, get as low as possible and protect your head. See earthquakecountry.org/disability for more.

### Kitchen
Get away from the stove, sinks, windows and cabinets with glass. Drop and cover near an interior wall or under a table.

### High-rise building
Drop, cover and hold on, and stay **away from windows.** Expect **sprinklers** and **alarms** to go off. Do not use elevators. Do not rush to the stairs.

### In a store
Drop next to a **shopping cart,** under clothing racks, or in the first level of warehouse racks. Stay away from items that can fall.

### In a theater, stadium or classroom
Drop in front of your seat or **bend over as far as possible** and cover your head and neck. Walk out slowly after the shaking stops, watching for falling debris.

### Outside
Move to a **clear area** away from buildings, trees, signs, utility lines and vehicles, then drop, cover and hold on. Falling objects are the main danger.

### Driving
**Pull over to a clear place,** away from buildings, trees, overpasses and utility lines. **Stop and set the brake.** Stay inside until the shaking stops. Then drive carefully, watching for road damage. If a power line falls on the car, **stay inside** until trained help arrives.

### On a bridge or under an overpass
If you are on it, keep driving if safe to get off, or stop if not. Do not stop on or under a bridge, overpass or tunnel if you can avoid it.

### Near the shore
After the shaking stops, **go to high ground or inland immediately,** on foot. Do not wait for a warning.

### Near a steep slope
Watch for **landslides** and rockfalls. Move away from the base of slopes.

### Below a dam
Follow the standard steps. Know your flood zone and evacuation route.

## Prepare your body
- **Drill:** practice until it is automatic.
- **Teach children** as a game, with a clear rule: "Drop, Cover, Hold On."
- **Practice in the dark.**

## After the shaking stops
Wait a moment, then look around before you move. Go on to the next lesson.

## Checklist
- Everyone knows the three actions
- Practiced at home and at work
- Know the best spot in each room
- Know what to do in a car, in bed, and outdoors
- Know you should not run outside or stand in a doorway

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
