The shaking lasts a minute. Recovery lasts months or years. Getting through it takes people.

## Reconnect
- **Reach out** to family, friends and neighbors.
- **Let your out-of-area contact know** where you are and how you are.
- **Check in** with those who may be alone.
- **Join community efforts:** cleanups, food, shelter, care.
- **Share information.**

## Restore daily life
- **Return to routines** as soon as it is safe. Work, school, meals and sleep help people recover.
- **Re-open the household plan** and rework it using what you learned.
- **Repair carefully:** get a **licensed engineer** to inspect structural damage before you move back in.
- **Hire licensed contractors** and watch for scams.
- **Keep records:** photos, receipts and notes.
- **Apply for aid:** insurance first, then FEMA if a disaster is declared, state and local programs, and nonprofit help.

## Do an after-action review
Within a few weeks, sit down with your household or group:
- **What worked?**
- **What did not?**
- **What did we lack?**
- **What do we change?**
- Update your supplies and plan. See the Admin course for how to run an after-action review.

## Mitigate for next time
- **Retrofit the home.**
- **Secure contents** better.
- **Review insurance.**
- **Improve supplies.**
- **Practice more.**

## Care for each other
- **Check on people's mental health** for weeks and months after.
- **Be patient.** Some people recover slowly.
- **Seek help** if you or someone else has trouble sleeping, panics, or cannot function.

## Help your community recover
- **Volunteer** with recognized groups. Avoid showing up with no coordination.
- **Donate** money to established organizations, because cash is the most flexible.
- **Support local businesses** as they reopen.
- **Take CERT or Red Cross training** to be more useful in the future.

## Checklist
- Contacted family and neighbors
- Documented damage and contacted insurers
- Structural inspection before moving back in
- After-action review held
- Plan and supplies updated

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
