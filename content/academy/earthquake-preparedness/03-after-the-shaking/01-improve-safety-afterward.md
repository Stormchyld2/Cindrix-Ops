When the shaking stops, the danger is not over. This is the order to think in.

## First minutes
1. **Stay calm and wait.** Count to 60, because aftershocks are likely. Look around before moving.
2. **Check yourself for injury.** Then help others.
3. **Put on sturdy shoes** and protect your hands.
4. **Expect aftershocks.** If one comes, drop, cover and hold on again.
5. **Check for hazards:** fire, gas, smell, downed lines, broken glass, damaged walls.
6. **Text** rather than call to let your contact know you are safe.

## Help the injured
- **Check for life threats first:** severe bleeding, no breathing. See the Medical and CPR courses.
- **Do not move** seriously injured people unless there is danger.
- **Call 911 for life-threatening needs only.** Lines will be busy. If you cannot get through, keep trying or text.
- **Use your kit.**

## Fire
Fires cause a large share of earthquake damage.
- **Put out small fires** with an extinguisher if you can do it safely. If not, get out.
- **Smell gas** (rotten egg), hear hissing, or see damage to the gas line:
  1. **Open windows and doors.**
  2. **Do not use switches,** lighters, matches or phones inside.
  3. **Get everyone out.**
  4. **Shut off the gas at the main valve outside** if you can do it safely, using a wrench. Turn the handle a quarter turn so it is crosswise to the pipe.
  5. **Call the gas company or 911** from a safe distance. **Do not turn it back on yourself.**
- **Electric hazards:** if you see sparks or frayed wires, shut off the main breaker if you can do it safely, and stay out of water.
- **Keep clear** of downed power lines, and anything touching them.

## Check your home
- **Look for damage** outside first: cracks in the foundation or walls, tilting, a leaning chimney, shifted frames.
- **Do not enter** a building that looks unsafe, leans, or has major damage.
- **Inside,** watch for fallen items, broken glass, damaged stairs and ceilings.
- **Check the chimney** from a distance.
- **Check water and sewer lines.** Shut off the water main if there are leaks. Do not use toilets if sewer lines may be broken, as they may back up. Use a bucket toilet.
- **Cleaning spills:** clean up medicines, bleach and other chemicals and flammable liquids right away, with care.
- **Open cabinets carefully,** because items may fall.
- **Wear gloves, a mask and goggles** when cleaning up.
- **Take photos** for insurance before cleanup.

## Water and food
- **Do not drink** tap water until told it is safe, if lines may be damaged.
- **Use stored water.** Your water heater tank is a source.
- **Open the fridge** as little as possible. Eat perishables first.

## Tsunami
If you are near the coast and the shaking was strong or long, **move to high ground now.**

## Landslides and ground failure
Stay away from slopes, cliffs and damaged hillsides. Watch for **cracks** and **sinkholes** and keep children away.

## Information
- **Listen to a battery radio** for official instructions.
- **Check emergency alerts** on your phone.
- **Follow evacuation orders.**

## If you are trapped
- **Do not light a match.**
- **Do not move about** or kick up dust.
- **Cover your mouth** with a cloth.
- **Tap on a pipe or wall,** or use a whistle, so rescuers can find you. **Shout only as a last resort,** because shouting makes you inhale dust.

## If you must leave
- **Take your go bag,** documents and medicine.
- **Leave a note** where you have gone.
- **Lock up,** if safe.
- **Go to your meeting place.**
- **Avoid damaged roads,** bridges and downed lines.

## Checklist
- Know the order: protect yourself, check injuries, check hazards
- Know the gas shutoff steps
- Know when not to enter a building
- Know how to signal if trapped

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
