Build your kit in phases so you are able to talk at every step, and so each purchase makes you more capable than the one before.

## What to look for when you shop
Every radio you buy should have three things:

1. **FCC Part 95E certification.** This is the FCC's approval for GMRS radios, and it is what makes transmitting on GMRS legal.
2. **A removable antenna.** The antenna is one of the biggest factors in how well your radio performs, and the cheapest upgrade you can make. A fixed antenna cannot be improved.
3. **Repeater capability.** The radio should let you set separate transmit and receive frequencies and a tone, so you can use local repeaters.

## Phase 1: Get on the air
- **Handheld radio (HT).** One per adult and one per older child. A handheld is a radio you carry. Choose a certified GMRS model with a removable antenna and a good battery.
- **A spare battery per radio.** A radio with a dead battery is a brick.
- **A printed channel card.** Write your channels, repeaters and tones on a card and laminate it.

## Phase 2: Make it better
- **A speaker-microphone.** A small microphone that clips to your collar. It lets you talk with the radio in your pack or on your belt.
- **A single earbud.** For privacy, and so you can listen when noise is a problem.
- **An upgraded antenna.** See the next lesson.

## Phase 3: Stay on the air
- **A way to charge.** A solar charger, a car charger, a small power station, or a power bank that charges your radio's battery. A radio is only as good as its power.
- **A case of AA batteries.** Many radios accept a case that holds AA batteries, which is a great fallback if the main pack is dead.
- **A waterproof bag.** A dry bag or a heavy zip bag keeps the radio and cables dry.
- **A notebook and pencils.** For taking messages. Pens fail in the cold. Pencils work.

## Phase 4: Reach farther
- **A mobile radio for your vehicle.** A radio wired to the car's battery with a roof antenna can reach much farther than a handheld.
- **A base station at home.** A radio on a good outdoor antenna, ideally with a backup power source, gives your family a place to call.
- **A scanner or receiver.** To listen to weather, local agencies and other radio traffic. Listening does not need a license.

## Test it before you need it
1. Charge every radio and every spare battery.
2. Program the same channels into every radio in the family.
3. Take two radios a mile apart and talk. Note the places where the signal fades.
4. Try the earbud and microphone so you know how to use them.
5. Write down what you learned and fix it.

## Checklist
- Handheld radios, one per adult and older child
- A spare battery for each radio
- A speaker-microphone
- A single earbud
- An upgraded antenna and the right adapter
- A charging method that works without the grid
- A case for AA batteries, if your radio supports one
- A laminated channel card
- A notebook and pencils in a waterproof bag
- A scanner or receiver for listening

> A kit you have never used is a guess. Take it out and use it before you need it.

## Check yourself
? What does FCC Part 95E certification mean for a GMRS radio?
- The radio has a better battery
+ It is the FCC approval that makes transmitting on GMRS legal
- The radio can use any frequency
- The radio is waterproof
> Part 95E is the certification for GMRS radios.

? Why should you choose a radio with a removable antenna?
- It is required by law
+ So you can upgrade it to improve range
- So you can use the radio without an antenna
- So it fits in smaller bags
> The antenna is one of the biggest factors in range, and a removable one can be upgraded.

? Why do pencils beat pens in a radio kit?
- They are cheaper
- They are required for ham radio
- They are easier to see
+ They keep working in the cold and when wet
> Many pens fail in cold or wet conditions. Pencils work.

? What is the right time to test your kit?
- Only when you buy it
- During the first emergency
+ Before you need it, with family radios on the same channels
- Never, since the box says it works
> Test it ahead of time so you find the problems when there is no pressure.
