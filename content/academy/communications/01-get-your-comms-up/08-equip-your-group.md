Your goal: every member of the group has the right equipment, training and license to take part in the group's PACE plan.

## Start with a GMRS radio system
We suggest the group begins with GMRS because it is:

- **Inexpensive.** Certified handhelds cost little.
- **Easy to learn and use.** There is no exam and the radios are simple.
- **Able to use repeaters.** Repeaters extend your range far beyond what a handheld can do alone.

## What every member should be able to do
- Turn the radio on, change channels and set the volume and squelch.
- Switch between high and low power.
- Use the microphone and earpiece so conversations stay private.
- Transmit, say their callsign and give a clear short message.
- Switch to a repeater channel and back to a direct (simplex) channel.
- Replace a battery and swap an antenna.

## Matching radios across the group
- Every radio in the group has the same list of channels programmed in the same order.
- Everyone has a laminated copy of the channel card.
- Every radio is labeled with the owner's name and callsign.
- Each radio has a spare battery.

## What about inexpensive imported radios?
Many groups own low-cost handhelds like the Baofeng UV-5R. They are popular because they are cheap and easy to program. You need to know where they fit:

- They are **not certified** for GMRS or FRS. The FCC does not allow uncertified radios to transmit on those channels.
- A **licensed ham operator** may use them on the amateur bands. They work well as a ham radio.
- Anyone can **listen** on any frequency, with no license.
- In a **real emergency where life is at stake**, the FCC rules recognize the need to communicate. That is a last resort, not a plan, and it is not a license to test or practice on the wrong channels.

The safe plan: buy certified GMRS radios for GMRS, and keep the imported handhelds for listening and for ham use.

## Programming safely
Use a free programming program such as CHIRP and the right cable to load channels into every radio the same way. See the Radio Licenses course for the CHIRP lesson. Keep one master file for the group and give a copy of it to every member.

## Group radio rules
1. Agree which channel is the **calling channel** and which are **working channels**.
2. Use the same words and procedures, taught in the third module.
3. Keep calls short, so channels are free for urgent traffic.
4. Use a callsign or an agreed code name on every call.
5. Do not discuss sensitive details on an open channel. Use agreed code words.

## Checklist
- Every member has a GMRS-certified radio, a spare battery and an upgraded antenna.
- Every member can program and operate the radio.
- Every radio carries the same channel plan.
- The group has one master programming file.
- Every member has the channel card.
- Every member knows the rules for their license.

> The group is only as strong as its least-practiced radio operator. Train together.
