A plan you have not tested is a guess. Most communication plans fail not because the idea is wrong but because nobody practiced the lower levels, or because two levels turned out to share one weak point.

## Why testing matters
Practice reinforces skills, shows gaps and builds confidence. Emergency planners recommend training the people who will operate the Alternate, Contingency and Emergency methods regularly, and practicing cascading failures, where one level fails after another. Groups that do this tend to be more resilient in odd situations.

## Three kinds of test
1. **A walk-through (talk it out).** Sit together with the plan and ask "what if" questions. What if the Comms Specialist is away? What if the repeater is down? What if two people are hurt? It costs nothing and finds many gaps.
2. **A drill (do one part).** Practice a single level. For example, turn off the Wi-Fi and cell data on every phone and run the Alternate. Or hold a radio check at the Contingency times.
3. **A full exercise (do the whole thing).** Start at Primary. The leader announces that Primary has failed, then Alternate has failed, then Contingency, and the group carries out each step in turn.

## Run a simple exercise
1. **Choose a date and tell everyone.** Plan for 60 to 90 minutes.
2. **Set the scene.** "A storm has knocked out cell service and power. We are all in different places."
3. **Start at Primary.** Everyone tries to reach the Comms Specialist by call and text. Record who got through and how long it took.
4. **Fail Primary.** The leader announces it has failed. Everyone moves to Alternate, using Wi-Fi chat and email.
5. **Fail Alternate.** Everyone moves to Contingency. Turn on the radios and listen at the calling channel. The CS makes a call and records who answers.
6. **Fail Contingency.** Everyone goes to the Emergency meeting place, or marks on paper that they would.
7. **Debrief.** Gather and talk about what went well and what did not.

## What to record
- How long each level took.
- Who could not be reached.
- Which radios had weak batteries or wrong channels.
- Which instructions were unclear.
- What each person would change.

## After-action review
Ask four questions: What did we expect to happen? What actually happened? Why was there a difference? What will we change? Write down each change and give it to someone to do by a date.

## Keep it current
- Review the plan every quarter and after any change in the group.
- Update phone numbers, callsigns, channels and batteries.
- Re-run the exercise at least twice a year.
- Test a case where the Comms Specialist is not available.

## Checklist
- Hold a walk-through of the plan.
- Run one drill on each level.
- Run a full exercise twice a year.
- Debrief after every test and write down the changes.
- Update the plan and send the new copy to every member.

> A failed test is a good result. It showed you the problem before an emergency did.
