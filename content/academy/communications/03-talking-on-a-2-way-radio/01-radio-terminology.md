The shared vocabulary that makes radio communication clear and unambiguous.

Demonstrate proper voice procedures on radio. Sloppy procedure creates confusion — exactly what you cannot afford in an emergency.

1. Roger That: The quickest way of saying you comprehend what the other person said. The word Roger goes back to Morse code where "R" stands for received — meaning the message is understood.
2. Mayday: An international distress signal indicating a transport or vehicle is in a life-threatening emergency. Coined in the 1920s from the French "M'aidez" — meaning "come help me." Only use it when a life is at stake.
3. Over: Used at the end of a message to signify that you are finished speaking and a response is expected.
4. Out: Indicates you are signing off. The conversation is complete and no response is expected. Never say "Over and Out" — they contradict each other.
5. Read / Copy: Used as a query to ask if you are being understood: "Do you copy?" or "Do you read me?"
6. Wilco: Short for "will comply" — indicates that you heard the instruction and will carry it out.
