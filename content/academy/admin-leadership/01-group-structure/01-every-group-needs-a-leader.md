Every group needs a leader. We are not talking about a tin-pot dictator who goes around barking orders. We are talking about someone who takes responsibility for getting things done and for the people in the group.

Leading a mutual assistance group or community preparedness team is a lot of work. You will spend time creating training sessions, coordinating meet-ups and managing relationships and expectations. It takes wisdom, patience and perseverance. For many people it becomes a calling.

## What it takes to lead
If you have the desire to start a group, you already meet the first test: **initiative.** Do not wait for someone else to do it. Be the change you want to see in your community.

The Army describes a leader by what the leader **is**, what the leader **knows** and what the leader **does.** You can use the same three ideas.

**Character: who you are.**
- **Honesty and integrity.** You do what you say. People follow people they trust.
- **Humility.** You admit mistakes and learn from them.
- **Courage.** You make hard calls and speak up when something is wrong.

**Presence: how you come across.**
- **Calm.** A steady leader steadies a group.
- **Confidence without ego.** You are sure of the mission and open to other views.
- **Fitness and readiness.** You lead by example.

**Intellect: how you think.**
- **Sound judgment.** You gather facts before deciding.
- **Willingness to learn.** You study your area, your skills and your people.
- **Good communication.** You say clearly what is needed.

And what a leader **does:**

1. **Leads.** Sets the direction and the standards, and builds trust.
2. **Develops.** Trains others, coaches them and prepares successors.
3. **Achieves.** Gets results by planning, organizing and following through.

## The jobs of a group leader
- Keep the group's **purpose** clear.
- Make and keep a **plan.**
- Run the **meetings** and keep to the rhythm.
- Make sure **training** happens.
- Look after **people:** know their strengths and their limits.
- Make sure **communication** works.
- Settle **disagreements** fairly.
- **Delegate** so the load is shared.
- Name and train **backups.**

## Leadership is serving
A group leader does not outrank the others in life. The leader is the person who carries the extra work so the group can function. Good leaders ask, "What do you need from me?" more often than they give orders.

## Name a deputy
Every leader needs a backup who can lead when the leader is away, hurt or overwhelmed. Name one early and keep that person in the loop.

## Checklist
- Write your group's leader and deputy at the top of your group plan.
- Write down the leader's jobs from the list above and decide who owns each.
- Decide how a new leader is chosen and when.
- Ask each member what they would like the leader to do better.

> Leaders serve. The best leaders make the group stronger, not themselves bigger.
