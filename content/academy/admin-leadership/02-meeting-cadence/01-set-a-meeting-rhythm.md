Readiness drifts without a rhythm. People get busy, enthusiasm fades and the plan sits in a binder. A regular meeting rhythm turns "we should get together" into something that happens.

## A rhythm that works
Use different kinds of meetings for different purposes.

- **Weekly check-in (5 to 15 minutes).** A quick radio check or group message. Who is OK, anything urgent, a task of the week. Keeps people connected.
- **Monthly meeting (1 to 2 hours).** The main business meeting. Reviews actions, plans the next month, includes a short training.
- **Monthly or bi-monthly training day (2 to 4 hours).** Hands-on practice: first aid, radio, navigation, fire, water.
- **Quarterly drill or exercise (half a day).** Test the plan: a PACE exercise, a bug-out drill, a fire drill, a tabletop scenario.
- **Annual readiness review (a day).** A full review of the area study, the plan, the roster, the supplies and the training record.

Write the schedule down for the whole year and put it on everyone's calendar in this program.

## Set fixed times
- Pick a **fixed pattern**, such as "first Saturday of each month at 10 a.m." People plan better around a pattern.
- Choose a **regular place** and a backup.
- Share the schedule at least a month ahead.
- Cancel as rarely as you can. Skipped meetings kill momentum.
- Offer **several options** for training sessions, because not everyone can attend every one.

## Make it worth attending
People attend meetings that are useful and short. People skip meetings that are boring or disorganized.

- Start and end on time.
- Have a clear agenda.
- Include something to **do or learn** every time.
- Share the work. Different members run different parts.
- Include time for people to connect. Food helps.
- Recognize contributions.

## Track attendance and accountability
- Keep a simple record of who attended.
- Follow up kindly with those who miss. Ask if something is getting in the way.
- Keep a record of training completed for each member (see the Academy sign-off).

## Keep it flexible
Life happens. A group that can meet in person, by phone or by radio keeps its rhythm in different circumstances. Set a minimum: even a ten-minute call keeps things going.

## Checklist
- Regular meeting cadence set and on the Calendar for the year
- Standard agenda documented
- Attendance and accountability tracked
- Action items recorded
- Action item follow-up reviewed at each meeting
- Annual readiness review scheduled

> A meeting that happens on a rhythm beats a perfect meeting that never happens.
