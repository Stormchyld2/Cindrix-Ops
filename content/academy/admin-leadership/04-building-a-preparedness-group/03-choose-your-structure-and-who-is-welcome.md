Before you invite people, decide what kind of group you are building and who it is for.

## Ideas for group types
- **Family and friends group.** Small, close and trusted. Often the first step.
- **CERT team.** A Community Emergency Response Team, trained and organized with your local emergency management office. Good for learning real skills and working with officials.
- **Mutual assistance group.** Neighbors who agree to help each other in an emergency.
- **Neighborhood preparedness team.** A block or street working on shared plans.
- **Church or community group preparedness team.** Uses an existing community and a meeting place.
- **Workplace group.** Prepares staff and colleagues.
- **Homesteading or skills group.** Shares gardening, food and building skills.
- **Radio or communication group.** A club or net built around radios.

Pick the one that fits your community. You can combine them over time.

## Decide your purpose
Write one sentence:

> "We are a group of [who] who train together and help each other [how] in [what situations]."

Example: "We are a group of neighbors on Maple Street who train together and help each other with water, first aid and communications during outages and storms."

## Who is welcome?
Think carefully and early.

- **Friends and family.** Will you include them? If you do not, they will still turn up in a crisis. If you do, include them early in your plans and training.
- **Neighbors.** Those within walking distance are the most valuable.
- **People with needs.** Older people, people with disabilities and families with small children can be strong members with the right support, and are often the ones who need a plan most.
- **People with skills.** Medical, trades, communications, farming, teaching and cooking.
- **Anyone who disagrees with the group's values or rules** may not be a fit.

Be fair, welcoming and clear. A group is not for everyone, but it should not be built on prejudice. Decide membership by **commitment, skills, reliability and proximity,** and treat everyone with respect.

## Write a short group agreement
Put these in a page everyone reads and agrees to:

1. **Purpose.** What we do and why.
2. **Values.** For example: honesty, respect, safety, helping others, privacy.
3. **Expectations.** Attend meetings, train, keep your gear ready, share the load.
4. **How we decide.** (See the decision lesson.)
5. **Confidentiality.** What we do not share outside the group.
6. **Safety rules.** Safe handling of tools and weapons, if relevant.
7. **What happens if someone cannot keep the agreement.**
8. **How members join and leave.**

Review it once a year.

## Proximity matters
A well-known expert said: **"If you cannot see someone from your front porch, they really are not able to help you when trouble starts."** You may be cut off from the help you planned on. Build your core group from people who live close.

## Checklist
- Choose the type of group you are building.
- Write a one-sentence purpose.
- List who is welcome and why.
- Write a one-page group agreement.
- List the households near you who could join.

> A clear purpose and a short agreement save a lot of trouble later.
