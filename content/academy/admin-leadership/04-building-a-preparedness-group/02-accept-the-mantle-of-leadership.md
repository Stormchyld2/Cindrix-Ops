**Your mission, should you choose to accept it.** Accept that you will need to be the leader, at least at first. Every group starts with just **you and one other person.**

You do not have to lead forever. Or maybe you will. But someone has to get the ball rolling. It will not roll on its own.

## Take action
- **Decide.** Say to yourself, "I will start this."
- **Tell one person** and ask them to join you.
- **Set the first date** for a meet-up.
- **Do the next small step.**

## Be confident in your cause
You are on a worthy mission: helping people become **resilient** and **self-reliant.**

- **Resilient** means able to withstand or recover quickly from difficult conditions, stress or change. It means having the flexibility to bounce back from adversity.
- **Self-reliant** means depending on your own judgment, abilities and resources to manage your life, solve problems and achieve goals.

You want a group of **generally self-reliant** people who join together to make a powerful team.

## Nobody is perfect
Everyone has weak areas. A group covers each person's weaknesses with another person's strengths. Do not hide what you do not know. Say it, and find the person who does.

## Common leader pitfalls
- **Doing everything yourself.** You will burn out, and the group will depend on you. Delegate.
- **Perfectionism.** A good plan today beats a perfect plan next year.
- **Taking criticism personally.** Listen, thank people, and change what makes sense.
- **Avoiding hard conversations.** Small problems become big ones. Speak early, kindly and clearly.
- **Ego.** The group is not about you.
- **Neglecting family.** Your household comes first. A leader who burns out their family is not helping anyone.

## Look after yourself
Leading a group is hard work. To keep going:

- **Set limits.** Decide how much time you can give.
- **Share the load.** Name a deputy and role leads.
- **Rest.** Take breaks and holidays.
- **Celebrate wins.** Notice progress.
- **Find support.** Talk with other leaders.
- **Keep learning.** Read, attend training and ask for feedback.

## Prepare to hand over
Good leaders train their successor from the start. When the time comes, step back and let someone else lead. A group that can outlast its founder is a success.

## Checklist
- Write your reasons for leading.
- Pick a deputy and talk to them.
- Decide how many hours a week you can give.
- Choose three people to ask to take on a role.
- Schedule the first small meeting.

> Someone has to start. It might as well be you.
