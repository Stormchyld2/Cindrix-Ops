Starting a group is hard. **Keeping it going** is harder. Most groups fade within a year or two. The ones that last plan for change from the start.

## Why groups fade
- **The leader burns out.**
- **Meetings become boring or pointless.**
- **No clear next step.**
- **People move, change jobs or lose interest.**
- **Conflict goes unresolved.**
- **One person does all the work.**
- **The group drifts from its purpose.**

## Keep it fresh
- **Vary the activities.** Mix training, practice, social events and community service.
- **Set goals** each quarter and celebrate when you reach them.
- **Learn new skills** and bring in guest instructors.
- **Do real things:** join a community drill, help at a local event, volunteer with the fire department's open day.
- **Rotate roles** so people grow and no one burns out.
- **Hold social events.** Friendship keeps people coming.
- **Invite feedback** and act on it.

## Share the load
- **Write down** how the group works: the plan, roles, schedule and procedures.
- **Delegate jobs** and give them to people to own.
- **Avoid single points of failure.** Each important role has a backup.
- **Train new leaders,** with real tasks and responsibility.

## Succession planning
Plan for the time when the leader steps back.

1. **Name a deputy** from the start.
2. **Share leadership tasks** with the deputy so they gain experience.
3. **Write down** the leader's duties, passwords, contacts and files.
4. **Agree how a new leader is chosen:** vote, appointment, or rotation.
5. **Hold a proper handover:** meeting, documents and introductions.
6. **The old leader stays available** as an advisor for a while, but lets go of the lead.

## Renew the purpose
Once a year, ask:

- Why do we exist?
- Is this still what we need?
- What is working? What is not?
- What do members want?
- What should we start, stop or change?

Adjust the group to match.

## Handle growth and change
- **Growth:** split into teams, add roles, strengthen the structure.
- **Shrinkage:** merge with a nearby group, or reduce your goals and keep going.
- **A crisis:** use the structure you have built. After it, hold an AAR and rest.

## Look after the members
Check in on people. Notice who is struggling. Offer help. Celebrate birthdays, new babies and milestones. A group that cares about its members lasts.

## Know when to end
Sometimes a group has done its job. Close it with thanks, return equipment, store records and keep friendships.

## Checklist
- Write a one-page "how our group works" guide.
- Name and train a deputy.
- Set quarterly goals and celebrate them.
- Plan a social event each quarter.
- Hold a yearly "purpose and priorities" review.
- Write down the leader's duties and contacts for handover.

> A group that is built to outlast its founder can last for years.
