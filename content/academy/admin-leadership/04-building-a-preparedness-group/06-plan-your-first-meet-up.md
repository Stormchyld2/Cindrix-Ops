If you hold a group meet-up, **write out an agenda.** Plan either focused activities that let people learn about each other, or a simple training that everyone can benefit from.

## Ideas for the first meet-up
Choose something useful, friendly and not scary.

- **Stop the Bleed** basics.
- **Introduction to GMRS radios.**
- **How to build an emergency kit for your vehicle.**
- **What is in a go-bag?** Bring yours and compare.
- **Walk-through of a home fire escape plan.**
- **A show-and-tell of favorite preparedness items.**
- **An introduction to your area's hazards,** using public maps.
- **A simple cooking demonstration** with stored food and a camp stove.

## A sample agenda (two hours)
1. **Welcome and introductions (15 minutes).** Names, where they live, one thing they would like to learn.
2. **Why we are here (10 minutes).** The purpose in one sentence.
3. **Training or activity (45 minutes).** Hands-on and useful.
4. **Break and food (15 minutes).**
5. **Discussion (20 minutes).** What do people want from the group? What worries them?
6. **Next steps (10 minutes).** Who will do what, and when the next meeting is.
7. **Close.**

## Ideas for locations
- Someone's house.
- A neighborhood barbecue or picnic area.
- A park pavilion.
- The back room of a local restaurant.
- A local grange or community hall.
- A fire station public classroom.
- A library meeting room.
- A church hall.

Choose a place that is easy to find, has parking, has seating and is comfortable. Check access and any booking requirements.

## Before the meet-up
- **Pick the date** and time, and give plenty of notice.
- **Invite people personally,** not just by group message.
- **Prepare the activity** and gather materials.
- **Plan the food and drink.**
- **Prepare a sign-in sheet** (names and contact details, with consent).
- **Arrive early** and set up.

## At the meet-up
- Welcome each person at the door.
- Keep to the agenda and the time.
- Make sure everyone has a chance to talk.
- Ask what people want to learn.
- Take notes.

## After the meet-up
- **Thank everyone** within a day.
- **Send a short summary** and the action items.
- **Ask for feedback.**
- **Plan the next meet-up** (see the next lesson).

## Checklist
- Pick a theme and write the agenda.
- Choose a place and date.
- Invite people personally.
- Prepare a sign-in sheet.
- Send a thank-you and summary afterward.

> The first meet-up should leave people feeling that it was worth their time and that they want to come back.
