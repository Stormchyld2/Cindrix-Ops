In an area study, an **Area of Operation (AO)** is the specific, defined geographic boundary where a mission is carried out. It holds the people, terrain and resources you work with. For your group, it is the area you intend to cover.

## Decide how big
- **Too small,** and you will not find enough interested people, skills or resources.
- **Too big,** and you will not be able to reach people who need help within a reasonable time, and members will not know each other.

## Think about how people actually move
Many disasters destroy or close freeway overpasses, bridges and roads. Travel that takes ten minutes now may be impossible, or take hours.

A useful test: **how far can a member reach on foot in an hour or two, carrying a pack?** Most people walk about three miles an hour on flat ground, less with a load and on rough ground. That is a good guide for your core AO.

## Use real boundaries
Draw the boundary on a map using features you can recognize:

- Rivers, creeks and ridges.
- Major roads and rail lines.
- Neighborhood or school-district edges.
- Municipal boundaries.

Mark crossings and choke points: bridges, tunnels and underpasses. These are the places where trouble can cut your area in two. (See the Area Studies course.)

## Layers
- **Core AO.** The neighborhood where your members live, within walking distance of each other.
- **Wider AI (area of interest).** Neighboring communities, hospitals, utilities and main roads that affect you.
- **Outside links.** A relative or group farther away who can help, and a place to go if you must leave.

## Match the AO to the group
- A group of eight households might cover a few blocks or a small neighborhood.
- A group of 30 might cover a district.
- Larger groups can be organized as linked teams, each with its own area.

## Consider the neighbors
- Talk to neighboring groups and associations.
- Agree on boundaries so you do not duplicate effort, and share information.
- Be a good neighbor to people outside your group.

## Write it down
- Draw the AO and AI on a map.
- List the households and key places inside it.
- Put the map in the group binder and share it with members.
- Review it each year.

## Checklist
- Draw a core AO that members can reach on foot.
- Mark boundaries, crossings and choke points.
- Draw the wider AI.
- Talk to neighboring groups.
- Put the map in the binder.

> An AO you can walk across is an AO you can protect and support.
