Movement is mostly feet, legs, back and shoulders. Most failures on a long walk are blisters, tired legs and a pack that is too heavy.

## Fitness
Build before you need it.
- **Walk:** weekly walks with a loaded pack, building to hours at a time.
- **Strength:** legs, core and back: squats, lunges, step-ups, planks, carries.
- **Endurance:** brisk walking, hiking, cycling or running.
- **Mobility:** stretching, especially hips, calves and hamstrings.
- **Rest and food:** recovery matters as much as work.
A rough benchmark for a prepared adult: carrying a pack of about 25 to 30 pounds for 10 miles over mixed terrain, with water, in a day, and being able to go on the next day. Build up gradually, and see a doctor before starting if you have health concerns.

## Load
- **Rule of thumb:** a pack should be about **20 percent of your body weight** for a long move, and no more than about 30 percent in the best condition.
- **Weight high and close to the back,** heavy items near the spine, between the shoulder blades.
- **Use a good pack,** fitted to your torso, with a padded hip belt that carries most of the weight.
- **Keep the essentials in reach:** water, food, map, compass, rain gear, first aid.
- **Pack waterproof liners.**
- **Cut weight:** each ounce counts. Take what you need, not what you might want.

## The kit for a movement
- **Water** and treatment: bottles or a bladder, a filter and chlorine tablets.
- **Food:** high-calorie, easy to eat.
- **Shelter:** a tarp or bivy, a sleeping bag or blanket, a ground pad.
- **Clothing:** layers, a hat, gloves, rain jacket, spare socks.
- **Fire:** lighters, matches in waterproof case, a fire starter.
- **Navigation:** map, compass, a watch.
- **Light:** a headlamp and spare batteries.
- **First aid:** a compact kit with a tourniquet.
- **Tools:** knife, multi-tool, cord.
- **Signals:** whistle, mirror, bright cloth.
- **Communications:** radio and phone with a charger.
- **Documents and cash.**

## Footwear and feet
- **Boots or trail shoes** that fit, broken in before the trip. Fit with the socks you will wear, and leave a thumb's width in front of the toes.
- **Socks:** wool or synthetic, never cotton. Carry spares and change when wet.
- **Blister prevention:** stop at the first hot spot and cover it with tape or moleskin. Keep feet dry and clean. Trim toenails.
- **Foot care:** at each rest, take off boots, air and dry feet, and check for hot spots.
- **Gaiters** keep out debris.

## Clothes
Layer: a base layer that wicks, an insulating layer and a shell. Avoid cotton, which soaks and chills. Dress cool at the start and add layers when you stop.

## Pacing
Start slow. Walk at a pace that lets you hold a conversation. Rest for 5 to 10 minutes each hour. Eat and drink before you are hungry or thirsty.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
