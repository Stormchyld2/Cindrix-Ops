On a long move, you must meet your needs without a base.

## Water
- **Plan your water:** know sources along the route. Carry enough to reach the next one: at least two liters in warm weather, more in heat.
- **Drink steadily,** and before you are thirsty. Pale urine is a good sign.
- **Treat all water** (see the Water and Sanitation course): filter and disinfect, or boil.
- **Collect** from moving water upstream of camps and animals.
- **Do not drink** from stagnant ponds, or water near dead animals, mines, farms or industry.
- **Dry country:** travel in the cool, rest in shade by day, and watch for green vegetation and animal signs. Do not eat if you have no water, since digestion uses water.

## Food
- **Pack high-calorie, light food:** nuts, nut butter, dried fruit, bars, jerky, cheese, tortillas, instant oats, couscous.
- A hard-working adult needs **3,000 to 4,000 calories** a day in the field.
- **Eat small amounts often.**
- **Have a hot meal** if safe, since it lifts morale and warmth.
- Foraging and hunting are slow. Do not count on them.
- **Handle food safely.** Store away from sleeping areas in wildlife country, hanging it or using a bear canister where required.

## Shelter
- **Choose** a site that is flat, dry, above flood level, away from dead trees ("widowmakers"), cliffs, rockfall, avalanche paths and insects.
- **Use** natural windbreaks, but not under a lone tall tree in a storm.
- **Tarps and bivy sacks** are fast and light. A simple tarp pitched with cord and trees works well.
- **Insulate from the ground:** pads or boughs. Ground cold is the main heat thief.
- **Keep dry.** Dry clothing for sleeping.
- **Stay warm:** eat before bed, use a hat, and keep your core warm.
- **Cold-weather shelters:** snow trench, quinzhee, lean-to with a reflector fire.

## Fire
- Use only where safe and allowed. Clear a ring, keep water nearby, and **put it out fully.**
- Fire gives heat, light, water boiling and morale, but it also shows where you are, by light and smoke.
- Use a small, **low-smoke** fire in dry hardwood. Never leave it unattended.
- Carry multiple ignition sources.

## Sanitation on the move
- Use a cat hole 6 to 8 inches deep, 200 feet from water and camps.
- Wash hands.
- Pack out trash.

## Leave no trace
Take only what you need and leave the place as you found it, unless safety demands otherwise.

## Rest and sleep
Rest is a mission need. Plan sleep. Tired people make bad decisions.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
