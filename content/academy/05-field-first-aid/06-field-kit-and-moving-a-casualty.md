## A basic field kit
Pack in a waterproof bag, in order of use:
**Bleeding:** tourniquet, hemostatic or plain gauze, compression bandage, gloves.
**Wounds:** adhesive bandages, gauze pads, roller gauze, tape, antiseptic wipes, antibiotic ointment, tweezers, safety pins.
**Fractures:** SAM splint, triangular bandages, elastic wrap.
**Blisters and feet:** moleskin or tape, scissors.
**Burns:** burn gel or dressings, non-stick pads.
**Warmth:** space blanket, hand warmers.
**Tools:** trauma shears, thermometer, whistle, a pen and notepad (to record times and findings), a headlamp.
**Medicines:** your own prescriptions, an epinephrine auto-injector if prescribed, oral rehydration salts, and over-the-counter products for pain, allergy and stomach upset, **used per their labels.**
**Information:** a first aid guide card and emergency contacts.

## Know the kit
Practice using each item. Check expiration dates when the clocks change.

## Decide: treat, stabilize or evacuate
After first aid, decide:
- **Can the person walk out?** With a sprain, they may with a splint and a stick.
- **Do they need help getting out?** Plan a carry.
- **Do they need a rescue?** Call for help (phone, satellite messenger, signal).

## Call for help
Give rescuers: location (coordinates if you have them), what happened, number of injured, their condition, your resources, and the weather.

## Moving a casualty (only when necessary)
**Do not move** someone with a suspected spine injury unless they are in danger. If you must, keep the head, neck and back in line with helpers.

### Short moves
- **One-person drag:** grasp under the arms and drag the person on their back, supporting the head.
- **Blanket drag:** roll the person onto a blanket and drag it.
- **Fireman's carry:** one person lifts and carries the other over the shoulders, for those who can be lifted.
- **Two-person seat carry:** two rescuers lock arms to make a seat.
- **Pack-strap carry:** the injured person's arms over the carrier's shoulders.

### Long carries
- **Improvised stretcher:** two long poles with a blanket or coats rolled around them, or a tarp, a door or a sleeping pad. Four people are best.
- **Carry feet first** (head uphill), with the head raised slightly, unless going uphill.
- **Rest often** and change carriers.
- **Keep the person warm,** padded and strapped.

## Stay with them
Do not leave an injured person alone unless you must get help. If you must, leave them warm, with water, shelter and signals, and note their position.

## Record
Write the time of injury, tourniquet, medicines, symptoms and changes. Hand the notes to responders.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.

## Build and learn your kit
A kit is only as good as the skill of its owner. Buy a basic kit, learn each item and add supplies for your needs. Check the kit each season.

## Core items
Gloves, gauze and rolled bandages, adhesive bandages, tape, elastic wrap, a tourniquet, trauma shears, tweezers, a thermometer, an emergency blanket, a splint, antiseptic wipes, burn dressings and a small notebook and pencil.

## Moving a casualty
Move a person only when you must, such as for fire, rising water or danger. Support the head and neck, use a blanket drag, a two-person carry or an improvised stretcher of jackets and poles.

## Keep a record
Write down the time, what happened, vital signs and what you did. Hand it to the responders.
