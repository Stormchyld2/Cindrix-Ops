[video] Wounds, burns and broken bones

Far from help, small injuries can grow into big ones. This lesson covers field basics. The Medical course gives the full detail.

## Cuts and scrapes
1. **Stop bleeding** with direct pressure.
2. **Wash your hands,** or use gloves.
3. **Clean the wound** with the cleanest, safest water you have, treated if possible. Rinse well. Remove dirt with clean tweezers.
4. **Cover** with a clean dressing and bandage.
5. **Change** the dressing daily, and watch for infection: redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, fever.
6. **Keep it clean and dry,** and protect from dirt.
7. **Evacuate** for deep wounds, wounds that gape, animal bites, or signs of infection. In remote areas, infection can become dangerous within days.

## Blisters
- **Prevent:** well-fitting boots, dry socks, tape or moleskin on hot spots **as soon as you feel them.**
- **Treat:** a small blister: cover with a donut-shaped pad. A large painful blister: clean the skin, make a tiny hole with a clean needle at the edge, drain, and cover. Keep it clean.

## Sprains and strains
- **Rest, ice or cold water, compression and elevation.**
- **Wrap** the ankle with an elastic bandage and use a walking stick.
- **Evacuate** if you cannot bear weight.

## Broken bones
- **Signs:** pain, swelling, deformity, bruising, inability to use the limb.
- **Stop bleeding.** Cover open wounds.
- **Splint** the joints above and below, in the position found. Use a SAM splint, sticks, a rolled jacket or a pad, with padding.
- **Check** fingers or toes for color, warmth and feeling, before and after splinting.
- **Sling and swathe** for an arm.
- **Cold pack** on the injury.
- **Evacuate:** a broken leg or hip, a possible neck or back injury, or an open fracture needs a rescue.
- **Suspected spine injury:** do not move the person unless there is danger. Keep the head still.

## Burns
- **Stop the burning.** Remove from the source.
- **Cool** with cool water for at least 10 minutes. No ice.
- **Remove** rings and watches before swelling.
- **Cover** loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing.
- **Do not pop** blisters or apply grease.
- **Evacuate** for burns on the face, hands, feet, genitals, over joints, larger than a palm, or any deep burn.

## Eye injuries
- **Flush** chemicals with clean water for at least 15 minutes.
- **Do not rub.**
- **Cover** an eye with a cup or shield, not pressure, if there is an object in it.
- **Seek care.**

## Head injuries
Watch for confusion, vomiting, severe headache, drowsiness, unequal pupils, clear fluid from the nose or ears. These need evacuation.

## Splinters, ticks, stings
- **Remove splinters** with clean tweezers.
- **Remove ticks** by pulling steadily with fine tweezers close to the skin.
- **Remove stingers** by scraping.
- **Treat allergic reactions** as an emergency.

## Decide when to evacuate
Evacuate if: a life-threatening condition, a head, neck or back injury, an open fracture, severe burns, an infection spreading, trouble breathing, chest pain, a severe allergic reaction, or if the person is getting worse.

## The basics of wound care
Stop the bleeding first, then clean. Wash your hands, rinse the wound with clean, running water, remove visible dirt, cover it with a clean dressing and change it daily. Watch for infection: spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus and fever. Seek medical care for deep, gaping, dirty or animal-bite wounds.

## Burns
Cool a burn with cool running water for at least 10 minutes. Do not use ice, butter or ointments. Remove rings and jewelry before swelling begins. Cover loosely with a clean dry dressing. Seek care for burns that are large, deep, on the face, hands, feet or genitals, or that blister widely.

## Fractures and sprains
Immobilize in the position found with a splint padded and tied above and below the injury. Check circulation, feeling and movement beyond the splint. Treat sprains with rest, ice, compression and elevation. Do not try to straighten a broken bone.

## Head injuries
Any head injury with loss of consciousness, vomiting, confusion or worsening headache needs medical care. Keep the person still and watch.

## When to evacuate
If the injury is severe, if the person is getting worse or if you cannot treat it with what you have, get help.
