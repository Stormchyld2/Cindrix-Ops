Heat is among the leading weather killers. The body cools by sweating, and fails when it loses too much water and salt, or when it cannot cool.

## Dehydration
Signs: thirst, dry mouth, dark urine, headache, tiredness, dizziness. Severe: confusion, fast pulse, fainting.
- **Rest in shade.**
- **Sip water** (or oral rehydration solution) steadily.
- **Eat** salty food.
- **Avoid alcohol,** and limit caffeine.

## Heat cramps
Painful muscle spasms in heat. Rest in shade, stretch gently, and sip water with a little salt, or sports drink.

## Heat exhaustion
- **Signs:** heavy sweating, cool, pale, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea, headache, dizziness, fatigue, fainting.
- **First aid:**
  1. **Move to shade** or a cool place, and **lie down,** legs raised.
  2. **Loosen or remove** extra clothing.
  3. **Cool the skin:** wet cloths, a spray, a fan, or a cool shower. Put cool cloths on neck, armpits and groin.
  4. **Sip water** if awake and not vomiting.
  5. **Get medical help** if symptoms get worse or last more than an hour.

## Heat stroke: an emergency
- **Signs:** body temperature of 103 °F (39.4 °C) or higher, hot red skin (dry or damp), fast strong pulse, headache, confusion, slurred speech, seizures, unconsciousness. The person may stop sweating.
- **First aid:**
  1. **Call 911.**
  2. **Move to shade or a cool place.**
  3. **Cool them fast:** immerse in cold water if possible (the fastest method), or soak the skin with cool water and fan, and apply ice packs wrapped in cloth to the neck, armpits and groin.
  4. **Do not give fluids** if confused or unconscious.
  5. **Keep cooling** until help arrives or their temperature drops to about 101 °F.
  6. **Be ready to start CPR.**

## Exertional heat illness
Hard work or exercise in heat can cause heat stroke even in fit young people. Slow down, rest, hydrate and acclimatize over 1 to 2 weeks.

## Sunburn
Cover up and use sunscreen. Cool and moisturize burns, and drink water.

## Who is at risk
- **Infants and children,** who overheat fast.
- **Older adults.**
- **People with heart disease,** diabetes, or on certain medicines.
- **People who work or exercise outdoors.**
- **People without cooling at home.**

## Never leave anyone in a parked car
Temperature inside a car can climb 20 °F in 10 minutes. Children and pets die each year in hot cars.

## Prevention in the field
- **Drink before you are thirsty.**
- **Wear light, loose clothing and a hat.**
- **Rest in shade.**
- **Plan hard work** for early morning and evening.
- **Watch each other.**
- **Acclimatize.**
- **Know the signs.**

## Checklist
- Know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke
- Know how to cool a person fast
- Water, salt, shade and cooling cloths in the kit
- Never leave children or pets in a car

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
