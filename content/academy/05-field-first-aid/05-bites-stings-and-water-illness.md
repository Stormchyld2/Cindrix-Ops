In the field, animals, insects and unclean water cause many problems. Prevention is easier than treatment.

## Insect and tick prevention
- **Wear long sleeves and pants,** tucked in.
- **Use repellent** with DEET, picaridin or permethrin on clothing (follow the labels).
- **Check yourself** for ticks every day, including scalp, armpits, groin and behind the ears and knees.
- **Avoid** tall grass and brush.
- **Remove** ticks promptly with fine-tipped tweezers.
- **Mosquito nets** in bug country.

## Bee and wasp stings
- **Remove the stinger** by scraping.
- **Wash,** and apply cold.
- **Watch** for allergic reaction. This is an emergency. See the Medical course.

## Snakes
- **Watch** where you step and put your hands.
- **Wear** boots and long pants.
- **Back away** slowly from a snake. Most bites happen when people try to handle or kill them.
- **If bitten:** stay calm, keep the limb still and at about heart level, remove tight items, call or go for help, and note the time. Do not cut, suck, ice or use a tourniquet.

## Large animals
- **Make noise** so you do not surprise bears and other animals.
- **Keep food** stored in sealed containers and hung or in bear-proof boxes, away from where you sleep.
- **Never feed** animals.
- **If you meet a bear:** stay calm, back away slowly, do not run, talk in a firm voice. **Carry bear spray** where bears live, and know how to use it.
- **Never approach** wildlife.

## Rabies
Any bite or scratch from a wild mammal (bats, raccoons, foxes, skunks) can carry rabies. **Wash well and seek medical care at once.**

## Water and food illness
- **Giardia and other bugs** from untreated water cause diarrhea, cramps and nausea days later.
- **Treat all water.**
- **Wash hands** before eating.
- **Keep food** cold and covered.
- **Cook thoroughly.**

### Diarrhea and vomiting
- **Replace fluids:** small, frequent sips of clean water or oral rehydration solution (mix packets with treated water as directed).
- **Rest** and eat bland foods once you can.
- **Wash your hands** often.
- **Seek help** if there is blood, a high fever, signs of dehydration (dark urine, dizziness, confusion), if it lasts more than a couple of days, or in babies, older adults or people who are very ill.

## Poisonous plants
- **Know** poison ivy, oak and sumac ("leaves of three, let it be").
- **Wash** skin and clothes after contact.
- **Never eat** wild plants or mushrooms unless an expert has identified them.
- **Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.**

## Carbon monoxide
Never burn fuel in a closed tent or shelter.

## Checklist
- Repellent, long clothing and a tick check routine
- Water treatment
- Oral rehydration packets
- Poison Control number saved

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
