Cold does not need to be freezing to kill. Wind, wetness and exhaustion can cause **hypothermia** at 50 °F.

## Hypothermia
A core body temperature below about 95 °F (35 °C).
### Signs
- **Mild:** shivering, cold hands and feet, "the umbles" (stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles), poor coordination, slurred speech.
- **Moderate:** violent shivering that may stop, confusion, drowsiness, irrational behavior (such as taking off clothes).
- **Severe:** no shivering, stiff muscles, very slow breathing and pulse, unresponsive. They may look dead.
### First aid
1. **Get out of the wind and wet.** Shelter, tent, or a windbreak.
2. **Handle gently.** A cold heart is irritable.
3. **Remove wet clothing** and replace with dry layers. Cut it off if needed.
4. **Insulate from the ground,** and wrap in blankets, sleeping bags and a vapor barrier (a space blanket or plastic sheet) with the head and neck covered.
5. **Warm the core:** warm (not hot) water bottles or packs wrapped in cloth, to the chest, neck, armpits and groin. **Skin-to-skin contact** under blankets works.
6. **Give warm sweet drinks** if the person is awake and can swallow. No alcohol or caffeine.
7. **Do not rub** the limbs or use hot water or direct heat.
8. **Call for help** for moderate and severe cases. Evacuate.
9. **If they stop breathing,** begin CPR. Do not stop until help arrives or the person is warm, because "nobody is dead until warm and dead."

## Frostbite
Freezing of skin and tissue, often on fingers, toes, ears, nose and cheeks.
### Signs
- **Frostnip:** cold, numb, pale skin that returns to normal when warmed.
- **Superficial frostbite:** white or gray-yellow, waxy, numb, hard on the surface.
- **Deep frostbite:** hard, cold, no feeling, blisters (often blood-filled), blackening.
### First aid
- **Get to shelter.**
- **Remove** wet or tight clothing and jewelry.
- **Do not rewarm** if there is a chance the part will freeze again. Walking on a frozen foot is better than walking on a thawed, injured foot.
- **Rewarm** in warm (not hot) water, about the temperature of a comfortable bath, for 20 to 30 minutes, until the skin turns red and feeling returns. It will hurt.
- **Do not rub** or use dry heat (fire, heater).
- **Cover** with loose, dry, sterile dressings. Place gauze between toes and fingers.
- **Do not pop** blisters.
- **Seek medical care.**

## Trench foot and immersion foot
Prolonged wet and cold (not freezing). Feet become numb, swollen and pale, then painful. **Keep feet dry,** change socks, dry feet and elevate.

## Snow blindness
Sunburn of the eyes from bright snow. Wear sunglasses or goggles. If it happens, cover the eyes with a cloth, rest in the dark and seek care.

## Prevent
- **Dress in layers,** with a shell. Avoid cotton.
- **Stay dry.**
- **Eat and drink** enough, and keep moving.
- **Watch each other** for signs.
- **Know when to turn back.**
- **Avoid alcohol,** which makes you lose heat.
- **Be careful** with children, older adults and people who are ill.

## Checklist
- Know the signs of mild, moderate and severe hypothermia
- Know the steps to warm a person safely
- Know how to treat frostbite and when not to rewarm
- Spare layers, a space blanket and a stove in the kit

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
