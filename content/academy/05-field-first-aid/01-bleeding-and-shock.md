> **Call 911 or your local emergency number first if you can.** This lesson is a reminder of basics, not a replacement for a hands-on class.

## Severe bleeding
Heavy bleeding can kill in minutes.
1. Protect yourself. Use gloves or a plastic bag if you have them.
2. Press **hard, directly on the wound** with a thick clean cloth. Use both hands and your body weight.
3. Keep pressing without lifting to look. If the cloth soaks through, add more on top.
4. If a limb is bleeding badly and pressure is not enough, a **tourniquet** placed higher on the limb (between the wound and the heart), above any joint, tightened until bleeding stops, can save a life. Note the time and **leave it on** for medical workers.

## Shock
Shock means the body is not getting enough blood flow. Signs: pale, cool, clammy skin; fast pulse; weakness; confusion.
- Lay the person flat and keep them warm.
- Raise the legs only if there is no head, neck, back or leg injury.
- Do not give food or drink if they may need surgery or are confused.

> Get trained. A short Stop the Bleed or first aid class is worth far more than reading.

## Check yourself
? What is the first thing to do for heavy bleeding?
+ Call for help and press hard directly on the wound
- Rinse the wound slowly with water first
- Elevate the person and wait
> Firm, direct pressure is the first and most useful step.
? What should you do with a tourniquet once it is on?
+ Leave it in place and note the time
- Loosen it every few minutes
- Remove it when the person feels better
> Only medical staff should remove it. Note when it was applied.
? Which is a sign of shock?
+ Pale, cool, clammy skin with a fast pulse
- Warm flushed skin and a slow pulse
- Loud, steady speech
> Shock reduces blood flow to the skin, causing pale clammy skin and a fast pulse.
