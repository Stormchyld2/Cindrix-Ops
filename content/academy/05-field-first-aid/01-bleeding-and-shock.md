## Severe bleeding
Heavy bleeding can kill in minutes.
1. **Protect yourself.** Use gloves or a plastic bag if you have them.
2. **Press hard, directly on the wound** with a thick clean cloth. Use both hands and your body weight.
3. **Keep pressing without lifting to look.** If the cloth soaks through, add more on top.
4. **For a deep wound,** pack gauze into it, all the way to the bottom, and press for at least three minutes.
5. **If a limb is bleeding badly and pressure is not enough,** a **tourniquet** placed high on the limb (between the wound and the heart), 2 to 3 inches above the wound and never on a joint, tightened until the bleeding stops, can save a life. Write down the time and **leave it on** for medical workers.
6. **Bandage firmly** once bleeding stops, and watch it.
7. **Do not remove** objects stuck in a wound. Pad around them.

## In the field
- **Out of range of help:** you may be alone with the injured person for hours. After bleeding stops, keep them warm, protect them from the ground, and plan to signal or send someone for help.
- **A tourniquet can stay on for hours** if needed. The goal is life first.
- **Improvise** with a wide strip of cloth and a stick if you have no tourniquet.

## Shock
Shock means the body is not getting enough blood flow. Signs: pale, cool, clammy skin; fast pulse; fast breathing; weakness; confusion; thirst; nausea.
- **Lay the person flat** and keep them **warm.** Put insulation under them and over them.
- **Raise the legs** only if there is no head, neck, back, pelvic or leg injury and it does not cause pain.
- **Do not give food or drink** if they may need surgery or are confused.
- **Treat the cause:** stop bleeding, splint injuries.
- **Reassure** and stay with them.
- **Watch breathing,** and be ready for CPR.

## Shock in a group
In a larger incident, check everyone for hidden bleeding. People often do not notice they are hurt.

## Prevent
- Carry a **tourniquet and gauze,** and know how to use them.
- Take a **Stop the Bleed class.**

> Get trained. A short Stop the Bleed or first aid class is worth far more than reading.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.

## Seconds count
A person can bleed to death from a major wound in a few minutes. Your first aid skills matter most in this one area, and they are simple to learn. Take a Stop the Bleed class, which takes about two hours and is offered locally and online.

## The steps
1. **Check the scene** is safe, and put on gloves if you have them.
2. **Call 911** or send someone to.
3. **Apply direct pressure** with a clean cloth or gauze, pressing hard on the wound with both hands.
4. **Pack the wound** with gauze if the bleeding is deep and does not stop, and press firmly.
5. **Use a tourniquet** for severe limb bleeding that does not stop, or when you cannot keep pressing. Place it two to three inches above the wound, not over a joint, and tighten until the bleeding stops. Note the time. Do not remove it.
6. **Treat for shock:** lay the person down, keep them warm and raise the legs if there is no head, neck or back injury.
7. **Stay with them** and watch breathing until help arrives.

## Signs of shock
Pale, cool, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, fast breathing, confusion, weakness and thirst. Shock is life-threatening. Lay the person down, keep them warm and still and do not give food or drink.

## Practise
Practise with a training tourniquet on your own arm and leg. Know where your kit is, and keep it where you can reach it quickly.
