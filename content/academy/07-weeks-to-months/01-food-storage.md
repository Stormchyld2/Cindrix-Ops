[video] Storing and rotating food

A few weeks of food gives you a cushion against outages, job loss, shortages and storms. Store what you eat, and eat what you store.

## How much
- **Start with 3 days,** then **2 weeks,** then **1 month,** and build toward **3 months** if you can.
- Plan about **2,000 calories** per adult per day (more for hard work, less for small children). A simple target: 1 pound of dry food per person per day.
- **Count everyone,** including pets.

## What to store
- **Staples:** rice, beans, pasta, oats, flour, dried lentils, cornmeal.
- **Canned goods:** meat, fish, soups, beans, vegetables, fruit, tomatoes.
- **Fats and oils,** peanut butter and nuts.
- **Sweeteners:** sugar, honey.
- **Salt,** spices and seasonings.
- **Dairy and eggs alternatives:** powdered milk, shelf-stable milk, powdered eggs.
- **Dried and freeze-dried food.**
- **Comfort foods:** coffee, tea, chocolate, hard candy.
- **Vitamins** if you cannot get a varied diet.
- **Baby formula, pet food.**
- **Foods for special diets.**
Choose food your family **will eat.** Try it before you stock up.

## How long does it last
- **White rice, dried beans, sugar, salt, honey:** many years when stored properly (decades for some).
- **Canned food:** best quality for 2 to 5 years, often safe longer if the can is undamaged. Acidic foods (tomatoes, fruit) last shorter than low-acid.
- **Pasta, flour, oats:** one to several years, depending on storage.
- **Oils and nuts:** months to a couple of years (they go rancid).
- **Freeze-dried food:** 10 to 25 years sealed.
- **Brown rice and whole grains:** shorter, around a year, because of oils.
- **"Best by" dates** are about quality, not safety. Use judgment.

## Store it right
- **Cool, dry and dark.** Heat shortens life. Aim for under 70 °F.
- **Off the floor,** on shelves, away from walls and water.
- **Airtight containers** protect from pests: food-grade buckets with gamma lids, Mylar bags, jars, glass.
- **Oxygen absorbers** extend the life of dry goods in sealed containers (not for sugar, salt or moist foods).
- **Keep out of the sun.**
- **Protect from rodents** and insects.
- **Label** with contents and date.

## Rotate: first in, first out (FIFO)
1. **Date** everything.
2. **Put new items at the back** and use older ones first.
3. **Eat from your storage** each week and replace.
4. **Inspect** each season for damage, bulging cans, leaks and pests.
5. **Keep an inventory** (use the Inventory screen).

## Pests
- **Freeze** flour and grains for several days before storing to kill eggs.
- **Keep** storage clean.
- **Use** tight lids.
- **Check** for webbing, holes and bugs.

## Safety
- **Do not eat** from bulging, leaking or badly dented cans.
- **Discard** food that smells off or has mold.
- **Wash hands** and use clean utensils.

## Budget
- **Buy a little extra** each shop.
- **Watch sales.**
- **Buy in bulk** staples.
- **Avoid waste.**

## Build in steps
Move from days to weeks to months as time and money allow. The first goal is two weeks. A good rule is to store what you eat and eat what you store.

## Store it right
Cool, dry, dark and off the floor. Use sealed containers to keep out pests and moisture. Label with dates, and rotate first in, first out.

## Quick shelf-life guide
White rice, dried beans, sugar and salt keep for many years if stored properly. Canned goods keep for a few years or more. Flour, whole grains and oils last less long and should be rotated.

## Safety
Never eat food from bulging, leaking or badly dented cans, or food that smells off. Wash hands, keep surfaces clean and keep raw and cooked food apart.

## Work out the numbers
For each person, plan about 2,000 calories a day. A month is about 60,000 calories per person. A month of rice, beans, oats, oil, canned food and treats for a family of four will fill several shelves. Count your current stock, subtract it from your goal and make a monthly shopping list to close the gap.

## What to store, by category
**Staples:** rice, beans, pasta, oats, flour, cornmeal.
**Proteins:** canned meat and fish, peanut butter, dried beans, powdered eggs and milk.
**Fats:** cooking oil, shortening, nuts.
**Fruit and vegetables:** canned, dried, freeze-dried.
**Flavor:** salt, sugar, honey, spices, bouillon.
**Comfort:** coffee, tea, chocolate.
**Special needs:** formula, baby food, pet food, medical foods.

## Shelf life guide
White rice, dried beans, sugar and salt can last decades if stored dry and cool. Canned food keeps its best quality for two to five years and is usually safe beyond that, unless the can is damaged. Whole wheat and brown rice keep for a few years. Oils turn rancid in a year or two. The USDA FoodKeeper app lists many foods.

## Rotate with a system
Keep a list on the shelf. Put new items at the back and old at the front. Eat from the front. Mark each item with the date you bought it. Do an inventory each season. Move food that is nearing its date into your daily meals.

## Pests and spoilage
Store in sealed, rodent-proof containers. Keep the area clean and dry. Inspect for moisture, signs of pests and bulging cans. Dispose of anything doubtful.

## Budget
Spend a fixed amount a month, buy on sale and in bulk when you will use it and avoid fads. Buy what you will eat.
