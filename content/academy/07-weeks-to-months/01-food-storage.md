For longer emergencies, plan food you will actually eat and can keep safe.

## Principles
- **Store what you eat.** Familiar food keeps morale up and avoids waste.
- Start with **two weeks**, then build toward a month and beyond.
- Choose foods that need little water and little cooking: canned goods, rice, beans, pasta, oats, peanut butter, dried fruit, nuts.
- Include comfort items and vitamins for children and special diets.

## Rotate: first in, first out
1. Put new food at the back, old food at the front.
2. Write the purchase date on each item.
3. Use and replace one item at a time so your stock stays fresh.

## Keep it safe
- Store in a cool, dry, dark place in sealed containers that pests cannot chew.
- Throw away cans that are bulging, leaking or badly dented along the seam.
- When power fails, keep the refrigerator closed. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours; a full freezer for about two days if the door stays shut.

> A well-rotated pantry is the cheapest emergency insurance.

## Check yourself
? What does "first in, first out" mean for a pantry?
+ Use the oldest items first and put new ones at the back
- Use the newest items first
- Never use stored food
> Rotation keeps food fresh and prevents waste.
? Why store foods you normally eat?
+ They keep morale up and are less likely to go to waste
- They are always cheaper
- They never spoil
> Familiar food is easier to eat and easier to rotate.
? What should you do with a can that is bulging or leaking?
+ Throw it away
- Cook it well and eat it
- Save it for later
> Bulging or leaking cans can signal dangerous contamination.
