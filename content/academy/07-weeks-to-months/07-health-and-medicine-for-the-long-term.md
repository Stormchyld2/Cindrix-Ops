Weeks and months without normal medical care mean you must protect health and manage conditions carefully. Talk to your own doctor about your plan.

## Prescription medicines
- **Ask your doctor** about keeping an extra supply of medicines you depend on. Many insurers allow early refills for emergencies, and during declared disasters often waive limits.
- **Keep a list** of all medicines, the conditions they treat and your pharmacy and doctor numbers.
- **Rotate** so older stock is used first.
- **Store** cool, dry and dark. Heat shortens life. Insulin and some others need refrigeration. Ask your pharmacist about keeping them cool during a power loss.
- **Never share** prescription medicines.
- **Do not** make your own plan for antibiotics or stockpile them without medical guidance. Misuse harms you and others.
- **Check** expiration dates, and ask a pharmacist about use after the date for each product.

## Chronic conditions
- **Diabetes:** keep supplies, testing strips, a cooler for insulin, and sugar sources. Ask your doctor for a sick-day and disaster plan.
- **Heart disease and blood pressure:** keep medicines, and avoid overexertion.
- **Asthma:** keep rescue inhalers, and avoid triggers.
- **Mental health:** keep medicines, a routine and contacts.
- **Dialysis and oxygen:** talk to providers about emergency plans and backups.
- **Allergies:** keep epinephrine, in date.

## Prevention matters most
- **Vaccinations** up to date, including tetanus.
- **Hygiene:** handwashing, safe water, safe food and sanitation.
- **Nutrition and hydration.**
- **Sleep and rest.**
- **Exercise** daily.
- **Safe practices:** avoid injuries by being careful with tools, ladders and fire.
- **Dental care:** keep supplies. Dental problems can become serious.
- **Eye care:** spare glasses and contacts.

## Common problems and what to do
- **Colds and flu:** rest, fluids, and isolate. See a doctor for trouble breathing or high fever.
- **Diarrhea and vomiting:** fluids and hygiene.
- **Wounds:** clean and cover.
- **Infections:** watch for spreading redness and fever, and seek care.
- **Pain and fever:** follow product labels.
- **Dental pain.**
- **Skin problems:** keep clean and dry.
- **Women's health:** keep supplies for periods, pregnancy needs and contraception.
- **Pregnancy and birth:** plan with a provider.
- **Babies and children:** keep fever and pain relievers made for children, with the label's age and weight chart.
- **Older adults:** extra care for hydration, medicines and falls.

## Know when to get help
Plan where you would go: hospitals, clinics, field hospitals, community health, and who has medical skills. Keep fuel for a trip.

## Mental health
Long disruptions wear on people. Keep routines, connection, exercise and light. Watch for depression and anxiety. **988** and the **Disaster Distress Helpline (1-800-985-5990)** are available in the US.

## Checklist
- A list of medicines, doctors and pharmacies
- A talk with your doctor about an emergency supply
- Vaccines current
- A health plan for chronic conditions

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
