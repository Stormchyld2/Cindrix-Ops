[video] Community and skills

Over weeks and months, people with skills, friends and a role do better than lone individuals with a pile of supplies.

## Why community matters
- **Neighbors** are your first responders.
- **Skills** are the supply that does not run out: first aid, cooking, repair, gardening, teaching, communications.
- **Trust** helps you share and avoid conflict.
- **Mutual aid** shares the load: childcare, meals, watch and repairs.

## Build it now
- **Meet your neighbors.** Exchange phone numbers and note skills, equipment and needs.
- **Host a gathering:** a potluck or a block meeting.
- **Join** a CERT, a neighborhood group or a faith community.
- **Make a list:** who has medical skills, tools, vehicles, generators, chainsaws, ham radios, wells.
- **Plan together:** a neighborhood emergency plan with a meeting place, check-in system and responsibilities.
- **Check on** older and isolated neighbors.

## Skills to learn
- **First aid and CPR** (see the Medical and CPR courses)
- **Water treatment**
- **Cooking** from scratch
- **Gardening** and food preservation
- **Basic repair:** plumbing, electrical safety, carpentry, sewing
- **Radio and communications**
- **Navigation**
- **Fire safety**
- **Bike and vehicle maintenance**
- **Childcare and teaching**
- **Mediation and leadership**

## Practice and teach
- **Practice** in small ways: a weekend without power, a camping trip.
- **Teach** a skill to someone each month.
- **Keep records:** the program's Academy sign-off page tracks learning.

## Make the group work
- **Agree** on goals and rules.
- **Share** responsibilities.
- **Meet** regularly.
- **Handle conflict** early and fairly.
- **Keep records** and be transparent about supplies.
- **See** the Admin and Leadership course for more.

## Barter and sharing
- **Share** generously where you can, because goodwill returns.
- **Be careful** about advertising what you have.
- **Trade** skills and items fairly.
- **Do not** make promises you cannot keep.

## Staying useful
In a long disruption, people recover better when they have a role. Find tasks that fit: cooking, caring for children, repairing, tending the garden, staffing the radio.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.

## Community is your best resource
In a long emergency, people who are connected survive best. Neighbors share tools, skills, information and care. Start now by meeting neighbors, learning who has what skills and agreeing on how to help each other.

## Skills worth building
First aid, cooking from scratch, gardening, repair and carpentry, sewing, radio operation, teaching and organizing. Ask each neighbor for one skill and offer yours.

## Make the group work
Hold regular meetings, keep a list of skills and resources, set simple rules and share workload fairly.

## Map your neighborhood
Walk your street and note who lives where and who might need help: the elderly, the disabled, families with babies and people living alone. Write down skills you know of: nurses, mechanics, builders, gardeners, ham radio operators, teachers. Offer help before asking for it.

## Start a simple group
You do not need a formal organization. Four or five households who agree to look out for each other is enough. Hold a short meeting each quarter. Exchange phone numbers. Agree on a few signals, such as a colored cloth on a door to say "we need help." Share tips on supplies and training. Keep it friendly and practical.

## Skills worth learning, with ways to learn them
First aid and CPR (Red Cross, local fire department), amateur radio (local clubs), gardening (county extension offices and master gardeners), home repair (community workshops), sewing and mending, cooking from scratch, preserving food, community emergency response training (CERT, through your local emergency management agency).

## Barter, sharing and fairness
In a long crisis, trade becomes useful. Make fairness the rule: agree on what is shared, what is traded and what is private. Keep promises. Share information freely. Be wary of anyone who asks for much and offers little.

## Stay useful
People who have a role cope better. Find your role, whether it is cooking, teaching, repairs, childcare or watching. Help others find theirs.
