Long emergencies are survived by groups more than by individuals.

## Know your neighbors
- Introduce yourself now. Learn who has medical, repair, language or trade skills.
- Share a simple plan: who checks on whom, who has what equipment.
- Note neighbors who may need extra help: older adults, people with disabilities, families with infants.

## Learn skills that last
- First aid and CPR
- Water treatment and storage
- Basic repairs and tool use
- Cooking from simple staples
- Gardening and food preservation
- Navigation and radio communication

## Share and trade fairly
Agree on rules early: shared work, shared watch duty, fair sharing of scarce items. Clear agreements prevent fights.

## Keep learning
Practice drills regularly, rotate supplies, and update your plans every season or when your family changes.

> The best preparation is not a pile of gear but a group of people who trust each other and know how to help.

## Check yourself
? Why get to know neighbors before an emergency?
+ They can share skills, help and information
- They will give you all their supplies
- It avoids paperwork
> Prepared communities recover faster because people already know who can do what.
? Which of these is a lasting skill worth learning?
+ First aid and water treatment
- Collecting unneeded gadgets
- Memorizing fiction
> Skills cannot be lost, stolen or broken the way gear can.
? Why agree on rules for sharing early?
+ Clear agreements prevent fights when things are scarce
- It means you never have to help
- It makes work unnecessary
> Fairness and clear roles keep groups working together.
