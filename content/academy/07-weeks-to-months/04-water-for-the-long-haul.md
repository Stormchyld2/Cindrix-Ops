Stored water covers days. Over weeks and months you need a source.

## How much, again
Plan on at least **1 gallon per person per day** for drinking and basic hygiene, and about **3 to 5 gallons** for a more comfortable routine with washing, cooking and laundry.

## Sources
- **Municipal water,** when it returns, may need boiling if a notice says so.
- **Wells:** an electric pump needs power. A **hand pump** or a bucket works if the well is safe. After flooding, a well may be contaminated and needs testing and disinfection.
- **Rainwater:** collect from roofs with gutters into barrels or tanks. Screen out debris, keep out mosquitoes, and **treat before drinking.** Avoid roofs of treated wood or with heavy bird droppings.
- **Springs, streams, rivers and lakes:** treat all.
- **Snow and ice:** melt and treat.
- **Water heater and plumbing:** drain pipes.
- **Dehumidifier and AC condensate:** not safe to drink without treatment, and may hold metals.

## Treat
Use the methods in earlier modules. For many people at once, a **gravity filter** (such as a ceramic or hollow-fiber filter) and **chlorine** work well. Keep spare filter cartridges and chlorine tablets or unscented bleach.

## Disinfecting a well
After a flood or contamination, follow guidance from your health department: typically flush, shock-chlorinate according to instructions, wait, flush again, and **test** the water before drinking. Until then, treat water.

## Store
- **Several large containers** (55-gallon barrels, IBC totes with lids) with tight caps, food-grade.
- **Clean** and sanitize before filling.
- **Keep** out of sunlight, in a cool place, and off concrete in cold climates.
- **Add** chlorine to stored tap water if your supply is not already chlorinated (follow guidance).
- **Rotate** every 6 months.

## Conserve
- **Use** dishwater for flushing.
- **Reuse** greywater for plants (soap and food-free).
- **Take** quick sponge baths.
- **Fix** leaks.
- **Wash** clothes less.

## Test and know
- **Know** your local water sources before a disaster, such as streams, ponds and wells, and how far they are.
- **Map** them.
- **Keep** containers to carry water (a wheeled cart or backpacks).

## Signs of unsafe water
- Bad smell, color or taste.
- Fuel or chemical sheen.
- Dead fish or animals.
- Algae blooms. Do not drink water with **blue-green algae,** which can produce toxins that boiling does not remove.

## Checklist
- Know at least two sources of water near home
- A filter and spare cartridges, and chlorine
- Large water storage if possible
- A plan to carry water

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
