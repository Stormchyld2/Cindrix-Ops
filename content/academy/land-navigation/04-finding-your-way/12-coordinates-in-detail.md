## Latitude and longitude
- **Latitude** measures distance north or south of the equator, from 0 to 90 degrees. Lines of latitude run east-west.
- **Longitude** measures distance east or west of the prime meridian (through Greenwich, England), from 0 to 180 degrees. Lines of longitude run north-south.
- Always give N or S, and E or W.
- Each degree has **60 minutes** and each minute has **60 seconds**.
- One degree of latitude is about **111 km (69 miles)**. One second is about 30 meters (100 feet). One minute is about 1.85 km.
- A degree of longitude is also 111 km at the equator but gets narrower toward the poles.

Convert to decimal degrees when you need it: degrees + minutes/60 + seconds/3600. 32 degrees 15 minutes 30 seconds is 32 + 0.25 + 0.0083 = **32.2583**.

On a topographic map the four border lines are latitude and longitude, and small tick marks along the edge show the values. You can join opposite ticks to draw a line.

## Grid coordinates (UTM / military grid)
Many topographic maps overlay a square grid. Each line has a number along the edge.

**The rule: read RIGHT, then UP.** First the number of the vertical line to the left of your point (the easting), then the number of the horizontal line below it (the northing).
- **4 digits** (such as 0182) gives the grid square, to the nearest 1,000 meters.
- **6 digits** (such as 018825) gives 100 meters. Estimate tenths across the square.
- **8 digits** (such as 01948253) gives 10 meters.
- More digits means more exact. Always use an even number, half for the easting and half for the northing.

## How to find 6 digits
1. Find the grid square your point is in. Write the two-digit easting of its left-hand line, and the two-digit northing of its bottom line.
2. Divide the square into tenths across and up (the grid scale on a protractor or card does this).
3. Read how many tenths right and how many up. Place them after the easting and northing digits.
4. Write easting digits first, then northing.

Example: a point 4 tenths of the way right from line 01 and 2 tenths of the way up from line 82 is written **014 822**.

## The full reference
A full military reference starts with the grid zone and the 100,000-meter square letters: **16S GL 0194 8253**. Inside one map sheet, you usually only need the digits.

## Reporting a position
Misreading a digit sends help or travellers to the wrong place. Read the numbers back to confirm. Say "easting, northing". Use a known landmark too.

## Other grids
Some maps use other systems, such as British grids or GEOREF. Check the margin for the instructions for the grid you have.

> When giving a rescue team your position, give the coordinates **and** the format ("UTM, 6 digits"), and a landmark.
