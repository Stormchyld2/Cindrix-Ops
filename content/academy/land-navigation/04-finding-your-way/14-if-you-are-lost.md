Nearly everyone gets turned around at some time. What matters is what you do in the first few minutes.

## S.T.O.P.
- **S**top. Sit down. Do not walk on in a panic.
- **T**hink. When did you last know exactly where you were? What have you passed since?
- **O**bserve. Look at the ground, the sun, your map, the sounds around you. What can you see that is on the map?
- **P**lan. Choose the safest action, then act.

## Options, in order
1. **Stay put** if people know your plan, if you are injured or if it is nearly dark. Make yourself easy to find: stay near the open ground, wear bright colors, use a whistle (three blasts), a mirror or a fire, and keep warm and dry.
2. **Go back** along your own tracks to the last place you were sure of, if you can do so in daylight and you have noticed where you came from. Mark your way.
3. **Find your position** with the map, using the terrain and resection (see the earlier lessons). Once you know where you are, you are no longer lost.
4. **Follow a handrail** to people. A road, power line or fence leads somewhere. A stream leads to larger water and often to people, but may lead through dangerous ground. Use caution.

## What not to do
- Do not run, climb a cliff, or cross fast water to "save time".
- Do not split up a group.
- Do not leave a shelter, a trail or a vehicle that is easier to see from the air or the road.
- Do not forget water, warmth and shelter. Cold and exposure are a bigger danger than being lost.

## Leave a note
If you leave, write a note with the date, time, your names, your direction and your plan. Put it where it will be seen.

> Staying calm and making a plan is worth more than any skill.
