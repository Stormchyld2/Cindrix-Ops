**Dead reckoning** means travelling a set distance along a set direction. First you work out the direction and distance on the map. Then you use a compass and a way of measuring distance (pace count, odometer or time) on the ground.

## Do not walk with the compass in front of you
Out in the open, don't watch the needle as you walk. It will not stay steady or level. Instead:
1. Face the right direction with the compass.
2. Pick a landmark on that line. This is a **steering mark**.
3. Close the compass and walk to the landmark.
4. Repeat from there.

## Good steering marks
- **Choose them on the ground, not from the map.** Pick them as you go.
- They have a color, shape and size you will recognize as you get close.
- Pick the **farthest** one you can clearly see, and the highest one, which stays visible longer.
- Keep it in view as you walk.
- If nothing is ahead, use something behind you (take its back direction).
- At night and in fog they need to be even more distinctive, and nearer.

## No marks at all
In featureless ground, fog or dark: send a partner ahead as far as they can still be seen. Direct them onto your line with hand signals, then walk to them and repeat.

## Deliberately aim off
Use the aiming-off method from the previous lessons when your goal is near a long feature.

## Why errors build up
Compasses and pace counts are not perfect. A small error each leg adds up. The longer you go without checking, the farther off you may be.
- Confirm your position whenever you pass something on the map.
- Every so often do a resection.
- Restart your pace count at each confirmed point.
- Steering marks can be farther apart in the open and should be close together in forest, dark or fog.

## Do not just follow one distant peak
It's dangerous to fix on a single mountaintop and walk blindly to it. If something goes wrong (injury, a call for help), you won't know where you are.

## Advantages and disadvantages
- It is easy to learn, and accurate over short distances even where there are few clues.
- It is slow, needs constant attention and builds up errors.

> Use dead reckoning for short legs, and check against the land as often as you can.
