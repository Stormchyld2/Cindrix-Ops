Every journey, long or short, comes down to four steps.

## 1. Know where you are
Know it on the map and on the ground, all the time.
- Which way am I facing?
- Where is my goal, in direction and distance?
- What landmarks are around me?
- Where is impassable ground or danger (cliffs, water, roads with traffic, unsafe areas)?
- What is between me and my goal?
Use map reading, terrain features, direction and distance, and resection.

## 2. Plan the route
Weigh:
- Travel time and distance.
- Ground: soft, steep, wet, or blocked.
- How much energy it takes, especially with children, injured or heavy loads.
- Good checkpoints and places to change direction.
- Cover, if you need to stay unseen.
- A backup route.

## 3. Stay on the route
Compare what you meet with your plan. Use a compass for direction and the land for confirmation. Use both.

## 4. Recognize the goal
The goal is often small and hard to spot. Pick an easy-to-find landmark close to it (a bigger "target") and plan a short, careful last leg from there. Walk a direction and count paces for the last stretch.

## Common mistakes, in order of how often they happen
- Judging the distance wrongly.
- Not travelling the right distance.
- Putting the goal in the wrong place on the map.
- Choosing checkpoints that are hard to recognize.
- Choosing a route that is too hard.

> Before you go, study the route and think about where you are most likely to go wrong. Then plan how to avoid it.
