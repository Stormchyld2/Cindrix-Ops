Most people get lost when they cannot see: in fog, snow, rain or darkness. Travel in these conditions is slower and riskier. The best choice is often to **stop**.

## Should you move?
Stay put if:
- you are not sure where you are,
- it is dark and the ground is rough, wet or steep,
- someone is hurt or very tired, or
- people know your plan and will look for you.

Move only if staying is unsafe, and only along something you can trust: a track, a river you can follow safely, or a bearing to a **close** target.

## If you must move
- **Slow down.** Keep the group close enough to see each other, or tie a cord between people.
- Use **short legs** to clear landmarks, 100 meters at a time.
- Keep a **pace count** and write down the time at each change.
- Use **handrails** such as a fence or a stream bank. Do not follow a stream down steep cliffs or into a gorge.
- Use the **leapfrog** method: one person walks ahead on the bearing, a partner lines them up with the compass.
- Check the compass often and **do not trust a feeling** that the compass is wrong. It is usually the feeling.

## At night
- Use a dim red or low light so your eyes stay used to the dark.
- Look slightly to one side of dim objects: your eyes see faint things better that way.
- Listen: water, roads and wind tell you where things are.
- Do not climb or cross water in the dark unless you must.

## Marks and signals
If you stop, mark where you left the route and where you went, with arrows from sticks or stones. Make yourself easy to see (a bright cloth, a fire, a whistle: three blasts is a distress signal).

> When in doubt, wait for daylight. A night in a good shelter costs less than one mistake in the dark.
