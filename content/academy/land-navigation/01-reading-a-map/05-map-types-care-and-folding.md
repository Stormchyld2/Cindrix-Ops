Not every map is the same kind. Knowing what you are holding tells you what to trust it for.

## Kinds of maps
- **Planimetric (line) map.** Shows where things are (roads, towns, rivers) but not the height of the ground. Most road maps are this kind.
- **Topographic map.** Also shows the shape and height of the land with contour lines, so you can measure it. This is the best kind for travelling on foot.
- **Photomap.** A photograph taken from the air with grid lines, names and a rough scale added.
- **City and special maps.** Street maps, trail maps and hunting maps. Good for their purpose, but check scale and date.

## Large scale and small scale
The names sound backwards. Think of the scale as a fraction: 1/24,000 is a bigger fraction than 1/1,000,000.
- **Large scale** (1:75,000 and larger, such as 1:24,000 or 1:50,000): a small area in great detail. These are the maps for walking.
- **Medium scale** (about 1:100,000 to 1:250,000): planning a trip by car.
- **Small scale** (1:1,000,000 and smaller): a whole region with little detail, for general planning.

## Symbols are drawn bigger than life
To be readable, symbols are exaggerated. On a 1:250,000 map a building symbol covers about 500 feet of ground and a road symbol is as wide as a road 520 feet across. Do not measure a road's width or a building's size from the symbol.

## Looking after a map
- Keep it in a waterproof case or clear bag, in a pocket where you can reach it.
- Mark it in **pencil**, with light lines, so you can erase cleanly.
- If you ever trim the margin, first copy the grid, scale and declination notes onto the part you keep.
- Keep a spare copy. A single map is a single point of failure.

## Folding
Fold the map so the part you need is on top and you can read it without opening the whole sheet. An accordion fold (back and forth like a fan) and then one fold across works well. For a map you will use a lot, try a practice fold with plain paper first, then fold the real one and keep it in a folder or clear sleeve.

> The best map is the one you have studied before you needed it.
