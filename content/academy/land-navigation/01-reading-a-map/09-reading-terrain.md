You can learn to see the shape of the land from the contour lines, the way you see a house from its plan.

## SOSES: describe a landform
When you try to match something on the ground to the map, describe it in five ways.
- **Shape:** the outline of its base.
- **Orientation:** which way it runs compared with you (towards you, across, at an angle).
- **Size:** how long and wide at the base.
- **Elevation:** how high, compared with its neighbours.
- **Slope:** uniform, convex or concave, and steep or gentle.

## Ridgelining
Gives you the big picture of the high ground.
1. Find the crests of the ridges by looking for the closed lines along the hilltops.
2. Trace each crest with a colored pencil.
3. Trace the big ridges and spurs that come off them.

## Streamlining
Gives you the drainage and low ground.
1. Trace all the mapped streams in another color.
2. Trace the valleys and draws that feed them.

Done together, the two tracings show you which ground is high, where water collects, and where the easy walking runs (along valley floors and just below the crests).

## Profiles: will I see that point?
A **profile** is a side view of the land along a line. It tells you if you can see from one point to another, which is useful for a hidden camp, a signal, a radio or a lookout.
1. Draw a line on the map between two points.
2. Find the highest and lowest contour lines the line touches. Add one line above and below.
3. On lined paper, number the lines to match the contour values (top line highest).
4. Lay the paper next to the map line. From every contour crossing, drop a straight line to the paper line with the same number and mark a tick.
5. Join the ticks with a smooth curve. That is your profile.
6. Draw a straight line between the two points on the profile. If it cuts through the curve, you cannot see one point from the other.

Where trees show as dark green, add about 15 meters (50 feet) to their heights. For a quick answer, do a **hasty profile** with just hilltops and ridges.

> Draw the profile bigger vertically than horizontally if you wish. Exaggeration makes small hills easier to see.
