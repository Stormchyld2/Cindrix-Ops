## Types of slope
- **Gentle (uniform):** lines evenly spaced and wide apart.
- **Steep (uniform):** lines evenly spaced and close together.
- **Concave:** lines close together at the top and wide apart at the bottom. It gets easier to walk as you go down and harder as you climb. You can see the whole slope from the top.
- **Convex:** lines wide apart at the top and close at the bottom. The slope is hidden from the top, and the last part of a climb is easier.

## Working out a slope
All slope measures compare the **vertical distance** (how much it rises) with the **horizontal distance** (how far across the map).
- Percent slope: vertical / horizontal x 100.
- Degrees (good for slopes under about 20 degrees): vertical x 57.3 / horizontal.
- Gradient: one in so-many. 1 in 5 is a 20 percent slope.

Example: a rise of 210 m over 1,000 m across is 21 percent, or about 12 degrees.

## The five major terrain features
- **Hill:** high ground with lower ground on all sides. Closed circles on the map.
- **Saddle:** a dip between two higher areas. High ground on two opposite sides, low on the other two. Looks like an hourglass.
- **Valley:** a stretched groove, usually with a stream. High on three sides and low on the fourth. Contour lines form a **U or V that points uphill (upstream)**.
- **Ridge:** a sloping line of high ground. Low ground on three sides. The U or V of the contours points **downhill, away from the high ground**.
- **Depression:** a low spot with higher ground all round.

## The three minor features
- **Draw:** a small valley with no flat ground in it. U or V pointing uphill.
- **Spur:** a short ridge sticking out from a larger ridge. U or V pointing downhill.
- **Cliff:** very close or touching contour lines, or one line with ticks pointing down.

## Made by people
- **Cut:** a road or rail bed dug through a rise.
- **Fill:** a raised bed built across low ground.

## Telling a ridge from a valley
Both make U shapes. Ask which way the closed end points. If it points **toward higher numbers** it is a valley or draw. If it points **toward lower numbers** it is a ridge or spur. Streams run down valleys, never along ridges.

> Water can show you the way. A stream always flows downhill, so the closed end of the contour shape points upstream.
