**Scale** tells you how far a distance on the map is on the ground. It is printed in the margin in two or three ways.

## Three ways to show scale
- **A ratio**, such as 1:24,000. One unit on the map is 24,000 of the same unit on the ground.
- **A word statement**, such as "1 inch equals 2,000 feet" (that is the same as 1:24,000).
- **A bar scale**, a ruler printed on the map. This is the easiest to use and it stays right if the map is copied or enlarged.

## Small numbers show more detail
A **larger scale** map (1:24,000) shows a small area in great detail. A **smaller scale** map (1:250,000) shows a big area with little detail. Use the large scale to walk. Use the small scale to plan a long trip.

## Handy conversions
- 1:25,000: 1 centimeter is 250 meters.
- 1:50,000: 1 centimeter is 500 meters.
- 1:100,000: 1 centimeter is 1 kilometer.
- 1:24,000: 1 inch is 2,000 feet, and about 2.6 inches is a mile.

## Measuring a straight line
Lay the edge of a piece of paper along the two points, mark each point on the paper edge, then slide the paper to the bar scale and read the distance.

## Measuring a curved route
Lay a piece of string or a blade of grass along the road or trail, following every bend. Straighten it out against the bar scale. For a rough answer, add up the short straight pieces.

## Ground distance is longer
The map shows distance as if the ground were flat. Hills add to the real walk. Plan more time, not just more distance.

> Make your own ruler. Mark a strip of paper with the bar scale and keep it with the map.

- 1:250,000
- 1:1,000,000
> The smaller the second number, the larger the scale and the more detail.
