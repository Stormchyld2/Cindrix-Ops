You met scale earlier. Here is how to work it out exactly, even when a map has no scale printed on it.

## The representative fraction
A scale of **1:50,000** (written as RF = 1/50,000) means one unit on the map equals 50,000 of the same unit on the ground. Units do not matter as long as you use the same one on both sides.

## Map distance to ground distance
Measure on the map, then **multiply by the bottom number**.
- Map distance 4.4 cm on a 1:50,000 map: 4.4 x 50,000 = 220,000 cm = **2,200 meters**.

## Ground distance to map distance
Divide instead. 2,200 meters is 220,000 cm, divided by 50,000 = 4.4 cm.

## A map with no scale
You can work the scale out if you know one real distance between two points that are on the map.
1. Measure the two points on the map (say 4.32 cm).
2. Know the real distance between them (say 2.16 km = 216,000 cm).
3. Divide ground by map: 216,000 / 4.32 = 50,000. The scale is **1:50,000**.

Or compare with a second map of the same place that does have a scale: find the same two points on both, work out the real distance from the good map, then do the sum above for the other.

## Reading a bar scale
A bar scale has a main part to the right of zero in whole units, and an **extension** to the left of zero cut into tenths.
1. Mark the two points on the edge of a strip of paper.
2. Slide the paper to the bar scale. Put the right-hand mark on a whole number so the left-hand mark lands in the extension.
3. Add the whole number and the extension reading (read the extension from zero going left). Example: 3,000 + 950 = **3,950 meters**.

If the strip is longer than the scale, mark the end of the extension, slide that mark back to zero and keep adding.

## Measuring along a bend
Mark the start on a strip of paper. Lay the strip along a straight part of the road. Where the road bends, put your pencil point on the mark and swing the strip round to follow the next straight part. Mark again and repeat. Only the first and last marks count. Then read it on the bar scale.

## Time on the map
**Time = distance / speed.** 12 km at 4 km per hour is 3 hours. To draw a time scale for a 1:50,000 map, make a line 24 cm long (12 km), mark it into 3 equal one-hour parts, and divide one part into minutes. It works only for that speed.

## Map distance is flat distance
Hills add real distance the map does not show. Add time, as in the pace lesson.

> A larger scale gives a more accurate measure. On a small-scale map, features are exaggerated, so measurements are rougher.

## Check yourself
? A 1:50,000 map shows two places 4.4 cm apart. How far apart are they on the ground?
+ 2,200 meters
- 220 meters
- 22,000 meters
> 4.4 cm x 50,000 = 220,000 cm = 2,200 m.
? On a bar scale, which way do you read the extension?
+ From zero toward the left
- From zero toward the right
- From the biggest number
> The extension is cut into tenths to the left of zero.
? How long does a 12 km walk take at 4 km an hour?
+ 3 hours
- 4 hours
- 48 minutes
> Time = distance / speed.
