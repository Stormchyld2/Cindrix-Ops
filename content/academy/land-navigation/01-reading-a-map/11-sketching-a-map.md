A sketch is a freehand drawing of an area or route. Use one when you have no map, when the map is not detailed enough, or to show someone else where something is.

## What every sketch needs
- A **north arrow**.
- A **scale**. This can be a line marked in paces or in kilometers or miles.
- A **legend** that explains your symbols.
- The main features: roads, rivers, power lines, railways, towns and villages, forests and major hills.

## How to make one
1. Start with a rough frame on the page and mark north.
2. Pick a clear landmark and put it on the page.
3. Add other landmarks by direction and distance (compass bearing and paces, or estimate by eye).
4. Draw linear features (roads, streams, power lines) next, because they are easiest to see and connect everything.
5. Add the legend and scale.
6. Check by walking or looking back at the real ground.

## Kinds of sketch
- **Route sketch.** Shows a road or trail and the useful features within about a few hundred meters on each side.
- **Area sketch.** Shows one place such as a camp, farm or shelter. You need to be able to walk all round it.
- **Place sketch.** A view from one spot. It shows what is in front of you, handy for describing a lookout or a view to someone else.

## Uses at home and away
- A neighbourhood map of water sources, shelters and meeting places that is not on any printed map.
- A record of a route you have walked, so others can follow it.
- A drawing to put on your local map when a road is blocked.

> Make the sketch the same way each time and put the date on it. Anyone who picks it up should understand it without you.

## Going deeper: a sketch can save your life
When you have no map, a sketch of what you see and know gives you a working picture. It helps you plan a route, share information and leave a record for rescuers.

## Add these to every sketch
- **North arrow,** found by a compass, the sun or stars.
- **A rough scale,** such as "one thumb width is about 100 paces."
- **Your position,** marked with a clear symbol.
- **A title, the date and the time.**
- **A key** for your symbols.

## Technique
1. Start with a few big features: a river, a ridge, a road, a lake.
2. Add smaller ones in their relative positions: buildings, trails, bridges.
3. Estimate distance by pace count or travel time.
4. Show bearings between features if you have a compass.
5. Mark hazards, water sources and shelters.

## Panoramic sketch
Sketch the skyline from a high point, with bearings to peaks and notable features. Later you can match it to a map.

## Keep it up to date
Each time you learn something new, add it. Keep a copy at camp and carry a copy.

## Check yourself
? What must a field sketch always show?
+ North arrow, scale and legend
- The names of everyone nearby
- The weather
> Without these, no one can use it.
? What do you draw first after your landmarks?
+ Linear features like roads and streams
- Individual trees
- The title
> Lines connect everything else.
? What is a place sketch?
+ A view drawn from one spot
- A map of a whole country
- A sketch of a road only
> It shows what you see in front of you.
