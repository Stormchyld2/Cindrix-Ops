A map is a picture of the ground seen from straight above, drawn smaller than real life. Before you look at the picture, read the edges. The border of a good map is full of facts.

## The margin
- **Title and place.** Which area and which map sheet this is. Maps in a series have the sheet name or number on every edge so you can find the next one.
- **Scale.** How much smaller the map is than the ground (next lesson).
- **Legend.** A key that shows what each symbol and color means. Symbols change from one map maker to another, so check it.
- **Date.** When the map was made or last updated. New roads, dams and clearings will not be on an old map, and a forest may have been cut.
- **North arrows and declination.** Usually three arrows are drawn: true north, grid north and magnetic north, with the angle between them. You need this for the compass lessons.
- **Contour interval.** The height between brown lines (see the contour lesson).
- **Grid numbers.** Numbers along the edges that you use to give a location.

## North is usually at the top
Almost all maps are drawn with north at the top, but not always. **Look for the arrow** instead of assuming.

## A habit that saves trouble
When you pick up any map, spend one minute on the margin first: title, scale, legend, date, north arrow. Then find where you are and one big feature you can see around you, such as a river, a road or a hill.

> Fold the map so the area you are using is on top and keep it in a clear bag. A wet map is of no use.

## Check yourself
? Where do you find the meaning of the map's symbols?
+ The legend (key) in the margin
- The title only
- The grid numbers
> Symbols differ between maps, so check the legend.
? Why does the date on a map matter?
+ New roads and changes will not be on an old map
- It tells you the weather
- It is the date to return the map
> Land changes. Treat an old map as a guide and trust what you can see.
? What should you do before assuming north is at the top?
+ Look for the north arrow
- Ask someone
- Turn the map upside down
> Most maps put north at the top, but the arrow is the proof.
