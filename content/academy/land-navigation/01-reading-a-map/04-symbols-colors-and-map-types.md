Map makers use colors and small pictures (symbols) to fit a lot of information on one sheet. The legend explains each one, but a few meanings are very common.

## Common colors on a topographic map
- **Brown:** contour lines and the shape of the land.
- **Blue:** water. Lakes, rivers, streams, marshes. Dashed blue lines are streams that may be dry for part of the year.
- **Green:** woods and thick plants. White areas are open ground.
- **Black:** things people built: buildings, bridges, railways, trails, place names.
- **Red:** major roads and boundaries (the use of red varies).
- **Pink or purple:** built-up areas or later updates, on some maps.

## Symbols to know
- Small black squares: buildings. A square with a flag or cross may be a school or a church.
- A line with cross-ticks: a railway.
- Dashed black lines: trails or minor tracks.
- A small triangle with a number: a surveyed high point.
- A blue symbol shaped like a drop or a small circle: a spring or a well (check the legend).

## Kinds of map
- **Topographic maps** show height with contours. The best kind for travel on foot.
- **Road maps** show roads and towns, with no height. Good for driving.
- **Street maps** show every street in a town. The Maps screen in this program has street maps that work offline.
- **Nautical and aeronautical charts** are special maps for water and air.
- **A sketch map** that you draw yourself is fine. A hand-drawn map of your neighborhood with water sources, shelters and meeting places can be worth more than a printed one.

## Check the legend
If a symbol is not familiar, **do not guess**. Look it up in the legend. A well can look like a spring and a mine can look like a quarry.
