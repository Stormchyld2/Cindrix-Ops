Many surplus and military-style compasses are **lensatic**: a lid with a sighting wire, a floating dial, a ratchet bezel ring and a lens that folds up. The ideas are the same as for a baseplate compass, but the handling is different.

## Parts
- **Cover** with a sighting wire and two glow dots.
- **Floating dial** with a north arrow and two scales: degrees (inner, normally red) and mils (outer). Use the degrees.
- **Fixed black index line** under the glass. You read your direction under it.
- **Bezel ring** that clicks. It has 120 clicks, so each click is **3 degrees**. It has a glow line.
- **Lens and rear sight.** It must be opened more than 45 degrees for the dial to float. Folded down it locks the dial, which protects it.
- **Straight edge** on the left with a scale. Older ones may be 1:25,000, so check which scale before using it on a 1:50,000 map (values must be halved).

## Center-hold: the everyday way
1. Open the compass fully so the cover is flat with the base.
2. Hold it in both hands with your thumb through the loop, elbows pressed into your sides, and the compass at the level of your chest.
3. Turn your whole body, not just your arms, until the cover points at your target.
4. Look down and read the direction under the black index line.

It works in any light, over any ground and without removing glasses.

## Compass-to-cheek: for sighting
1. Fold the cover straight up and the rear sight slightly forward.
2. Raise the compass to your eye and look through the rear slot, putting the sighting wire on a distant object.
3. Glance down through the lens to read the dial.

This is more exact for sighting a distant landmark.

## Presetting to follow a direction
1. Turn the compass until the wanted direction (say 320) is under the black index line.
2. Turn the bezel ring until its glow line sits on the north arrow. The compass is **preset**.
3. Then hold it center-hold and turn your body until the north arrow is back on the glow line. The sighting wire now points at your direction.

## The click method (in the dark)
1. Turn the bezel until the glow line is over the black index line.
2. Divide the direction you want by 3.
3. If the direction is **under 180**, turn that many clicks **counterclockwise**. Example: 51 / 3 = 17 clicks counterclockwise.
4. If it is **over 180**, take it away from 360, divide by 3, and turn that many clicks **clockwise**. Example: 330 gives 30 / 3 = 10 clicks clockwise.
5. Hold center-hold and turn until the north arrow is on the glow line.
6. If your direction is not exactly divisible by three, rounding up moves the target to your left and rounding down moves it to your right.

## Quick right angles at night
Without changing the bezel, to turn 90 degrees right, turn until the glow letter **E** is under the glow line. To turn 90 degrees left, turn until the **W** is under it.

## Things that spoil the reading
Keep the compass away from metal and electricity. Suggested distances:
- High-voltage power lines: 55 meters.
- A vehicle: 18 meters.
- Fences, barbed wire, phone and telegraph wires: 10 meters.
- Heavy steel machinery: about 2 meters.
- A steel helmet, gun, knife or belt buckle: about half a meter.
Phones and magnets also affect it. Nonmagnetic metals such as aluminum and brass do not.

A good compass is accurate to about 3 degrees. Check it against a known line, and do not use one that is off by more than that.

> If you carry the compass open, fold the rear sight flat against the glass so the dial locks and the glass is protected.
