A compass is simple, but it can be wrong. Test it before a trip, and again when something happens to it.

## Test 1: does it point the right way?
Hold it flat, well away from metal. The red end should point to north. Check it against a second compass, a known landmark, or a spot where you know the direction (a street that runs exactly north-south on your map).

## Test 2: does it settle?
The needle should settle quickly and without sticking. Tilt the compass a little. The needle should dip and then swing level. A needle that sticks, or a large bubble, means the compass is damaged.

## Test 3: a field bearing check
1. Pick a clear landmark and take a bearing to it.
2. Walk a measured distance to the side and back, or use a known bearing between two points on your street.
3. Compare. If your results are off by more than a few degrees, find out why.

## Things that cause errors
- **Metal and electronics.** Cars, fences, power lines, steel tools, phones, radios, watches and belt buckles.
- **Magnetic rock.** In a few places the ground itself pulls the needle. Compare with a landmark.
- **Holding it tilted.** The needle can drag on the dial.
- **Wrong declination.** The most common one.
- **Cold.** In very low temperatures the liquid may thicken or form a bubble. Keep the compass inside your coat until you need it.

## Storage
- Do not store it near a magnet, a speaker, a motor or a radio.
- Keep a spare in a different bag.
- Practice with it every few months.

> A compass you trust is one you have tested.
