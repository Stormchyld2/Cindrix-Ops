## Going around an obstacle
You come to a swamp, a fence or a flooded field in your way. To stay on your line:
1. Turn 90 degrees. To turn **right**, add 90 to your direction. To turn **left**, subtract 90.
2. Walk far enough to clear the obstacle. Count the paces.
3. Turn back to your original direction and walk past the obstacle.
4. Turn 90 degrees the other way and walk the same number of paces as in step 2.
5. You are back on the original line. Carry on.

Example: walking east on 90 degrees. Turn to 180 (south) for 100 m. Turn to 90 and go 150 m. Turn to 360 (north) for 100 m. Turn to 90. You are back on your line.

**Count only paces forward.** The paces sideways to dodge the obstacle are not part of your forward distance.

If you can see something across the obstacle on your direction, simply walk round the obstacle and aim for that thing. Or choose a landmark behind you and take its back direction so you can look back to get lined up again.

## Aiming off (a deliberate offset)
You are heading for a hut, bridge or camp that stands beside a long line such as a road, stream, power line, railway or ridge. Your compass work cannot be perfect, so you may reach the line and not know which way to turn.

To fix that, **aim to one side on purpose**.
1. Choose a side, say left. **Subtract** about 10 degrees from your direction (add 10 to aim right), so you end up well to the left of the target.
2. Walk until you hit the long line.
3. You know the target is to the right, so turn right and follow the line.

Each degree of offset moves you about **18 meters per 1,000 meters** walked. 10 degrees over a kilometer puts you about 180 meters to one side. Make the offset big enough to be more than your likely error.

## Why not aim straight?
Aim straight and you'll get to the line either side of the target and have to guess. A bit of extra walking is better than a search.

> Plan for a few extra steps rather than an aimless search once you reach the long feature.
