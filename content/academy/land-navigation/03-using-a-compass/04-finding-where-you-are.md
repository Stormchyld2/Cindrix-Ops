If you are lost but have a map and compass, you can find your position by two methods. Use the easier one first.

## 1. Terrain association (look and match)
1. Orient the map.
2. Look at the big features around you: hills, a lake, a road, a power line, a river bend.
3. Find the same features on the map. The place where several of them are in the right direction and the right distance is your position.
4. Check with the contour lines: are you on a slope, a ridge or in a valley? Does the map agree?

This often works with no compass work at all.

## 2. Resection (two or three bearings)
Use when you can see at least **two known landmarks** that you can find on the map.
1. Take a compass bearing to the first landmark. Correct for declination (magnetic to map: add east, subtract west).
2. Work out the **back bearing** (add or subtract 180).
3. Draw a line on the map from the landmark along the back bearing. You are somewhere on that line.
4. Repeat with a second landmark. **The crossing of the lines is your position.**
5. A third landmark makes a small triangle. If it is small, you are in the middle. If it is large, check your bearings.

Best results come when the landmarks are **far apart in direction**, about 60 to 120 degrees, not almost in a straight line.

## 3. When you are on a line feature
If you are on a road or a stream, one bearing to a known landmark plus the line of the road gives your position where the two lines cross. This is called a **linear resection**.

## Check
A position is only good if it makes sense. Are you really on the steep ground the map shows? If not, find the mistake before you move.

> Note the time and the position in a notebook every hour. If you get lost, the last good fix is your starting point.

? In a resection, what do you draw on the map from each landmark?
+ A line along the back bearing
- A circle
- A line along the forward bearing
> You are on the line from the landmark back toward you.
? What gives the best position from two landmarks?
+ Landmarks in very different directions
- Landmarks in a straight line from you
- Landmarks very close to each other
