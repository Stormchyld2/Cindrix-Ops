Skills you have never used will fail you when you are stressed. Spend a few afternoons building the skills in this course on safe, known ground.

## Week 1: the map
1. Take a map of a park or your neighborhood. Find your house and three landmarks.
2. Walk a loop and name each feature on the map as you pass it.
3. Have each person draw a simple map from memory, then compare.

## Week 2: the compass
1. Measure your pace count over 100 meters on level ground and on a hill.
2. Take a bearing to a tree 100 meters away. Walk to it. How close were you?
3. Do a square: walk 100 paces on 0 degrees, then 90, 180 and 270. You should end up where you started.

## Week 3: a route
1. Plan a three-leg route in a park. Write a route card.
2. Walk it with a partner who has the map. The person with the compass leads.
3. Try it with a hat pulled down, to practice following only the compass.

## Week 4: the lost drill
1. A parent picks a spot. Others use the map and compass to find where they are, using terrain association and a resection.
2. Practice STOP: sit down, think, observe, plan, out loud.
3. Practice a whistle: three blasts, and answer.

## Make it a game
Hide small items at grid references or bearings and distances: "from the big oak, 130 degrees, 80 paces". Children learn quickly this way.

## Tracking progress
Use the Academy's quiz for each lesson, and note your pace counts and best results in the journal.

> Keep the map, compass, notebook and pencil in the same bag every time. When you need them, you will know where they are.

## Going deeper: make progress measurable
Navigation is learned by doing. A four-week plan works best when each session has a goal, a place and a way to check success.

## Tips
- **Practice in daylight first.** Add dusk and darkness after you are confident.
- **Go in pairs.** Compare answers, and teach each other.
- **Keep a log:** date, place, what you practiced, what went wrong and what you will fix.
- **Set a standard:** for example, finding a marker within 20 meters from a bearing and a pace count.
- **Practice declination** and check your numbers.
- **Mix terrain:** open fields, woods, hills and towns.

## Add stress gradually
Time yourself, use poor light, walk with a heavy pack, or have a partner mislead you with a false clue to see if you check. Practice relocating after a deliberate mistake.

## Safety
Tell someone where you are going, carry water and a whistle, and keep to safe, known ground. Do not practice in extreme weather.

## Reward progress
Mark milestones. Make a game: a family treasure hunt using bearings and grid references makes practice fun for children.
