A map of your own area, drawn and marked by you, is one of the most useful things you can make before trouble comes.

## What to put on it
- **Home** and the roads and paths out of it in every direction.
- **Water**: streams, ponds, wells, hydrants, springs. Mark which are safe to drink without treatment (probably none) and which need treatment.
- **Shelter**: schools, churches, libraries, stores, community halls.
- **Medical**: hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, vets.
- **Hazards**: dams, chemical plants, rail lines, flood zones, bridges that may fail, steep ground.
- **Resources**: farms, hardware stores, fuel, fire stations, ham radio operators you know.
- **Meeting places**: see below.

## Meeting places
Choose, in writing:
1. **A place near home** (outside the house) for a sudden emergency such as a fire.
2. **A place in the neighborhood** if you cannot get home, such as a park, a school or a church.
3. **A place outside the area** if the neighborhood is closed, reached by a route you have walked.
Mark each with a **grid reference** or a bearing and distance from a landmark. Write it on your comms plan and in every pocket-sized notebook.

## Routes
For each meeting place, plan **two routes** (a main one and a backup), one avoiding the main roads. Walk them on foot, by bicycle and by car if possible. Note the time each takes.

## Copies
Make several copies, in pencil and permanent marker, on paper in plastic bags: one in the house, one in each vehicle, one in each go-bag, and one with a friend or relative far away.

## Update
Review it every six months. New roads, fences and building works change the best way.
