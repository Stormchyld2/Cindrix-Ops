**Orienteering** is a sport of finding marked points with a map and compass, anywhere from a park to a forest. It is a very good way to practice for real and it is fun for all ages.

## Kinds of games
- **Route:** a guide walks a route. Others trace it on their own maps and circle the markers they notice. No timing.
- **Line:** follow a line drawn on the map, and circle each marker you find.
- **Cross-country (point-to-point):** visit all the markers in the same order. The fastest wins. With 6 to 12 markers, there is a choice between a short hard way and a long easy way.
- **Score:** many markers, each with points (the farther or harder, the more). A time limit, such as 90 minutes. You pick which to visit. Finishing late loses points. Plan first.

## Techniques to learn
- **Pacing:** measure your distance on a 100 meter course.
- **Thumbing:** fold the map small and keep your thumb next to your last known spot. Move it as you go, so you never have to search the whole map to find where you are.
- **Handrails:** follow a path, fence, stream or other line.
- **Attack points:** a clear, permanent landmark near the marker. Go there, then move precisely.
- **Orient the map by the land, not the compass,** whenever you can.

## Marker descriptions
- When the marker is on a feature that is on the map, say "**the** pond". If the feature is not on the map, say "**a** trail junction", and make sure no similar feature is within 25 meters.
- For large things, say which side: "the east side of the pond".
- Don't use bushes or animals.

## Safety
- Give every group a **return time**. Everyone comes back at that time, finished or not.
- Choose a **safety lane**: a road, fence or river along the edge of the area. A person who is lost or tired sets a "panic direction" on the compass and walks until they reach it.
- A whistle, a first aid kit and water at the start and finish.
- Never use hazardous ground for markers, and send no one alone if there is any risk.

## Real orienteering maps differ
They are large scale (about 1:15,000) with 5 meter contours, they show tiny details like boulders, and they have north lines drawn to magnetic north, so no declination conversion is needed. The legend changes from map to map, so read it.

> A game with a prize for the best route is the best way to teach children map reading.

## Check yourself
? In a score event, what do you do?
+ Choose which markers to visit within a time limit
- Visit all in order
- Walk only on roads
> You plan a route for the highest points.
? What is thumbing?
+ Keeping your thumb on the map at your last known place
- Counting on your fingers
- Marking a tree
> It shows you where you are on the map quickly.
? What is a safety lane?
+ A long feature on the edge of the area to walk to if lost
- A fast route
- A shortcut
> Anyone lost sets a direction and walks to it.
