If your compass is lost or broken, nature can give you a rough direction. These methods are not exact, but they are good enough to keep you walking in a straight line toward a known road or river.

## The sun
- The sun rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west. It is exactly east and west only twice a year, at the equinoxes.
- In the Northern Hemisphere, the sun is due **south** at midday (at its highest). In the Southern Hemisphere, it is due **north**.

## The shadow stick
1. Push a straight stick upright into level ground in the sun.
2. Mark the tip of its shadow with a small stone.
3. Wait 15 minutes or more. The shadow tip moves. Mark the new tip.
4. A line through the two marks runs roughly **west to east**. The first mark is west and the second is east.
5. Stand with the first (west) mark on your left and the second (east) mark on your right. You are now facing north, in either hemisphere.

## The watch method (an analog watch with hands)
- **Northern Hemisphere:** point the hour hand at the sun. Half way between the hour hand and 12 is **south**. In daylight saving time, use the 1 o'clock mark instead of 12.
- **Southern Hemisphere:** point 12 at the sun. Half way between 12 and the hour hand is **north**.
- This is rough. It is less reliable close to the equator.

## The stars
- **Northern sky:** find the Big Dipper. The two stars at the end of its bowl point to **Polaris**, the North Star, about five times the gap between them. Polaris is within about one degree of true north.
- **Southern sky:** find the Southern Cross. Draw a line along its long axis, about four and a half times its length, then straight down to the horizon. That point is roughly **south**.

## Clues that mislead
Moss, tree shapes and ant hills are **not** reliable. Do not use them alone.

> Whatever method you use, pick a far-off landmark in that direction and walk to it. Then pick another.
