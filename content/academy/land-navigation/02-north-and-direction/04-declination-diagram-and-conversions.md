A compass gives **magnetic** directions. A map grid is drawn to **grid north**. The angle between them is the **grid-magnetic angle (G-M angle)**, printed in the declination diagram in the map margin. The diagram's lines are not drawn to scale, so read the number.

## Direction of the angle
- **Magnetic north to the right (east) of grid north:** east declination.
- **Magnetic north to the left (west) of grid north:** west declination.

## The quick rules
- **East declination:** map (grid) direction to compass = **subtract**. Compass to map = **add**.
- **West declination:** map (grid) direction to compass = **add**. Compass to map = **subtract**.

## Always draw it
A slip of 1 degree matters, so do not trust memory. Draw the picture.
1. Draw a vertical line for **grid north** (parallel to the map's vertical lines).
2. From its base, draw any line out to the side to stand for the direction you are converting.
3. Draw a second line from the base for **magnetic north**, to the right (east) or left (west) as the map shows. Mark the G-M angle between the two north lines.
4. Look at the picture. If the direction you want is larger than the one you have, add the G-M angle; if smaller, subtract it.

## Rules for a direction that goes past 0 or 360
There are no negative directions. 0 and 360 are the same. If you need to subtract and the number is too small, add 360 first (2 degrees is the same as 362 degrees). If the answer goes past 360, subtract 360.

## Examples
- East declination 10 degrees. Map direction 90 degrees. Compass = 90 - 10 = **80**.
- West declination 11 degrees. Map direction 40 degrees. Compass = 40 + 11 = **51**.
- East declination 21 degrees. Map direction 15 degrees. 15 + 360 = 375, minus 21 = **354**.

## Checking a map is lined up with the compass
Put the compass with its straight edge on a north-south grid line, cover pointing to the top of the map. Turn map and compass together until the needle is under the index line.
- **West declination:** the compass should now read **the G-M angle** (11 degrees west gives 11).
- **East declination:** it should read **360 minus the G-M angle** (21 degrees east gives 339).

## Using a protractor on the map
Lay the protractor's center on your starting point with its base line along a vertical grid line, and 0 at the top. Read the angle to the second point. The result is a **grid** direction, so convert it for the compass. Always measure **from** your start **to** your target. Reading from the wrong end gives you the back direction.

> Many modern compasses can be set for declination. If yours can, set it and write the number on the back anyway.
