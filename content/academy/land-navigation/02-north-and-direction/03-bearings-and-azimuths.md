To give a direction, we use the **angle from north**, measured clockwise. It is written in **degrees** from 0 to 360, and called an **azimuth** or a **bearing**.

## The circle
- North is 0 (also 360).
- East is 90.
- South is 180.
- West is 270.
- Northeast is 45, southeast 135, southwest 225, northwest 315.

A full turn is 360 degrees. Speak the number digit by digit on the radio: "two seven zero" for west.

## Magnetic or true?
Always say which one you mean. A bearing read from a compass needle is **magnetic**. A bearing drawn on the map is **true** (or grid). Use the declination lesson to convert.

## The back bearing
A **back bearing** (back azimuth) is the opposite direction, the way you came.
- If the bearing is **less than 180**, **add 180**.
- If the bearing is **180 or more**, **subtract 180**.

Examples: 60 becomes 240. 300 becomes 120.

## Why it matters
- A bearing lets you describe a direction to someone else without a map: "go 240 degrees for two kilometers."
- A back bearing lets you return, or check a landmark you are using.
- You will need both for finding your position, in a later lesson.

## Quick practice
Your trail goes at 135 degrees. What is the back bearing? (315.)

> Write bearings with a small circle (135°) and the letter M or T if you may mix them up.
