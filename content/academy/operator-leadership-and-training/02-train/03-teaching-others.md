A skill that only one person has is a weakness. Teaching spreads it and deepens your own mastery.

## How people learn
- **Adults** want to know why, and learn best by doing and solving real problems.
- **Children** learn through play, repetition and short sessions.
- **Everyone** remembers more when they practice, not just listen.
- Stress reduces learning, so start in a calm setting.

## Teach with the four steps
1. **Tell:** explain what and why in simple words.
2. **Show:** demonstrate at normal speed, then slowly with each step named.
3. **Do:** the learner practices while you coach.
4. **Review:** the learner explains or repeats alone to the standard.

A short phrase: "I do, we do, you do."

## Plan a lesson
- **Objective:** one clear skill.
- **Time:** 10 to 30 minutes.
- **Materials:** ready beforehand.
- **Safety:** brief the risks.
- **Steps:** the order.
- **Check:** how you will know they learned it.

## Coaching
- Correct errors **right away,** kindly and specifically.
- Praise what is right.
- Ask questions: "What would you do if...?"
- Be patient. Skills take repetition.
- Let them struggle a little. Do not take over.

## Teaching children
- Short, fun, active.
- Games and stories help.
- Give real jobs: carry the radio, check the smoke alarm, pour water.
- Teach "stop, drop and roll," how to call 911, the rally point, and when to get an adult.

## Teaching elders
- Respect experience.
- Allow more time.
- Consider eyesight, hearing and joints.
- Use large print and clear steps.

## Check learning
Ask them to **teach you back.** If they can teach it, they know it.

## Record
Mark the person's progress on the Academy sign-off page, with the date and the standard met.

## Become an instructor
Take recognised courses: Red Cross first aid and CPR instructor training, CERT train-the-trainer, NOLS and others.
