In a crisis, people look to someone to decide. A leader does not need rank. A leader needs a clear head, a plan and the trust of the people involved.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- Spot people risks early: exhaustion, conflict, fear, poor skills, unclear roles.
- Know each person's health, limits and strengths.
- Remove avoidable friction: unclear jobs, missing gear, no schedule.

## 2. Preparedness
- Written roles, a chain of command and a backup for each role.
- Training and rehearsals for every key task.
- Agreed rules before the emergency, so they are not argued during it.

## 3. Response
- Take charge calmly, make decisions, give clear orders, check they are understood.
- Keep people fed, rested, informed and busy with useful work.
- Watch for injuries and stress.

## 4. Mitigation
- Spread tasks so no one is overloaded or alone in a critical role.
- Rotate shifts. Cross-train.
- Protect morale and fairness.

## 5. Recovery
- Review what happened with an after-action review.
- Care for those who are hurt or shaken.
- Fix the plan and train again.

## What leaders do
- **Be:** a person of good character, calm and honest.
- **Know:** the job, the people and the plan.
- **Do:** decide, direct, motivate and set the example.

## The family leader
In a family, the leader may be a parent or another adult, but every capable member leads something: water, medical, communications, children, food. Shared leadership with one clear overall decision-maker works best.

> Preparing people is the leader's first job. The second is looking after them.
