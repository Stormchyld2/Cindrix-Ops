A good briefing makes sure everyone knows the situation, the job and what to do if things change. The military five-paragraph order is a good model. Here it is for civilian use as **SMEAC**.

## SMEAC
1. **Situation:** what is happening. Hazard, weather, time, who is where, what resources we have.
2. **Mission:** the one-sentence goal. "Move to the shelter by dark." "Be ready to leave in 20 minutes."
3. **Execution:** the plan. Who does what, in what order, when, and the routes. Include what to do if the plan fails.
4. **Administration and logistics:** food, water, medicine, gear, transport, rest, rules.
5. **Command and communication:** who is in charge, who takes over if that person is lost, how we communicate, signals, check-in times, and the rally point.

## A short example
**Situation:** A storm is coming tonight. The power may go out for days. All eight of us are home.
**Mission:** Be ready for a long power outage by 6 pm.
**Execution:** Dad fills the water containers and checks the generator. Mom sets up the kitchen and charges devices. Sam and Jo bring in the lanterns and radios. Aunt Lee checks on the neighbors. We meet at 5:30 to check progress.
**Administration:** Food in the pantry. Water in the garage. Medicine is in the red bag.
**Command:** Dad is in charge. Mom is second. We use the handheld radios on channel 3 if we need to split up. The meeting place if we have to leave is the school parking lot.

## The five-minute briefing
- Keep it short, standing and facing everyone.
- Use a map, board or paper.
- Ask: "What questions do you have? Tell me back what your job is."
- Write it down.

## Intent
Always share your **intent:** the purpose behind the plan. If things change, people can adapt and still reach the goal. "We are leaving to get the children away from the smoke. If the road is blocked, go to the second route."

## Prepared contingencies
Say "if... then..." for likely problems: "If you cannot reach me, go to the rally point and wait one hour."

## Warning order
When you do not have time for a full plan, give a **warning order:** a heads-up with what is coming and when to be ready.

## Rehearse
Walk through the plan with the people who will carry it out. Practice leaving the house, loading the car and finding the rally point.

## Debrief
Use a quick review after the event: what happened, what went well, what to improve.
