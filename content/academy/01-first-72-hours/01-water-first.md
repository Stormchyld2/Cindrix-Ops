Most people can live about three days without water, but clear thinking fails sooner when you are short of it. In most home emergencies, water is the first thing to secure.

## How much
- Plan on **one gallon per person per day** for drinking and basic hygiene.
- Add more for children, nursing mothers, sick people and hot weather.
- Count pets too.

## In the first hour
1. If you know the supply may be cut off, fill clean bathtubs, pots and bottles right away.
2. Know where your hidden water is: the water heater tank, toilet tanks (not the bowls, and not if you use cleaning tablets) and melted ice.
3. Do not drink flood water or water you cannot trust.

## Making water safe
- **Boiling** is the surest method. Bring it to a rolling boil for **1 minute** (3 minutes above about 6,500 feet). Let it cool and keep it covered.
- If you cannot boil, plain unscented household bleach can disinfect clear water. Follow the label on your bottle, wait the full time it says, and the water should smell faintly of chlorine.
- Cloudy water: let it settle, then pour it through a clean cloth before you boil it.

> Store treated water in clean, covered containers and label them with the date.

## Check yourself
? About how long can a person last without water?
+ About three days
- About three weeks
- About one day only
> The rule of threes: 3 minutes without air, 3 hours in harsh weather without shelter, 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food.
? How much water should you plan per person per day?
+ About one gallon
- About one cup
- About ten gallons
> One gallon covers drinking and basic hygiene. Add more for heat, illness, children and nursing mothers.
? Water is cloudy and you can make a fire. What is the best order?
+ Let it settle, strain it through clean cloth, then boil it
- Boil it, then strain it
- Drink it right away if it looks fine after settling
> Straining first removes particles; boiling then kills germs. Settling and straining alone do not make water safe.
