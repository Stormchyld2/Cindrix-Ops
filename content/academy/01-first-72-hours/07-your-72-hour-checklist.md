## The 72-hour checklist
**Water:** one gallon per person per day for three days, a way to treat more, and a way to carry it.
**Food:** three days of food you will actually eat, a manual can opener, plates, utensils, and a way to cook outdoors.
**Warmth:** blankets or sleeping bags, extra layers, hats and gloves, and a safe heat plan with a carbon monoxide alarm.
**Light and power:** flashlights, headlamps, spare batteries, power banks, chargers.
**Information:** a battery or crank radio with weather band.
**First aid:** a kit, prescription medicines, and basic over-the-counter supplies (see the Medical course).
**Sanitation:** a bucket, bags, soap, hand sanitizer, toilet paper, wipes and trash bags.
**Documents:** copies of IDs, insurance, medical information and contacts, in a waterproof bag.
**Cash:** small bills.
**Tools:** a multi-tool, tape, a whistle, a fire extinguisher, work gloves and a wrench for utility shutoffs.
**People:** needs of babies, older adults, pets and anyone with a disability.
**Comfort:** a deck of cards, books and toys.

## The household plan
- **Meeting places:** one outside your home and one outside your neighborhood.
- **Out-of-area contact:** a person everyone calls or texts.
- **Roles:** who gets the kit, who checks utilities, who checks neighbors.
- **Triggers:** when you stay and when you go.
- **Practice:** hold a drill twice a year.

## The household drill
Run an "outage weekend" at least once a year:
1. **Turn off the main power** (not the water heater gas) for 12 to 24 hours, or all weekend.
2. **Live on your kit:** your stored water, food, light and warmth.
3. **Note what you miss.** Write it down: a can opener, a charger, a medicine, an entertainment for the kids.
4. **Fix the gaps** the next week.
5. **Repeat** and extend.

## Keep it fresh
- **Check dates** when the clocks change.
- **Rotate** food and water.
- **Update** the household details: contacts, medicines and sizes of clothing.
- **Use the Inventory screen** in this program to track what you have and when it expires.

## Run it as a real test
A checklist on paper tells you what you intend to have. A drill tells you what you actually have. This weekend, pick a Saturday and run a 24-hour household test, with the main water, power and heating switched off if conditions allow.

## The drill, step by step
1. **Gather everyone** and explain the scenario: a storm has cut power and water for three days.
2. **Set the clock.** Note the start time and keep a notepad.
3. **Live on your supplies.** Use only stored water, camp-stove cooking and battery light.
4. **Keep a log** of what runs out, what is missing and what surprised you.
5. **Debrief together** at the end. What worked? What did not? Who did what?
6. **Fix the list** and restock before the next drill.

## What drills usually reveal
- Flashlights without batteries, or batteries in the wrong size.
- No can opener, or no lighter for the stove.
- Water that tastes of its container.
- Food the children refuse.
- A phone charger that does not fit the power bank.
- A child who cannot tell you your address or phone number.
- A house that is much colder or hotter than expected.

## Keep it fresh
Tie the review to events that happen anyway: when the clocks change, review the batteries, alarms and food dates. Replace the water twice a year. Update the list after any move, birth or change in health.
