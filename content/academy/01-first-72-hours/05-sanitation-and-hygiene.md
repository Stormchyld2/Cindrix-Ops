[video] Sanitation and hygiene

Disease kills more people after disasters than the disasters themselves. Clean hands and safe toileting are the best protection.

## Handwashing
Wash your hands:
- **After using the toilet** or changing a diaper.
- **Before** eating or preparing food.
- **After** touching garbage, raw meat or sick people.
- **After** cleaning up.

How: wet, soap, scrub for **20 seconds,** rinse and dry with a clean cloth. A little water goes a long way, using a bottle with a small hole or a jug with a tap. If you have no water, use **alcohol-based hand sanitizer** with at least 60 percent alcohol, but wash when you can, since sanitizer does not work on dirty hands.

## If the toilets will not flush
When water or sewer service is out, do not use the toilet as normal. A blocked sewer line can back up.

### A bucket toilet
1. **Line a 5-gallon bucket** with a heavy plastic bag. Add a second bag.
2. **Add a toilet seat** or pool noodle on the rim, if you have one.
3. After each use, **add a handful of absorbent material:** cat litter, sawdust, wood shavings, dry soil or a small amount of household bleach powder.
4. **Cover** with a lid.
5. **Tie the bag** when it is half full, double bag, and store it in a container with a lid in a shaded spot, away from food and water.
6. **Follow local advice** on disposal. If services are down for a long time, you may need to dig a pit (see below).

### A pit latrine for long outages
- Dig at least **100 feet away** from water sources, wells and streams, and downhill from them, and away from where you eat.
- Make it **2 to 3 feet deep.**
- Cover each use with a layer of soil.
- Mark the spot, and cover it when it is full.

## Personal hygiene
- **Wash your body** with a little soap and a cloth, face and hands, armpits and groin, and feet. Keep feet dry.
- **Brush teeth,** using treated water.
- **Keep clothing clean,** and change socks and underwear.
- **Menstrual and baby supplies:** keep a stock of pads, tampons, diapers and wipes. Seal used items in bags.
- **Trim nails** and keep wounds clean and covered.
- **Keep your sleeping area clean.**

## Trash
- **Bag** all garbage and keep it closed, away from food and living areas.
- **Burying or burning** is for long outages and only if allowed. Never burn in a closed space.
- **Keep pests away:** they carry disease.

## Cleaning and disinfecting
- Use **a solution of household bleach and water** to disinfect surfaces: follow the label for mixing. Make a fresh batch each day, because bleach loses strength. **Never mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners.**
- **Clean first, then disinfect.**
- **Wear gloves** and eye protection.

## Warning signs of illness
Diarrhea, vomiting and fever can cause dehydration. Give small, frequent sips of clean water or oral rehydration solution. **Seek medical care** for blood in the stool, high fever, signs of dehydration, or illness in a baby, older adult or sick person.

> Wash your hands, keep human waste away from water and food, and keep the garbage covered.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.

## Why this matters most
In most disasters the people who die of disease die of dirty water, dirty hands and mishandled waste, not of exotic infections. Diarrheal illness is the most common serious problem in shelters and after floods, and it is almost entirely preventable with a few simple habits. That makes sanitation a top priority, alongside water and food.

## Build a bucket toilet
1. **Line** a five-gallon bucket with a heavy trash bag.
2. **Add** a few inches of absorbent material, such as cat litter, sawdust or shredded paper.
3. **Fit** a toilet seat if you have one, or use a seat made for buckets.
4. **After each use,** add a scoop of absorbent material.
5. **Tie the bag** when it is two-thirds full, and place it inside a second bag.
6. **Store** it away from food and living areas, in a covered container, until it can be collected or disposed of as the authorities direct.
7. **Wash your hands** every time.

## Handwashing, step by step
Wet, soap, scrub for 20 seconds including between fingers and under nails, rinse, dry with a clean cloth or paper. A tippy-tap or a jug with a spout saves water. Use hand sanitizer with at least 60 percent alcohol if you have no water, and wash with water when you can.

## Keep clean without much water
Use wet wipes or a washcloth, wash key areas daily and change underwear and socks. Brush teeth. Keep nails short. Women and girls should have what they need for periods, and everyone should have a way to deal with incontinence if needed.

## When someone is sick
Keep sick people apart from others if you can, give them their own towel and cup, clean surfaces with a disinfectant, and wash hands after caring for them. Watch for dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea, and call a clinician for severe signs. This course does not give medicine amounts, so follow the label and ask your pharmacist.
