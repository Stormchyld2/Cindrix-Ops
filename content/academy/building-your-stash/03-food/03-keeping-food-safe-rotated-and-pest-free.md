> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

## Check often
Every few months, look through the stash:
- Dented, swollen, rusty or leaking cans: **throw them out**. A bulging can may hold botulism.
- Smells, mould, sticky packages, pests: throw out.
- Move the oldest food to the front and use it.

## Pests
- Rodents chew bags and boxes. Use metal, glass, or hard plastic with tight lids.
- Insects can be in flour and grains when bought. Seal and store cool.
- Keep the area clean and dry. Seal gaps.

## If the power fails
- A **closed fridge** keeps food cold about four hours. A full **freezer** about two days if closed (about one day if half full).
- Throw out refrigerated food that has been above 40 °F for more than two hours.
- **When in doubt, throw it out.**

## Cook safely
- **Never use a grill, camp stove or generator inside** the house, garage or tent. They give off carbon monoxide, which kills without warning.
- Cook outside, away from walls and trees, with a bucket of water nearby.

> A stash only helps if the food is safe to eat. Check it, and never taste food to test it.

## Going deeper: power cuts and food safety
FoodSafety.gov gives these rules when the power goes out:
- A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about **4 hours**. A full freezer holds its temperature for about **48 hours**, and a half-full freezer for about 24 hours, if you keep the door shut.
- Group food together in the freezer, since it stays cold longer in a tight pack.
- Use an appliance thermometer. Food above **40 °F** for more than two hours is unsafe, and above 90 °F for more than one hour.
- **Throw out** meat, poultry, fish, eggs, milk, soft cheeses and leftovers that have been warm too long. When in doubt, throw it out.
- Do not taste food to decide. Some dangerous germs have no smell or taste.
- Dry ice can help in a large cooler. Handle it with gloves, in a ventilated space, never in a closed car or room.

## Cans and jars
Do not use cans that are bulging, leaking, deeply dented along a seam, or spurting when opened. Discard them without tasting. Rinse and wash the can top before opening. Home-canned jars that leak, bulge or smell bad should be thrown out safely, away from pets and people.

## Pests
Check bins regularly for webbing, holes, droppings or small flying insects. Use metal or thick plastic with tight lids. Clean up spills at once. Bay leaves in flour bins and freezing flour before storage help against moths and weevils. Keep a sealed bin of pet food too, since it draws pests.

## Cook safely
Wash hands with soap and clean water (or sanitizer when water is short). Cook meat to safe internal temperatures, keep raw and cooked foods apart, and refrigerate leftovers fast where you can. Use boiled or treated water to wash produce and dishes.
