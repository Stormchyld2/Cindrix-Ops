For rice, dried beans and similar dry food, sealed storage can keep it good for many years. Here is how to do it right.

## The right bucket
- Use **food-grade buckets**, marked with a **food-safe symbol** (a cup and fork) or labelled food grade, usually made of HDPE (plastic code 2).
- **Many plain hardware-store buckets are not made for food.** They can pass chemicals into it. If you are not sure, put the food in a **Mylar bag first** or use a food-grade liner inside the bucket.
- A **gamma-style screw lid** makes a bucket easy to open and close, and keeps pests out.
- Ask a bakery or deli for free used food-grade buckets, and wash them well.

## Mylar bags and oxygen absorbers
1. Fill a Mylar bag with the dry food.
2. Add the oxygen absorber sized for the bag, from the supplier's chart.
3. Squeeze out the extra air, seal the bag with a hot iron or hair straightener, put it in the bucket and close the lid.
4. Work fast. Absorbers start working as soon as the packet is open. Keep the rest in a sealed jar.

## What works and what does not
- **Good:** white rice, dried beans, wheat, oats, pasta, dried potatoes, sugar, salt, dried milk.
- **Not good for this method:** brown rice, whole grain flour, nuts and anything oily (they go rancid), and **any moist food**. Sealing moist food with no air can allow dangerous germs such as botulism to grow.
- Food must be properly dry.

## Write on the bucket
Name of food, date, and your own use-by guess.

> Dry, clean, sealed and labelled. If in doubt about moisture, do not seal it.
