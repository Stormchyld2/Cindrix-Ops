> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

Treat any water that might be unsafe, even if it looks clean. Clear water can still carry germs.

## Step 1: Clear it
If cloudy, let it settle and pour it through a clean cloth or coffee filter.

## Step 2: Make it safe, in this order of preference
1. **Boil.** A rolling boil for **one minute**. At high altitude (above about 6,500 feet), boil for three. Let it cool and keep it covered. This is the best method.
2. **Plain household bleach.** Use bleach with only sodium hypochlorite in it, no scents or added cleaners, and check the label for strength. The common guide is about **8 drops per gallon** of clear water for the usual 5 to 9 percent kind, 16 for cloudy water. Stir, wait **30 minutes**. The water should smell faintly of chlorine. If not, repeat once and wait another 15 minutes. Bleach weakens with age, so use fresh bottles. Read the exact amounts on the EPA or CDC page for your bottle.
3. **Purification tablets** made for water, following the packet.
4. **A water filter** that is rated to remove germs. Read what it removes. Many do not remove viruses unless they are rated for it.

## What none of these fix
Boiling, bleach and most filters **do not remove chemicals**, fuel, pesticides or heavy metals. Do not use water that smells of fuel or chemicals, or that is from a flooded industrial area.

## If you cannot treat it
Do not drink water you are unsure of. A day without is better than a week of diarrhoea.

> Save your best water for drinking and make rainwater and pond water safe first.

## Going deeper: what each method does and does not do
- **Boiling** kills viruses, bacteria and parasites. Bring water to a full rolling boil for one minute, or three minutes above about 6,500 feet. Let it cool covered. It does **not** remove chemicals, fuel or heavy metals.
- **Household bleach** works if it is plain and unscented, with no added cleaners. Follow the label or CDC directions for the amount per volume, mix, and wait at least 30 minutes. Cloudy or cold water needs longer or more careful treatment. Bleach is weaker against some parasites.
- **Filters** differ widely. Look for a label that says it removes bacteria and protozoa, such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium, and check whether it also handles viruses. Filters with a pore size of 0.1 micron or smaller (absolute, not nominal) are best. Many camp filters do **not** stop viruses, so add boiling or a chemical step when the source may be contaminated with sewage.
- **Purification tablets** (chlorine dioxide or iodine) are light and good for a bag. Follow the package. Iodine is not suited to pregnant women or people with thyroid trouble.
- **Distilling** removes many chemicals and salts but is slow and needs fuel.

## Clear it first
Pour cloudy water through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter, or let it settle, then pour the clear water off the top. Dirt shields germs from treatment.

## Choose your source with care
Moving, clear water from high up is safer than a pond. Avoid water that is downstream of farms, towns, dead animals or industrial sites, and never drink floodwater, because it may carry sewage and chemicals that nothing here removes. Prefer stored water and home sources first, and treat found water as the last resort.

## After treating
Store treated water in a clean, covered container and label it. If it smells or tastes odd, let it sit open, or pour it between two containers to bring back some air. Wash your hands before you handle drinking water, because dirty hands re-contaminate it.
