**Calcium hypochlorite** (sold as pool shock) is a powerful, cheap, long-lasting way to disinfect water. A small container treats a great deal. It is also a **strong oxidiser**, so handle it with real care.

## Buy the right kind
- Pure **calcium hypochlorite**, at about 65 to 78 percent strength, with **no other ingredients**.
- **Not** the stabilised chlorine pucks (trichlor), not "shock plus algaecide", not clarifier or scented kinds.
- Read the label for added ingredients.

## Step 1: make the stock solution (the "mother" batch)
Do this **outside**, wearing gloves and eye protection.
- **US measures:** stir **one heaping teaspoon** of granules into **two gallons** of water.
- **Metric:** **5 mL (about 7 grams)** of granules into **7.5 litres** of water.
- Stir until the granules are gone. Pour into a clean plastic or glass jug with a lid.
- This stock is strong (about 500 parts per million of chlorine). **It is not for drinking.**
- Label the jug **"CHLORINE STOCK - DO NOT DRINK"** with the date. Keep it closed, cool and dark. It weakens over days, so make a fresh batch about every week or two, only when you need it.

## Step 2: add the stock to your water (1 part stock to 100 parts water)
| Water to treat | Stock to add |
|---|---|
| 1 quart (1 litre) | 2 teaspoons (10 mL) |
| 1 gallon (4 litres) | 2 1/2 tablespoons (about 40 mL) |
| 5 gallons (20 litres) | about 3/4 cup plus 1 tablespoon (200 mL) |
| 10 gallons (40 litres) | 1 1/2 cups plus 2 tablespoons (about 400 mL) |
| 55-gallon drum (200 litres) | about 9 cups, or 2 quarts and 1 cup (2 litres) |

Rule to remember: **about 1 cup of stock for every 6 1/2 gallons.** For any other amount, use **one part stock to 100 parts water**.

## Step 3: mix, wait, test
1. Stir or shake well.
2. **Wait 30 minutes** (longer if the water is very cold).
3. Smell it. It should have a **faint chlorine smell**. If there is none, add the same amount of stock again and wait another 15 minutes.
4. If the water is cloudy, **let it settle and strain it first**, because mud and dirt use up the chlorine.
5. If it tastes strongly of chlorine, pour it back and forth between clean containers, or let it stand uncovered for a few hours.

## A worked example
You have a 5-gallon water jug from a stream. Strain it, add **3/4 cup and 1 tablespoon** of stock, stir, wait 30 minutes, smell for chlorine. Done.

Always check these amounts against the current CDC page ("Making Water Safe in an Emergency") before you rely on them.

## Handle it safely
- **Wear gloves and eye protection.** Do not breathe the dust.
- **Never mix** with other chemicals, acids, fuel, oil, brake fluid or cleaners. It can start a fire or give off poisonous gas.
- Keep it **dry, cool, in its original tightly closed container**, away from fuel, paint and anything that burns. Never in a hot car or a small closed shed.
- Keep it **away from children and pets.**
- Use a clean measuring spoon only for this.

> A little goes a long way, and so does a mistake. Treat it like the strong chemical it is.

## Going deeper: handling a strong chemical sensibly
Calcium hypochlorite pool shock is a strong oxidizer. It is stable in a cool, dry, sealed container, but it can start a fire or release toxic chlorine gas if it touches fuels, acids, ammonia, oils, other pool chemicals or even a small amount of water in a closed space. Treat it as a hazardous material, not as a pantry item.

## Rules that prevent accidents
- **Buy only the plain kind.** Check the label for the active ingredient and the percentage. Avoid products with added algaecides, stabilizers, clarifiers or fragrance.
- **Keep it away from living space.** Store it outdoors, or in a cool, dry, ventilated shed or garage, in its original sealed container, on a high shelf out of reach of children and pets.
- **Keep it apart** from fuel, paint, cleaners, fertilizer, vinegar and any other chemical, since mixing is the main cause of fire and gas release.
- **Never mix the dry powder** with other chemicals, and never add water to a bucket of dry powder. Add powder to water slowly, outdoors or in good airflow.
- **Wear protection:** splash goggles, chemical-resistant gloves and a long-sleeved shirt. Do not breathe the dust or fumes.
- **Use dedicated tools** and containers. Label each one so nobody drinks from them.
- **Dispose of spills** carefully. Sweep up dry powder with a clean, dry tool into a clean container and follow your local hazardous-waste rules. Flood small wet spills with plenty of water outdoors.

## Test your batch if you can
Chlorine test strips or a pool test kit let you check the strength of your stock solution, since the powder weakens over time. If you cannot measure it, use boiling or a tested filter instead.

## When not to use it
If you are unsure of the product, the numbers or the safety steps, do not guess. Boiling and filtering need no chemicals. Pool shock is a backup for larger volumes, used only by an adult who has practiced, read the label and follows these steps exactly.
