> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

## Light
- **Low light that runs a long time** is better than a very bright light that dies fast. You only need to see your hands and the next few steps.
- A **headlamp** keeps hands free. Many have a red setting that keeps your eyes used to the dark.
- Keep a lantern for a room, and spare batteries stored **outside** the devices, in a cool place.
- Consider **candles** carefully: they cause house fires. Use sturdy holders, never leave them alone, and keep away from curtains and children.

## Heat
- Dress in layers. Close off rooms. Use blankets and sleeping bags.
- **Never** burn charcoal, run a grill, a camp stove, a gas oven or a generator **inside a home, garage or tent.** The invisible gas, carbon monoxide, kills. Run a generator outside, well away from windows and doors.
- Install **battery-powered carbon monoxide and smoke alarms** and test them.
- Keep a **fire extinguisher** where you can reach it.

## Power
- A **power bank** keeps phones charged for a while.
- A **hand-crank or battery weather radio** gets warnings when the internet is down.

## Fuel
- Store fuel only in approved containers, outside the living space, away from heat.
- Do not store more than the law and your fire code allow.

> A dim light and a working alarm beat a bright light and a house fire.

## Check yourself
? Which light is usually more useful in a long outage?
+ A low light that runs a long time
- A very bright light with a short life
- A candle left burning
- None
> Run time matters.
? Where must you never run a generator?
+ Inside the house or garage
- Outside, away from windows
- In the open yard
- On the lawn
> Carbon monoxide builds up indoors.
? Which alarm should you have?
+ A battery carbon monoxide alarm
- A doorbell
- A clock
- A bell
> It warns you of invisible gas.
