> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

Some of the most useful items are plain and cheap. Here is the short list and how to use each one well.

## Cord
**Tarred bank line** or paracord. Bank line is strong, thin and holds knots well. Paracord has an inner core you can use. Either is fine. Practise a few knots: the bowline, the clove hitch and the taut-line hitch.
> Use cord for shelters, repairs and tying loads. **Do not use it as stitches in a wound.** That causes infection.

## Tape
Buy good **cloth duct tape**. Keep it on the **original roll**, flat, in a cool dry place, and swap old rolls out. Old tape dries and loses grip.
> Tape is for sealing, patching and gear. **Do not use it to seal a chest wound.** A proper vented chest seal is made for that (see the Casualty care course).

## Heavy contractor trash bags
Strong, cheap, and light. Use them as:
- a rain poncho or pack cover
- a ground sheet
- a lining for a bucket toilet
- a carrier for rubbish and dirty clothes
> **Never put a bag over a child's head or your own.** In a bag shelter, breath and sweat can soak you, so keep air moving.

## Lighters and matches
Keep **several lighters** and a box of waterproof matches in different places. Keep them dry and **never leave a lighter in a hot car**. Heat can make it burst or leak. Always have a backup, as cold and wet affect lighters.

## Pry bar and zip ties
A **flat pry bar** is a good tool for jammed doors, boards and nails. Heavy **zip ties** bundle, lash and repair gear. Use them for tools and repairs only.

## Work gloves, a utility knife, a hammer and a multi-tool
Gloves protect hands in rubble. Keep tools clean and dry.

> Cheap does not mean careless. Learn each tool before you need it.
