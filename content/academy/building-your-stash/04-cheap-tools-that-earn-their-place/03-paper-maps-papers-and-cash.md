> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

## A paper map
When phones fail, a paper map still works.
- Get a map of your **county and the counties around you**, and a state road map.
- Free topographic maps are available from the **US Geological Survey**. Print them at a print shop, or order them. Laminate or put them in a clear sleeve.
- Mark **water sources, hospitals, shelters, fuel and your routes**. Plan **three ways** home from work and from school, and a few ways out of town. Highways may be blocked, so learn the back roads.
- Practise reading it. See the course on map reading and land navigation.

## Papers
Keep **copies** of IDs, insurance papers, bank details, medical lists and phone numbers on paper, and a copy on an encrypted drive. Cindrix Safe is built for this. Put paper copies in a waterproof bag.

## Cash
Machines and cards may not work. Keep **small bills** in a safe place.

## Contacts
Write down phone numbers and addresses by hand. Choose a **meeting place** near home and another farther away. Pick one person out of the area for everyone to call.

> If it is not on paper, it may not be there when you need it.

## Going deeper: the paper that matters
After a disaster, phones fail, networks jam and offices close. Paper and cash keep working.

## Maps
Keep a paper road map of your region and a local street map in each car and in your bag. Mark home, work, school, relatives, hospitals, shelters, and at least two ways out of town, in case one road is blocked. Practice reading it. The Land Navigation course shows how.

## Papers
Make copies of the documents you would need to prove who you are and to restart life:
- IDs, passports, birth and marriage certificates, Social Security cards
- Insurance policies, with the agent's phone number
- Deeds, leases, vehicle titles and registrations
- Bank, credit card and investment account lists, with contact numbers
- Medical records, immunization records, and a list of each person's medicines and allergies
- Pet records and vaccination papers
- Photos of family members and pets, in case you get separated
Keep one set in a waterproof, fire-resistant bag in your go-bag, one at a trusted relative's home and a protected digital copy (encrypted) on a drive or in a secure vault.

## Cash
ATMs and card readers fail in a power cut. Keep a small stash of cash in small bills, such as twenties, tens, fives and ones, so you can buy fuel, food and supplies without needing change. Hide it in more than one safe place.

## Contacts
Write down key phone numbers and addresses on paper, including family, doctors, your out-of-town contact, work, school and insurance. Phones die and you may not remember numbers. Agree on a family contact person outside your area, because long-distance calls and texts often work when local lines are jammed.

## Update
Check the papers every year when you change the batteries in your smoke alarms.
