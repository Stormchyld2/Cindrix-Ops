> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

## Where to store
- **Cool, dry, dark.** Heat and damp spoil food fast. A basement, a closet on an inside wall, under beds.
- Not in a hot attic, a hot garage, or a car in summer.
- **Off the floor** in case of flood.
- **In more than one place.** If the house is damaged, you can still reach some of it.
- Keep a small kit in each car and one at work.

## Keep it tidy and low-key
Tell only people you trust. A neat shelf draws no attention. Do not post pictures of it online.

## Keep a list
A list you can read at night is worth more than a full cupboard you cannot remember.
- Write what you have, how many, and the **use-by date**.
- In Cindrix Ops, use the **Inventory** screen and the Cindrix Scan phone app to scan items in and out.
- Check the list against the shelf every few months.

## First in, first out
Put new items at the **back** and pull old items from the **front**. Eat from the stash and replace it.

> If you cannot find it, you do not have it. Label, list and tidy.

## Going deeper: the enemies of stored supplies
Heat, damp, light, pests and temperature swings spoil more stored food than time does. A cool, dry, dark, **steady** place is best. Food kept at a steady cool temperature lasts far longer than food that bakes in an attic or freezes in a shed.

- **Heat:** keep food off hot walls, away from water heaters and out of sunny rooms. Many foods last noticeably longer at about 60 °F than at 90 °F.
- **Damp:** avoid basement floors that sweat. Put items on shelves or pallets, never straight on concrete.
- **Light:** it fades labels and breaks down some foods and plastics. Use opaque bins or a closet.
- **Pests:** mice chew through cardboard and thin plastic. Use metal, glass or thick food-grade plastic for anything edible.
- **Flood and fire:** keep key supplies above the floor, and keep a second small cache somewhere else, such as a relative's home, so one disaster cannot take everything.

## Spread it out
Do not keep everything in one spot. A basic set in the kitchen, a grab-and-go bag by the door, a small kit in each car and some water in the garage means you can reach something from wherever you are when trouble starts.

## A simple inventory
Use a notebook or the program's inventory screen. For each item, note what it is, how many, where it is stored and its use-by date. When you buy something, write it down. When you use something, cross it off and add it to your shopping list. Every few months, walk the shelves with the list and fix the differences.

## Quiet, not secret
Tidy, labeled bins in a closet draw no attention. You do not need to hide your supplies from your own family, so make sure every adult knows where things are and how to use them.
