> Basic planning guidance. Prices change, so this course names no prices. Check anything about chemicals or food storage against the sources named in the lesson before you rely on it.

The best test of a stash is to **live on it for a day or two** while the power is on and help is a phone call away.

## Plan it
- Pick a weekend. Tell everyone the plan.
- Turn off the **main power switch** at set times, or just agree not to use electricity.
- Use only your **stored water** and **stored food**. No shop trips.
- Cook as you would in an outage, **outside and safely**.

## Watch for
- What ran out first?
- What did no one like?
- What did you not know how to use?
- Was it hard to find things?
- What were the children's worries? Were older relatives warm and comfortable?

## Afterwards
- Write down what went wrong and **fix it that week**.
- Update the list and the shopping list.
- Do it again in six months, a little longer.

## Make it fun
Play cards. Read by lamp. Teach the children a knot. People who enjoy practice keep doing it.

> Better to learn it was missing on a Sunday than in an ice storm.

## Going deeper: a plan for the weekend
A practice weekend finds the gaps while a store is still open. A good one runs 24 to 48 hours.

## Friday evening
Brief everyone on the rules and the goal: no running water from the tap, no grid power and no store runs. Decide the safety limits: anyone can stop the test at any time, and medical needs always come first.

## Saturday
- Shut off the main power at the breaker if it is safe, or simply do not use outlets.
- Use only stored water. Treat and filter some water to test your method.
- Cook all meals from stored food, using your camp stove outdoors.
- Use the emergency toilet setup and handwashing plan.
- Light the home with lanterns and headlamps. Keep warm with blankets rather than the thermostat, but only within safe temperatures for babies and older people.
- Listen on the battery radio. Practice your communications plan.
- Take a walk with a map. Find your meeting places.

## Sunday
- Work out your evacuation: grab the bags, load the car, and time yourself.
- Review the food, water, light and warmth you used.
- Hold a family meeting.

## Afterwards
Write down what failed, what took too long, what everyone disliked and what was missing. Make a shopping list and a task list. Repeat the test in six months, with a different scenario, such as a winter storm, a long summer outage or an evacuation.

## Keep it fun
Games, board books, cards and a family movie night by battery light make it a memory, not a chore. Children who practice stay calmer in real emergencies.
