**Easy first: beans, peas, tomatoes, lettuce, peppers.**

## How
1. Leave bean and pea pods on the plant until dry.
2. Scoop tomato seeds into water for a few days, rinse and dry.
3. Let one lettuce plant bolt and collect the seed heads.
4. Always save from your best plants.

## Careful: squash and corn
- They cross with other types. Keep varieties far apart.
- Plant a block of corn, not a single row.

## Potatoes
- They are grown from tubers, not seed.

> Dry seed fully before storing.

## Check yourself
? Which crop is easy for a beginner to save seed from?
+ Beans
- Corn in a tiny plot
- Seedless watermelon
> Beans pollinate themselves, so types rarely cross.
? Why keep corn varieties apart?
+ Wind carries pollen and they cross
- They fight
- They need different soil
> Corn pollen travels on the wind.
? Which plants should you save seed from?
+ Your best ones
- Your weakest ones
- Any at random
> Saving from the best improves the line.
