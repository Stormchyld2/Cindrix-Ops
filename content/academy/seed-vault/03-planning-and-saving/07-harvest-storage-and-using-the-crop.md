A harvest has to be picked at the right time, stored properly and used well.

## Harvest at the right time
- **Greens:** pick leaves often. Cut and come again.
- **Peas and beans:** pick young for fresh eating, and leave on the plant to dry for storing.
- **Tomatoes and peppers:** pick when fully colored. Pick all green tomatoes before the first hard frost and ripen them indoors.
- **Squash:** summer squash young and small. Winter squash when the skin is hard and the stem dries.
- **Corn:** sweet corn when the silks brown and kernels give milky juice. Field corn when dry.
- **Potatoes:** after the tops die back.
- **Onions and garlic:** when the tops fall over and dry.
- **Carrots and beets:** when they reach size, and before hard freeze (they sweeten after light frost).
- **Cabbage:** when heads are firm.
Harvest in the cool of the morning when possible.

## Curing
- **Onions and garlic:** lay in a warm, dry, shaded, airy place for 2 to 3 weeks until necks are dry.
- **Potatoes:** 1 to 2 weeks in a dark, cool, humid place.
- **Winter squash and pumpkins:** about 10 days in a warm, dry place.
- **Sweet potatoes:** about a week in a warm, humid place.

## Storing
- **Root cellar or cool room:** 32 to 40 °F and humid for carrots, beets, cabbage, apples and potatoes.
- **Cool, dry, dark, airy:** onions, garlic, winter squash (50 to 55 °F).
- **Dry beans and grain:** dry thoroughly and store in sealed containers, with pests controlled (freeze first).
- **Do not store** apples next to potatoes (ethylene gas spoils them).
- **Check** stored crops often and use damaged ones first.

## Preserving
Follow tested methods only (see the Survival basics course, and the USDA National Center for Home Food Preservation):
- **Pressure canning** for low-acid foods.
- **Water-bath canning** for high-acid foods, jams and pickles.
- **Dehydrating** for fruit, vegetables, herbs and jerky.
- **Freezing** (needs power).
- **Fermenting** (sauerkraut, pickles).
- **Salting and sugaring.**
Do not invent canning recipes. Botulism is deadly.

## Using the crop
- **Eat the perishable first.**
- **Plan meals** around what you have.
- **Share** surplus with neighbors, or a food bank.
- **Save** the best for seed.
- **Compost** the rest.

## Winter and cover crops
After harvest, plant a cover crop, or mulch the beds. Plant garlic in fall.

## Review the year
Write down what you grew, how much, what worked and what to change. Update your seed list and your plan for next year.

## Checklist
- Know harvest signs for each crop
- A place to cure and store
- Tested preservation methods
- A year-end review

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
