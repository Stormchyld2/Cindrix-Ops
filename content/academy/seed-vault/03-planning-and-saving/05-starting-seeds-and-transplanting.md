Many crops do best if you start them indoors early, or sow them directly in the soil at the right time.

## Direct sow or start indoors?
- **Direct sow** (plant straight in the garden): beans, peas, corn, carrots, beets, radishes, lettuce, squash, cucumbers, melons, turnips and spinach. Many dislike root disturbance.
- **Start indoors** and transplant: tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, cabbage family, onions and celery, and in short-season areas, squash and melons.
- **Check** the packet for timing.

## Seed starting step by step
1. **Count back** from your last frost date. Tomatoes and peppers: 6 to 8 weeks before. Cabbage family: 4 to 6 weeks. Squash and cucumbers: 2 to 4 weeks.
2. **Containers:** trays, cell packs, yogurt cups or egg cartons with drainage holes. Clean them in a weak bleach solution and rinse.
3. **Seed-starting mix:** a light, sterile mix, not garden soil. Moisten it.
4. **Sow** seeds at the depth on the packet (a rule of thumb is two to three times the seed's width). Cover lightly.
5. **Label.**
6. **Warmth:** most seeds sprout at 70 to 80 °F. A heat mat or a warm spot helps.
7. **Moisture:** keep damp, not soaked. A clear lid or plastic wrap holds humidity until sprouting, then remove it.
8. **Light:** once sprouted, seedlings need strong light, 12 to 16 hours a day. A sunny south window is often not enough. Use a shop light or LED grow light 2 to 4 inches above the plants. Raise the light as they grow.
9. **Air:** a gentle fan strengthens stems and prevents damping off.
10. **Feed** weakly with diluted fertilizer after the first true leaves appear.
11. **Thin** to one seedling per cell. Snip the weaker ones.
12. **Pot up** to larger containers if they outgrow the first.

## Hardening off
Seedlings raised indoors are tender. Over **7 to 10 days,** set them outside for a little longer each day, in a sheltered, shady spot, then in more sun and wind. Bring them in if a frost or hard rain threatens.

## Transplanting
1. **Wait** until the danger of frost is past, and the soil is warm. Plant warm-season crops after the last frost.
2. **Choose** a cloudy day or evening.
3. **Water** the plants well first.
4. **Dig** a hole as deep as the root ball. Tomatoes can be planted deeper, burying part of the stem, so they root along it.
5. **Space** as the packet says.
6. **Firm** the soil gently.
7. **Water** well.
8. **Protect** with a row cover, cloche or shade cloth if needed.

## Direct sowing tips
- **Soil temperature** matters. Beans, corn and squash need warm soil (60 °F or more). Peas and spinach tolerate cool soil.
- **Depth:** two to three times the seed's width.
- **Keep** the seedbed moist until sprouting.
- **Thin** to the spacing on the packet.
- **Mark rows** with labels.
- **Protect** from birds and pests.

## Common problems
- **Leggy seedlings:** too little light. Add more.
- **Damping off:** seedlings collapse from fungus. Use clean containers and sterile mix, avoid overwatering, and add airflow.
- **Slow sprouting:** too cold or too dry.
- **Transplant shock:** water well, harden off, and shade for a few days.

## Checklist
- A planting calendar based on frost dates
- Trays, mix, and a light
- A hardening-off plan

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
