Put the course together into a plan you can follow, and a vault you can trust.

## Year 1: learn and build
**Winter**
- Find your frost dates and zone.
- Make a garden map (100 to 200 square feet).
- Buy or gather a starter set of open-pollinated seed: beans, peas, lettuce, tomatoes, carrots, kale, squash, herbs.
- Store it properly, and log it in the Seed vault screen.
- Order a soil test.
**Spring**
- Test germination of any old seed.
- Prepare beds with compost.
- Start tomatoes and peppers indoors, and direct sow peas and greens.
**Summer**
- Plant warm-season crops. Water and mulch. Keep a journal.
- Save seed from beans, peas, lettuce or tomatoes (easy ones).
**Fall**
- Harvest and store. Plant garlic. Add cover crops.
- Review the year: what worked, what to change.

## Year 2: grow and diversify
- Expand the garden by 50 to 100 percent, adding calorie crops: potatoes, winter squash, corn and dry beans.
- Add a compost system and a rain barrel.
- Save more seed, including tomatoes (fermenting) and herbs.
- Try one biennial (carrots or beets) for seed.
- Preserve some of the harvest (drying, canning).
- Share seed with a neighbor.
- Test all stored seed.

## Year 3: build resilience
- Aim for a garden that produces a meaningful part of your calories.
- Keep a **reserve** of seed that you do not plant, stored in a second location.
- Teach someone else.
- Start a seed swap, or join a seed library.
- Consider fruit trees, berries and perennials (asparagus, rhubarb) for long-term food.
- Think about small livestock if you have the space and rules allow.
- Update your plan.

## The vault contents (a sample)
- **Seed for planting this year and next:** about 2 years of each crop you grow.
- **Reserve seed:** a smaller amount of each, in a separate place, sealed and frozen if possible.
- **Seed for sharing.**
- **A log** of every packet: crop, variety, year, source, test date and result.

## A yearly checklist
- Winter: inventory, test, order and plan.
- Spring: prepare soil, start seeds, plant.
- Summer: water, weed, harvest, save seed.
- Fall: harvest, store, plant garlic and cover crops, review.

## Keep going
- **Join** a local gardening group or Master Gardener program (your Extension office runs one).
- **Read** and practice.
- **Share** what you learn.
- **Keep** the vault, the soil and the skills alive.

> A seed vault is a promise to your future self. Keep it, plant it, and pass it on.

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
