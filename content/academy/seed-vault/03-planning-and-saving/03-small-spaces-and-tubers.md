No yard? Little space? You can still grow real food.

## Containers
- **Size matters:** bigger pots hold more water and soil. Use at least 5 gallons for tomatoes and peppers, 10 gallons or larger for squash and potatoes, 1 gallon or more for herbs and greens.
- **Drainage:** every container needs holes in the bottom.
- **Soil:** use potting mix, not garden soil. Mix in compost.
- **Water:** containers dry out fast. Check daily in summer.
- **Feed:** nutrients wash out. Feed with compost, compost tea or a balanced fertilizer.
- **Materials:** food-safe buckets, grow bags, wooden boxes, barrels and old tubs all work. Avoid containers that held chemicals, and treated wood for edible crops.
- **Location:** sunny, sheltered and near a water source.

## Vertical growing
- **Trellises** for pole beans, peas, cucumbers and small squash.
- **Cages and stakes** for tomatoes.
- **Hanging baskets** for herbs, lettuce and strawberries.
- **Wall planters** and shelves.

## Raised beds
Beds 3 to 4 feet wide and 6 to 12 inches high (or more) improve drainage, warm up early, and avoid bad soil. Fill with a mix of topsoil and compost. They are a good fit for small yards and poor soil.

## Windowsills and indoor growing
- **Herbs,** microgreens, sprouts and lettuce grow on bright windowsills. Add a grow light in dark homes.
- **Sprouts** need no light and are ready in days.

## Potatoes: the calorie crop
- **Seed potatoes:** use certified seed potatoes. Cut large ones into pieces with 2 or 3 eyes, and let them heal for a day or two.
- **Planting:** 2 to 4 weeks before the last frost, in loose soil. Plant pieces 4 inches deep, 12 inches apart.
- **Hilling:** as stems grow, mound soil around them, to cover the stem and increase the crop. Potatoes exposed to light turn green and become toxic, so keep them covered.
- **In containers:** put 4 to 6 inches of soil in a large bucket or bag, plant, and add soil as the plants grow.
- **Harvest:** when the plants die back, dig carefully. Cure potatoes in a dark, cool, humid place for 1 to 2 weeks, then store at about 40 to 50 °F in the dark.
- **Never eat green potatoes** or sprouts. They contain solanine, which is poisonous.

## Sweet potatoes
- **Slips:** grow from slips (rooted sprouts), planted after the soil is warm.
- **Need** a long warm season (90 to 120 days) and loose soil.
- **Cure** the tubers in a warm, humid place for about a week, then store at room temperature.
- **Greens** are edible, too.

## Other tubers and roots
- **Garlic and onions:** plant garlic cloves in fall for harvest next summer.
- **Jerusalem artichokes:** easy but can spread, and may cause gas.
- **Carrots, beets, turnips and radishes:** grow well in deep containers.
- **Ginger and turmeric:** in warm climates.

## What to grow in small spaces first
Easy, productive crops: leaf lettuce, kale, chard, radishes, green onions, bush beans, cherry tomatoes, peppers, herbs, zucchini and potatoes in a bag.

## Checklist
- A sunny spot for containers or a raised bed
- Good potting mix and a way to water
- A potato bag or bucket to try
- A trellis for vertical crops

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
