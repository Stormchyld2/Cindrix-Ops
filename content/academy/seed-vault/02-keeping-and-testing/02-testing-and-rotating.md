Old seed may not grow. Test it before you depend on it, and use the oldest seed first.

## The germination test
Do this each year for seed you plan to use, and before relying on any packet more than a year or two old.
1. **Count out 10 seeds** (or 20 for a better estimate).
2. **Dampen** a paper towel so it is wet but not dripping.
3. **Place the seeds** on half of the towel, spaced out. Fold the other half over them.
4. **Slide** the towel into a plastic bag or a container with a loose lid (to let a little air in).
5. **Label** with the crop and date.
6. **Keep it warm,** about 70 to 80 °F (on top of the refrigerator or in a warm room). A few crops like cooler conditions (lettuce, spinach).
7. **Check** every few days and keep the towel damp.
8. **Count** the number that sprouted after the usual germination period: 3 to 7 days for beans, peas, lettuce and radish; 7 to 14 for tomatoes and most vegetables; 14 to 21 for carrots, parsley and peppers.
9. **Calculate** the germination rate: sprouted seeds divided by the number tested, times 100.

## What the result means
- **80 to 100 percent:** excellent. Plant as usual.
- **60 to 80 percent:** good. Plant a bit thicker.
- **40 to 60 percent:** weak. Sow twice as thick, and plan to replace the seed.
- **Under 40 percent:** poor. Replace it or use it for a thick sowing and expect gaps.
- **Zero:** discard, and check storage.

## Sow thicker to make up
If your test shows 50 percent germination, sow twice as many seeds as the packet says. You can thin extra seedlings later.

## Rotate your stock
1. **Date** every packet.
2. **Plant** the oldest seed first.
3. **Replace** what you plant with fresh seed or seed you saved.
4. **Never** plant all of one batch. Keep a reserve.
5. **Track** what you have and when it expires, using the Seed vault screen.
6. **Review** each winter, before you order.

## Make planting a habit
Plant at least a portion of your vault every year, so you replace seed and practice. If you plant none for several years, the seed ages and your skills fade.

## Record results
Keep a log: crop, variety, year, germination rate and notes on how the plants did. Over time it becomes your local guide.

## Troubleshooting a bad test
- **Moldy towel:** too wet, or seed was moist.
- **No sprouting:** seed too old, too cold, or killed by heat or moisture. Retest with a fresh towel.
- **Some seed does not germinate right away:** some have dormancy (parsley, some herbs, and some perennials) and need cold or soaking. Check the crop's guide.

## Checklist
- A yearly germination test for the seed you plan to use
- Packets dated, and a plan to use the oldest first
- Replacement seed on a schedule

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
