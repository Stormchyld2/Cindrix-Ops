Many people pass the Technician exam after a few weeks of study. It is a good group project.

## Study tools
- **A current study guide** for the exam you are taking (the ARRL, Gordon West and others publish them). Make sure it matches the current question pool.
- **Free practice sites and apps** such as HamStudy.org and hamexam.org, which give you the real questions.
- **A class** from a local ham club, often a weekend, with free or low-cost materials.
- **A study partner.** Quiz each other.
- **Flash cards** for the formulas and band plans.

## A study plan
1. **Week 1:** read the rules and regulations chapters, and the safety chapter.
2. **Week 2:** radio waves, antennas and feed lines. Read, then quiz.
3. **Week 3:** basic electronics: voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's law.
4. **Week 4:** operating practices and emergency communications.
5. **Practice exams.** Keep taking practice tests until you pass them **comfortably** (for example 85 percent).
6. **Review** the questions you miss. Find out why the right answer is right.

Don't memorize without understanding. A few ideas, such as how frequency and wavelength relate, appear in many questions.

## Find an exam session
Exams are given by **Volunteer Examiners (VEs)** accredited through a **Volunteer Examiner Coordinator (VEC).** Find sessions through:
- **ARRL** (arrl.org/find-an-amateur-radio-license-exam-session)
- **Local ham clubs**
- **Online exam sessions,** which several VEC groups offer by video

## On exam day
Bring:
- **Photo identification.**
- **Your FCC Registration Number (FRN)** from CORES (create it first).
- **Payment** for the exam fee.
- **A pencil, and a simple calculator** (if allowed; a basic one is fine).
- **Your current license and copies,** if upgrading.

At the session:
- Examiners check your ID, and take your fee.
- You take the test on paper or on a computer.
- It is graded right away.
- If you pass, you are told, and your application goes to the FCC. Your call sign usually shows up in the FCC database in a few days.
- If you fail, you can usually try again for another fee, sometimes on the same day.

## After you pass
- **Check the FCC site** for your call sign, then print your license.
- **Get on the air.** Join a local **net,** talk on a repeater, and try simplex.
- **Join a club** and ask for a mentor (an "Elmer").
- **Buy a radio** that covers both 2-meter and 70-centimeter. Many handhelds do.
- **Join an emergency group** in your county.
- **Plan your upgrade** to General.

## For groups
- **Study together** one night a week.
- **Share the cost** of books and exam fees.
- **Schedule an exam session** with a local club to test several people at once.
- **Record** who passed, and when it expires, in the Academy sign-off page.

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
