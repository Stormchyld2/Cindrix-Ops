A radio is only useful if it is charged, in good order and part of a plan everyone knows.

## Batteries and power
- **Charge** all radios and spares every month, and after every use.
- **Rechargeable lithium packs** hold a charge but wear out over several years. Store at about half charge if unused for months, in a cool place.
- **Carry spare packs,** or a battery case that holds AA batteries as a backup.
- **Do not leave radios** in a hot car or in the freezer. Cold cuts battery life, so keep the radio inside a pocket.
- **Charge from many sources:** wall, car, power bank and solar panel. Test them.
- **Use a USB charging cable** with your radio if it supports it, and keep spare cables.
- **Turn the radio down** (lower power) when you can, and **turn it off** when not needed, to save battery. Use the "monitor" schedule in the plan.

## Radio care
- Keep them **dry and clean.** Use a bag in the rain.
- **Check the antenna** and the connector for damage.
- **Protect from drops** with a case.
- **Label** each radio with the owner's name and unit number. (Do not put a phone number where it is easily seen.)
- **Keep manuals** or a one-page guide for each model.

## Test regularly
- **Check batteries** monthly.
- **Run a radio check** weekly. Everyone calls in at a set time.
- **Check every radio** has the same channel plan. Use CHIRP and the group's file.

## Group radio plan
Write a one-page plan and give each member a copy:
1. **Primary channel** (for example a GMRS channel the group uses).
2. **Alternate channel** if the first is busy or noisy.
3. **Emergency monitoring** schedule: who listens, and when (for example the top of each hour for ten minutes in a long outage).
4. **Call names** for each person or home.
5. **Check-in times** and how to run a roll call.
6. **Who relays** to the outside, and how (a ham in the group).
7. **Code words** are not needed. Use plain language.
8. **What to do if radios fail:** runners, signs, whistles.
9. **Where the radios are kept** and who charges them.

## Security and privacy
- **Do not share personal details** on the air: names of children, addresses, your plans to leave, supply details.
- **Assume you are overheard.** Others may use scanners.
- **Do not use encryption** to hide your meaning (it is not allowed in the amateur and GMRS services).
- **Talk about the situation,** not about what you have.

## Keep learning
- **Join a local club.**
- **Listen** to nets to learn.
- **Take part** in drills and field days.
- See the **Communications course** for the full plan, nets and the PACE plan.

## Checklist
- All radios charged and tested this month
- Spare batteries and charging sources ready
- A one-page group radio plan handed out
- A weekly radio check on the calendar
- No personal details on the air

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
