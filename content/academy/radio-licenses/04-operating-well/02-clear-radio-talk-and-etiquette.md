Good radio habits get messages through, and keep a channel usable when many people share it.

## Plain language
- **Use plain words,** not "ten-codes" that differ between agencies. FEMA's incident system (NIMS) asks responders to use plain language in emergencies.
- Say what you mean. Say **where, what and what you need.**
- **Keep it short.** A radio channel is shared.
- **No chatter** on emergency channels.

## How to make a call
1. **Listen first.** Make sure the channel is clear.
2. **Press the push-to-talk button, wait a second,** then speak. Hold the radio a few inches from your mouth.
3. **Say who you are calling, and who you are:** "Home base, this is Scout One."
4. **Wait for a reply.** If none, try once more, then change position.
5. **Say your message** in short pieces.
6. **Confirm the message was understood.** Ask for a read back for numbers and names.
7. **End** with your call sign (required on GMRS and ham) and release the button.

## Words that save time
- **"Say again"** (not "repeat," which can mean something else in military use).
- **"Copy"** or **"Roger"** means "I understood."
- **"Stand by"** means wait.
- **"Over"** means I am done and expect a reply (optional).
- **"Out"** means the conversation is over.
- **"Break"** is used to interrupt for an emergency.
- **"Emergency, emergency, emergency"** starts a real emergency call. Use it only for real emergencies.

## Phonetic alphabet
Use it for names, call signs and spelling: Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.
Say numbers one digit at a time ("one-five" for 15), and say "niner" for 9 if there is confusion.

## Identifying yourself
- **GMRS:** use your call sign at the end of a conversation and every 15 minutes during one. Family members use the licensee's call sign.
- **Ham:** identify at least every 10 minutes and at the end of a contact.
- **FRS, MURS, CB:** no call sign is required, but use agreed names (call names) in your group.

## Good manners
- **Do not interrupt** a conversation unless it is an emergency.
- **Do not hog** a channel. If a channel is busy, move to another agreed one.
- **Do not argue** on air.
- **Use agreed channels** and keep the emergency channel clear.
- **Say thank you.**
- **Stay calm.** Speak slowly and clearly, because fear speeds you up.

## Handling an emergency call
1. **Listen,** and write down the location and what is needed.
2. **Say you copy,** and tell them to stand by.
3. **Get help:** use a phone if one works, or relay to a station that can reach emergency services.
4. **Keep the channel clear** and keep helping until relieved.
5. **Write down** the times and what was said.

## Practice
Run a **radio drill** every month: a roll call, a message relay with written messages, and a test of an alternate channel.

## Checklist
- Everyone knows the call format and the common words
- Everyone can use the phonetic alphabet
- Call names agreed for the group
- A monthly drill on the calendar

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
