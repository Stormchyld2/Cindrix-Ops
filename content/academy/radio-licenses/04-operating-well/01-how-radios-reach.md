The number on the box ("up to 36 miles") is a dream. Real range depends on a few things you can understand and plan for.

## Line of sight
VHF and UHF signals (the ones handhelds use) travel mostly in straight lines.
- **Open ground, and height, give range.** On a hilltop with no trees you may talk for many miles.
- **Hills, buildings, and dense woods block** or weaken signals.
- **Cities** with steel and concrete can limit a handheld to a few blocks.
- **Inside buildings** costs you range. Go to a window or outside.

## Power
Handheld radios put out between 0.5 and 5 watts. More power helps but not as much as height and a better antenna. **Doubling range takes about four times the power,** so a few extra watts do little.

## Antennas
- **The antenna matters more than the radio.**
- Replace a short rubber antenna with a longer, better one where the rules allow (not on FRS, where the antenna is fixed).
- **Vehicle** antennas mounted on the roof with a proper feed line give much better range than a handheld.
- A **base station** antenna on a mast or roof gives the best reach.
- Keep antennas **vertical,** because most are vertical and you need to match.
- **Never transmit without an antenna connected,** because it can damage the radio.

## Simplex and repeaters
- **Simplex:** radios talk directly on the same frequency.
- **Repeater:** a tall, powerful station that **receives on one frequency and re-transmits on another,** extending range a lot.
- You need to know the repeater's **input and output frequencies,** the **tone** (CTCSS or PL) needed to open it, and whether it is open to you.
- Repeaters need power, and may fail when the grid does. Many hams run on batteries and solar. Do not depend on one repeater.

## Tones and codes
- **CTCSS and DCS** are tones that tell a receiver to open only for your group. They do **not** give privacy, because others can still hear you if they listen without the tone.
- Use them to avoid hearing other users, not to hide anything.

## Test your range before you need it
1. Take two radios and drive or walk away from each other in steps.
2. At each stop, call and listen, and note where signals get weak.
3. **Draw a map** of your reliable range for the group, and record it.
4. **Test indoors,** in the car, and at key sites such as your homes, the meeting spot and the rally points.

## Improve range
- Move to higher ground.
- Move away from metal and walls.
- Use a better antenna.
- Hold the radio upright, and do not cup your hand around the antenna.
- Use a repeater.
- **Relay:** one member in a good spot passes messages between others.
- Lower your data. Short, clear messages are easier to hear than long ones.

## Checklist
- Tested range between your group's homes
- Know the difference between simplex and a repeater
- Know tones do not give privacy
- Wrote down the weak spots and plan for relays

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
