Typing channels into a handheld radio one button at a time is slow. **CHIRP** is free, open-source software for Windows that lets you fill in a table of channels on a computer and send it to the radio through a programming cable. It works with a long list of radios, including most Baofeng and other low-cost handhelds.

**The installer is on this drive.** Open the **resources** folder at the top of the drive, then the **CHIRP** folder, and run the installer there. It installs on the computer, so you do not need the internet. Nothing in Cindrix Ops runs it for you.

CHIRP is made by its own volunteer team, not by CindrixOps. Its home is chirpmyradio.com, and the program is licensed under the GPL-3.0, which lets you copy and share it. A newer version may exist by the time you read this. If you have internet, check that site before relying on the copy on the drive.

## Before you start
- You need the **right programming cable** for your radio. Cheap cables often need a driver, and a cable that does not match the radio is the most common reason programming fails.
- Charge the radio first. A radio that turns off partway through programming can be left in a strange state.
- Plug the cable in firmly, all the way, at both ends.

## Program a radio safely
1. Turn the radio on, with the cable connected, and choose the radio's make and model in CHIRP.
2. **Download from the radio first.** This copies what is on the radio into CHIRP.
3. **Save that copy as a file** and name it for the radio. It is your backup, so you can always put the radio back as it was.
4. Fill in or paste in your channels: name, frequency, tone, and power.
5. Send the table to the radio ("Upload to radio") and wait for it to finish. Do not touch the cable.
6. Check a few channels on the radio itself.

## Rules that still apply
- Programming a frequency into a radio does not give you permission to transmit on it. You may only transmit where your license or the rules allow. Listening is always allowed.
- Check the power limit for each channel. The channel lists in this course show which channels are for which service.
- Keep one file with your group's agreed channel plan, and give every member the same file, so every radio in the group matches.

+ Download what is on the radio and save it as a backup file
- Upload your channels straight away
- Turn the radio off
- Delete the radio's current channels
> The first copy is your way back if anything goes wrong.
