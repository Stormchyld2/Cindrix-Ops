Create time and space, have escape routes, make yourself a hard target.

## Checklist
- Develop a habit of knowing your primary and alternative exits everywhere you go.
- Practice verbal boundary statements with another teammate.
- Practice controlled disengagement
- Identify your household safe room or fallback space
- Document emergency contacts
- Review home lighting and lock weaknesses

## Be aware of your body language

1. Be aware of your body language: Don't stand in a confrontational manner, but don't cower in fear either.  Maintain a neutral stable stance. Give them enough space so they can't surprise you with a punch. Keep your hands up and visible.
2. Adjust your verbal approach: Let them vent their frustrations without interrupting. Speak slowly and softly, don't raise your voice. Let them know you understand what they're going through and how that must feel.
3. Plan an escape route: Be ready to exit immediately should things get out of control. Decide in advance what your level of force will be in response to an attack. It's always best to avoid physical confrontation when possible but be ready to respond appropriately should the need arise.
