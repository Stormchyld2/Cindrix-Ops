Your home is where you spend the most time and where your family sleeps. Good home security makes you a **hard target** and gives you **time**. Most break-ins are quick and opportunistic. Burglars look for easy entry: unlocked doors, open windows, dark yards, no one home.

## Layers of security
Think of layers. Each one adds time and risk for the intruder, and gives you time to respond.

1. **Outside:** lighting, clear sightlines, no places to hide.
2. **Doors and windows:** strong, locked and reinforced.
3. **Alerts:** alarms, dogs, cameras, neighbors.
4. **Inside:** a safe room and a plan.

## Doors
Doors are the most common entry point.

- **Use solid-core or metal doors** for all outside doors.
- **Fit a good deadbolt** with a bolt at least one inch long on every outside door.
- **Fit a reinforced strike plate** with long screws (at least 3 inches) that go into the wall stud, not just the door frame. A door usually fails at the frame.
- **Reinforce the hinges** on outward-opening doors and use security screws.
- **Fit a wide-angle peephole or a door camera.** Never open the door without knowing who is there.
- **Lock sliding doors** with a bar or a rod in the track.
- **Secure the garage door.** Keep the door from the garage to the house locked, and unplug or lock the manual release.
- **Never hide a key** under a mat or a flowerpot. Give spare keys to a trusted neighbor.

## Windows
- Keep windows locked, and fit secondary locks or pins so they cannot be opened far.
- Window film or security laminate makes glass harder to break.
- Trim shrubs below the windows.
- Keep fire exits in mind. Bedroom windows must open from the inside so you can leave in a fire.

## Lighting and landscaping
- **Motion lights** at doors and dark corners.
- **Trim hedges** under 3 feet near the house and branches high.
- **Lock tools and ladders** away, since they can be used to break in.
- **Mark your house number** clearly so responders can find you.

## Alarms and cameras
- A **monitored alarm** can bring help. A local alarm alone may deter.
- **Cameras** help to deter and to identify. Consider privacy laws, and do not point cameras at neighbors' property.
- **Signs** make a difference. Display them only if you have the system.
- **A dog**, even a small one that barks, is an effective early warning.
- **Talk to neighbors.** A neighborhood that watches itself is much safer.

## When you are away
- Use timers on lights and a radio.
- Stop deliveries or ask a neighbor to collect them.
- Do not announce travel plans online.
- Ask a neighbor to park in your driveway.

## The family safe room
A **safe room** is a room you can reach quickly, with a strong door, a lock, and the means to call for help.

1. **Choose the room.** Usually a bedroom or bathroom with a solid door.
2. **Reinforce the door.** Solid-core door, good deadbolt and strike plate.
3. **Keep a phone, a charger and a flashlight** in the room.
4. **Keep a first aid kit and water.**
5. **Have a way to call for help** even if cell service is bad. A radio can help.
6. **Practice.** Each family member should know where to go and what to do.

## If someone breaks in
1. **Do not confront them** unless you must. Go to your safe room with your family if you can.
2. **Lock the door and call emergency services.** Say your address and that someone is in the house.
3. **Stay on the phone** and follow instructions.
4. **Stay put** until police arrive and identify themselves.
5. If you cannot reach the safe room and the intruder is coming, leave through another exit if you can.
6. If you have trained and lawful self-defense tools, they are for protecting life, within the law.

## Fire and carbon monoxide: security of a different kind
- **Smoke alarms** on every level and in every bedroom. Test monthly.
- **Carbon monoxide alarms** near sleeping areas.
- **A fire escape plan** with two ways out of each room, a meeting place, and practice twice a year.
- Never block exits with security bars that cannot be opened from the inside.
- Never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors or in a garage.

## Checklist
- Check every outside door: solid, deadbolt, strong strike plate.
- Lock and pin all windows, and keep bedroom windows usable for escape.
- Fit motion lights and trim shrubs.
- Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.
- Choose and stock a safe room.
- Practice the safe room and fire escape with the family.
- Meet your neighbors.

> Layers buy time. Time lets you get your family to safety.

## Check yourself
? Where does a door usually fail when someone kicks it in?
- At the peephole
- At the hinges only
+ At the frame and strike plate
- At the handle
> Reinforce the strike plate with long screws that reach the wall stud.

? What is the first thing to do if someone breaks in?
- Wait to see what they do
+ Go to your safe room, lock the door and call emergency services
- Confront them
- Search the house
> Avoid confrontation and get help while keeping your family secure.

? What must bedroom windows allow?
- To be nailed shut
+ To be opened from the inside to escape a fire
- To be covered by bars that lock from outside
- To be painted over
> Security must never block your own escape.

? Where should you never run a generator?
- Outside away from windows
+ Indoors or in a garage
- On a dry flat surface
- With the right fuel
> Generators produce deadly carbon monoxide.
