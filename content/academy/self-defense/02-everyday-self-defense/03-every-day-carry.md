EDC are items you normally have with you every day to deal with situations that may arise.

The essential items you regularly keep on your person or close at hand. These tools are meant to help you handle common problems, unexpected delays, minor injuries, communication failures, and emergencies until you can reach additional supplies or assistance.
A good setup is not about carrying as much gear as possible. It is about choosing practical, familiar tools that fit your daily routine, environment, responsibilities, and level of training.

Your everyday carry should be useful in more than one situation, small and comfortable enough to carry consistently, legal where you live and travel, appropriate for your skills and experience and regularly inspected, maintained, and replenished.

## Layer 1: On-Body Essentials
Includes the items you carry directly on your person. These are the tools that should remain available even if you become separated from your vehicle, backpack, or larger emergency kit.

- Should include a flashlight (gear:Self Defense), pocketknife, tactical pen, cash, and phone
- Ladies might add feminine hygiene products, panic alarm
- Other items: Multi-tool, write-in-rain pad, firearm, pepper spray, sap, handcuff key, USB thumb drive, sky is the limit!

## Layer 2: Expanded Carry
Adds capability beyond what can comfortably fit in your pockets. These items may be carried in a small backpack, purse, waist pack, work bag, or belt-mounted pouch.
This layer is designed to help you remain functional for several hours, travel on foot, address injuries, or deal with changing weather and disrupted services.

- Mini Survival kit
- IFAK
- Fixed Blade Knife
- Fire kit
- Layered clothing to survive elements

> An EDC kit becomes less useful when it is too heavy, too complicated, or filled with unfamiliar equipment. Avoid:
- Carrying equipment you have never practiced using
- Letting batteries, medication, food, or medical supplies expire
- Relying entirely on your phone for navigation and information
