**Every day carry (EDC)** is the set of items you normally have with you every day to deal with situations that may arise. They help you handle common problems, unexpected delays, minor injuries, communication failures and emergencies until you can reach additional supplies or help.

A good setup is **not** about carrying as much gear as possible. It is about choosing practical, familiar tools that fit your daily routine, environment, responsibilities and level of training.

Your EDC should be:

- **Useful in more than one situation.**
- **Small and comfortable** enough to carry every day.
- **Legal** where you live and travel.
- **Appropriate** for your skills and experience.
- **Inspected, maintained and replenished** regularly.

## Layer 1: On-body essentials
These are items you carry directly on your person. They stay with you even if you are separated from your vehicle, backpack or kit.

- **Phone.** Charged. With emergency contacts and medical information set on the lock screen.
- **A small flashlight.** A bright, small LED light is one of the most useful items you can carry.
- **Pocketknife or multi-tool.** Where legal.
- **Cash.** Small bills, in case card systems are down.
- **ID, insurance and a card with emergency contacts.**
- **A pen and small notepad** (write-in-rain paper is best).
- **A whistle or a personal alarm.**
- **Any essential medication** you need for a day or two, if you take regular medication.
- **A tourniquet and a pair of gloves,** if you are trained to use them.
- **Other items** to suit your routine: lip balm, hand sanitizer, a small first aid pouch, a handcuff key, a USB thumb drive with copies of important documents.
- **Optional, and depending on training and law:** pepper spray, a firearm.

Many women also add feminine hygiene supplies and a personal panic alarm.

## Layer 2: Expanded carry
This layer adds capability beyond what fits in your pockets. Carry it in a small backpack, purse, waist pack, work bag or belt pouch. It helps you stay functional for several hours, travel on foot, treat injuries or cope with changing weather and disrupted services.

- **Mini survival kit.** Fire starter, a small knife, cordage, a mylar blanket.
- **An individual first aid kit (IFAK).** Trauma supplies plus basic first aid.
- **A power bank and charging cable.**
- **Water and a snack.** A bottle and a bar.
- **A fire kit.** Lighter, matches, tinder.
- **A fixed-blade or folding knife** where legal.
- **Layered clothing,** a hat, gloves and a rain jacket suited to the season.
- **A small radio** if you are trained to use one.
- **A paper map** of the area.
- **A headlamp.**

## Build it in steps
1. **Start with Layer 1.** Carry it for two weeks. Add and remove items based on what you actually use.
2. **Add Layer 2** that fits your commute and your workday.
3. **Keep a vehicle kit** for longer needs.
4. **Keep a go-bag at home** for emergencies at home.

## Common mistakes
- Carrying equipment you have never practiced using.
- Letting batteries, medication, food or medical supplies expire.
- Relying entirely on your phone for navigation and information.
- Carrying something that breaks the law or workplace rules.
- Carrying so much that you stop carrying it at all.
- Forgetting to check your kit after you use something from it.

## Know the rules
- **Airplanes.** Knives, multi-tools, pepper spray and some other items may not be carried through airport security. Check TSA rules before you travel.
- **Schools, courts and workplaces.** Many have rules about tools and weapons.
- **Local law.** Blade length and type, pepper spray, stun devices and firearms are regulated differently from place to place.

## Inspect it every month
Pick a date each month (for example, the first of the month). Check the batteries, the medication, the expiration dates, the cash and the condition of each item. Replace what is used or tired.

## Checklist
- Assemble your Layer 1 and carry it for two weeks.
- Add a bright small flashlight, a whistle and a pen if you do not have them.
- Build an IFAK and learn to use it.
- Add a Layer 2 bag that suits your day.
- Check each item is legal where you live and travel.
- Set a monthly inspection.

> A kit you never practiced with, or never replenish, gives a false sense of security.
