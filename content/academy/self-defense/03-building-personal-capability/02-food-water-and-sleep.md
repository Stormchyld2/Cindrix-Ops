Make a healthy diet a priority in your preparations. Diet is a major contributor to disease and to poor fitness, and it affects your energy and your thinking. You do not need a fancy program or a fad diet.

## Eat mostly whole foods
A **whole food** is one that comes from nature with little done to it: usually a single ingredient.

- **Protein:** meat, poultry, fish, eggs, beans, lentils, nuts.
- **Fruits and vegetables:** fresh, frozen or canned without added sugar.
- **Whole grains:** oats, brown rice, whole wheat.
- **Healthy fats:** olive oil, nuts, seeds, fish.
- **Dairy or alternatives.**

A useful rule: **most of your shopping on the outside edge of the grocery store**, where the fresh food is, and less in the middle aisles.

## Limit
- **Heavily processed foods,** which are made in a factory with a long list of ingredients you cannot pronounce.
- **Added sugar** and sugary drinks.
- **Excess salt.**
- **Excess alcohol.**

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans recommend limiting added sugars to less than 10 percent of calories, and limiting sodium and saturated fat.

## Water
- Most adults need about **2 to 3 liters of fluid a day,** more in heat, with exercise, or at altitude.
- Plain water is best. Thirst, headache, dark urine and tiredness are signs you need more.
- Do not drink only when thirsty on hard days. Sip steadily.
- Your emergency water plan should include enough for everyone's drinking and cooking. (See the water and Basic Preparedness courses.)

## Sleep
- Most adults need **seven or more hours** of sleep a night.
- Poor sleep reduces judgment, reaction time, mood and immunity.
- Keep regular sleep and wake times, a dark and cool bedroom, and limit screens before bed.
- In an emergency, rest when you can, and share watches so no one goes without sleep for long.

## Plan the same way for emergencies
Your food storage should be foods you eat and enjoy. In an emergency, keep up energy with a balance of carbohydrates, protein and fat, and keep up fluids. Stress and hard work increase your needs.

## Special needs
Children, pregnant women, older adults, and people with health conditions have special needs. Talk to a doctor or dietitian and plan for them.

## Habits that work
1. **Cook at home most days.**
2. **Plan meals** and keep healthy foods in the house.
3. **Read labels.** Look at ingredients and added sugar.
4. **Make small changes.** Swap one sugary drink for water.
5. **Do not skip meals** on training days.
6. **Eat with others.** It makes the habit last.

## Checklist
- Replace one processed food a week with a whole food.
- Drink water through the day.
- Aim for seven or more hours of sleep.
- Check that your emergency food is food you will eat.
- Plan for family members with special diets.

> You are what you eat, drink and sleep. Get those right and everything else gets easier.
