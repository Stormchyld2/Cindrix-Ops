There are many ways to defend yourself:

With the right **mindset & awareness** you can... → Avoid the problem

With** just your hands **(aka empty-hand skills ) you can...→ Handle close contact, generally a last resort

With the **proper tools** you can... → Extend your ability and increase effectiveness

Relying on only one is a mistake.

Tools can fail, be forgotten, or be taken. Skills take time to develop and may not be enough alone. **The strongest approach is layered capability.**

## Tools (Weapons / Equipment)

These are external tools you carry to increase your ability to stop a threat.

Learn the capabilities and limitations of that pepper spray or taser in your purse - owning equipment without understanding it can give a false sense of security.

## In order to be effective, you must learn:

- What your tools can and cannot do

- Their effective range and limitations

- When and how to use them safely

## Examples:

- Pepper spray can fail in wind or require proper aim

- Tasers have distance and reliability limitations

- Firearms require strict safety handling and awareness

→ The goal is not just possession - it's competence. This requires time and practice.

## Empty-Hand Skills (Unarmed Self-Defense)

In the heightened moment of stress, where you face a real threat of an attacker, everything you think you "know" is out the window.

Only with much time invested into practice can you gain the actual confidence to defend yourself using the many techniques available to you.

In general, self-defense, the goal is to **avoid** an altercation. There are postures, movements, and stances that can help you do this.

As a last resort, you can learn basic movements to escape a grip, etc.

→  Your goal is not to fight - it's to avoid, create distance, and get to safety.
