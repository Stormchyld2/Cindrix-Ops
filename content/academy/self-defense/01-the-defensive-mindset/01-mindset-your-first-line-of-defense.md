Your personal safety is not one object. It is not a can of pepper spray, and it is not a handgun. It is a **system** made of four parts that work together: **mindset, knowledge, skills and equipment.**

- With the right **mindset and awareness**, you can **avoid** the problem.
- With the right **skills**, you can **de-escalate** the situation.
- With the proper **tools**, you can **extend** your ability and increase your effectiveness.

When these work together, you greatly improve your ability to avoid, deter or survive a threat. Take one away, and the system is weaker. A person with a tool and no awareness is surprised. A person with awareness and no tool can still walk away.

## Mindset is the foundation
It is the hardest part to teach, because you have to believe it before you can act on it. Four beliefs matter:

1. **You cannot count on someone else to rescue you.** Police and responders are often minutes away when the problem takes seconds. In a major disaster they may not come at all.
2. **It can happen to you.** Most people who are attacked did not expect it. Believing "not here, not me" is what makes people easy targets.
3. **Being prepared means not having to be scared.** Preparation removes fear. A person who has a plan is calmer than a person who hopes.
4. **With the right training and equipment, you can even the odds.** You may not be bigger or stronger, but you can be smarter, earlier and better prepared.

## What mindset is not
- It is not paranoia. You do not need to live in fear.
- It is not looking for a fight. The winner of a conflict you avoid is **you.**
- It is not aggression. Calm, clear and confident beats loud and angry.

## Why avoidance comes first
Even a "won" fight can leave you hurt, sued or traumatized. Every step in this course is aimed at keeping you from ever needing force:

1. **Awareness** lets you see trouble early.
2. **Distance and exits** give you time.
3. **Words and body language** can calm things down.
4. **Leaving** is nearly always the best choice.
5. **Force** is only a last resort, for a real and immediate threat to life or serious harm, and only within the law.

## The decision before the moment
In a crisis you do not rise to the occasion. You fall back on what you have already decided and practiced. So decide now:

- What would make me leave a place?
- What is my escape route?
- Where would I go for help?
- What will I do if someone tries to take my bag? (Usually: let it go. Property can be replaced.)
- What is my line, the point where I would use force to protect my life or the life of someone I love?

## Train the mind
- **Rehearse.** Picture a scene and walk through what you would do. This is called mental rehearsal and it speeds up your reactions.
- **Breathe.** Slow, deep breathing lowers your heart rate and keeps your thinking clear. Breathe in for four counts, hold for four, out for four, hold for four.
- **Stay calm.** The calmest person in a crisis often leads.

## Checklist
- Write down your own answers to the "decision before the moment" questions.
- Talk with your family or group about them.
- Practice four-count breathing for two minutes a day for a week.
- Read this course through before buying any equipment.

> Prepared people are calm people. Calm people make better decisions.
