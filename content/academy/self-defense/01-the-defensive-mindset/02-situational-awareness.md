Self-defense begins long before force. **Awareness, distance and early recognition** prevent more problems than any tool.

## What situational awareness is
Be aware of your surroundings and of potential threats. You do this every day when you drive: you notice the car that drifts, the child near the road, the light about to change. Do the same on foot, in a shop, in a car park, at home.

- Live with your head up. Not in fear, but in **awareness.**
- Do not walk with your head down looking at a phone.
- Know where the exits are, wherever you are.
- Notice what is normal in a place, so you notice what is not.

## The Cooper color code
Colonel Jeff Cooper described five levels of awareness. They describe your **state of mind**, not your equipment.

- **White: relaxed and unaware.** At rest, safe and secure, such as in your bed at night. If you are in white in public, anything will surprise you.
- **Yellow: relaxed but alert.** Calm and observant. You are aware of what is around you and you have an easy plan for it. **This is where you should spend most of your life outside the home.**
- **Orange: alert to a probable danger.** You have noticed something that could be a threat, and you focus on it. You decide what you would do.
- **Red: action.** The danger is real and you act on your plan: you leave, you shout, you defend.
- **Black: panic.** Mental and physical performance breaks down. Training and practice keep you out of black.

A person in yellow notices: a car idling or moving oddly, someone behaving strangely or out of place, a sound that does not match the environment. Trust your instincts early, not late. Most threats are avoidable when you see them before they develop.

## Baselines and anomalies
Awareness depends on knowing the **baseline**, which is what is normal here, now. Then you spot **anomalies**, which are things that do not fit.

- People in clothes that do not match the weather.
- Someone watching rather than doing.
- A person who changes direction when you do.
- A vehicle that appears twice.
- An unusual silence, or a crowd that suddenly goes quiet or leaves.
- Animals reacting to something unseen.

One anomaly may mean nothing. **Two or three together** is a reason to move.

## Stop, Look, Listen, Smell (SLLS)
Use this when something feels wrong, or when you arrive somewhere new.

1. **STOP.** Stand still. When you move, you make noise that masks other signs, and it is hard to observe while walking.
2. **LOOK.** Scan in sectors. Look a few meters out first, then double the distance and scan back, then look farther. Look for movement, colors and shapes that do not belong.
3. **LISTEN.** Voices, footsteps, gear noises, engines. Anything that might be company you do not want.
4. **SMELL.** People nearby, smoke, cooking, fuel. Cooking smells travel much farther than you might think.

## Awareness in everyday places
- **Car parks.** Have your keys out before you reach the car. Look under and around it. Park in light and near the entrance. Check the back seat before you get in.
- **Cash machines and shops.** Use machines in busy, well-lit places. Cover the keypad. Count money later, not at the machine.
- **Public transport.** Sit near the driver or in a busy car. Keep valuables out of sight.
- **Walking.** Face traffic. Keep one ear free of headphones. Walk with purpose.
- **Hotels and travel.** Learn the exits. Do not announce your room number. Use the door latch.
- **Home.** Know who is at the door before you open it. Keep doors and windows locked.
- **Online.** Do not post your location, travel plans or the details of your supplies.

## Trust your instincts
Your body senses things before your mind explains them: a prickle at the back of the neck, a gut feeling. When it speaks, listen and act. You do not owe anyone politeness at the cost of your safety. Leave, cross the street, walk into a shop.

## Checklist
- Practice "yellow" on your commute for a week and note how much you notice.
- Learn the exits in every building you use regularly.
- Practice SLLS once a day in a new place.
- Look up from your phone when walking.
- Review your online habits and remove location details.

> Most threats are avoidable if you see them before they develop.
