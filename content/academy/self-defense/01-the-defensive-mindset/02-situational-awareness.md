Build the habit of noticing exits, anomalies, and danger indicators.

## Tier 3 self-defense begins long before force. Awareness, distance, and early recognition prevent more problems than any tool.

## Situational awareness
- Be aware of your surroundings and potential threats
- Remember the old drivers ed videos? Can you spot the potential problem?
- Not walking with head down on iPhone!

Live with your head up - not in fear, but in awareness. Most of your life outside the home should be spent in "yellow" - calm, but observant.

A person operating in yellow-level awareness should notice things like: a car moving or idling unusually, someone behaving oddly or out of place, or a sound that doesn't match the environment

Trust your instincts early, not late. Most threats are avoidable if you see them before they develop

## Cooper Color Code
- White- at rest, safe and secure
- Yellow- prepared, alert & relaxed. Good sit Awareness
- Orange- alert to probable danger. Ready to take action
- Red- Action Mode. Focused on the emergency at hand
- Black- panic, breakdown of physical and mental performance

## S.L.L.S

## STOP, LOOK, LISTEN, SMELL
- STOP: Stop moving and stay perfectly still.  You make noise when moving that can mask other signs.  It's hard to be observant when moving.
- LOOK: Scan the area around you.  Scan in sectors... a few meters out first. Then double the distance and scan back.  Then look out a longer ways... Anything out of place? Look for movement, colors, shapes that don't belong.
- LISTEN: Try to hear movement, voices, gear noises.  Anything that might be a clue to unwanted company.
- SMELL: People nearby? Odors from fire smoke or food.  Cooking smells travel much farther than you might think.
