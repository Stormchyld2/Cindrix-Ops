A kit for waiting is bigger than a kit for the first ten minutes. It is also useless without practice.

## The layers
1. **Individual kit:** on your body: tourniquet, a pressure bandage, gloves.
2. **Aid bag:** the carry-in kit for a team.
3. **Base kit:** a bigger bag or box for a camp or home, for sustained care.

## Bleeding
- 2 to 4 commercial tourniquets, spare windlass straps if you carry reusable ones
- Hemostatic gauze, plain gauze rolls, pressure bandages
- Wound packing practice trainer

## Airway and breathing
- CPR face shield or pocket mask
- Nasopharyngeal airways (training needed)
- Vented chest seals (pair)
- Pulse oximeter (a small finger clip that reads oxygen and pulse)
- Bag-valve-mask only if trained

## Circulation and shock
- Blankets, mylar blankets, a hypothermia wrap or sleeping bag
- Oral rehydration packets
- Cloth and tape for packing
- IV fluids and needles are clinician skills and often illegal to possess without authority

## Wounds
- Irrigation syringe, clean water
- Antiseptic wipes, saline
- Dressings, gauze, non-stick pads, tape, elastic wraps
- Burn dressings
- Wound closure strips

## Fractures and spine
- SAM-type splints, triangular bandages, padding
- Cervical collar or improvised stabilization
- Traction splint training

## Monitoring
- Thermometer (a low-reading one for cold)
- Watch with a second hand
- Pulse oximeter
- Notebook, pencil and a casualty card
- Headlamps with red light

## Comfort
- Sleeping pad, insulation, a tarp, hand warmers
- Food and fluids

## Medicines
Any prescription medicines, pain relief or antibiotics should be only those that your own clinician has advised, with written plans. Do not stockpile without medical advice. This course gives no amounts.

## Skills check
You should be able to do, with a stopwatch and a partner:
- Tourniquet on a limb in under a minute.
- Pack and bandage a deep wound in under three minutes.
- Place a chest seal on a trainer.
- Take a pulse, breathing rate and temperature and write them down.
- Hypothermia wrap in under five minutes.
- Splint a forearm and a lower leg.
- Log roll and move a person with three helpers.
Record your times, and practice until they are steady.

## Keep it fresh
Check dates on gauze, batteries and wipes twice a year. Replace what you use. Update the kit with training.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.

## Check yourself
? What does a pulse oximeter read?
- Temperature only
- Blood pressure
- Weight
+ Pulse rate and blood oxygen level
> It helps you monitor.

? Who should decide on stockpiling medicines?
+ Your own clinician
- A stranger
- A neighbor
- An advertisement
> Follow professional advice.

? Why practise with a stopwatch?
- It is a game only
+ To measure and improve speed and skill
- To scare people
- To waste time
> Speed matters in bleeding.
