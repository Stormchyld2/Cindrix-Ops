The basics of these were taught at Tier 2. Here are the finer points for a person you must keep alive for hours.

## Bleeding: the long view
- **Re-check tourniquets** every 15 minutes: is the bleeding still stopped? Do not loosen it. If bleeding returns, tighten, or add a second tourniquet next to the first.
- **Tourniquet time:** write it down. Tourniquets are generally left in place for up to two hours and may be left longer when needed. Only a clinician should convert or remove one, since removal risks bleeding and a rush of toxins to the body. Help from higher care is the priority.
- **Packed wounds:** check for soaking. If it soaks through, add more packing and pressure rather than removing the first.
- **Wound pressure over time:** a pressure bandage should stay snug but not cut off circulation.
- **Hidden bleeding:** blood inside the chest, belly, pelvis and thighs is not visible. Signs: shock without an obvious source, a swollen or rigid belly, a bruised, unstable pelvis, a swollen thigh. These need a hospital. Keep warm and flat.
- **Pelvic fracture:** wrap a sheet or a pelvic binder snugly around the hips, centered on the bony bumps (greater trochanters), and tie the knees together. Do not rock the person.

## Airway
- **The unconscious person** breathing on their own: recovery position, mouth angled down, so fluids drain.
- **Jaw thrust** if a neck injury is possible.
- **Suction:** a hand-held suction pump removes blood and vomit if you are trained.
- **Nasopharyngeal airway (NPA):** a soft tube that holds the airway open in an unconscious, breathing person. Needs training, lubricant, and care. Not for a person with a severe face or skull-base injury.
- **Blocked airway** that you cannot clear is a rescue emergency: use airway maneuvers and CPR steps. Cricothyrotomy is a surgical procedure for experts only.
- **Burned airway:** swelling can close the airway. Watch for hoarseness, soot, singed hair. Keep the person upright if breathing allows, and get urgent help.

## Breathing
- **Rate:** over 30 per minute is dangerous. Look for tiring.
- **Chest seals:** apply a vented seal to an open chest wound. If breathing worsens after a seal, lift a corner to let trapped air escape and re-seal.
- **Tension pneumothorax:** a worsening chest injury with severe breathlessness, rapid pulse, falling oxygen and a swollen neck. Needle decompression is a clinician's skill. Burp the seal, position upright if tolerated, and arrange urgent evacuation.
- **Rib fractures:** pain makes breathing shallow, leading to pneumonia. Encourage slow deep breaths with a pillow brace, and sit the person up.
- **Oxygen:** if you carry a tank, learn its use and safety, as fire risk is serious.
- **Rescue breaths:** if breathing stops but a pulse remains, give rescue breaths about once every 6 seconds for an adult, and keep checking.

## When breathing and pulse both stop
Start CPR (see the CPR course). In trauma, CPR is less likely to succeed, but do it if nothing else is available and the scene is safe. Decide in advance, with your group, when it is appropriate to stop.

## Keep the carer safe
Use gloves and eye protection. Wash hands after.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.

## Check yourself
? What should you do with a tourniquet during prolonged care?
- Cover it
- Remove it at once
+ Check it often and leave it for a clinician to remove
- Loosen it every 15 minutes
> Do not loosen it yourself.

? What suggests bleeding you cannot see?
- Warm skin
- A clean wound
- A smile
+ Shock with no visible source, a swollen belly or pelvis
> Needs a hospital.

? Who performs needle decompression?
- The patient
- A neighbor
- Anyone
+ A trained clinician
> It is a medical procedure.

? How can you ease rib pain and prevent pneumonia?
- Drink cold water
+ Encourage slow deep breaths with a pillow brace and sit upright
- Stay still lying flat
- Hold breath
> Shallow breathing risks lung infection.
