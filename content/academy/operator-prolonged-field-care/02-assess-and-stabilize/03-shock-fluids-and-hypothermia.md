These are the things that quietly kill a casualty during a long wait.

## Shock
Shock is poor blood flow to the organs. It builds with blood loss, burns, severe pain, infection and cold.
- Stop bleeding.
- Lay the person flat unless they have breathing trouble or a head or chest injury.
- Keep warm.
- Calm and reassure.
- Give small sips of fluid only if the person is awake, not vomiting, and has no belly or head injury needing surgery.
- Raise legs only if they have no leg, pelvic or spinal injury and it helps.

## Fluids
Hospitals use blood and IV fluids for severe bleeding. In the field without a trained clinician you cannot safely give them.
- **Oral fluids:** if the person is alert, can swallow and does not need surgery, give frequent sips of water or oral rehydration solution. Small amounts often are better than a lot at once.
- **Avoid giving large amounts** to a person who is losing blood from an uncontrolled bleed, since higher blood pressure can dislodge clots. The aim is a pulse at the wrist and thinking clearly, not a normal pressure.
- **Food:** offer simple, calorie-rich food when awake and not facing surgery: bars, honey, broth. Energy helps the body make heat and heal.
- **Urine:** note how much and the color. Very dark or very little means dehydration or poor kidney flow.

## Hypothermia: the lethal triad
Trauma, cold and acidosis feed each other. A cold casualty bleeds more. Preventing heat loss is as important as stopping bleeding.
- **Insulate from the ground** with pads, packs, branches.
- **Remove wet clothes,** replace with dry ones, and wrap in blankets or a sleeping bag.
- **Vapor barrier:** a tarp or a plastic or foil layer over the insulation holds heat.
- **Cover the head and neck.**
- **Heat sources:** warm water bottles wrapped in cloth at the chest, armpits and groin (never directly on skin).
- **Keep the person dry, out of the wind, under a shelter.**
- **Handle gently:** severely cold people can develop dangerous heart rhythms with rough handling.
- **Stages:** shivering and clumsy (mild), loss of shivering, confusion and slurring (moderate to severe), unresponsive (very severe). The cold person is not dead until warm and dead.
- **Warm sweet drinks** only if alert and able to swallow.

## Heat illness
Heat stroke (confusion, hot skin, collapse) is an emergency: cool the body fast with water and fans, ice packs to the neck, armpits and groin, and get help.

## Pressure and position
Change position every two hours if injuries allow, padding bony areas, to prevent pressure sores.

## Toileting and hygiene
Offer a bottle, bucket or pad and keep the person clean and dry. It protects skin and dignity.

## Sleep
Allow rest, with someone to wake and check them at intervals. Do not let a head-injured person sleep unobserved for the first night. Wake them regularly to check response.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.
