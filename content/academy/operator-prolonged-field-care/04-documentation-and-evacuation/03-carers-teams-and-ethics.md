Prolonged care is hard on the carer. It is also full of difficult choices.

## Look after the carer
- **Rotate roles.** A single carer will tire in hours.
- **Rest, eat and drink** on a schedule. Fatigue causes errors.
- **Stay warm and dry.**
- **Use checklists,** so memory does not have to work.
- **Share the load:** one person monitors, one handles supplies, one logs, one comforts.
- **Talk it through:** ask for a second opinion on key decisions.

## Teams
Assign:
- **Team lead:** decides and keeps the big picture.
- **Primary carer:** hands-on with the patient.
- **Assistant:** supplies, vital signs, note taking.
- **Communicator:** calls for help, plans evacuation, deals with others.
- **Security and logistics.**
Use closed-loop communication: say, repeat, confirm.

## Ethics
- **Do no harm.** If you are unsure, do the least invasive thing that helps.
- **Work within your training.**
- **Consent:** if the person can decide, ask. Explain risks and choices. If they cannot, act in their best interest.
- **Dignity and privacy:** cover, comfort, speak kindly, and respect culture and faith.
- **Equality:** treat people fairly, based on need.
- **Honesty:** be truthful with the patient and family, with kindness.
- **Triage choices:** in a mass casualty event, you may have to choose. Use a clear method (START) and do not second-guess.
- **End-of-life care:** if a person is dying and cannot be saved, give comfort, warmth, pain relief within what a clinician allows, company and dignity. Be present.

## When a casualty dies
- Confirm carefully.
- Cover the body respectfully.
- Record the time.
- Tell the right people kindly.
- Preserve the scene if it is a crime scene.
- Care for the survivors.

## Stress after care
It is normal to feel shaky, sad, guilty or numb afterward. Eat, sleep, talk, and take time. A debrief with the team helps. If distress lasts, see a counselor. In the US, call or text **988** or the Disaster Distress Helpline 1-800-985-5990.

## Keep learning
Review each case. Train again. Take courses. Mentors and clinicians are the best teachers.

## Final word
Prolonged field care is about steady, repeated, humble work: look, listen, record, treat what you can, and get the person to someone who can do more.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.
