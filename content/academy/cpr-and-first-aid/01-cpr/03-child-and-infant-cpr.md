Children are not small adults. Their hearts usually stop because of **a breathing problem** (choking, drowning, an overdose, asthma), so breaths matter more.

## Ages
- **Infant:** under 1 year.
- **Child:** 1 year to puberty (signs of puberty include chest or underarm hair).
- **Adult:** puberty and above.

## Child CPR (1 year to puberty)
1. **Check the scene,** then **tap and shout.**
2. **If alone and no response:** give **2 minutes of CPR first, then call 911** (or use your phone on speaker while starting). If someone else is there, have them call and get an AED.
3. **Check breathing** for up to 10 seconds.
4. **Hand position:** **heel of one hand** on the lower half of the breastbone. For a small child, one hand may be enough. For a larger child, use two hands like an adult.
5. **Compress** at **100 to 120 per minute,** to a depth of **about 2 inches (5 cm) or one-third the depth of the chest.**
6. **Let the chest recoil fully.**
7. **After 30 compressions, give 2 breaths,** each about a second, making the chest rise. (With two trained rescuers, use **15 compressions to 2 breaths.**)
8. **Continue** until help arrives, the child moves or you are exhausted.

## Infant CPR (under 1 year)
1. **Tap the foot** and shout. Do not shake.
2. **Alone: give 2 minutes of CPR first,** then call 911.
3. **Place the infant on a firm surface** on the back.
4. **Hand position:**
   - **One rescuer:** **two fingers** in the center of the chest, just **below the nipple line.**
   - **Two rescuers:** **two thumbs** side by side on the same spot, with your hands **encircling the chest.**
5. **Compress** at **100 to 120 per minute,** to a depth of **about 1.5 inches (4 cm) or one-third the depth of the chest.**
6. **Let the chest recoil fully.**
7. **After 30 compressions, give 2 breaths** covering both the infant's **mouth and nose** with your mouth. Use gentle puffs, just enough to make the chest rise. (**Two rescuers: 15 compressions and 2 breaths.**)
8. **Continue** until help takes over.

## Breaths for children
- **Open the airway** gently. For an infant, put the head in a **neutral** position. Do not tilt it back far.
- **Use puffs, not big breaths.** Just enough to make the chest rise.
- **If the chest does not rise,** reposition the head and try again. If you still cannot, begin compressions and **look in the mouth** each time for an object you can remove.

## AED for children
- **Use an AED** as soon as one arrives.
- **Use child pads** (or a child setting) if the person is under 8 years old, or under about 55 lbs. Place them in the front and back of the chest if they are too big to fit on the front.
- **If you have no child pads,** use adult pads. Make sure the pads do not touch. **Use an AED rather than none.**

## Choking and CPR
If a child collapses because of choking, give CPR, and check the mouth each time you open the airway for breaths. Remove an object only if you can see it. See the choking lesson in the Medical course.

## Common causes in children
- Choking.
- Drowning.
- Poisoning and overdoses.
- Asthma.
- Suffocation.
- Congenital heart conditions.
- Sudden infant death. Always place infants on their backs to sleep.

## Emotional care
It is hard to do CPR on a child. Take a breath, trust your training, and **keep going.** Afterward, talk to someone. **988** and the Disaster Distress Helpline (1-800-985-5990) can help.

## Checklist
- Know the age groups
- Know to give 2 minutes of CPR first when alone with a child or infant
- Know two fingers for infants (one rescuer), two thumbs (two rescuers)
- Know to use puffs for breaths
- Child pads for the AED

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
