These are common emergencies you may meet at home or in a group.

## Seizures
A seizure is a burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. Some are dramatic: the person falls, stiffens and jerks. Others are subtle: staring, confusion or twitching.

### What to do
1. **Stay calm,** and **note the time** it started.
2. **Keep them safe.** Clear nearby objects. Ease them to the floor if they are standing.
3. **Cushion the head** with something soft.
4. **Loosen** anything tight around the neck.
5. **Turn them on their side** once the jerking stops, to help them breathe and let fluids drain.
6. **Stay with them** until they are fully awake. Speak gently.

### Do not
- **Do not hold them down.**
- **Do not put anything in their mouth.** They cannot swallow their tongue.
- **Do not give** food or drink until they are awake and alert.

### Call 911 if
- The seizure lasts **more than 5 minutes.**
- **A second seizure** follows soon after.
- They **do not wake** or have trouble breathing afterward.
- They are **injured,** pregnant, or have **diabetes.**
- It is their **first seizure.**
- It happens in water.

## Fainting
Fainting is a brief loss of consciousness from a temporary drop in blood flow to the brain.
- **Signs before:** lightheadedness, pale, sweaty skin, tunnel vision, nausea.
- **If someone feels faint:** **sit or lie down** and put the head lower than the heart, or sit with the head between the knees.
- **If they faint:** **lay them flat** and raise the legs about 12 inches, if there is no injury. Loosen tight clothing. Check breathing.
- **They should wake in a minute or two.** If not, **call 911** and check breathing.
- **Let them rest** and sit up slowly. Give water when awake.
- **Call 911 if** they have chest pain, trouble breathing, a head injury, are pregnant, are older and have no clear cause, or faint again.

## Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)
Common in people with **diabetes** who take insulin or certain pills, and when they skip food, exercise, or take too much medicine.

### Signs
- **Shakiness,** sweating, hunger.
- **Dizziness,** headache, blurry vision.
- **Confusion, irritability,** strange behavior (it can look like drunkenness).
- **Pale skin,** fast heartbeat.
- **Severe:** seizure or unconsciousness.

### What to do
1. **If the person is awake and can swallow,** give a **fast sugar source:** juice, regular soda (not diet), glucose tablets or gel, or candy. Follow the label on glucose products for how much. Ask the person what they usually use.
2. **Wait about 15 minutes** and recheck. If still low, give another round.
3. **Follow with a snack** with protein and carbs when they feel better.
4. **If they are confused but can swallow,** you can still give sugar. **If they cannot swallow, are unresponsive or are having a seizure,** do **not** put food or drink in the mouth.
5. **Call 911** for unresponsive, seizure or no improvement.
6. **Glucagon:** some people carry emergency glucagon. Follow its printed instructions and the person's plan. Do not give any dose from memory.
7. **Never give insulin** to another person.

### High blood sugar
If someone with diabetes is very thirsty, urinating a lot, has fruity breath, nausea or deep fast breathing, **they may need urgent care.** Call 911 if they are confused or very ill.

## Checklist
- Know the first aid for a seizure and when to call 911
- Know what to do when someone faints
- Know how to treat low blood sugar with sugar by mouth
- Never put anything in the mouth of a person having a seizure
- Never give insulin to another person

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
