## Drowning
Drowning can happen in seconds, quietly, in only a few inches of water. It is a leading cause of death in children.

### Recognize
- **No splashing or calls for help.** A drowning person often cannot yell. Look for a head low in the water, a vertical body, gasping, or glassy eyes.

### Rescue
1. **Do not become a victim.** Do not jump in unless trained. **Reach or throw** first: a pole, rope, towel, a life ring or something that floats. **Get help.**
2. **Call 911.**
3. **Once the person is out of the water,** check responsiveness and breathing.
4. **If not breathing normally, start CPR,** and **give breaths,** because drowning is a lack of oxygen. Give **2 breaths first** if trained, then **30 compressions and 2 breaths.** Use an AED. Dry the chest first.
5. **Keep going** until help arrives.
6. **Keep them warm.**
7. **All drowning victims should go to hospital,** even if they seem fine. Lungs can be injured.

### Prevent
- **Watch children** within arm's reach near water.
- **Fence** pools and lock gates.
- **Wear life jackets** on boats and when swimming in open water.
- **Learn to swim.**
- **Never swim alone.**
- Avoid alcohol near water.
- In floods, **never walk or drive through moving water.** Turn around, don't drown.

## Poisoning
Poisons include medicines, cleaners, chemicals, plants, mushrooms, carbon monoxide and spoiled food.

### What to do
1. **Make sure the scene is safe.** Get away from fumes.
2. **Call Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222** (free, 24 hours, in the US) or **911** if the person is unresponsive, having seizures or having trouble breathing.
3. **Have information ready:** the person's age and weight, what they took, how much, and when. Bring the container.
4. **Follow the instructions** from Poison Control.
5. **Do not make the person vomit,** and do not give anything to drink or eat unless told to.
6. **Skin exposure:** remove contaminated clothing and **rinse with water** for at least 15 minutes.
7. **Eye exposure:** rinse with lukewarm water for at least 15 minutes.
8. **Inhalation:** get into fresh air.
9. **If they are unresponsive,** check breathing and start CPR if needed.

### Prevent
- **Store medicines and chemicals** locked and out of reach.
- **Keep them in original containers.**
- **Never mix** cleaning products, such as bleach and ammonia, because they produce toxic gas.
- **Install carbon monoxide detectors.**
- **Know your plants,** and do not eat wild mushrooms or plants unless an expert has identified them.

## Bites and stings
### Insect stings
- **Remove the stinger** by scraping it with a card or fingernail. Do not squeeze.
- **Wash** the area and apply a cold pack.
- **Watch for allergic reactions:** hives, swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing, dizziness. This is an emergency. Use epinephrine if prescribed and **call 911.**

### Snakebites
- **Call 911.** Stay calm and still.
- **Keep the bitten area at or slightly below heart level,** and keep still.
- **Remove** rings, watches and tight clothing near the bite before swelling begins.
- **Wash** the bite gently.
- **Note** the time and what the snake looked like, if safe. **Do not chase it.**
- **Do not cut or suck** the wound. **Do not apply ice,** tourniquets, or electric shock.
- **Get to a hospital.** Antivenom is the treatment.

### Spider bites and scorpion stings
- **Clean,** apply a cold pack, and keep the area still.
- **Seek care** for severe pain, spreading redness, muscle cramping, trouble breathing or an expanding wound. Black widow and brown recluse bites need medical care.

### Animal and human bites
- **Wash** with soap and running water for several minutes.
- **Cover** with a clean dressing.
- **Seek care.** Bites carry the risk of infection and rabies.
- **Report** animal bites to local authorities.

### Ticks
- **Remove** with fine-tipped tweezers close to the skin, pulling straight up and steadily. Do not crush.
- **Clean** the area.
- **Watch** for a rash or fever in the following weeks, and see a doctor if they appear.

## Checklist
- Know how to reach and throw, and not go in
- Poison Control number saved in every phone and kit
- Know the steps for a snakebite
- Know how to remove a tick
- Medicines and chemicals locked away

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
