Reading helps. Practice is what works when your hands are shaking.

## Take a class
- **Hands-on** CPR and AED with a manikin and feedback.
- **First aid** with bleeding, burns, fractures, allergic reactions and more.
- **Renew** every two years.
- Look through **Red Cross, American Heart Association,** local fire departments, hospitals and community groups.

## Drills for your household or group
Run these at least twice a year:
1. **Collapse drill:** someone lies still. Others: tap and shout, call 911 (pretend), get the AED, begin compressions on a manikin or a pillow for a minute, and call out the steps.
2. **Choking drill:** talk through back blows and thrusts with a partner (gently), or on a manikin.
3. **Bleeding drill:** apply a tourniquet and pack a wound with a trainer.
4. **Stroke and heart attack scenario:** talk through BE FAST and 911.
5. **Allergic reaction scenario:** with a trainer auto-injector.
6. **Seizure scenario:** how to cushion, time and turn.
7. **Radio or phone drill:** make a clear 911 call, with the location and condition.

## Make a clear 911 call
Say:
- **Where** you are, with cross streets.
- **What** happened.
- **How many** are hurt.
- **Their condition.**
- **What you are doing.**
Stay on the line.

## Keep the tools in place
- **Pocket mask or face shield** in your bag, car and kit.
- **Gloves.**
- **An AED** if your home or group can afford one.
- **A first aid kit** (see the Medical course).
- **A tourniquet.**
- **Phone** with emergency contacts and medical ID.

## Medical ID
Set up the **Medical ID** on your phone (it can be seen from the lock screen), and wear a **bracelet** if you have a condition or allergy.

## Record training
Use the Academy sign-off page to record who has been trained and when it expires.

## After an emergency
- **Debrief** with those involved.
- **Restock** supplies.
- **Take care of your mind.** If you feel upset, talk to someone. **988** is the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

## Checklist
- Everyone has taken a CPR and first aid class
- Drills on the calendar twice a year
- Masks, gloves, AED and kits in place
- Medical ID set up on phones
- Training dates recorded

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
