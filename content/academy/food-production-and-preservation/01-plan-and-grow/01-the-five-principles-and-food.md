Food can fail slowly or all at once. A household that makes and keeps its own food has more choices.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- What could cut your food: a truck strike, a storm, a job loss, a drought, a power cut that thaws the freezer, a pest in the pantry?
- What can you grow or raise where you live, and when? Know your frost dates, soil and water.
- Which family members have food limits, such as allergies or a diet?

## 2. Preparedness
- **Store:** two weeks, then three months, then a year of food you eat (see Building your stash).
- **Grow:** a garden planned for calories, not only salad (see Seed Vault).
- **Preserve:** the tools and skills to put food by.
- **Cook without power:** a stove, fuel, pots and a way to bake.

## 3. Response
When food gets short: eat what spoils first (freezer, fridge), keep a menu, ration fairly, protect children and the sick, and avoid risky foods.

## 4. Mitigation
- **Diversify:** several crops, several animals, several storage methods.
- **Spread** food across rooms and a second site.
- **Protect** from pests, moisture, heat and theft.
- **Preserve surpluses** when they come.

## 5. Recovery
Replace stores, restart the garden, repair the coop, review what worked.

## A rough target
A family of four needs about 8,000 calories a day. A year is about 3 million. A pantry of rice, beans, oats and oil can supply a large share. A garden of potatoes, squash, beans and corn can supply the rest, in a good year, with enough land and skill.

## Layout of this course
1. **Plan and grow:** calories, small livestock, foraging safely.
2. **Preserve:** canning, drying, fermenting, curing, cellaring.
3. **Store and cook:** food safety, power-free cooking, a year-round rhythm.

> Practice every method now, while a failed batch only costs a lunch.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
