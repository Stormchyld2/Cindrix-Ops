A hobby garden gives tomatoes and herbs. A calorie garden gives bulk food. Plan for what feeds people.

## The calorie crops
Rough yields per 100 square feet in good conditions:
- **Potatoes:** 100 to 150 pounds. About 350 calories per pound.
- **Sweet potatoes:** 50 to 100 pounds.
- **Winter squash and pumpkin:** 50 to 100 pounds, and they keep for months.
- **Dry beans:** 5 to 10 pounds, rich in protein.
- **Field corn or flour corn:** 10 to 20 pounds of grain.
- **Cabbage, carrots, beets:** 100 to 150 pounds, with fewer calories but good nutrition and keeping quality.
- **Wheat, oats and other small grains:** much less per square foot, and need space and tools.

## A garden for a family of four
A rough guide: about **4,000 to 8,000 square feet** of well-managed ground can supply a large part of a family's calories, with potatoes, beans, corn, squash and a wide range of vegetables. That is much bigger than most backyard gardens. Start with what you can manage, and grow in stages.

## Principles that give high yield
- **Soil first:** compost, mulch and cover crops. Healthy soil grows more per square foot.
- **Intensive beds:** 3 to 4 feet wide with close spacing.
- **Succession:** replant as crops finish.
- **Season extension:** row cover, cold frames and low tunnels add weeks.
- **Water:** drip lines and mulch.
- **Rotation:** do not plant the same family in the same place in consecutive years.
- **Save seed** from open-pollinated varieties.

## Storage crops are key
Plan more of the crops that keep: potatoes, winter squash, onions, garlic, cabbage, carrots, beets, apples and dry beans. They need a cellar or cool room.

## Perennial food
Plant fruit trees, berry bushes, asparagus, rhubarb and nut trees. They take years to start, then give food with less work. Plant them now.

## Work and time
Gardening at scale takes hours each week. Count the labor, choose tools (broadfork, hoe, wheel hoe, hand tools), and share the work.

## Keep records
Track what you planted, when, how much came and what failed. Your own numbers beat any book.

## Do not forget protein and fat
A calorie garden needs beans, eggs, meat, nuts or oil to balance it. Plan for them too.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
