Wild food can add variety and protein, but it is slow, uncertain and risky if you are unprepared. Treat it as a supplement, not a plan.

## Rules first
Hunting, fishing, trapping and foraging are regulated. Seasons, bag limits, licenses and rules for public and private land differ. In a true grid-down emergency, local rules may be relaxed, but do not assume that.

## Foraging plants: the iron rules
- **Never eat a plant unless you are 100 percent sure of what it is.** Many edible plants have deadly lookalikes (wild carrot and poison hemlock, for example).
- Learn **a few common plants** very well rather than many badly: for example dandelion, plantain, cattail, wild berries that you can name and nuts such as acorns (leached to remove tannins).
- Use a good local field guide, and learn with an experienced forager.
- **Avoid** plants near roads, industry, old orchards or sprayed fields.
- Try new foods in small amounts and wait.
- **Mushrooms:** do not eat wild mushrooms unless an expert identifies them. Poisoning can be fatal and cooking does not make them safe.

## Fishing
- Simple gear (line, hooks, weights, a small net) is light and cheap.
- Learn local species, rules and sizes. Cook fish well. Avoid fish from polluted water. Follow health advisories for mercury and other contaminants.
- Passive methods such as set lines work while you do other jobs.

## Hunting and trapping
- **Safety first:** take a hunter education course, learn the four rules of firearm safety, and know what is behind your target.
- Game animals provide meat, but need field dressing, cooling and cooking to safe temperatures. Handle with gloves, avoid animals that look sick, and cook meat to a safe internal temperature.
- Trapping needs skill, humane methods and legal compliance.

## Insects and other foods
Some insects and grubs are nutritious, but only use well-known species, cooked, and avoid allergy risks.

## Energy cost
Hunting and gathering burn calories. In a long emergency, a few hours a day of low-return foraging may not feed you. Gardens, livestock and stored food are more reliable.

## Water and cooking
Cook wild foods thoroughly. Wash hands and tools. Clean game on a clean surface, away from water sources.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
