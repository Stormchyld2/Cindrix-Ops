Not every food needs processing. Many keep well if you give them the right place.

## Root cellars
A root cellar is a cool, humid, dark place that stays between about 32 and 40 °F in winter. It can be a basement corner, a buried barrel, an insulated closet by an outside wall or a cellar dug into a hillside.
- **Cool and steady:** below 40 °F but above freezing.
- **Humid:** 85 to 95 percent for roots and cabbage, lower (60 to 70 percent) for onions, garlic and squash.
- **Dark and ventilated:** a small intake low, an exhaust high, to move air.
- **Separate:** keep apples away from potatoes (ethylene gas), and strong-smelling crops like cabbage away from others.
- **Check** weekly, remove spoiled items.

## What stores where
- **Cold and moist (32 to 40 °F):** potatoes (cooler, 40 to 45 °F is best), carrots, beets, turnips, cabbage, apples, parsnips and leeks.
- **Cool and dry (50 to 60 °F):** onions, garlic, winter squash, pumpkins, sweet potatoes (55 to 60 °F).
- **Cure** crops first (see the Seed Vault harvest lesson).

## Freezing
- Works for most foods, but needs power. Blanch vegetables, use airtight freezer bags or containers, label and date.
- A full freezer holds its temperature about 48 hours without power if unopened. Group food, add ice and cover with blankets.
- Fill gaps with water jugs, which also gives you water.
- Plan to cook or can the contents if the power will be off a long time.

## Cool storage without power
Bury a cooler or a metal can in the earth in shade. Use a spring house, a cold stream, or a clay-pot cooler (a pot in a pot with wet sand, which cools by evaporation). Water cools quickly in a shaded stream.

## Pantry storage
Keep jars, dried goods and cans in a cool, dry, dark pantry, off the floor, and use first-in first-out.

## Pest control
Rodents, insects and mold are the enemies. Use metal bins, tight lids, clean surfaces and traps.

## Record
Keep a map of what is stored where, and check against it each month.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
