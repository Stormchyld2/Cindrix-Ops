Home canning keeps food for years without power. Done wrong, it can kill. Follow tested methods exactly.

## Why safety matters
Clostridium botulinum is a bacterium that lives in soil, survives boiling and, in a sealed, low-acid, low-oxygen jar, makes a deadly toxin. You cannot see, smell or taste it. Safe canning destroys the spores with heat, or prevents their growth with acid.

## Two methods, and which to use
- **Boiling-water canner:** for **high-acid foods** (pH below 4.6): fruits, jams, jellies, pickles, tomatoes with added acid, and salsas from tested recipes.
- **Pressure canner:** for **low-acid foods:** vegetables, meat, poultry, fish, beans, soups and stock. Pressure raises the temperature above boiling, which kills botulism spores. Never use a water bath for these.

## Equipment
- A boiling-water canner or a large pot with a rack, **a pressure canner** (not a pressure cooker, unless it is rated and tested for canning), jars made for canning, new lids for each batch, rings, a jar lifter, funnel, bubble remover and a clean towel.
- Have the gauge of a dial pressure canner tested every year at a Cooperative Extension office.

## Basic steps
1. **Use tested recipes** from the USDA, the National Center for Home Food Preservation or the makers of canning jars. Do not change ingredients, jar size, or processing time.
2. **Check jars** for chips and cracks, and wash them.
3. **Prepare the food** exactly as the recipe says.
4. **Fill hot jars** leaving the headspace the recipe gives. Remove bubbles, wipe the rim, apply lids and rings finger-tight.
5. **Process** for the exact time for your jar size and **your altitude.** Higher places need longer times or more pressure.
6. **Cool** jars undisturbed for 12 to 24 hours.
7. **Check seals:** the lid should be concave and not flex. Refrigerate and use any that failed to seal soon, or reprocess within 24 hours.
8. **Label** with contents and date, remove rings, wash jars and store in a cool, dark place.

## What not to do
- Do not can **pumpkin or winter squash puree, mashed potatoes, dairy, grains, or thick sauces** at home. There are no safe tested times.
- Do not use open-kettle canning, oven canning, microwave canning or dishwasher canning.
- Do not use old recipes, or add thickeners such as flour or cornstarch before canning.
- Do not use jars with chipped rims, or reuse single-use lids.

## Spoilage signs
Bulging lids, leaking, spurting liquid when opened, mold, cloudiness, off smell. **Discard** the jar and contents safely. When in doubt, throw it out, without tasting. Boiling low-acid home-canned vegetables and meats for 10 minutes before eating is advised as an extra safeguard by some authorities.

## Storing
Use within a year for best quality, though properly sealed jars stay safe longer. Keep dark, cool and dry.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
