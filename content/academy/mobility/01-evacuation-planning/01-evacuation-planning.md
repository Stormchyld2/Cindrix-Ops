Know when to move, where to go, and how to get there.

Most people who get hurt in evacuations didn't leave too slowly, they decided too late. This lesson helps you make the hard decisions now, while it's calm: what will make you leave, where you'll go, how you'll get there, and who's counting on you.

## Step 1: Know your risks and your zone
Identify the hazards where you live (wildfire, flood, etc.), find your evacuation zone number, and sign up for your county's emergency alerts.

## Step 2: Set your trigger
Learn the difference between a Warning and an Order. Decide in advance what makes your household leave, for example, if you have kids, pets, livestock, or anyone with limited mobility, leave at the Warning.

## Step 3: Pick your meeting places and destination
Choose one spot near home, one outside the neighborhood, and two destinations in different directions. Types of meeting places include:

- **Neighborhood meeting spot:** Pick a specific landmark close enough for kids to walk to, safely away from the house, and reachable without crossing hazards. It should be visible to responders and not depend on anyone being home.
- **Out-of-neighborhood meeting spot:** Choose a public landmark, like a library or school parking lot, that everyone can reach from work or school, outside likely hazard zones. It should be open at any hour with room to wait, plus a backup spot.
- **Evacuation destination:** Line up two options in different directions, at least 50 miles out and reachable on one tank of gas. Confirm they'll take you, pets and all, write down the address and phone number, and keep a hotel as a backup.
- **For any destination:** Every family member, including kids, should be able to get there alone, with or without the main road, and have it written down.

## Step 4: Map at least two routes out
Plan routes in different directions, and print them with **written** directions. GPS can send you toward closed roads or into the hazard, and cell networks often fail.

## Step 5: Plan for everyone who depends on you
Think -- What if my kids are at school? I'm away at work? What about elderly relatives, pets and livestock, medications, and medical equipment? If you don't have a vehicle, arrange a ride now.

## Step 6: Practice it
This is where you take it from an idea to a plan. Do a timed drill: can everyone be in the car with go-bags in 10 minutes? Drive your routes once.

## Checklist
- Designate primary rendezvous location
- Designate secondary rendezvous location
- Ensure Out-of-area Contact Plan is complete
- Go-Bag packed and ready for each family member
- Evacuation zone recorded
- Signed up for local emergency alerts

## Check internet, local radio or television stations, or NOAA weather radio to find out evacuation needs in your area.
