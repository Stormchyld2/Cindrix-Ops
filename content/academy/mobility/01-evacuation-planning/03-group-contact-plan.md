When people scatter, the biggest problem is knowing who is safe and where everyone is. A contact plan fixes that before it happens.

## The layers of the plan
1. **Out-of-area contact.** Phone lines and cell towers near a disaster jam, while a call or text to someone far away often goes through when local calls do not. Pick a person outside your region. Every family member keeps their number. Everyone checks in with that person, who can pass news on. Texts often go through when voice calls do not.
2. **Family rendezvous points.** Each family has a **primary** and an **alternate** place to meet (see the evacuation lesson).
3. **Local group contact.** Each family or closest local group contact has a primary and alternate rendezvous point between their homes.
4. **Area rendezvous points.** Each area has a primary and an alternate rendezvous point.
5. **Group rendezvous points.** The group has a primary and an alternate rendezvous point **outside of the region.**
6. **In case of emergency (I.C.E.).** Every family member knows how to contact the closest local group contact.

## The plan in order
When something happens:

1. **Make sure your own household is safe.** Account for everyone.
2. **Call or text your out-of-area contact** to say who you are with and that you are safe.
3. **Go to your family rendezvous** if you are apart and it is safe.
4. **Contact your closest local group contact.**
5. **Use your group communications plan** (see the Communications course and its PACE plan) if phones fail.
6. **Move to your group rendezvous** if the situation calls for it.

## What every member carries
A small card in a wallet or phone case, plus a copy in the go bag and the car:
- Out-of-area contact name and number.
- Family rendezvous A and B.
- Group rendezvous A and B.
- Local group contact.
- Medical information and allergies.
- A PACE plan summary (primary, alternate, contingency, emergency).

Do not rely on a phone alone. A dead battery means a lost contact list.

## Kids
- Teach children their phone number and the out-of-area contact.
- Put the card in their school bag.
- Practice what to do if no adult is there: where to go, who to find.
- Give the school the names of everyone who may pick up your child.

## Test your family
Hold a drill:
- Pretend phones are down. Can everyone get to the rendezvous?
- Pretend the main route is closed. Use the alternate.
- Time it and note the problems.

**Immediately fix any deficiencies noted during the exercise.**

## Checklist
- List each household's rendezvous locations (primary and alternate)
- List the group rendezvous locations
- Choose and share the out-of-area contact
- Make wallet cards for everyone
- Test the family and fix the gaps

> The plan only counts if each person can carry it out without asking anyone.

## Check yourself
? Why use an out-of-area contact?
- It is required by law
- Distant people are always safer
+ Local lines jam while distant calls and texts often still go through
- It is cheaper
> A far-away person can relay news.

? What should you do first when something happens?
- Post online
+ Make sure your own household is safe and accounted for
- Call everyone you know
- Drive to the group rendezvous
> Safety of your household comes first.

? What do you do if your test finds a gap?
+ Fix it immediately
- Drop the plan
- Wait for next year
- Ignore it
> Drills exist to find problems while they are cheap.

? Where should the group have a rendezvous point?
- Only at one home
+ Primary and alternate, one set outside of the region
- Only online
- None
> A fallback outside the hazard region matters.
