Prepare the vehicle you expect to rely on first.

Neglecting regular maintenance means your vehicle may break down at the worst time. Imagine being in a convoy with your teammates heading for a destination when your car dies. Your family would have to rely on others to get them and their gear there. Do not be that person.

## Have a mechanic check
Ready.gov lists these items to have checked before an emergency, ideally before each winter and before each long trip:

- Antifreeze (coolant) level
- Battery and ignition system
- Brakes
- Exhaust system
- Fuel and air filters
- Heater and defroster
- Lights and hazard lights
- Oil
- Thermostat
- Windshield wipers and washer fluid level

## Basic maintenance items
- **Oil and filter changes** at the interval in your owner's manual.
- **Tire rotation** and tread checks. Check pressure monthly, including the spare. A tire can pass the penny test and still be old: look for the date code and replace tires that are very old or cracked.
- **Belts and hoses.** Check for cracking, fraying or bulging, and replace according to the manufacturer's schedule.
- **Lights.** Check headlights, taillights, brake lights and turn signals. Keep spare bulbs and fuses.
- **Battery.** Keep terminals clean of corrosion. Batteries typically last three to five years. Pushing one past five years can cause failure without warning, so many manufacturers recommend replacement around that age, sooner in hot climates.
- **Fluids.** Keep brake, power steering, transmission and washer fluids topped up.
- **Wipers.** Replace them once a year.
- **Air filter and cabin filter.** Replace as scheduled. A clean cabin filter helps in smoky air.

## Fuel habits
- **Never let the tank fall below half.** Keep the tank full when an evacuation or power outage seems likely. A full tank also helps prevent fuel-line freezing in winter.
- Fuel pumps need power. During an outage, stations may be closed.
- Aim to be able to drive about **300 miles** without refueling elsewhere.
- Rotate stored fuel (see the vehicle type lesson).

## Know your vehicle
- Know **how to change a tire,** and practice once. Check the jack and the lug wrench, and that the spare is inflated.
- Know **how to jump-start** a battery safely.
- Know where the fuse box, the hood latch and the jack points are.
- Know **how to open the fuel door and the hood** if the electrics fail.
- Know your **tow capacity** and recovery points.
- Keep the owner's manual in the glovebox.

## Keep records
Keep a small log in the glovebox: last oil change, tire rotation, battery age and the next service due. Record your odometer when you fill up.

## Good driving habits
- Keep your phone charged and a car charger in the vehicle.
- Park nose-out where possible so you can leave quickly.
- Keep the car clean inside so you can find gear in the dark.
- Lock the car and keep valuables out of sight.
- Monitor the weather before you drive.

## Checklist
- Fuel level maintained above half tank
- Stock vehicle first aid kit
- Food and water supply in vehicle
- Paper map in vehicle
- Basic recovery items
- Emergency blankets and shelter tarp
- Oil, lube and filter change within recommended interval
- Battery age recorded
- Spare tire checked and jack tested

> Maintenance is the cheapest preparedness you will ever do.
