Your plan should have two parts: **escape** and **shelter.** Run, hide, tell is the common guide: get out if you can, hide if you cannot, and call for help.

## Escape plan
- **Two ways out** of every room, if possible.
- A **meeting place** outside, such as a mailbox or a tree.
- Keep exits clear of furniture, boxes and clutter.
- Practice at night and in the dark with the whole family twice a year.
- Teach children to crawl low in smoke, to feel doors, and to never go back inside.
- Keep shoes and a flashlight by the bed.

## Fire
- Smoke alarms and extinguishers (ABC) in the kitchen, garage and near bedrooms.
- Know **PASS:** pull, aim, squeeze, sweep.
- Close doors behind you to slow fire.
- Get out, stay out, call 911 from outside.

## Shelter-in-place room
A safe room is a room where you can secure yourself while help comes.
- **Choose** an interior room with a solid door, near the bedrooms, with no windows or only small ones.
- **Reinforce:** solid-core door, deadbolt, strong frame.
- **Stock:** a phone with a charger, a flashlight, a first aid kit, water, a whistle, a copy of the family plan, and a way to call out (cell and a backup).
- Tell everyone that the room is for **calling for help and waiting,** not for confrontation.
- Do not trap yourself without a way out in a fire.

## If someone breaks in
1. **Get yourself and others away,** if you can do so safely: out the back, to a neighbor.
2. **If you cannot leave,** get to the safe room, lock the door and call 911. Stay on the line.
3. **Be quiet and give information:** your address, the number of people, whether you have seen the person, and any weapons.
4. **Do not go looking** for the intruder. Wait for police, and when they arrive, show empty hands and follow instructions.

## Children
Practice a code word. Teach children to hide, call and wait. Do not ask them to confront anyone.

## Pets
Plan how to get pets out. Do not risk life for property.

> Laws on self-defense, firearms and force differ by state and country. This course is general safety education, not legal advice. Talk to a local attorney and take a certified class.
