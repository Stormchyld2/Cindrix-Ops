When it is over, the work is not. Looking after people, the record and the property all matter.

## First: people
- **Check everyone** for injury. Give first aid. Call 911 for serious injuries.
- **Look after fear:** stay together, use a calm voice, give water, warmth, and quiet. Children may need extra comfort.
- **Do not touch** the scene more than needed.

## Call it in
- **Police:** report burglary, assault, threats or vandalism. A report supports insurance and helps others.
- **Insurance:** call your insurer soon.
- **Landlord or HOA** if relevant.

## Preserve evidence
- Do not clean up before police see it, unless safety demands it.
- Photograph everything: damage, tracks, items missing.
- Write down a description of events with times while fresh.
- Note serial numbers of stolen items from your records.

## Make it safe
- Board up broken windows and doors, change locks, and change codes.
- Cancel stolen cards and replace IDs.
- Change passwords if devices were taken.
- Ask police if there is more risk.

## Repair and strengthen
- Use the incident as a lesson. Fix the weak point that was used.
- Add lighting, locks, alarms or habits.
- Tell neighbors, and ask them to keep watch.

## Emotional recovery
It is normal to feel shaky, angry, sad or afraid, to have trouble sleeping or replay the event. Most of this fades. Talk with trusted people. If it lasts weeks or interferes with life, seek a counselor. In the US, call or text **988** for support in a crisis. Victim assistance services are free.

## Money and legal
- Keep receipts for repairs and replacements.
- Ask a lawyer if there was a use of force or if you need to protect your rights.
- Apply for victim compensation where it exists.

## Review
Within a week, hold a family review: what we did well, what to change, and update the plan.

> Laws on self-defense, firearms and force differ by state and country. This course is general safety education, not legal advice. Talk to a local attorney and take a certified class.
