Security is not about fear or weapons. It is a system of choices that make harm less likely and less severe. Avoidance and early warning come first, and force is a last resort.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- List what you protect: **people first,** then pets, supplies, vehicles, documents, tools and the home itself.
- List what could threaten it: burglary, theft, vandalism, fire, a violent stranger, a domestic or neighbor dispute, looting after a disaster, stalking, scams, an outsider who learns that you have supplies.
- Rate each by how **likely** it is and how **bad** it would be.

## 2. Preparedness
- Build layers of protection (next modules).
- Make a **plan** and rules for the household.
- Equip: lighting, locks, alarms, communications, first aid, fire safety, and legal means of defense if you choose them.
- Train: drills for fire, intruder response and leaving quickly.

## 3. Response
When something happens: **get safe, get help, protect life, preserve evidence.** Do not chase. Call 911 as soon as you safely can.

## 4. Mitigation
Reduce the harm: hide valuables, spread supplies, fire-proof papers, lock storage, keep copies, keep your profile low.

## 5. Recovery
After an incident: care for injuries and fear, report to police, repair, review and improve.

## What security is not
- It is not a lifestyle of fear.
- It is not guns first. Most home safety comes from lighting, locks, habits and neighbors.
- It is not vigilante action. Your job is to protect, not to punish.

## Principles
- **Avoid:** do not be where danger is.
- **Deter:** make yourself a harder target.
- **Detect:** know early.
- **Delay:** slow a threat down.
- **Respond:** act safely, call for help.
- **Recover:** care and learn.
These are the **layers** of defense, and the rest of the course follows them.

> The best defense is a place nobody wants to bother, a family that has practiced and a neighbor who will call you.

> Laws on self-defense, firearms and force differ by state and country. This course is general safety education, not legal advice. Talk to a local attorney and take a certified class.
