A shelter protects you from wind, rain, cold and sun. In most weather, **a small, well-insulated shelter** beats a big one.

## Choose the spot
- Out of low ground that floods and away from dead branches that can fall.
- Near water and firewood, but not on a game trail or a dry streambed.
- Shielded from the wind.

## Insulate
1. Make a thick bed of dry leaves, grass, boughs or foam. **Heat is lost to the ground** faster than to the air.
2. Keep the roof low and the inside small so your body heat warms the space.
3. Leave a small vent for fresh air.

## A simple lean-to
- Prop a strong pole between two trees or on a forked branch.
- Lean branches along it at an angle.
- Cover with layers of leaves, bark or a tarp, starting at the bottom.

> Do not seal yourself in with a fire or stove. Smoke and carbon monoxide are deadly in a small closed space.

## Check yourself
? Where do you lose the most heat when resting outdoors?
+ Into the cold ground, so insulate underneath
- Through your hat only
- Into dry air
> Ground contact pulls heat from your body. Build a thick bed first.
? Which campsite is safest?
+ Out of flood-prone low ground and away from dead branches
- A dry streambed
- Under a dead tree for cover
> Flash floods and falling limbs kill campers. Choose higher, sheltered ground.
? Why leave a small vent in a small shelter?
+ Fresh air; smoke and carbon monoxide can build up
- To let heat escape on purpose
- To keep insects out
> Never seal yourself in with a fire or stove.
