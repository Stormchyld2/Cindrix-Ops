[video] Clothing, layers and staying dry

Good clothing is the first shelter. Dressing well can keep you alive when everything else goes wrong.

## The rule: COLD
- **C**lean: dirt and sweat reduce insulation.
- **O**verheating: avoid it. Sweat then chills you. Vent and remove layers.
- **L**ayers: wear several, and adjust.
- **D**ry: wet clothing can lose heat many times faster. Keep dry.

## Layers
1. **Base layer:** next to the skin, wicking material that moves sweat away (wool or synthetic).
2. **Middle layer:** insulation (fleece, wool, down or synthetic fill).
3. **Outer layer:** a shell that blocks wind and rain.
4. **Extras:** hat, gloves or mittens (mittens are warmer), neck gaiter, warm socks.

**Avoid cotton** in cold and wet. It soaks up water and dries slowly ("cotton kills"). Wool and synthetics keep insulating when damp.

## Hot weather
- **Light, loose, long-sleeved clothes** protect from sun.
- **A wide-brim hat.**
- **Light colors** reflect heat.
- **Wet** a cloth on your neck.
- **Rest in the shade** at midday.
- **Drink** and eat salty snacks.

## Feet
- **Socks:** wool or synthetic. Carry spares.
- **Boots** that fit. Break them in before the trip.
- **Dry your feet** at every stop. Treat hot spots at once with tape or moleskin.
- **Gaiters** keep debris and water out.

## In an emergency, improvise
- **Stuff dry leaves, grass or newspaper** inside clothing for insulation (keep it dry).
- **Use a trash bag** as a rain poncho or vapor barrier, with a hole for the head.
- **Use a space blanket** to reflect heat, and as a ground sheet.
- **Wrap** a blanket, towel or extra shirt around your head and neck.
- **Put your feet in your pack** if you have no spare insulation.

## Keep gear dry
- Use **dry bags** or trash bags inside your pack.
- Keep **spare clothes** in a bag.
- **Dry wet items** at a fire, or on your pack during the day. Do not dry boots too close to a fire.

## Warning signs
- **Shivering, numb fingers,** stumbling and mumbling are signs of **hypothermia.** See the first aid module.
- **Heat cramps, headache,** dizziness and nausea are signs of **heat illness.**

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.

## Clothing is your first shelter
What you wear keeps you alive before any shelter exists. Dress for the weather you expect and the weather that might happen, and carry spare layers. Wet and wind turn mild weather deadly, so always carry a rain shell.

## The layer system
- **Base layer:** wicks sweat. Wool or synthetic, never cotton in the cold.
- **Insulating layer:** traps warm air. Fleece, wool or a puffy jacket.
- **Shell layer:** blocks wind and rain, with vents.
- **Extras:** hat, gloves, neck gaiter, spare socks, a space blanket.
Add or remove layers to avoid sweating. Open vents, take off a layer before you heat up, and put it on before you chill.

## COLD
Remember **C-O-L-D**: keep clothing **C**lean, avoid **O**verheating, wear **L**ayers and keep it **D**ry.

## Feet and hands
Feet carry you. Keep them dry, change wet socks at once, check for hot spots and treat blisters early. Keep hands covered and warm, because cold hands make every task harder.

## Improvise
In an emergency, stuff dry leaves or grass in clothing for insulation, wrap a trash bag over your torso under a jacket, and use a space blanket as a vapor barrier. Avoid anything that makes you sweat inside plastic for long.

## Warning signs
Shivering that will not stop, numbness, clumsiness, slurred speech or confusion mean hypothermia is starting. Get out of the weather, add dry layers, give warm drinks and food if the person is awake, and call for help.
