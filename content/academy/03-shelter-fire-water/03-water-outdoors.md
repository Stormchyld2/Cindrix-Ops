Water outdoors can look clean and still carry germs that cause severe diarrhea. **Treat all wild water.**

## Where to look
- Moving streams are usually better than still ponds.
- Water collects at the bottom of valleys. Following a stream downhill often leads to people.
- Rainwater caught in clean containers or a tarp is the safest wild source.
- Morning dew can be soaked up with a cloth and wrung out.

## Make it safe
1. **Strain** cloudy water through cloth.
2. **Boil** at a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above about 6,500 feet).
3. If you cannot boil, use a commercial filter or purification tablets exactly as the package says.

## Do not
- Drink seawater or urine. They dehydrate you.
- Wait until you are very thirsty to start looking.
- Ration water you have while sitting still. Drink when thirsty and look for more.

> Pale urine is a good sign. Dark urine means you need more water.

## Check yourself
? How should you treat water from a stream?
+ Strain, then boil or filter it
- Drink it if it is moving
- Just let it settle
> Moving water can still carry germs. Treat all wild water.
? Which natural source is usually the safest to collect?
+ Rainwater caught in clean containers
- A muddy pond
- Seawater
> Rain caught directly is clean. Seawater dehydrates you.
? What does dark urine usually tell you?
+ You need more water
- You are well hydrated
- You need more salt
> Pale urine means good hydration; dark means you are getting short.
