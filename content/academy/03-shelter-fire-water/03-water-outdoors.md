[video] Finding and treating water outdoors

Without water you weaken quickly, especially in heat. Outdoors, finding and treating water is a priority.

## How much
In moderate conditions plan on about **a half gallon (2 liters)** a day or more. In heat or hard work, you may need **a gallon (4 liters)** or more. Drink **before** you are thirsty. Urine that is **pale yellow** means you are hydrated, and **dark** means you need more.

## Where to find water
- **Running water:** streams and rivers are better than still water. Collect upstream from camp.
- **Springs** and seeps, where water comes out of the ground. Look for green plants.
- **Lakes and ponds,** with caution.
- **Rain:** collect with a tarp, poncho, or containers.
- **Snow and ice:** melt first. Eating snow lowers body temperature.
- **Dew:** wipe grass with a cloth in the morning and wring it out.
- **Dry stream beds:** dig at the outside of a bend where water may seep.
- **Vegetation:** green plants and animal trails can lead to water. Birds fly toward water in the morning and evening.
- **Solar still:** it gives little water. It is generally not worth the effort and sweat unless you have a plastic sheet and the right conditions.
- **Do not drink** seawater, urine, blood or alcohol. They make dehydration worse.

## Which water is risky
Wild water can hold **bacteria** (E. coli), **protozoa** (Giardia, Cryptosporidium), **viruses** and **chemicals.** Clear water is not safe water. Cloudy water, algae, dead animals upstream, farms and mines are warning signs.

## Treat it: pick a method
1. **Boil:** the surest. A rolling boil for **1 minute** (3 minutes above 6,500 ft). Let it cool.
2. **Filter:** backpacking pump or squeeze filters (0.2 micron or smaller) remove bacteria and protozoa. For viruses, use a purifier or add chemicals.
3. **Chemical:** chlorine dioxide tablets, or bleach as in the first module, or iodine for short use. Follow the package. Wait the full time, longer when cold or cloudy.
4. **UV pens:** work on clear water. Pre-filter cloudy water.
5. **Combine:** filter then chemical gives the most protection.

## Pre-treat cloudy water
Let it settle, pour through a cloth, coffee filter or a sand and charcoal filter, then boil or treat.

## Make a simple filter
A bottle with the bottom cut off, filled with layers of cloth, charcoal, sand and gravel, clears the water. **It does not make it safe.** You still must boil or treat it.

## Stay hydrated
- **Sip** regularly.
- **Rest in shade** in heat.
- **Eat salty snacks** if sweating heavily.
- **Watch the signs:** dark urine, headache, dizziness, dry mouth, fatigue.
- See the first aid module for heat illness.

## If you cannot find water
- **Stay put** if rescue is likely, and stay in shade.
- **Move at night** or early morning in heat.
- **Do not ration water** in your body: drink when you have it.
- **Do not eat** if you have no water, since digestion uses water.
- **Signal for help.**

> Clear is not clean. Boil, filter, or treat every time.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.

## Think like water
Water flows downhill and collects in low places. Look in valleys, at the base of cliffs, along game trails and where green plants thrive. Birds fly to water at dawn and dusk. Rainwater caught in a tarp is the cleanest source in the field. In dry country, dig in the outside bend of a dry stream bed.

## Treat everything
Clear-looking water is not safe. Streams and ponds carry bacteria, protozoa and viruses, and water near farms, mines and towns can carry chemicals. Always treat: pre-filter, filter and then boil or use chemicals.

## A pre-filter you can build
Cut a bottle's bottom off and invert it. Layer, from top to bottom, cloth, grass, sand, charcoal and gravel, and pour water through. This removes sediment only. It does not make water safe. You must still boil or treat the result.

## Stay hydrated
Drink small amounts often. Watch your urine: pale means hydrated, dark means drink. In heat, rest in shade during the day and travel at dusk and dawn. Eat little when water is short, because digesting food uses water.

## If you cannot find water
Stay put and signal if rescue is likely. Collect dew with a cloth in the early morning. Make a solar still only if you have plastic and time, and know it yields little. Never drink urine or seawater, which dehydrate you.
