In a crisis, most people on the road are scared, not dangerous. A small number of threats are worth planning for, and the plan is mostly avoidance.

## Carjacking and robbery
- **Prevent:** keep doors locked, windows up and valuables out of sight. Keep a gap in traffic. Be alert at stoplights, in parking lots, at gas stations and at ATMs.
- **If confronted:** your life is worth more than the vehicle. **Do not resist** if they want the car. Give up the keys. Move away and call 911. Try to remember details to report.
- If someone is forcing you into a car, this is a very dangerous situation that needs a different judgment, and the best choice depends on the situation. Resist, make noise and try to escape if you can.
- **Do not follow** a thief.

## "Bump and rob" and staged problems
- Someone hits your car lightly or flags you down to make you stop. If you feel unsafe, **do not get out.** Drive to a public, well-lit place or a police station and call 911.
- Do not stop for unmarked vehicles with lights, if you are alone, at night or in a remote place. Slow down, turn on your hazard lights, call 911 to confirm, and drive to a well-lit public place.

## Checkpoints and roadblocks
- **Official checkpoints** (police, National Guard, emergency crews): slow down, be polite, have documents ready, keep hands visible, and follow instructions.
- **Unofficial roadblocks** (armed civilians, debris): do not approach. Turn around and leave if you can safely do so. Report to police.

## Blocked roads and gridlock
- **Prepare** several routes. Keep maps and a compass.
- If a road is blocked by debris, flooding or a crash, **turn around.** Do not cross private land or break barriers.
- In gridlock, keep a gap, stay in the vehicle if it is safe, conserve fuel, and listen to the radio.
- Do not drive on shoulders or the wrong way. It blocks emergency vehicles.

## Convoys
If traveling with others: agree on routes, signals, speeds, and a regroup point. Keep a distance but stay in sight. Use radios. The last vehicle should stay with anyone who breaks down.

## Gas stations and stops
- Fill up in daylight at busy, lit stations.
- Keep valuables out of sight, lock the car when you go inside.
- Stay alert and leave if you feel unsafe.

## Trust yourself
If something feels wrong, leave. Do not argue, bluff or challenge anyone.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
