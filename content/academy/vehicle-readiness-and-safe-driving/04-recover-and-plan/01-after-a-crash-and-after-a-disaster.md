What you do in the first minutes after a crash, and in the days after a disaster, shapes the outcome.

## At a crash
1. **Check for injuries.** Call 911 if anyone is hurt or if the road is blocked.
2. **Stay safe.** If the car is drivable and the crash is minor, move it off the road. If not, turn on hazard lights and stay clear of traffic.
3. **Give first aid** within your training. Do not move seriously hurt people unless there is fire or other danger.
4. **Exchange information:** names, phone numbers, insurance, license and plate numbers.
5. **Do not argue** or admit fault.
6. **Take photographs** of vehicles, damage, the scene, road conditions and injuries.
7. **Get witnesses' contact details.**
8. **Report** to police if required or if there is injury or major damage.
9. **Call your insurer.**

## Mental health
A crash can be frightening. It is normal to feel shaky or upset. Seek care for pain after a crash, even delayed, since injuries such as whiplash can appear later.

## After a flood
- **Do not start a flooded engine.** Water can wreck it.
- Disconnect the battery if safe, and have the vehicle towed and inspected.
- Dry and clean the interior. Mold grows fast.
- Check with your insurer about flood coverage.

## After a storm or fire
Check for damage, debris and leaks, glass, and hail. Document it with photographs.

## Keep papers
Keep a folder with title, registration, insurance, service records and a list of items in the vehicle.

## Review
After any incident, ask what helped, what you lacked, and what you will change in the kit or plan.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
