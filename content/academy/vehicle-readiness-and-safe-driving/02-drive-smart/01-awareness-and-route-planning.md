Good drivers see trouble early and have a plan.

## Before you go
- **Check the weather, traffic and road conditions** (state 511 services, NWS).
- **Plan the route** and an alternate. Know where fuel, hospitals and safe places are on the way.
- **Tell someone** your route and when you expect to arrive.
- **Rest.** Tired driving is as dangerous as drunk driving.
- **Check the vehicle** (lights, tires, fuel).
- **Leave early** in an emergency. Evacuation roads clog fast.

## While driving
- **Scan:** look far ahead, check mirrors every 5 to 8 seconds, check blind spots.
- **Keep a gap:** at least 3 to 4 seconds behind the car ahead, more in rain, snow or at night.
- **Expect mistakes** from other drivers. Cover the brake at intersections.
- **Avoid distractions:** no phone in hand, set the route first.
- **Watch pedestrians, cyclists and animals,** especially at dusk and dawn.
- **Keep an "out":** space to the side, an open lane or a shoulder you can move to if the car ahead stops suddenly.
- **Do not drive impaired** or when angry. If someone is road-raging, let them go.

## Awareness of threats
- Notice a vehicle that stays behind you through several turns. Drive to a busy, public place such as a police station, a fire station or a busy store and call for help.
- Stay in lanes that keep you mobile. Do not box yourself in at lights: leave a car length so you can pull out.
- Keep doors locked and windows up in slow traffic. Keep valuables out of sight.
- At night, park in lit, busy places. Look inside before you get in. Have keys ready.

## Parking
- Back in, if you can, so you can leave easily.
- Keep the vehicle facing out.
- Do not park in isolated places.
- Take valuables with you or hide them before arriving, not after you park.

## Paper maps and landmarks
GPS and phones fail. Know the main roads and the names of towns on your routes.

## Know the exits
Learn at least two ways out of your neighborhood, your work and your children's school. Drive them.

## Plan for the road
Keep a road atlas, a note of key phone numbers and spare keys in your kit.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
