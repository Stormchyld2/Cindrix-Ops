Most breakdowns come from a few neglected parts. Check them often.

## Monthly checks (five minutes)
- **Tires:** pressure (cold) as on the door-jamb sticker, tread depth (a penny test: if you can see all of Lincoln's head, replace), cracks and bulges, and the spare.
- **Fluids:** engine oil, coolant (only when the engine is cool), brake fluid, washer fluid, power steering.
- **Lights:** headlights, brake lights, signals, hazards.
- **Wipers and washer fluid.**
- **Battery terminals:** clean and tight, no corrosion.
- **Belts and hoses:** cracks, fraying, bulges.

## Seasonal checks
- **Before winter:** battery test, antifreeze strength, winter tires or chains, an ice scraper, a shovel and sand or kitty litter for traction.
- **Before summer:** cooling system, air conditioning, tire pressure and tread.
- **Before a long trip:** full service.

## Service schedule
Follow your owner's manual: oil changes, filters, brakes, spark plugs, belts, and fluids. Keep records in the glovebox or in a folder.

## Warning signs
Strange noises, vibration, pulling to one side, warning lights, hard starting, smells of burning or fuel, and leaks need attention. Do not ignore the check engine light or oil pressure warning. Pull over safely for red lights.

## The battery
Batteries last about 3 to 5 years. Weak ones fail in cold. Test yearly. Keep a jump starter pack or jumper cables.

## Tires
Tires are your only contact with the road. Rotate them, keep them inflated, and replace them when worn. Know how to change a flat, and practice once at home, with the jack, wrench and lug nuts.

## The tool kit
Jack and wrench, jumper cables or a jump pack, a tire pressure gauge, a small toolbox, tire sealant or a plug kit, a flashlight, duct tape, zip ties, work gloves, a funnel and a quart of oil.

## Know your car
Read the manual. Know where the spare, jack, fuses and fluid fills are. Know how to open the hood and release the fuel door.

## Choosing a vehicle
For emergencies, reliability, ground clearance, fuel range, parts availability and ease of repair matter more than looks.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
