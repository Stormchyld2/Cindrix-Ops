This is the lesson people want to skip to, and it's the one where people get hurt. A generator or power station isn't required to get through an outage, but it sure makes a long one easier.

Portable power stations are quiet, safe indoors, and great for phones, lights, CPAPs, and small appliances. Gas or propane generators can run a fridge, a well pump, or a furnace blower, but they come with rules. Outside only, at least 20 feet from the house. Never plug one into a wall outlet to power your home (called backfeeding), because it can electrocute a lineworker down the street. If you want your generator to run house circuits, have an electrician install a transfer switch or interlock.

And please, test it before you need it. A generator that's been sitting in the garage for two years with old gas in it probably won't start.

## Gear you might need

- Portable power station and solar panel
- Generator sized to what you actually need to run
- Heavy duty outdoor extension cords
- Fuel stored safely, with stabilizer

## Checklist
- Decided what you'd actually need to power in a long outage
- Generator placed and run outside only, 20 feet from the house
- Transfer switch or interlock installed if powering house circuits
- Ran your generator or power station in the last 3 months
- Fuel stored safely and rotated
