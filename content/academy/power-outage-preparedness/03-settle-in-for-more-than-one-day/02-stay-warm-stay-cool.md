Temperature is what turns a long outage dangerous, especially for babies, older folks, and anyone with a health condition.

In the cold, pick one room (ideally one with few windows), close the doors, and live in it. Layers, hats, sleeping bags, and a few people in one space keep things surprisingly warm. Only use a fireplace or wood stove if it's built and vented for heating.

In the heat, keep blinds closed during the day, drink more water than you think you need, and stay on the lowest floor. If it gets bad, find out where your county has opened warming or cooling centers. Leaving is a perfectly good plan.

## Gear you might need

- Warm sleeping bags rated for your climate
- Wool blankets
- Battery powered fans
- Electrolyte packets

## Checklist
- Picked your "warm room" for winter outages
- Sleeping bags or heavy blankets for everyone in the house
- A plan to stay cool that doesn't need AC
- Know how to find local warming and cooling centers
