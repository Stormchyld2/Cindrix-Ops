Somewhere around the 24 hour mark, an outage stops being an inconvenience and starts being a situation. Food starts to go, the house gets too hot or too cold, and everyone's phone is on 10 percent. This is where your prep really pays off.

If the outage is going long, start eating from the fridge first, then the freezer, then the pantry. Move the things you want to save into coolers with ice before the fridge warms up.

The rule from FoodSafety.gov is simple: perishable food (meat, dairy, eggs, leftovers) that's been above 40°F for more than 2 hours should go. Frozen food that still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below can go back in the freezer. When in doubt, throw it out. Food poisoning during an outage is a miserable combination.

If your stove is electric, you'll need another way to cook. Camp stoves, propane burners, and grills all work, but use them outside only, never in the house, garage, or a tent. They give off carbon monoxide (see Stay Safe While It's Dark). If you have a gas stovetop, you can usually still light it with a match and cook on it. Just don't use it to heat the house.

## Checklist
- Know the 40°F and 2 hour rule
- Coolers and ice ready to go for long outages
- A way to cook outdoors with enough fuel for a few days
