Somewhere around the 24-hour mark, an outage stops being an inconvenience and starts being a situation. Food starts to go, the house gets too hot or too cold, and everyone's phone is at 10 percent. This is where your preparation pays off.

## Eat in the right order
1. **The refrigerator first,** perishables most likely to spoil.
2. **The freezer second,** starting with items that thaw quickly (ice cream, fish, thin cuts).
3. **The pantry last.**

Move the things you want to save into coolers with ice **before** the refrigerator warms up.

## The rules from FoodSafety.gov
- A **refrigerator** keeps food safe for up to **4 hours** without power, **if the door stays closed.**
- Discard perishable food (meat, poultry, fish, eggs, leftovers) after 4 hours without power. The key number is food at **40°F (4°C) or above for more than 2 hours.**
- A **full freezer** keeps a safe temperature for about **48 hours.** A half-full freezer keeps for about **24 hours.**
- Frozen food that still has **ice crystals** or is at **40°F or below** can be **refrozen** or cooked, though quality may suffer.
- **Never taste food to judge safety.** When in doubt, throw it out. Food poisoning in an outage is a miserable combination.

## Discard (refrigerated food held above 40°F for over 2 hours)
- Raw or leftover cooked meat, poultry, fish and seafood
- Lunchmeats, hot dogs, bacon, sausage
- Soft cheeses, shredded cheese
- Milk, cream, sour cream, yogurt, opened baby formula
- Fresh eggs, hard-cooked eggs, custards, quiche
- Cut fresh fruit, cut vegetables, cooked vegetables
- Cooked pasta, rice, potatoes and potato salad
- Opened creamy dressings, spaghetti sauce

## Usually keep
- Hard cheeses (cheddar, Swiss, Parmesan), processed cheese
- Butter and margarine
- Uncut fresh fruits and vegetables, dried fruit, opened juice
- Peanut butter, jelly, mustard, ketchup, pickles, olives
- Bread, rolls, tortillas, cakes, muffins
- Fresh herbs and spices

## Frozen food after thawing
- **Meat, poultry, seafood, soups and stews:** refreeze if ice crystals remain. Discard if warm for more than 2 hours.
- **Ice cream and frozen yogurt:** discard if thawed.
- **Vegetables:** refreeze if ice crystals remain. Discard if held above 40°F for more than 6 hours.
- **Hard cheeses, bread, flour, nuts:** refreeze or keep.

## Use thermometers
Use an **appliance thermometer.** If you do not have one, check each item for ice crystals, and use the 2-hour rule as the limit.

## Keep it safe to cook
- Wash hands with soap and water, or use sanitizer when water is short.
- Cook meat to safe internal temperatures with a food thermometer.
- Keep raw and cooked foods apart.
- Use clean utensils and surfaces.
- Do not leave cooked food out for more than 2 hours (1 hour in heat above 90°F).

## Cooking without power
If your stove is electric, you need another way:
- **Camp stoves, propane burners and grills** all work, but **use them outside only,** never in the house, garage, tent or camper. They produce carbon monoxide.
- **A gas stovetop** can usually be lit with a match. Use it for cooking only, never to heat the house. Ventilate.
- **Sterno or similar fuel** is usable only with good ventilation. Read the label.
- Have **enough fuel** for several days.
- Plan **no-cook meals:** canned food, bars, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit.
- Use the sun: a solar oven works on clear days.

## Water for cooking and cleaning
Use bottled, boiled or treated water.

## Checklist
- Know the 40°F and 2-hour rule
- Coolers and ice ready to go for long outages
- A way to cook outdoors with enough fuel for a few days
- Food thermometer and appliance thermometers
- Plan for no-cook meals

> When in doubt, throw it out.
