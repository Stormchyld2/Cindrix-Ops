Plenty of outages come with a warning if you are paying attention. Utilities often text customers before planned shutoffs, and storms show up in the forecast days ahead. Sign up now so you hear about it before your neighbors do.

## Where warnings come from
- **Your electric utility.** Outage alerts by text, email or app, and planned shutoffs. Some utilities also run **public safety power shutoffs** during high fire danger, so check if yours does.
- **Your county or city.** Emergency alert systems (often "Reverse 911" or a local notification service).
- **Wireless Emergency Alerts (WEA).** Messages sent to your phone for severe weather and other threats. Make sure they are turned on in your phone settings.
- **NOAA Weather Radio.** A radio that sounds an alarm for watches and warnings from the National Weather Service. Choose one with battery backup and a Specific Area Message Encoding (SAME) setting for your county.
- **Local radio and TV.**
- **Weather apps** and the National Weather Service forecast for your location.

## Know the words
- **Watch:** conditions are right for the hazard. Prepare.
- **Warning:** the hazard is happening or about to. Act now.
- **Advisory:** be aware of less severe conditions.

## Know your utility
Save in your phone, and write on a card:
- The **outage reporting number.**
- The **outage map link,** the best source for the estimated restoration time.
- Your **account number.**
- Whether there are **planned shutoffs** in your area.

## Set up a radio that works without wall power
- Battery or hand-crank radio, ideally with the NOAA weather band.
- Spare batteries.
- A two-way radio for your group (see the Communications course).

## Pre-storm routine
When a major storm is forecast, run a short checklist:
1. Charge phones, power banks, lights and the power station.
2. Fill the car's fuel tank, and get cash.
3. Fill water containers and the freezer with water jugs.
4. Do laundry and dishes so you are clean in an outage.
5. Set refrigerator and freezer to the coldest setting.
6. Bring in or secure outdoor objects.
7. Check on neighbors who may need help.
8. Check your group by radio or text.

## Checklist
- Signed up for outage alerts from your electric utility
- Signed up for your county's emergency alert system
- Turned on Wireless Emergency Alerts on every phone
- Saved the utility outage number and outage map link in your phone
- Have a radio that works without wall power
- Wrote the pre-storm routine on a card

> Warning gives you hours. Use them.
