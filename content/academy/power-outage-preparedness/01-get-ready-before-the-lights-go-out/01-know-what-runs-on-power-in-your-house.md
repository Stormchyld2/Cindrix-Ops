The best time to deal with a power outage is on a sunny Tuesday when nothing's wrong. Everything in this section can be done in a weekend, and most of it costs very little.

Walk through your home and ask one question in every room: what stops working when the power does? Some answers are obvious, like lights and the fridge. Others catch people off guard.

If you're on a well, your water pump is electric. No power, no water, no flushing toilets. A lot of furnaces need electricity to run the blower even if they burn gas. Your garage door opener won't open the door your car is parked behind. Your cordless home phone is dead. Your WiFi router is dead, so even if cell service is up, anything that relied on home internet is gone.

Make a short list. That list is your plan for the rest of this course.

A quick note: a lot of this overlaps with your Go Bag and Prepper Pantry from Basic Preparedness. If you've done those, you're already halfway here. This course is about the stuff specific to losing power at home.

## Checklist
- Walked the house and listed everything that needs power
- Know whether your water depends on an electric well pump
- Know whether your heating system works without power
- Found and practiced the manual release on your garage door
- Know where your main electrical panel is and how to flip the main breaker
