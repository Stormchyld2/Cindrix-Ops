Your pantry from Basic Preparedness covers most of this, so think of it as a quick check. Here is what matters for an outage.

## Water
- Store at least **one gallon per person per day,** and more if you are on a well, for drinking and sanitation. Plan for at least three days, and aim for two weeks.
- Include **pets.**
- Fill clean containers or the bathtub **before** a predicted storm if you are on a well or if the water may be cut off.
- Have a way to treat water: a filter, purification tablets or unscented household bleach (see the Water module in Basic Preparedness).
- If the outage disrupts water treatment, your health department may tell you to boil or treat water. Use bottled, boiled or treated water for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth until told otherwise.

## Food
- Keep **at least three days of food** that needs little or no cooking, or that you can heat on a camp stove **outside.**
- Include a **manual can opener,** a pot, a lighter or matches and a cooking fuel supply.
- Keep comfort foods and foods for children and special diets.
- Choose foods that need no water or little water.

### Get the freezer ready
- Keep the freezer at **0°F (-18°C) or colder** and the refrigerator at **40°F (4°C) or colder.**
- Buy **two appliance thermometers,** one for each. They settle every "is this still okay?" argument.
- Keep the freezer full. A full freezer holds its temperature longer than a half-empty one. Fill gaps with water jugs or bags of ice.
- Freeze water in containers (leave room for expansion) and keep them in the freezer. They act as ice in a cooler, and as drinking water when they melt.
- Group foods together so they keep each other cold.
- Know where to get ice, and think about a large cooler and freezer packs.

## Medical needs
If anyone in the house relies on something that plugs in (an oxygen concentrator, a CPAP, a powered wheelchair, a nebulizer or medicines that must stay cold), this lesson matters more than any other.

1. **Ask your doctor, pharmacist or device supplier** how the device works on battery or backup power, and how long it lasts. Write the answers on a card.
2. **Tell your electric company and local fire department** that you have a powered medical device. Many utilities run a medical baseline or priority notification program.
3. **Plan a backup:** battery packs, a portable power station sized for the device, a generator used safely outdoors, or a plan to move to a place with power such as a friend's home or a hospital or shelter if told.
4. **Check the cords.** Do not plug in a power cord if the cord or the device is wet.
5. **Know your emergency numbers,** and when to call 911. If your life depends on a device, seek emergency services right away if your backup fails.
6. **After power returns,** check your device settings, since some devices revert to defaults after an interruption.

### Medicines that need cold
- Ask your pharmacist how long your particular refrigerated medicine can be kept without cold, and what to do. Do not guess.
- Keep a **cooler and freezer packs ready** to hold medicines.
- The CDC advises discarding refrigerated medicines after a day or more without power unless the label says otherwise. If a life depends on a refrigerated medicine that was kept warm, use it only until a new supply is available and replace it as soon as possible, and tell your doctor.
- Keep a **list of your medicines** and a few days' extra supply when allowed.

### Other special needs
- **Infants:** keep ready-to-feed formula and clean water for mixing. Discard opened formula that has been warm.
- **Older adults and people with disabilities:** plan for temperature, mobility, and communication. Ask them what they need.
- **Pets:** food, water, medicines, and a way to keep them warm or cool.

## Checklist
- At least 3 days of water stored for everyone in the house, pets included
- 3 days of food that needs little or no cooking
- Manual can opener in the kitchen
- Backup power plan for any medical device in the home
- Enrolled in your utility's medical or priority program, if it applies
- A cooler and freezer packs ready for medicines that need to stay cold
- Appliance thermometers in the refrigerator and freezer

> If it plugs in and keeps someone alive, it needs a written backup plan.
