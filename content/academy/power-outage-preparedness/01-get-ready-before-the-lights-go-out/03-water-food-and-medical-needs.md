Your Prepper Pantry covers most of this, so think of it as a quick check. Plan on at least one gallon of water per person per day, and more if you're on a well. Keep a few days of food that doesn't need cooking or that you can heat on a camp stove outside.

Get your freezer ready too. FoodSafety.gov recommends keeping the freezer at 0°F or colder and the fridge at 40°F or colder.

The bigger question is medical. If anyone in your house relies on something that plugs in (an oxygen concentrator, a CPAP, a powered wheelchair, insulin that needs to stay cold), this lesson matters more than any other in the course. The FDA adds one step most people miss: tell both your electric company and your local fire department that you have a medical device that needs power. Many utilities also run a medical baseline or priority notification program and may give you a heads up before planned shutoffs.

If you haven't taken the Baseline Preparedness course, learn more about the 3 month pantry below:

## Checklist
- At least 3 days of water stored for everyone in the house, pets included
- 3 days of food that needs little or no cooking
- Manual can opener in the kitchen
- Backup power plan for any medical device in the home
- Enrolled in your utility's medical or priority program, if it applies
- A cooler and freezer packs ready for medications that need to stay cold
