This is where most people start, and it's a good place to start. You want light you can grab in the dark without hunting for it, and a way to keep your phone alive for a few days.

Skip the candles if you can. The CDC recommends battery flashlights and lanterns over candles, gas lanterns, or torches because of the fire risk. If you do end up lighting a candle, keep it away from anything that can burn and stay in the room with it. The CDC also suggests keeping a fire extinguisher handy and making sure everyone knows how to use it. Put a flashlight somewhere you can find easily in the dark, like the drawer next to your bed.

For charging, a couple of power banks will get most people through a short outage. If you want more, a portable power station (basically a big rechargeable battery with outlets on it) can run a phone, a lamp, a CPAP, or a small fridge for a while, and you can recharge it with a folding solar panel.

## Gear you might need

- Headlamps and battery lanterns (one per person is a good goal)
- Extra batteries in the sizes your gear actually uses
- Two or more charged power banks
- Car phone charger
- Portable power station and solar panel (the extra mile option)

## Checklist
- A flashlight or headlamp in every bedroom
- At least one battery lantern for the main living area
- Spare batteries stored together and labeled
- Power banks charged and on a monthly recharge reminder
- Car charger kept in the vehicle
