More people get hurt during outages from the workarounds than from the outage itself. Carbon monoxide is the big one. It has no smell and no color, and it can kill a whole household while they sleep.

## Carbon monoxide (CO)
CO is an odorless, colorless gas that can cause sudden illness and death. You cannot see or smell it.

**Symptoms** are often described as flu-like: headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain and confusion. Heavy exposure can make you pass out or die. People who are sleeping, intoxicated or drugged can die before they show symptoms. Infants, older adults and people with heart disease, anemia or breathing problems are at higher risk.

### Rules that save lives
- **Never run a generator, grill, camp stove or anything that burns fuel inside your home, garage, basement, crawl space or tent,** even with doors and windows open.
- **Keep generators outdoors more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents,** with the exhaust pointing away from the house. Use an extension cord longer than 20 feet.
- **Never use a gas oven or stovetop to heat the house.**
- **Never burn charcoal indoors,** whatever its color. Do not use portable gas camp stoves or flameless chemical heaters indoors.
- **Never leave a vehicle running in a garage,** even with the door open, if the garage is attached to the house.
- **Have a CO detector** (battery-operated or with battery backup) near every sleeping area. Test it when you change your clocks. Replace detectors on the maker's schedule, often every five years.

### If an alarm sounds or someone has symptoms
1. **Get everyone outside into fresh air at once.** Do not stop to collect things.
2. **Call 911** and **Poison Control (1-800-222-1222).**
3. Do not go back in until responders say it is safe.
4. Anyone who had symptoms should be checked by a medical professional.
5. Report generator-related poisoning to the Consumer Product Safety Commission (saferproducts.gov).

## Fire
- Use battery flashlights, not candles.
- Keep an **ABC fire extinguisher** where you can reach it, and know how to use it with the **PASS** steps: **P**ull the pin, **A**im at the base, **S**queeze the handle, **S**weep side to side.
- Keep **smoke alarms** working, with batteries.
- Keep space heaters, fireplaces and wood stoves clear of anything that burns, and only use a fireplace or stove built and vented for heating.
- Have an escape plan and a meeting place, and use it.

## Gas
If you smell gas (a rotten-egg odor):
1. **Do not** use switches, lighters or phones inside.
2. **Get everyone out.**
3. **Call the gas company or 911 from outside.**
4. Do not return until cleared.

## Electrical dangers
- **Downed lines:** stay far away and assume they are live. Never touch anything touching a line.
- **Wet areas:** do not touch electrical equipment while wet. Turn off power at the main breaker if water has reached the circuits.
- **Do not backfeed** a generator through a wall outlet (see the backup power lesson).
- **Cords:** use outdoor-rated, heavy-duty extension cords with the right wire size. Do not run cords under rugs or through doors that close on them.

## Falls and injuries in the dark
- Keep paths clear and mark stairs with glow sticks or tape.
- Use headlamps when carrying things.
- Be careful on stairs, in bathrooms and in the kitchen.
- Keep children and pets close.

## Gear you might need
- Battery-powered carbon monoxide detector (or plug-in with battery backup)
- ABC fire extinguisher
- Smoke alarms with fresh batteries
- Glow sticks

## Checklist
- Working CO detector on every sleeping level of the house
- Everyone in the household knows the generator and grill rules
- Know where your gas shutoff is and what tool opens it
- Fire extinguisher where you can reach it, and everyone knows PASS
- Poison Control number saved (1-800-222-1222)

> Fuel burns outside, always.
