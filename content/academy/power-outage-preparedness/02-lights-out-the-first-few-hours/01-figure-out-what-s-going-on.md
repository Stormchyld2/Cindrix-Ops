The power just went out. Most of the time this is a minor annoyance, and you can treat it that way. But the first hour is when a few small moves make the next few days easier.

## Step 1: Is it just you?
Look out the window. If neighbors still have lights, **check your breaker panel** before you do anything else.
- A **tripped breaker** sits in the middle or "off" position. Switch it fully off, then on. If it trips again, stop and call an electrician. Do not keep resetting.
- If the **main breaker** tripped or you lost only half your power, call your utility or an electrician.
- If the **whole street** is dark, it is an outage.

## Step 2: Get light and safety
- Get your flashlights and headlamps. Do not use candles.
- Make sure everyone is accounted for. Check children and pets.
- Tell everyone what is going on and who will do what.

## Step 3: Report it
Report it to your utility, **do not assume someone else already did.** Use the phone, the app or the web. Give the address, what you saw (flashes, sparks, fallen wires, a blown transformer) and anyone who relies on medical devices.

Then check the **outage map** for an estimated restoration time. Treat the estimate as a guess. It often changes.

## Step 4: Decide how serious this is
Ask:
- **How long is it expected to last?** Hours, a day, many days?
- **What is the weather?** A thunderstorm outage in spring is different from a heat wave or an ice storm in January.
- **Who is at risk?** Babies, older adults, people on oxygen or other powered devices, anyone with a chronic illness.
- **What is at stake?** Food, medicines, water supply, heat or cooling.
- **Is it local or widespread?** Is there wider damage, flooding or unrest?

Use this to choose your **level:**
1. **Minor** (under about 4 hours, mild weather): sit tight and follow the basic rules.
2. **Extended** (over a day): start the long-outage steps.
3. **Severe** (several days, extreme weather, or a medical need): activate your plan, think about moving to shelter or a friend's home, and use your communications plan.

## Step 5: Watch for dangers outside
- **Downed lines:** keep far away. Assume every wire is live. Report to the utility and 911.
- **Gas smell:** get everyone out, then call from outside.
- **Traffic lights out:** treat every intersection as a four-way stop.
- **Neighbors:** check who may need help once your own home is safe.

## Step 6: Get information
Use a battery or hand-crank radio, your phone sparingly, and the utility's updates. Save phone battery. Text instead of calling.

## Do not do
- Do not open the refrigerator or freezer unnecessarily.
- Do not use gas cooking appliances for heat.
- Do not run a generator indoors.
- Do not drive unless you must.

## Checklist
- Know how to tell a tripped breaker from a real outage
- Know how to report an outage to your utility
- Know where to find the estimated restoration time
- Everyone knows the first steps
- You decided on your level (minor, extended, severe)

> Look, report, decide. Then settle in.
