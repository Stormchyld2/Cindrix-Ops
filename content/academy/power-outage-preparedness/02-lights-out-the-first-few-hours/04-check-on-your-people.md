Once your own house is settled, think about who else on your street might be struggling. An older neighbor living alone. Someone on oxygen. A family with a newborn. A quick knock on the door goes a long way, and it is the kind of thing a prepared community is built for.

## Check yourselves first
1. **Account for everyone in your household** and for pets.
2. **Make sure your own situation is stable:** light, warmth or cooling, water, food and any medical needs.
3. **Then go out.** You cannot help others if you are in trouble yourself.

## Contact your people
- **Out-of-area contact.** A short text: who you are with, where, that you are safe.
- **Your group.** Use your communications plan (see the Communications course): the primary method, then the alternate, contingency and emergency methods. A radio check works when phones do not.
- **Family and friends.** Use text rather than calls. Texts often get through when calls will not, and use less battery.
- **Buddy.** Your buddy checks that you are OK and you check them.

## Know who may need extra help
Make a list before the next outage, kept private and used only to help:
- Older adults living alone.
- People on oxygen or other powered medical devices.
- People with limited mobility or in a wheelchair.
- Families with infants and small children.
- People with chronic illness or who are dependent on medicines.
- People who do not speak English well.
- Anyone isolated.

## How to check on someone
1. **Go in pairs** if you can, in daylight if you can.
2. **Knock, call out and wait.** Identify yourself.
3. **Ask:** "Are you OK? Do you have light, food, water, heat or cooling, and any medicines you need? Do you need anything from me?"
4. **Offer simple help:** a hot meal, a ride to a warming or cooling center, a place to charge a phone, a bag of ice, a visit later.
5. **Note what they need** and who can provide it.
6. **Never enter a home** without permission unless someone is in danger. If you are worried and get no answer, call the non-emergency line or 911.
7. **Respect privacy** and pride. Do it kindly.
8. **Leave a contact** number or note.

## Call 911 if
- Someone is unresponsive, not breathing normally or confused.
- Someone shows signs of carbon monoxide poisoning, heat stroke or hypothermia.
- A medical device has failed and the person depends on it.

## Share resources
- Offer a place to warm up, cool down or charge phones.
- Share a hot meal or a stove.
- Pool ice and coolers.
- Share information: restoration time, shelter locations.
- Be careful not to give away what you will need yourself. Plan what you can share.

## Keep a log
Write down who you checked and what they needed. This helps your after action review.

## Checklist
- Checked in with your out-of-area contact
- Know which neighbors may need extra help
- Checked in with your group by radio, text or in person
- Have a short script for welfare checks
- Know when to call 911

> Prepared neighbors are the best emergency service there is.
