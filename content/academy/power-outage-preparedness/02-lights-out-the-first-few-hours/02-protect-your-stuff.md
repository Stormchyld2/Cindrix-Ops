When power comes back, it can come back with a surge or a flicker. A few minutes now saves money later.

## Protect your electronics
- Unplug the things you would hate to replace: **computers, televisions, microwaves, game consoles, audio equipment.**
- Unplug **sensitive appliances** with electronic controls, such as some washers, dishwashers and heat pumps, if advised by the maker.
- Leave **one lamp switched on** so you know the second it is back.
- Turn off or unplug the **air conditioner and furnace** at the thermostat. A sudden restart can overload circuits and damage compressors. Wait a few minutes after power returns before turning them on.
- Use a **surge protector** for expensive electronics, and consider whole-house surge protection from an electrician.

## Keep the refrigerator and freezer closed
This is the single easiest thing you can do. According to FoodSafety.gov:
- An unopened **refrigerator** keeps food safe for about **4 hours.**
- A **full freezer** holds its temperature for about **48 hours.** A half-full freezer holds for about **24 hours.**
- Every time the door is opened, the clock speeds up.

Tell everyone in the household, including children and guests. Put a note on the door. Decide what you want before you open it.

## Make the cold last
- Group items together.
- Fill empty spaces in the freezer with containers of water or ice.
- If you can get ice, put bags of ice or frozen water jugs in the refrigerator and freezer.
- Move things you most want to save into a **cooler with ice** before the refrigerator warms up, if the outage looks long.
- Cover the freezer with a blanket for extra insulation. Do not block vents.
- **Dry ice** can keep food frozen in a long outage, but it is dangerous to handle: use gloves or tongs, never touch it with bare skin, use it in a ventilated area, never seal it in an airtight container (it can burst) and never store it in a closed vehicle or small room, because the carbon dioxide gas can build up and cause suffocation.

## Protect your home from other damage
- **Pipes:** in freezing weather, let faucets drip and open cabinet doors under sinks. Shut off the main water and drain the lines if the house will be cold for many days.
- **Sump pump:** if it stops, move valuables up and know your plan for water in the basement.
- **Aquariums and pets with special needs:** keep them warm.
- **Garage door:** know how to open it manually.
- **Vehicles:** keep the fuel tank above half.

## Document
If an outage causes losses (spoiled food, damaged equipment), take photos and keep receipts. Check with your insurer and your utility on any claim or reimbursement program.

## Checklist
- Sensitive electronics unplugged
- One light left switched on as a signal
- Household knows to keep the refrigerator and freezer shut
- Appliance thermometers in the refrigerator and freezer
- Freezer filled with water jugs or ice
- Coolers and ice ready
- Know the dry ice safety rules

> Closed doors are the cheapest insulation you have.
