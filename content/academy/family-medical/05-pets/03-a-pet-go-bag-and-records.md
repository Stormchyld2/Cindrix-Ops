> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

If you leave home, your pets go too. Plan ahead.

## Go-bag for each pet
- A week or more of food in a sealed container, and bowls
- Water for them
- Any medicines, with a list
- A leash or harness, a carrier for cats and small pets
- A recent photo, and your phone number on the collar tag
- Waste bags or litter
- A blanket or familiar toy
- Copy of vaccination records and the vet's phone number

## Plan
- Not every shelter takes pets. Find pet-friendly places on your route before you need them.
- Practice getting cats into carriers.
- Keep pets leashed or carried in a crowd.
- A microchip with your current details helps if they get lost.

## Afterward
Keep calm around them. Pets pick up stress. Keep feeding times regular.

> Pets cannot plan. You must.

## Going deeper: pets are family, so plan for them
Many people refuse to leave without their pets, and many shelters do not admit them. Plan ahead for where pets can go.

## The go-bag
- Food and a collapsible bowl for at least a week
- Water, and a way to give it
- Medicines as prescribed, and flea, tick and heartworm preventives
- A leash, harness, carrier or crate for each animal, labeled with your name and number
- Litter, a tray, waste bags and cleaning supplies
- A blanket, a favorite toy and a towel
- A recent photo of you with your pet, and one of the pet alone
- A first aid kit

## Records
Keep copies of vaccination records, medical history, microchip numbers, your vet's number and photos. Store them in a waterproof bag and in a digital copy.

## Identification
Microchip your pet, and register and update the chip. Use a collar with a tag showing your phone number. Take a photo of each pet.

## Plan
- Find pet-friendly hotels, boarding, relatives and shelters along your evacuation routes.
- Arrange a buddy who can take your pet if you cannot get home.
- Practice getting pets into carriers, and take them on car rides.
- Never leave a pet behind or tied up. If you must leave, keep them inside a safe place with food and water and a sign on the door. Contact animal rescue as soon as you can.

## Large animals
Plan trailers, routes and destinations, and keep them ready.

## Afterward
Keep your pet leashed after a disaster, since familiar smells change and hazards remain. Watch for stress, illness and injuries.
