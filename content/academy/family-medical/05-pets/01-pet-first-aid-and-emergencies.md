> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Call a vet when you can. In a long disruption you may be the only help.

## Emergencies
- Trouble breathing, blue or pale gums
- Collapse, seizure that lasts more than a few minutes
- Heavy bleeding
- A swollen hard belly, or trying to vomit without result (can be bloat in dogs, an emergency)
- Hit by a car, even if they seem fine
- A cat that strains to pass urine and cannot: urgent

## Basic first aid
- **Stay calm and be careful:** hurt animals can bite. A cloth or soft muzzle can help. Do not muzzle if vomiting or struggling to breathe.
- **Bleeding:** press a clean pad firmly and hold.
- **Heat stroke:** move to shade, cool with room-temperature water (not ice), offer small sips, get help.
- **Wounds:** rinse with clean water and cover.
- **Do not give human pain medicine** to pets. Many are deadly to dogs and cats.

## Know normal
Learn your pet's normal breathing, gum colour and behaviour so you spot change.

> A pet that suddenly hides, will not eat or is in pain is telling you something is wrong.
