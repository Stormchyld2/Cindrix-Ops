> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Call a vet when you can. In a long disruption you may be the only help.

## Emergencies
- Trouble breathing, blue or pale gums
- Collapse, seizure that lasts more than a few minutes
- Heavy bleeding
- A swollen hard belly, or trying to vomit without result (can be bloat in dogs, an emergency)
- Hit by a car, even if they seem fine
- A cat that strains to pass urine and cannot: urgent

## Basic first aid
- **Stay calm and be careful:** hurt animals can bite. A cloth or soft muzzle can help. Do not muzzle if vomiting or struggling to breathe.
- **Bleeding:** press a clean pad firmly and hold.
- **Heat stroke:** move to shade, cool with room-temperature water (not ice), offer small sips, get help.
- **Wounds:** rinse with clean water and cover.
- **Do not give human pain medicine** to pets. Many are deadly to dogs and cats.

## Know normal
Learn your pet's normal breathing, gum colour and behaviour so you spot change.

> A pet that suddenly hides, will not eat or is in pain is telling you something is wrong.

## Going deeper: know your pet's normal
You can only spot trouble if you know the usual. Record these when your pet is calm and well:
- **Dogs:** temperature about 101 to 102.5 °F, resting heart rate roughly 60 to 140 (smaller dogs faster), breathing about 10 to 30 breaths a minute.
- **Cats:** temperature about 100.5 to 102.5 °F, heart rate roughly 140 to 220, breathing about 20 to 30.
- Gum color: pink and moist. Pale, white, blue or bright red gums are danger signs.
- Normal eating, drinking, stool and energy.

## Emergencies: go to a vet or emergency clinic at once
Trouble breathing, collapse, seizures, heavy bleeding, a swollen hard belly, repeated unproductive retching (bloat in dogs), inability to urinate (especially male cats), poisoning, heatstroke, a hit by car, eye injuries and broken bones.

## Basic first aid
- **Bleeding:** press a clean cloth on the wound firmly. Bandage snugly but not tight.
- **Choking:** look in the mouth and remove an object you can see. Never push blindly. For a large dog, a firm thrust below the ribs can help.
- **Hurt pets bite,** even loving ones. Use a soft muzzle or wrap a cat in a towel.
- **Fractures:** keep the pet still, support the limb and carry on a board or blanket.
- **Heat:** cool gradually with water, see the heat lesson.
- **Poison:** see the poison lesson.

## Medicines
Never give human medicines to pets without a veterinarian's advice, since many are toxic, including common pain relievers. Ask your vet what to keep and how to use it.

## Plan
Save the number of your vet, a 24-hour emergency clinic and a pet poison line. Keep a first aid kit for pets, with gauze, a muzzle, a thermometer, tweezers and records.
