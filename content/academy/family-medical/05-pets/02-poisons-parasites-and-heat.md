> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## Common poisons
Dogs and cats can be poisoned by:
- **Chocolate**, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic
- **Xylitol**, a sweetener found in gum, some peanut butter and baked goods
- Antifreeze, rat poison, slug bait
- Many human medicines
- **Lilies**, which are very dangerous to cats

If you think your pet ate something harmful, call a vet or an animal poison hotline straight away (in the US: ASPCA Animal Poison Control). Do not make a pet vomit unless told to.

## Parasites
- Fleas, ticks and worms spread disease. Keep prevention going if you have it.
- Check for ticks after time outside. Remove with fine tweezers close to the skin and pull straight.

## Heat and cold
- Never leave a pet in a parked car
- Always give shade and water
- In cold, give a dry warm bed. Short-haired dogs and cats feel cold fast.

## Feeding
- Keep dry food in a sealed container
- Feed a steady amount. Sudden changes upset the stomach.
- Fresh water every day

> A sealed bag of pet food and a bowl for water belong in the kit.

## Going deeper: common household poisons
- **Foods:** chocolate, grapes and raisins, onions and garlic, macadamia nuts, alcohol, raw dough, and anything with **xylitol**, a sweetener in gum, candy and some peanut butter, which is highly dangerous to dogs.
- **Plants:** lilies are deadly to cats. Sago palm, oleander, azalea and tulip bulbs harm dogs and cats.
- **Household:** antifreeze (sweet-tasting and deadly), rat poison, slug bait, cleaning products, human medicines, and cigarettes.
- **What to do:** call a vet or a pet poison hotline right away, with the product label ready, the pet's weight and the time. Do not make the pet vomit unless a professional tells you to.

## Parasites
- **Fleas and ticks** carry disease. Use vet-recommended preventives and check the skin after outdoor trips. Remove ticks with fine tweezers close to the skin.
- **Heartworm** spreads by mosquitoes. Prevention is much easier than treatment.
- **Worms:** have the vet check stool, and treat as advised.
- Keep a supply of preventives in your kit.

## Heat
Dogs and cats cool themselves poorly. **Never leave a pet in a parked car.** In hot weather, give shade and water, walk in the cool of the day and avoid hot pavement. Flat-faced breeds, old, young and overweight pets are at greater risk.
- **Heatstroke signs:** heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, weakness, vomiting, collapse.
- **Act:** move to shade, wet the fur with cool (not ice) water, fan, offer small sips and go to the vet right away.

## Cold
Bring pets indoors in freezing weather. Wipe paws after salted roads, and provide a warm, dry bed.

## Feeding
Keep at least two weeks of the usual food, sealed, and rotate it. Change foods slowly. Keep fresh water available.
