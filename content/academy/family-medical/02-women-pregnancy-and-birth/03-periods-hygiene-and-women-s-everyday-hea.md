> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Women's health needs are easy to forget in a plan. They are part of the plan.

## Supplies for each woman
- Pads, tampons or washable cloth pads, enough for months
- Soap, clean underwear, a way to wash and dry them
- Any regular medicine she takes, with spare supply
- Pregnancy tests if relevant

## Keeping clean
- Change pads and tampons often. Never leave a tampon in longer than the package says. Toxic shock syndrome is rare but serious: sudden fever, rash and dizziness mean take it out and get help.
- Wash with plain water and mild soap. Avoid douching. It upsets the natural balance.
- Wear loose cotton underwear when you can.

## Infections and when to get help
- Burning when passing urine, needing to go often, or pain low in the belly can be a urine infection. Drink water and get treatment. Left alone it can reach the kidneys.
- Unusual discharge with itching, smell or pain needs a check.
- A lump in the breast, bleeding after menopause or between periods, or severe pelvic pain needs a doctor.

## Breastfeeding
Breastfeeding feeds the baby, protects against infection and needs no equipment. Feed often, stay hydrated and eat well. A hard red painful area with fever needs medical care.

> Plan for periods and breastfeeding like you plan for food and water.

## Going deeper: everyday needs matter in a long crisis
Hygiene and privacy are health issues, and they affect dignity and morale. Plan for them as seriously as for food.

## Supplies
Keep pads, tampons or menstrual cups for each woman for at least a month, plus spare underwear, wipes, soap, a small waterproof bag for used items, and pain-relief choices as her own doctor has advised. Reusable cloth pads work if you have clean water, soap and a way to dry them. Do not leave tampons in longer than the package says, since toxic shock syndrome is rare but serious.

## Keeping clean
- Wash hands before and after changing.
- Wash the outside of the genital area with clean water, front to back. Avoid douches and strong soaps.
- Change out of wet clothing quickly.
- Have a private place and a safe way to dispose of waste.

## Infections and when to get help
- **Urinary infection:** burning, frequent urination, pain, cloudy urine. Drink water and see a clinician, since it can spread to the kidneys, especially in pregnancy. Fever, back pain or vomiting need urgent care.
- **Yeast and other infections:** itching, unusual discharge or smell. See a clinician.
- **Very heavy bleeding** (soaking a pad an hour for several hours), severe pain, fainting, or missed periods with pain: seek urgent care.

## Birth control and medicines
Women on regular medicines or birth control should keep a supply, a written list and their doctor's contact. Ask the doctor what to do if supply runs out. Do not guess at changes.

## Breastfeeding
Breastfeeding is safe and protective in an emergency, and keeps babies fed when formula or clean water is short. Keep the mother fed and hydrated, rested and supported. If she cannot feed, use only safe water and sterile equipment for formula.

## Respect
Make sure women have a voice in planning, privacy and the right supplies.
