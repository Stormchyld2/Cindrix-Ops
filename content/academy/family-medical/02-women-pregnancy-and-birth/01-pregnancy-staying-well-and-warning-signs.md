> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

If someone in your family is pregnant, plan early. Records, supplies and a plan to reach care matter more than anything you do in the moment.

## Keep ready
- Copies of prenatal records and the due date
- Any medicines the doctor prescribed, with a supply for weeks. Take them only as prescribed.
- Prenatal vitamins
- A plan for where the nearest birth help is, by more than one route

## Eating and drinking
- Steady meals with protein, fruit, vegetables and plenty of safe water
- Avoid raw or undercooked meat, eggs and unpasteurised dairy
- No alcohol. Ask the doctor before any new medicine or herbal remedy.

## Warning signs: get help urgently
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Severe belly pain
- A bad headache that will not go, with blurred vision or swelling of the face and hands (could be pre-eclampsia)
- Fever
- Fluid leaking before the due date
- The baby moving much less than usual
- Seizure

> Most pregnancies go well. The aim is to spot trouble early and get to help early.
