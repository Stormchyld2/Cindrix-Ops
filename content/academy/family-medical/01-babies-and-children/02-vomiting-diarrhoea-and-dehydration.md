> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

In a long disruption, dehydration from a stomach bug is one of the biggest dangers for small children and the old.

## Signs of dehydration
- Dry mouth, no tears when crying
- Sunken eyes, or a sunken soft spot on a baby's head
- Few wet nappies or little urine, dark urine
- Very sleepy, floppy or irritable

## What to do
1. Give **small, frequent sips** of an oral rehydration solution. Buy packets and keep them in your kit. Mix them **exactly as the packet says**, with clean safe water.
2. A spoonful or sip every minute or two works better than a big drink that comes straight back up.
3. If a baby is breastfed, keep breastfeeding. Offer the breast more often.
4. Once they can keep drink down, return to normal food early. Do not starve them.
5. Wash hands with soap after every nappy and toilet visit. Clean surfaces.

## Get help urgently if
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Green vomit, or a swollen hard belly
- No urine for many hours
- Too drowsy to drink
- A baby who cannot keep anything down

> Keep a few packets of oral rehydration salts in every kit. They are light and cheap, and they save lives.

## Check yourself
? Which is a sign of dehydration in a child?
+ Few wet nappies and no tears
- Rosy cheeks
- Hunger
- A loud cry
> Dry signs show the body is short of fluid.
? How should you give rehydration drink to a vomiting child?
+ Small sips often
- One big glass
- Only after 24 hours
- Cold milk only
> Small frequent sips are kept down more easily.
? How do you mix oral rehydration salts?
+ Exactly as the packet says
- By guess
- With sea water
- With extra sugar
> Wrong strength can make things worse.
