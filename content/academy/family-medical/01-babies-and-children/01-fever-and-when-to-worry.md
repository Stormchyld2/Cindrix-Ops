> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

A fever is a sign the body is fighting an infection. The number matters less than how the child looks and acts.

## Take a temperature
- Use a digital thermometer. For babies under three months, take it in the bottom.
- A fever is **100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher**.

## Get help at once if
- A baby **under 3 months** has any fever. This is an emergency.
- The child is hard to wake, limp, or very floppy.
- Breathing is fast, noisy or hard work.
- There is a stiff neck, a purple or dark rash that does not fade when pressed, or a seizure.
- The child will not drink, or has no wet nappy or urine for many hours.

## Comfort at home
- Offer small sips of fluid often.
- Dress them lightly. Do not bundle.
- Let them rest. Do not wake them just to check the temperature.
- Do not sponge with cold water or alcohol.
- **Never give aspirin to a child.** It can cause a dangerous illness.
- For fever medicine, follow the package for the child's weight and age or ask a pharmacist or doctor. We do not give amounts here.

> Watch the child, not the thermometer. A child with a fever who plays and drinks is usually fine. A child who is very sleepy and will not drink needs help.

## Going deeper: fever is a sign, not the enemy
A fever is the body fighting infection. How the child acts matters more than the number. A child who plays, drinks and smiles between fevers is usually less sick than one who is limp, hard to wake or not interested in anything.

## Age changes everything
- **Under 3 months:** a rectal temperature of 100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher is an emergency. Get medical help at once, even in the night.
- **3 to 36 months:** call a doctor for a fever lasting more than a day or two, or any fever with other worrying signs.
- **Older children:** watch behavior, drinking and breathing more than the number.

## Red flags at any age
A stiff neck, a purple or red rash that does not fade when you press a glass on it, trouble breathing, a seizure, constant crying, a bulging soft spot on a baby's head, repeated vomiting, signs of dehydration, severe pain, or a child who is floppy, grey or very hard to wake.

## Comfort measures
Dress lightly, keep the room comfortable, and offer fluids often in small amounts. Lukewarm baths are fine, but cold baths, ice or alcohol rubs are not. Rest matters. Fever-reducing medicines can ease discomfort, but only use them as the label, your doctor or a pharmacist directs for the child's age and weight. Never give aspirin to children or teens, because of the risk of a rare, serious illness called Reye syndrome.

## Febrile seizures
Some young children have a seizure with a fast-rising fever. Lay the child on their side on a soft surface, remove hard objects, do not put anything in the mouth and note the time. Most stop in a few minutes. Call 911 if it lasts over five minutes, the child does not wake up, or breathing is hard. Always have the child checked afterward.

## Keep a thermometer
Keep a digital thermometer and spare batteries in your kit. Learn to use it before you need it.
