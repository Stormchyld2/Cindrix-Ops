> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Many older people take several medicines. In a long disruption, **running out** or **mixing up** medicine is a real danger. This lesson is about handling, never amounts.

## Keep a medicine list
For each person write:
- the name of each medicine and what it is for
- who prescribed it
- allergies
- the name of the doctor and pharmacy

Keep a copy in the kit and one in a wallet.

## Supply
- Ask the doctor about a longer supply for important medicines
- Store in a cool dry place, in the original container
- Check expiry dates. Do not use medicine that is far out of date, and never share prescriptions.

## Safe habits
- Use a weekly pill organiser to avoid mistakes
- Never double up after a missed dose. Read the leaflet or ask a pharmacist.
- Never stop a heart, blood pressure, diabetes, seizure or mental health medicine suddenly without advice
- Keep medicines out of reach of children and pets

## If medicine runs out
Contact a doctor or pharmacist. Do not substitute guesses or someone else's pills.

> A written list is the most useful single page in the kit.

## Going deeper: medicines are the biggest risk and the biggest worry
Many older adults take several daily medicines. A gap in supply or a mix-up can hurt quickly. Planning ahead gives you options when pharmacies close.

## Keep a medicine list
For each person, write the name of each medicine, what it is for, the prescriber, the pharmacy and the schedule as printed on the label. Add allergies, past bad reactions and the doctor's number. Keep a copy in the go-bag and on the fridge. Photograph the labels.

## Supply
- Ask your doctor and insurer about getting an extra supply or an early refill before a forecast storm. Many insurers permit this in a declared emergency.
- Keep a week or more in a go-bag, in the original labeled containers.
- Know which medicines need refrigeration or special handling, such as insulin, and plan how to keep them cool. Ask your pharmacist how long they last unrefrigerated.
- Keep medicines out of heat, damp and sun, and out of reach of children.
- Know where your pharmacies are, and the nearest open one.

## Safe habits
- Use a weekly pill box, filled with someone checking.
- Never share medicines or use someone else's.
- Do not stop medicines suddenly unless a clinician says to.
- Ask about interactions when anything new is added, including herbal products.
- Dispose of old medicines through take-back programs.
- Keep reading glasses beside the pills.

## If medicine runs out
Call the prescriber or any pharmacist, who can often arrange an emergency supply. Go to an urgent care or emergency room if you cannot, particularly for heart, diabetes, seizure, breathing, blood-thinning or mental-health medicines. Do not stretch doses or substitute on your own.

## Dignity
Treat the person as the expert on their own life, and involve them in decisions.
