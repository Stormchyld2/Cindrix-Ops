> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## Dehydration in older adults
Thirst fades with age. Offer a drink every hour or two. Dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness and sudden confusion are warning signs.

## Sudden confusion
A sudden change in thinking is **a medical warning**, not just old age. Common causes: dehydration, infection (often urine), a fall, a medicine problem, low blood sugar. Check safety, give a drink if they can swallow, take their temperature, look for pain and get help.

## Skin and pressure sores
People who sit or lie for long stretches can get pressure sores.
- Change position at least every two hours
- Keep skin clean and dry, and look at heels, hips, tail bone and elbows every day
- Red skin that does not fade when pressed needs relief from pressure at once

## Dignity and company
- Ask before helping. Explain what you are about to do.
- Keep routines, light and contact. Loneliness harms health.
- For people with dementia: keep it calm and simple, same place, same schedule, a name tag or ID bracelet in case of wandering.

## Looking after the carer
Carers burn out. Share the work. Sleep, eat, and ask for help. A tired carer makes mistakes.

> Sudden confusion is never "just age". Look for the cause.
