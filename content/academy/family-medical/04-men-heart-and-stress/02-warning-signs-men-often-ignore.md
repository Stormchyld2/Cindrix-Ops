Men are less likely to see a doctor and more likely to put up with symptoms. In a long disruption, a small problem can become a big one. Know what to act on.

## Get checked for
- **Blood in urine or stool**
- **A lump or swelling** in a testicle, or sudden severe testicle pain. Sudden pain needs help the same day: it can be a twisting that damages the testicle within hours.
- **Trouble passing urine**, a weak stream, getting up often at night, or being unable to pass urine at all (urgent)
- **Chest pain, breathlessness on small effort**
- **Unexplained weight loss, a cough that will not go, a mole that changes**
- **Long low mood**, loss of interest, or heavy drinking

## Everyday care
- Treat cuts and blisters early. Infection spreads fast.
- Keep up with dental care. Infected teeth can become serious.
- Wear protection against sun, noise and dust.
- Check your own skin and testicles monthly.

## Fitness for the long haul
Strength, walking and sleep make the day-to-day work possible. Stretch and look after your back.

> Pride is not a plan. If something is wrong, say so early.

## Going deeper: why men put it off
Many men ignore pain, tiredness and changes, hoping they will go away, or avoid doctors. In a disaster, a small problem can become a big one. Telling the truth about symptoms is a strength.

## Signs to get checked
- **Chest discomfort or breathlessness** on effort, even if it settles.
- **Blood** in urine, stool or vomit, or black stools.
- **Trouble passing urine,** a weak stream, getting up often at night, or pain.
- **A lump** in a testicle, breast, neck or elsewhere, or a mole that changes.
- **Unexplained weight loss,** a cough that lasts for weeks, or persistent hoarseness.
- **Sudden or severe headache,** vision changes or numbness.
- **Constant tiredness,** low mood, or loss of interest.
- **Snoring with gasping,** which can mean sleep apnea.

## Everyday care
- Have regular checks of blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar, vision and teeth.
- Ask about cancer screening that suits your age and family history, such as colon and prostate discussions.
- Keep vaccinations current: tetanus, flu, COVID and others your doctor advises.
- Protect hearing, skin and back.
- Wear seat belts, helmets and protective gear.
- Keep to a safe weight and stay active.

## Fitness for the long haul
Build strength, balance and endurance, since survival work is physical. Include walking, carrying, stretching and rest days. Learn proper lifting, and avoid sudden heavy exertion if you are out of shape or have heart risk factors.

## Alcohol and tobacco
Both raise risk in a crisis. Cut down or quit. Talk to your doctor about help.

## Be a model
Younger men and boys copy what they see. Seeking care early and talking about health helps the whole family.
