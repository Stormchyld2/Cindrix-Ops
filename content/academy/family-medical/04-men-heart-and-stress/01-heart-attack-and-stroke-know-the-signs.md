> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## Heart attack signs
- Pressure, squeezing or tightness in the chest, lasting more than a few minutes or coming and going
- Pain or discomfort spreading to the arm, back, neck or jaw
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, light-headedness
- In women, older people and people with diabetes: tiredness, breathlessness or stomach discomfort with no chest pain

**Call emergency services at once. Do not drive yourself.** Sit them down, loosen tight clothing. The operator can tell you whether to chew an aspirin. They will say the amount.

If they collapse and are not breathing normally: start CPR (hard, fast chest compressions in the centre of the chest) and use an AED if one is available.

## Stroke: BE-FAST
- **B** Balance lost
- **E** Eyes: sudden vision loss
- **F** Face drooping
- **A** Arm weakness
- **S** Speech slurred
- **T** Time to call emergency services

Note the **time** it began. Treatment works only in the first hours. Do not give food, drink or medicine.

## Lower the risk
- Do not smoke
- Walk, move, keep weight healthy
- Know your blood pressure and keep taking prescribed medicine
- Eat less salt and processed food

> With heart attack and stroke, minutes matter. Do not wait to see if it passes.
