Space is time, and time is the thing you need most when something goes wrong.

## Keep a cushion
- Leave at least **three seconds** behind the vehicle in front in good weather (more in rain, fog, snow or when towing). Pick a fixed object, watch the vehicle ahead pass it, and count.
- At a stop, leave enough room to see the tyres of the vehicle in front touching the road. That gives you room to steer round if it stalls or you must leave.
- Stay out of other drivers' blind spots, and do not drive alongside someone for long.

## Read the road
- Look 12 to 15 seconds ahead (about a quarter of a mile in town, further on a highway), not only at the bumper in front.
- Scan mirrors every 5 to 8 seconds.
- Notice brake lights far ahead, pedestrians, parked vehicles with people inside, and traffic that changes behaviour all at once.

## Know more than one way
Learn two routes to places you go often. Learn the nearest safe place on each: a busy fuel station, a police or fire station, a hospital.

> If you only know one way, a single blocked road becomes a crisis.

## Check yourself
? How far behind the vehicle in front should you stay in good weather?
+ At least three seconds
- One car length
- Close enough to see the number plate
- Whatever the traffic allows
> More space means more time to react.
? At a stop, how much room should you leave in front?
+ You can see the tyres of the vehicle ahead touching the road
- A few centimetres
- Exactly one metre
- None, to stop others pulling in
> That gap lets you steer round or leave if the vehicle ahead stalls.
? Why learn two routes to places you visit often?
+ A blocked or unsafe road does not trap you
- It makes the trip shorter
- So you can drive faster
- It is a legal requirement
> A second route and a known safe place give you a plan.
