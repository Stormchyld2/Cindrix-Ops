Most people **dramatically underestimate** how much water they use every day. Turn on the faucet, take a shower, wash dishes...it all adds up quickly.

In fact, the average person  the U.S. uses 80 to 100 gallons per day.

Now imagine that suddenly drops to zero.

This lesson isn't about comfort, it's about what you actually need to stay healthy and functional when things go wrong.

## Minimum (Survival Level)
1 gallon per person per day. This is the bare minimum to keep your body functioning. It's not comfortable, and it's not sustainable long-term, but it can keep you alive in a short-term emergency. This covers:

- Drinking water
- Very limited hygiene

## Recommended (Realistic Level)
**3 gallons per person per day**. This is a much more realistic target if you want to maintain health, cleanliness, and morale during a disruption. This accounts for:

- Drinking
- Cooking
- Basic hygiene (handwashing, brushing teeth, etc.)

## Why the Gap Matters
The difference between 1 gallon and 3 gallons is the difference between surviving vs. functioning, or bare minimum vs. manageable daily life.

If you plan only for survival, you'll feel the strain quickly, especially with kids or during extended disruptions.

## Real-World Example
Family of 4: 3 gallons × 4 people × 7 days = **84 gallons.** That's just one week of water.

Now ask yourself:
- Where would you store it?
- How would you move it?
- Do you already have it on hand?

→ You don't need a year's supply to get started. But you do need enough to get through the first critical days without scrambling.
