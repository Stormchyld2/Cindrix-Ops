[video] Making water safe

If you suspect your water is contaminated, do not drink it untreated. Contamination can happen quickly and without warning. Knowing when to question your water, how to recognize problems and how to treat it is a core preparedness skill. The goal is to match the method to the problem, because no single method fixes everything.

## Common causes of contamination
- **Flooding,** which mixes sewage, chemicals and debris with water supplies.
- **Broken mains and pressure loss,** which let contaminants seep into pipes.
- **Treatment failure** at a plant, or a power loss that stops it.
- **Sewage and septic leaks.**
- **Farm and industrial runoff,** and spills.
- **Natural sources:** animal waste, algae blooms and stagnant water.
- **Old plumbing,** which can add lead and other metals.

## How to know
**Obvious signs:** cloudy water, an odd color, a smell like bleach, sewage or rotten eggs, an oily film or a strange taste.
**Less obvious but serious:** water can look and smell fine and still carry germs, heavy metals or chemicals. Treat official notices seriously: a **boil-water notice,** a **do-not-drink** or **do-not-use** notice, or a notice after a flood. Illness in several people at once, with vomiting or diarrhea, is also a warning.

## What threatens you
- **Bacteria,** such as E. coli and Salmonella.
- **Viruses,** such as norovirus and hepatitis A.
- **Protozoa,** such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium.
- **Sediment,** which makes water cloudy and weakens chemical treatment.
- **Chemicals and metals,** such as fuel, pesticides, lead and industrial chemicals.
Boiling, filters and chemicals handle germs. Most of them do not remove dissolved chemicals, fuel or heavy metals. If you suspect chemical contamination, use a different source.

## The treatment ladder
Use more than one step for the dirtiest water. A good order is: **pre-filter, then filter, then boil or chemically treat.** In an emergency, any one method is better than none.

### Step 1: remove sediment
Let cloudy water settle, then pour it through a coffee filter, a clean cloth or a bandana. This clears the water and helps later steps work.

### Step 2: filter
A good filter removes bacteria and protozoa. Look for a filter with a pore size of 0.2 microns or smaller, tested to a recognized standard such as NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 for cysts, or listing "removes 99.9 percent of bacteria and protozoa." Most small filters do **not** remove viruses. Check the label, and follow the cleaning and replacement instructions. Never let a filter freeze, because that can crack it unseen.

### Step 3: purify
- **Boil.** Bring water to a **rolling boil for one minute,** or three minutes above 6,500 feet. Let it cool and keep it covered. Boiled water tastes flat, so pour it between two clean containers to add air.
- **Plain unscented household bleach.** Use bleach with 5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite and no added scents, dyes or cleaners. For clear water, the CDC advises about eight drops of 6 percent bleach per gallon, or a little more for cloudy water. Always read the bottle and the CDC chart for the exact amount for your bleach strength. Stir, then wait **at least 30 minutes.** The water should smell faintly of chlorine. If it does not, repeat once and wait another 15 minutes.
- **Chlorine dioxide tablets.** Follow the package for amount and wait time. Cold and cloudy water need longer contact, up to four hours for some protozoa.
- **UV pens.** They work on clear water only, and need batteries and correct use.
- **Purifiers.** Devices rated as "purifiers" also remove viruses.

### Combine methods
For wild water, such as a stream: pre-filter, filter, then chemical or boil. For clear tap water under a boil notice: boil. For stored tap water that has been kept a long time: smell and look, then treat if in doubt.

## After an event
After a flood or fire, **flush pipes** and run taps only when the utility says the water is safe, and replace filters in appliances as advised. Clean and sanitize containers before reuse.

## Do not
- **Do not** use water from a flooded well or a flooded area without treatment and testing.
- **Do not** drink from water that smells of fuel or chemicals, even after boiling. Boiling does not remove them and can concentrate some.
- **Do not** use pool or hot-tub water, or water with dyes or additives, for drinking.
- **Do not** use scented, color-safe or splash-less bleach.
- **Do not** drink seawater or urine, which dehydrate you.
- **Do not** assume bottled water is safe if the bottle has been in floodwater.

## Practise before you need it
Buy the filter and the tablets now. Treat a bucket of tap water with each method so you know how it tastes and how long it takes. Write instructions and keep them with the gear. Replace chemicals each year, since bleach loses strength.

## Five principles
Knowing contaminants is **hazard assessment.** Owning the gear is **preparedness.** Treating water is **response.** Cleaning containers and flushing pipes is **recovery,** and keeping chemicals fresh is **mitigation.**
