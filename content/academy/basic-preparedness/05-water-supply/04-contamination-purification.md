**If you suspect contamination, do not drink the water untreated.** Contamination can happen quickly, often without warning. Knowing when to question your water, how to recognize problems, and what to do next is a critical preparedness skill.

Should your water supply be contaminated, you will need to filter and purify.

## Common Causes of Water Contamination
- Broken water mains or aging pipes
- Loss of water pressure (allows contaminants to enter)
- Treatment plant failures or maintenance issues
- Power Outages
- Flooding (introduces bacteria, chemicals, sewage)
- Wildfires (ash, debris, chemical runoff)
- Earthquakes (damaged infrastructure)
- Chemical spills or industrial runoff
- Agricultural contamination (fertilizers, pesticides)
- Intentional contamination (rare, but possible)

## How to Know Your Water May Be Contaminated
Sometimes it's obvious—but not always. Clear warning signs:

- Strange color (brown, yellow, cloudy)
- Unusual smell (chlorine, sulfur, chemical odors)
- Bad taste

Less obvious (but serious):
- Official "boil water" advisory
- Sudden drop in water pressure
- Recent flooding or nearby environmental event

> Important: Water can still look, smell, and taste normal—and be unsafe.

Next, we'll discuss how to obtain drinking water.

## How To Treat Water for Drinking

## Remove Bacteria, water filters
- Commercial water filters can remove bacteria. The Sawyer mini or Lifestraw are popular
- Gravity fed filters work well for larger amounts of water
- Pump style filters give you faster flow rate

## Remove Viruses, water purifiers
- Viruses can be removed by boiling water for at least 1 minute
- 8 drops per gallon of 6% Sodium hypochlorite (household bleach) and wait 30 minutes
- Chlorine dioxide tablets (e.g., Aquatabs) work as well
- Some water filters are actually purifiers and can filter down to 0.02 microns.

## Remove Sediment
If there is excessive sediment, pre-filter through a coffee filter or cloth like a t-shirt or bandana.
