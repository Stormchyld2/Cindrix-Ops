**If you suspect contamination, do not drink the water untreated.** Contamination can happen quickly, often without warning. Knowing when to question your water, how to recognize problems, and what to do next is a critical preparedness skill.

Should your water supply be contaminated, you will need to filter and purify.

## Common Causes of Water Contamination
- Broken water mains or aging pipes
- Loss of water pressure (allows contaminants to enter)
- Treatment plant failures or maintenance issues
- Power Outages
- Flooding (introduces bacteria, chemicals, sewage)
- Wildfires (ash, debris, chemical runoff)
- Earthquakes (damaged infrastructure)
- Chemical spills or industrial runoff
- Agricultural contamination (fertilizers, pesticides)
- Intentional contamination (rare, but possible)

## How to Know Your Water May Be Contaminated
Sometimes it's obvious—but not always. Clear warning signs:

- Strange color (brown, yellow, cloudy)
- Unusual smell (chlorine, sulfur, chemical odors)
- Bad taste

Less obvious (but serious):
- Official "boil water" advisory
- Sudden drop in water pressure
- Recent flooding or nearby environmental event

> Important: Water can still look, smell, and taste normal—and be unsafe.

Next, we'll discuss how to obtain drinking water.

## How To Treat Water for Drinking

## Remove Bacteria, water filters
- Commercial water filters can remove bacteria. The Sawyer mini or Lifestraw are popular
- Gravity fed filters work well for larger amounts of water
- Pump style filters give you faster flow rate

## Remove Viruses, water purifiers
- Viruses can be removed by boiling water for at least 1 minute
- 8 drops per gallon of 6% Sodium hypochlorite (household bleach) and wait 30 minutes
- Chlorine dioxide tablets (e.g., Aquatabs) work as well
- Some water filters are actually purifiers and can filter down to 0.02 microns.

## Remove Sediment
If there is excessive sediment, pre-filter through a coffee filter or cloth like a t-shirt or bandana.

## Going deeper: matching the method to the problem
Different contaminants need different fixes.
- **Sediment and cloudiness:** let it settle, and strain through cloth or a coffee filter.
- **Bacteria and protozoa** (E. coli, Giardia, Cryptosporidium): filters rated at 0.2 micron or smaller, boiling, or chemicals.
- **Viruses:** boiling, chlorine, chlorine dioxide, or a purifier rated for viruses. Most small camping filters do not remove viruses.
- **Chemicals** (fuel, pesticides, industrial waste, heavy metals): boiling does not remove them. Activated carbon helps with some. **Reverse osmosis** and **distillation** remove more. If you suspect a chemical spill, **do not drink the water,** and use a different source.
- **Radiological contamination:** a very rare event. Follow official guidance.
- **Salt (seawater):** needs distillation or reverse osmosis.

### Boiling details
Bring water to a **rolling boil for one minute** (three minutes above 6,500 feet). Let it cool and keep it covered. Boiled water tastes flat. Pour between two clean containers to add air.

### Bleach details (follow your bottle or the CDC chart)
Use plain, unscented household bleach with 5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite. Add the amount your label or the CDC chart gives for clear water, stir, and wait **at least 30 minutes.** The water should smell faintly of chlorine. If cloudy, add more as the guidance says or filter first. The "8 drops per gallon of 6 percent bleach" rule used in this lesson is for clear water.

### Chlorine dioxide tablets
Follow the package directions for amount and wait time. Longer waits (up to four hours) are required for some protozoa in cold or cloudy water.

### Filters
- **Squeeze and straw filters** (Sawyer, LifeStraw) are light and cheap.
- **Gravity filters** treat many gallons with no effort.
- **Pump filters** are faster and handle dirtier water.
- **Ceramic filters** can be cleaned and reused.
- **Care:** backflush hollow-fiber filters, and **never let them freeze,** because cracked fibers pass germs. Replace on schedule.
- **Test** a filter before you rely on it.

### Combine methods
Best practice for wild water: **pre-filter, filter, then chemical or boil.** In an emergency, any one method is better than none.

### After an event
After floods or fires, **flush pipes** only when told the water is safe, and replace filters in appliances as advised by the utility.

### Do not
- Do not rely on boiling for chemical spills.
- Do not use scented, splash-less or color-safe bleach.
- Do not drink water that is clearly contaminated by fuel or sewage.
- Do not use pool chemicals unless following a verified guide.
