Basic preparedness is about building** resilience and independence **- the ability to take care of yourself and your family when systems you rely on stop working.

And nothing matters more than water.

You can go weeks without food, but only a **few days** without water. When access to clean water is disrupted, even temporarily, things go downhill fast. What most people take for granted becomes your **top priority** overnight.

In this module, you'll learn how to secure, store, and source water so you're not dependent on a single point of failure.

## In this section we'll cover:
- How to determine where your water comes from

- How much water you actually need

- How to know when water is unsafe

- A PACE plan for water

- Basics of water treatment (how to make it safe)

## Going deeper: why water ranks first
In the Rule of Threes, water is the second most urgent need after air and shelter. The body is about 60 percent water. A loss of even 2 percent of body weight in water affects thinking and performance. Severe dehydration brings dizziness, confusion, fainting and kidney problems.

### What disrupts water
- **Power outages:** pumps and treatment plants need electricity.
- **Pipe breaks** from freezing or earthquakes.
- **Contamination** from floods, fires or chemicals.
- **Drought** and shortages.
- **Cyber or physical attacks** on utilities (rare, but possible).
- **Boil-water notices.**
Utilities have restored service within days in most events, but some disruptions last weeks. Flint, Michigan, and Jackson, Mississippi, are examples of long failures.

### Quick start (this week)
- **Store** at least one gallon per person per day for three days (a case of bottled water per person is a start).
- **Know** how to use your water heater as an emergency source.
- **Buy** a filter or purification tablets.
- **Write down** your water source.
Then continue through the lessons to build a full plan.

### Water and health
- **Thirst** lags behind need. Drink regularly.
- **Children and older adults** dehydrate faster.
- **Hot weather,** illness and exercise raise needs.
- **Never ration** drinking water when you have it. Cut back on other uses instead.
