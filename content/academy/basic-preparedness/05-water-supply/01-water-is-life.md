[video] Water is life

Preparedness is about resilience: the ability to look after yourself and your family when the systems you rely on stop. Nothing matters more than water. You can go weeks without food but only a few days without water, and well before then your judgement and strength fade. When the tap stops, or the water is unsafe, ordinary life turns into a scramble.

This module teaches you to secure, store and source water so you do not depend on one point of failure.

## Why water ranks first
In the rule of threes, a person survives about three minutes without air, three hours in harsh weather without shelter and three days without water. The body is about 60 percent water. Losing even two percent of your body weight in water cuts attention, mood and physical performance. Heavier loss brings headache, dizziness, confusion, fainting and kidney trouble. Heat, hard work, illness and diarrhea make it far worse. Children, older people and the sick dehydrate fastest.

## What disrupts water
Most outages are short, and utilities restore service within hours or days. But the cases that make the news show how bad it can be. Water systems in places such as Flint, Michigan and Jackson, Mississippi suffered failures that lasted weeks or months, and many places lose service after hurricanes, floods, freezes and power cuts. Common causes include:
- **Power failure,** which stops pumps at treatment plants and on private wells.
- **Broken mains,** from earthquakes, floods or freezing.
- **Contamination,** from floods, sewage overflow, chemical spills or treatment failure.
- **Drought,** which shrinks supplies and can lower wells.
- **Fire,** which drains pressure and can foul systems.
- **Boil-water notices,** which warn that tap water is unsafe until treated.

## Quick start this week
You can make a real difference in an afternoon.
1. **Store some water now.** Fill clean bottles or jugs, or buy a case of bottled water. A first target is one gallon per person per day for three days.
2. **Keep a way to treat water.** Buy a filter, purification tablets and a small bottle of plain unscented bleach, and learn how to use them.
3. **Find your shutoff.** Know where the main water valve in your home is, and how to close it. That stops contaminated water from entering the house if a notice is issued.
4. **Know your source.** Find out where your tap water comes from.
5. **Set a reminder.** Replace stored water on a schedule.

## Water and health
Water is more than something to drink. It is also the line between health and disease. Clean water is needed to wash hands, clean wounds, prepare food and wash dishes, and when it is not available illness spreads. Diarrheal disease is a leading killer in disasters, and it is mostly preventable with clean water, clean hands and safe food. Dehydration also makes everything harder, including thinking clearly under stress.

Signs of dehydration to watch for in yourself and others are thirst, dark urine, little urine, headache, dizziness, dry mouth and unusual tiredness. In children and infants, watch for fewer wet diapers, sunken eyes and listlessness. Drink regularly rather than waiting for thirst, rest in shade during heat and seek medical help for severe signs. This course does not give medicine amounts, so follow the label or your clinician.

## Do not ration the wrong way
In an emergency, never cut back on drinking water to save it for other uses. Ration the water you use for washing and cooking, not for drinking. A person who is thirsty is a person who is already behind.

## Five principles
Knowing your water risks is **hazard assessment.** Storing and treating water is **preparedness.** Closing the main valve and using stored water is **response.** Flushing and checking the system after a notice is **recovery.** Maintaining your tanks and containers is **mitigation.**
