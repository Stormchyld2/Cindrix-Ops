[video] Your hygiene supply reserve

When a disruption goes on for more than a few days, cleanliness decides who stays well. Hand washing, clean water, waste disposal and basic personal care prevent the diarrhea, skin infections and colds that move through every crowded shelter and stressed household. Hygiene also keeps morale up: people who are clean feel more normal.

Personal care items are also among the first things to vanish from store shelves when supply chains falter. A three-month reserve of what you already use is cheap, easy to rotate and a real cushion.

## The plan
Stock a **three-month supply** of the personal care items your household uses daily. Use the oldest first and replace as you go, so nothing expires, but do not use the reserve down to zero. A hygiene reserve can also be shared with neighbors, given to charity or traded in a long crisis, since these items are often the first to run out.

## What to stock, for each person, for three months
- **Soap:** bar soap or liquid hand soap, plus laundry soap.
- **Hand sanitizer** with at least 60 percent alcohol, for when water is short.
- **Toothpaste, toothbrushes and floss.**
- **Shampoo and conditioner,** or a simple soap substitute.
- **Deodorant.**
- **Razors, and nail clippers.**
- **Toilet paper and tissues.** A household often uses a roll or two per person per week.
- **Feminine hygiene products** for every person who needs them, in the sizes and types they use.
- **Diapers and wipes** for infants, plus diaper cream.
- **Incontinence supplies** for anyone who needs them.
- **Wet wipes.**
- **Sunscreen and lip balm.**
- **Contact lens supplies and spare glasses.**
- **Comb, brush and hair ties.**
- **Cleaning supplies:** dish soap, household cleaner, plain unscented bleach, sponges and rubber gloves.
- **Trash bags** in several sizes, and heavy-duty bags for waste.
- **Laundry supplies,** including a bucket, a plunger and a clothes line for washing by hand.

## Dental health
Dental problems become painful and even dangerous when you cannot see a dentist. Stock a basic dental kit: toothbrushes, toothpaste, floss, a mouth rinse, dental wax and a temporary filling material, with the directions that come on each product. Brush twice a day even when water is short. Keep regular checkups current. If you have a dental infection with swelling or fever, that is a medical emergency.

## Medical items
Keep a modest stock of non-prescription items: bandages, antiseptic wipes, a thermometer, and the other items covered in the medical courses. This course does not give medicine amounts. Follow the label, and ask your clinician or pharmacist for advice about what to keep.

## Sanitation: the other half of hygiene
If water and sewer stop, plan how you will handle human waste.
- **Short term:** line a bucket with a heavy trash bag, add a little absorbent material such as sawdust or cat litter, seal and store the bags out of the way and dispose of them as local authorities advise.
- **A toilet seat for a bucket** makes this much easier.
- **Wash hands** after toilet use, before cooking and eating and after cleaning, with soap and clean water, or sanitizer if water is not available.
- **Keep it away from water,** food and living areas.
- **Disinfect** surfaces with a bleach solution as directed on the label and the CDC chart.
The Water and Sanitation course covers this in more depth.

## Storage
- **Cool, dry and dark,** away from heat and moisture.
- **Contained,** in bins, with each bin labeled and dated.
- **Organized,** with the daily items near the top and the reserve beneath.
- **Protected from pests.**
- **Not in a hot attic or garage,** which spoils soap, sunscreen and liquids.

## Rotate and check
Use the oldest first and replace what you use. Soap lasts for years. Toothpaste, sunscreen, deodorant and hand sanitizer expire, so check the dates. Do an inventory twice a year, and make an extra stock list in the program's Inventory.

## Uses beyond personal
A hygiene reserve is a gift to neighbors, a donation after a local disaster or a trade good in a long crisis. Just remember to keep what your own household needs first.

## Five principles
The reserve is **preparedness.** Hand washing and waste handling are **mitigation** of disease. Using it in a shortage is **response,** and replenishing afterwards is **recovery.**
