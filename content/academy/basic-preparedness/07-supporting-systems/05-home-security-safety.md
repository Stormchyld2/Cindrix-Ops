Systems that protect your family in your castle

## Fire Safety

One of the easiest and most effective tools you can have around your house to prevent disaster is a fire extinguisher.

Every family should have at least one dry-chemical fire extinguisher. This can be placed in the kitchen under the sink or in the garage near the kitchen.

If you want to be even more prepared you can have one in both locations.

The larger the extinguisher, the more fire it can put out. Just be aware that children or smaller-sized people will not be able to lift a heavier extinguisher. I like to have a mix of both.

The following is a recommended minimum inventory of fire extinguishing equipment you should possess.

## Checklist
- Dry Chem Fire Extinguisher
- Pressurized water fire extinguisher
- Fire Blanket

## Security Systems

## Alarm System

There are different kinds of alarm systems available. Some are professionally installed, while there is a growing trend in companies providing user-installed systems.

Simply Safe has been one of the popular brands that I have used personally, and there are others.

Just be aware: if your system has any kind of monitoring or cameras, then people at those companies have access to the feeds and this may compromise your own personal privacy

## Camera System

## There are lots of different camera systems available.

- Simple Wi-Fi-enabled cameras that you access via an app on your cell phone (VicoHome, CloudPlus)
- Wireless and wired cameras that hook up to a hard drive that you control (Night Owl)
- Camera systems that are controlled by a company (Ring, Arlo, Vivint, ADT, google Nest)

You may choose what fits best for you according to your needs, but we **highly recommend** that you pick a camera system that is not monitored by another company and does not depend on the internet. That being said, any camera you can remotely monitor has to have some kind of internet coverage.

## Going deeper: protecting your home in layers
### Fire safety first
Fire is far more likely than a break-in.
- **Smoke alarms:** one in every bedroom, outside each sleeping area and on every level. Test monthly. Replace batteries yearly (or use 10-year sealed units). Replace alarms after 10 years.
- **Carbon monoxide alarms:** on each level and near bedrooms.
- **Extinguishers:** a dry chemical ABC extinguisher in the kitchen and one in the garage or on each level. Learn **PASS:** Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep side to side. Check the gauge monthly. Only fight a small fire, with an exit behind you. If in doubt, get out and call 911.
- **Fire blanket:** smothers a grease fire in a pan.
- **Escape plan:** two ways out of every room, a meeting place outside, and a practice drill twice a year. In a fire, get out, stay out and call 911.
- **Kitchen safety:** never leave cooking unattended, and never put water on a grease fire.
- **Space heaters:** 3 feet from anything that burns. Never leave them on when sleeping.

### Doors and windows
- **Exterior doors:** solid core or metal, with a deadbolt that has a one-inch throw.
- **Strike plate:** reinforce with 3-inch screws that reach the wall stud.
- **Door frame:** reinforce.
- **Sliding doors:** put a bar or dowel in the track, and a pin lock.
- **Windows:** locks and pins, and laminated safety film if you can.
- **Garage:** lock the door to the house and the garage door, and unplug the opener when away for long.
- **Do not hide a key** under the mat.

### Lighting and landscaping
- **Motion-activated lights** at entrances.
- **Trim** shrubs so there are no hiding places, and trim trees away from the home.
- **Keep** the yard tidy.

### Alarms and cameras
- **Alarm:** door and window sensors and a loud siren. Understand who monitors it and what data they hold.
- **Cameras:** prefer a system you control. Position them at entrances. Remember they need power and internet to be useful.
- **Signs and stickers** deter some intruders.
- **Privacy:** do not point cameras at neighbors' property.

### Habits
- **Lock** doors, even when home.
- **Do not advertise** valuables or absences.
- **Know your neighbors.**
- **Check** who is at the door.
- **Keep** a flashlight and phone by the bed.
- **Have a safe room** with a phone and a door that locks.

### In a break-in
Get your family to the safe room, call 911 and let the police handle it. Property can be replaced.

### Checklist
- Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms tested
- An extinguisher in the kitchen, and one more
- A family escape plan practiced
- Strong locks and good lighting
- A safe room plan
