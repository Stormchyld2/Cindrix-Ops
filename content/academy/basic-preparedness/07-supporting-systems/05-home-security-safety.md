[video] Protecting your home in layers

Your home is your first shelter, and protecting it is cheap compared with replacing it. The most important thing to know is the order of risks: **fire is far more likely than a break-in.** Most households will have a fire event, a smoke alarm sounding, a kitchen flare-up or a heater problem, long before they face an intruder. So start with fire and carbon monoxide, then deal with doors, windows, lights and alarms.

## Fire safety first
### Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms
- Put a smoke alarm **inside every bedroom, outside each sleeping area and on every level** of the home.
- Put a **carbon monoxide alarm** on every level and near sleeping areas.
- **Test monthly,** and replace batteries at least yearly, or use sealed ten-year units. Replace the whole alarm about every ten years.
- Interconnected alarms sound together, which is best.

### Fire extinguishers
Every family should have at least one dry-chemical extinguisher, rated **ABC** so it works on ordinary fires, liquids and electrical fires. A good minimum is one in the kitchen (but not next to the stove, where a fire would block it) and one in the garage or near the main exit.
- **Size matters.** A larger extinguisher puts out more fire, but children and smaller people may not be able to lift it. A mix of sizes is smart.
- **Use PASS:** **P**ull the pin, **A**im at the base of the fire, **S**queeze the handle, **S**weep side to side.
- **Stand with your back to an exit,** and only fight a small fire. If it spreads, if there is a lot of smoke or if the extinguisher runs out, **get out and call 911.**
- **Check the gauge** monthly and replace or service as the label says. Learn how to use it by reading and, better, by taking a class.
- Add a **fire blanket** for the kitchen, and a **kitchen-grease** extinguisher where you can.

### Escape plan
- Draw a floor plan with **two ways out of every room.**
- Pick a **meeting place** outside, such as a mailbox or a tree.
- **Practice** twice a year, including at night. Time it.
- Keep **escape ladders** for upper floors, stored by windows.
- Crawl low under smoke, check doors for heat and close doors behind you.
- Never go back inside.

### Prevention
- Never leave cooking unattended.
- Keep a three-foot clear zone around heaters.
- Do not overload outlets and extension cords.
- Have chimneys and heaters inspected yearly.
- Store flammables in approved containers, away from heat.
- Never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors.

## Doors and windows
Most burglars choose the easy way in: unlocked doors and windows.
- Lock doors and windows, **every time,** including when you are home.
- Use **solid-core or metal exterior doors** with a good deadbolt (at least one inch throw) and a **reinforced strike plate** held by three-inch screws into the frame.
- Add **security film or laminated glass** where practical, and **bars or pins** for sliding doors.
- A peephole or a video doorbell lets you see who is there.
- Never hide a spare key under a mat or planter. Leave one with a neighbor instead.

## Lighting and landscaping
- **Lights** at doors and dark corners, with motion sensors where possible. A lit property is less attractive.
- **Trim shrubs** below the windows and trees away from the roof, so that no one can hide, and so that fire cannot climb.
- Keep **ladders, tools and wheelie bins** put away, since they help intruders.
- **Mark** the address clearly so that responders can find you.

## Alarms and cameras
- **Alarm systems** may be professionally installed or self-installed. Popular consumer brands exist. If your system is monitored or has cameras, remember that people at the company may have access to feeds, which affects privacy.
- **Cameras.** We recommend a system that stores video locally and is not run by a third party. Any camera you can watch remotely needs internet access, so weigh the convenience against the privacy. Secure each device with a strong, unique password and updates.
- **Signs** and window stickers deter many opportunists.
- A **dog,** even a small one, is a good alarm.

## Habits
- **Know your neighbors** and watch each other's homes.
- **Don't advertise** your absences or valuables on social media.
- **Use timers** to turn lights on when away.
- **Hold mail and packages** while traveling.
- **Don't open the door** to strangers. Ask for identification and call the company.
- **Keep the safe room ready:** a room with a solid door, a phone, a light and a first aid kit.

## In a break-in
If you hear an intruder while at home, **get your family to the safe room, lock the door, call 911** and stay on the line. Let the police handle it. Property can be replaced. People cannot. Do not go looking. After the police leave, document damage for insurance.

## Five principles
Assessing your home's weak points is **hazard assessment.** Alarms, locks and extinguishers are **preparedness** and **mitigation.** The escape plan and the safe room drive **response.** Documenting and repairing damage is **recovery.**
