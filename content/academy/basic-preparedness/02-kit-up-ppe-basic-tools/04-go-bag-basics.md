[video] Go-bag basics: a weekend build

A go-bag, also called a 72-hour bag, holds three days of supplies for one person so that you can leave home on short notice. Every household member gets one. It is not a bunker in a backpack: it is the short list of things you would desperately want if you left in ten minutes.

The most common failure is not a missing item. It is a bag that never gets built. People intend to, and put it off, and the emergency arrives first. This lesson is about getting it done.

## Start with what you own
Do not wait for "top of the line" gear. Use what you have today: a school pack, a sweater, a flashlight, a lighter. Fill the gaps later, and upgrade to lighter or better gear when you can. A basic bag you finish this weekend is worth far more than a perfect one you build in six months.

## A build plan for one weekend
Three one-hour sessions. Use the full checklist from the lesson "Build your go-bag".
1. **Saturday morning: gather.** Walk through the house with the checklist and set aside everything you already own: bag, clothes, flashlight, lighter, bottles, cash and documents.
2. **Saturday afternoon: fill the gaps.** Write a short shopping list. Buy the cheapest items first: tarp, cord, batteries, hygiene items and a few days of food.
3. **Sunday: pack and test.** Pack in zip bags by category. Weigh it. Light the lighter. Turn on every light. Run a cup of water through the filter. Put on the rain gear.

## Organize so you can find things
Use clear zip bags or colored stuff sacks and label them with a marker. Pack by how fast you will need things.
- **Top and outside pockets:** rain shell, hat and gloves, headlamp, snacks, first aid, water bottle, map and radio.
- **Middle, near your back:** the heaviest items such as food and water gear.
- **Bottom:** sleeping gear, spare clothes and the least urgent items.

Tape a printed packing list inside, and a card with your emergency contacts, your allergies and your out-of-area contact.

## Keep it fresh with the seasons
Every spring and fall, do a swap:
- Change clothes for the season, and check that children's sizes still fit.
- Check the dates on food, water, medicines and batteries.
- Replace what you used or expired.
- Re-weigh the bag and walk with it.
- Update papers and contact numbers.

## Kits beyond the bag at home
- **At work:** a small get-home kit with walking shoes, water, snacks, a light, a radio, a charger and a jacket. You may be far from home when the emergency starts.
- **In the car:** a smaller kit, and keep the fuel tank at least half full. The Mobility course covers the car in detail.
- **At school and daycare:** make sure each child's school has your up-to-date contact card, and know how the school plans to evacuate and reunite families.
- **For pets:** food, water, a leash or carrier, medicines and records. Many shelters do not take animals, so plan ahead.

## When a go-bag is used
- A house fire, flood or wildfire evacuation.
- A gas leak, a chemical spill or other hazard that forces you out.
- A power outage that forces you to move to warmth or cooling.
- Any short-notice relocation, from a building collapse in your block to a storm warning.

Seen this way the bag is a tool for action, not fear. It lets you leave calmly and decisively instead of scrambling.

## What comes next
When the bag is done, move on to the household plan and your home supplies. The bag is the beginning of preparedness, not the end.
