Test your knowledge of Go-Bag & Sustainment concepts.

## Check yourself
? Why is a go-bag important?
- To store items you don't use at home
+ To leave quickly without scrambling during an emergency
- To replace your home's emergency supplies
- To carry everything you own
> A go-bag lets you leave immediately without scrambling to gather essentials under stress. Every second counts in an emergency evacuation.
? Which of the following is NOT a core need a go-bag addresses?
- Hydration
- Shelter
+ Entertainment
- Communication
> A go-bag prioritizes speed and efficiency — water, food, shelter, communication, and documents. Entertainment is not a survival priority.
? Why is multi-purpose gear preferred in a go-bag?
- It is always cheaper
+ It reduces weight and bulk
- It lasts longer than single-purpose items
- It is easier to find in stores
> Every pound matters. Gear that serves more than one function reduces total weight and frees up space for other essentials.
? How many independent light sources should your go-bag include?
- One
- Two
+ Three
- Five
> Three independent light sources ensures redundancy. If one fails or runs out of battery, you still have two backups.
? Why should you carry multiple fire-starting methods?
- To start larger fires
- One method is for cooking and one is for warmth
+ Redundancy in case one method fails
- Different methods work at different altitudes
> Lighters fail in wet conditions, matches can get damp, and ferrocerium rods require technique. Redundancy ensures you can always start a fire when needed.
? What does proper water planning for a go-bag include?
- Pack as many water bottles as you can carry
- Rely on finding water at shelters
- Water tablets alone are sufficient
+ Have water plus a way to collect and purify more
> Carrying water covers your immediate need, but emergencies can last longer than expected. A purification method lets you resupply from streams, rain, or other sources.
? You're packing your bag and it's getting too heavy. What should you prioritize removing?
- Water and food
+ Non-essential or single-purpose items
- Documents and cash
- First aid supplies
> Water, food, documents, and first aid are non-negotiable. Single-purpose or comfort items are where you cut weight first.
? You have food and water but no way to boil or purify. What go-bag principle are you missing?
- Redundancy
- Weight management
+ Sustainability
- Mobility
> Having supplies without the means to make them safe or usable long-term is a sustainability failure. Your kit must support extended self-sufficiency, not just immediate needs.
? During an evacuation you realize you forgot your documents and emergency cash. What lesson does this reinforce?
- Digital copies are sufficient
- Banks are always accessible during emergencies
- Documents are optional in short evacuations
+ Preparation must happen before the emergency
> Once an emergency is underway, it is too late to prepare. Documents, cash, and critical items must be staged in the bag before you ever need it.
? Why is a tarp considered sufficient shelter in a go-bag rather than a full tent?
- It is warmer than a tent
+ It meets the basic need of protection from elements
- It is required by most emergency shelters
- It doubles as a water container
> Shelter's core purpose is protection from wind, rain, and cold. A tarp is lightweight, versatile, and meets that need without the bulk of a tent.
? Your flashlight fails and you have no backup light source. What go-bag principle did you overlook?
- Weight management
- Multi-purpose gear selection
- Sustainability
+ Redundancy
> Critical gear should never have a single point of failure. Multiple independent light sources is a direct application of the redundancy principle.
? Your car battery dies at an evacuation checkpoint. Which vehicle kit item is most useful?
- Fix-a-flat
- Emergency blanket
+ Jumper cables or jump box
- Paper maps
> A dead battery stops your evacuation entirely. Jumper cables or a jump box restore mobility immediately — the most critical outcome in that moment.
