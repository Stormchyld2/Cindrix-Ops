## Option: Sleep in your vehicle
Your vehicle can be one of the most practical quick shelter options—especially if you need to leave home but don't have a fixed destination.

## Common Setups
- Pickup truck + mattress (ideal with a canopy or camper shell)
- Vans or SUVs with seats folded down
- Hatchbacks for compact setups
- Reclined front seats (last resort, but better than exposure)

**PROS**:
- No setup required
- Solid protection from weather
- More security than being fully exposed
- Mobile

**CONS**:
- Limited space and comfort
- Temperature control can be difficult
- Not ideal for long-term use

## Safety Warning: Carbon Monoxide Risk

- Never run your vehicle in an enclosed or obstructed environment.

- Exhaust blocked by snow, mud, or debris can cause deadly CO buildup

- Avoid idling for long periods, especially while sleeping

→ Best Use Case: Short-term shelter while traveling or evacuating

## Note: For some people, sleeping in a vehicle isn't part of a preparedness plan...

...it's something they've already had to do just to get by. If that's you, or someone you care about, it's worth saying clearly: that situation is hard, and it takes resilience to get through it.

If you or someone you know is currently relying on a vehicle for long-term shelter, there are resources that may be able to help:

- Local shelters and outreach programs often provide safe places to sleep, meals, and support services

- Community resource centers can help connect you to housing assistance, job programs, and healthcare

- In the U.S., dialing 211 can connect you to local social services and emergency support

- Churches, and nonprofit organizations can also be good starting points for finding help in your area

**We are helping people build out options and stability, but we also recognize that not everyone starts from the same place.**

## Option: Tents
Tents are nice due to compact size and weight, but lack true protection from dangerous animals or people!

There are many different kinds of tents, each with their own set of trade offs.

## Family style tent
Large, comfortable tents designed for group use.

PROS: Spacious (room for people + gear), More comfortable for families, Good for longer stays in one location

CONS: Bulky and heavy, Not practical to carry on foot, Requires time and space to set up

→ Best Use Case: Vehicle-based bug out or backyard fallback shelter

## Backpacking style tent
- Lighter weight, can be carried while on foot
- Less room than family size tent, but will keep you dry!
- No significant protection from animals or nefarious people.  Actually diminished situational awareness.

→ Best Use Case: Bugging out on foot

## Bivouac Sack (Bivy Sack)
A purpose built "single person" waterproof shelter. These can be expensive - the Gore tex breathable material is good but pricey. Emergency models that are very small and lightweight are not breathable and can cause excessive moisture buildup if not used properly.

→ Best Use Case: Emergency shelter or ultralight backup option

## Summary
These options aren't about comfort, they're about keeping you protected when your primary shelter is gone.

Vehicle = fast and mobile
Tent = flexible and portable
Bivy = last-resort backup

## Checklist
- Obtain a TENT that will fit you and your family
- Set up your vehicle as a CONTINGENCY shelter option
- Add a Bivy shelter to your 3 Day Bag
