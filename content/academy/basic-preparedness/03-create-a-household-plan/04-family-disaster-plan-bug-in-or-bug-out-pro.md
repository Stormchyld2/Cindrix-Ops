[video] Your bug-in and bug-out procedures

The decision tree tells you what to do. This lesson tells you how to do it, step by step, so the whole household can act without a debate. Write both procedures on a page each, put them in the binder, and practice them. When the moment comes, nobody should need to think about what comes next. They should just start at step one.

## Bug in: the shelter-in-place procedure
Use this when the tree says to stay.
1. **Check everyone.** Count heads. Give first aid and call for help for serious injuries.
2. **Make the home safe.** Check for gas leaks, fire, structural damage and flooding. If you smell gas, leave at once. Shut off utilities if there is damage and you know how.
3. **Set up the safe area.** Choose the safest room: warm or cool as needed, dry and close to water and your bathroom arrangement. Shelter from tornadoes, quakes or storms according to the hazard: lowest interior room for tornadoes, away from windows in storms.
4. **Count supplies.** Water, food, light, heat, medicine, hygiene, batteries and fuel. Write the numbers on paper.
5. **Start a routine.** Regular meals, rest, news at set times and a watch schedule if needed. Routine calms children and adults alike.
6. **Check on neighbors.** Especially people living alone, the elderly and anyone with a medical need.
7. **Contact your people.** Message your out-of-area contact and your group.
8. **Decide at set times.** Each morning and evening, ask again whether to stay or go.
9. **Keep go-bags by the door.** If the answer changes, you can leave in minutes.

### Do not make a bad shelter worse
- Never run a generator, grill or stove indoors. Carbon monoxide kills.
- Use flashlights or lanterns instead of candles where you can, because candles cause fires.
- Keep water and food rationing steady, not reckless.
- Stay out of floodwater, and away from downed power lines.

## Bug out: the evacuation procedure
Use this when the tree says to leave.
1. **Alert everyone** with a clear signal, such as "We are leaving now." Practice saying it.
2. **Grab each person's go-bag,** the binder, medicines, phones and chargers, pets and their supplies.
3. **Secure the home.** Turn off utilities if told to, close windows and doors, lock up and look around once.
4. **Load the vehicle** in a fixed order: people, pets, go-bags, then extra supplies according to space and weather.
5. **Leave a note** on the door or inside, with the time, your destination and contact details, so helpers know you are safe.
6. **Leave early,** by your planned route, and avoid known hazards such as flooded roads, bridges and downed trees.
7. **Start the group contact plan** at your first safe stop.
8. **Go to your destination,** either your prepared bug-out location or your rendezvous point.
9. **Check in** with your out-of-area contact.
10. **Reassess at each stop.** Check fuel, road news and everyone's condition.

## The pre-departure checklist
Print this and keep it near the door. Practice it until it takes minutes.
- **People:** everyone counted, shoes on.
- **Pets:** carriers, leashes, food, water, records.
- **Documents:** the binder.
- **Medicine and glasses.**
- **Phones, chargers and a power bank.**
- **Food and water.**
- **Clothing for the weather.**
- **Cash and keys.**
- **Fuel** at least half full.
- **House secured.**

## Give everyone a job
Leaving takes minutes if each person has a task, and hours if everyone does everything.
- **One adult** grabs the go-bags and the binder.
- **One adult** handles the vehicle and the route.
- **A teenager** takes the pets, and the youngest children have a buddy.
- **Someone** checks the utilities and locks the house.
Rotate roles at drills so everyone can do each job.

## Special cases
- **No vehicle.** Plan how you will leave on foot or by transit, and arrange rides with neighbors or relatives ahead of time.
- **Someone needs help moving.** Plan with them, including equipment and extra time, and register with your local emergency office if a program exists.
- **Pets.** Many public shelters do not allow them. Know pet-friendly hotels and relatives who can host.
- **Young children.** Put a card with their name, your numbers and allergies in a pocket.

## Drill it and time it
Practice a bug-out drill once or twice a year. Aim to be out of the door in 15 to 30 minutes with everything. Time yourselves, find what went wrong and improve. Also practice a bug-in drill: set up the safe room and count supplies.

## Five principles
These procedures are **response**, but they are built from **preparedness** (go-bags, binder), **mitigation** (leaving early, securing the home) and **hazard assessment** (the hazards you chose to plan for). They also set up **recovery** by leaving a clear trail.
