[video] Building your emergency binder

After a fire, a flood or an evacuation, one of the hardest problems is proving who you are and what you own. Insurance claims, bank access, benefit applications and even getting back into a damaged home all require documents. The same moment is when the documents are most likely to be lost or destroyed. The binder solves it: your most important papers, contacts and plans in one place, ready to grab.

## Format
Use a three-ring binder with clear plastic sleeves. It is cheap, it is easy to grab and everything is visible. Make **two copies**: one stays at home in a secure spot, such as a fireproof box or safe, and one goes with the go-bag. Keep a **scanned copy** on an encrypted USB drive or encrypted cloud storage, and tell your out-of-area contact where a copy is held if you trust them.

## Section 1: family information
- **A page for each person:** full name, date of birth, blood type if known, medical conditions, allergies, current medicines, doctor, pharmacy and insurance details.
- **A recent photo** of each person and each pet. Photos help others find a lost child or animal.
- **Contacts:** emergency contacts, your out-of-area contact, neighbors, schools, workplaces, your doctor, your vet and your landlord or property manager.
- **Phone numbers written on paper.** Phones die and get lost, and few of us remember numbers any more.

## Section 2: the plans
- The family disaster plan with the bug-in or bug-out decision tree.
- The group contact plan and the out-of-area contact plan.
- The bug-out plan with routes and destinations.
- Meeting places, marked on a map.
- Utility shutoff instructions, with a photo of each shutoff.

## Section 3: copies of important documents
- Identification: driver's licenses, passports, birth certificates, Social Security cards, marriage license and adoption papers.
- Insurance policies and agent numbers: home or renters, auto, health, life and flood.
- Housing and vehicle: mortgage or lease, deed and vehicle titles.
- Legal and medical: wills, powers of attorney and medical directives, vaccination records.
- Financial: a list of bank accounts and credit cards, with the issuer's phone numbers. Keep account numbers on a separate secure page or in a password manager rather than in the binder if you fear theft.
- Tax records for the last few years.
- **Photos or video of your home and belongings,** room by room, for insurance. Open cupboards and drawers. Store the files off site or encrypted.

## Section 4: maps and local information
- Local and regional maps with evacuation routes marked.
- The locations of shelters, hospitals and fire stations.
- Local radio stations, and information on how to sign up for local alerts.

## Section 5: reference
- First aid quick guides.
- Emergency radio frequencies and your group's radio plan.
- Instructions for the generator, stove, filter and other gear.
- A list of supplies and where they are kept. The program's Inventory can print this.

## Protect your privacy
A binder is a gift to an identity thief if it is lost. Keep it locked in a fireproof box or safe and tell trusted family where it is. When you travel with it, keep it on your person. Never post photos of it. Never put account passwords in it. If it is lost, tell your bank, freeze your credit and report it.

## Keep the digital backup honest
1. **Scan** each key document. Phone scanning apps are fine.
2. **Encrypt** the folder with a strong passphrase, or use encrypted storage.
3. **Store** a copy on a USB drive in the go-bag and one off site.
4. **Label** the files clearly: "Passport - Sam".
5. **Check** that you can open them on another device.

## Maintain it
Binders go stale. Set a reminder every six months to update medicines, phone numbers, photos, policies and expiry dates. Replace passports and licenses when renewed. Add a page after any life event.

## A first evening's work
You do not have to build all five sections in one night. Start with the family information pages and the contact list, because they matter most, then add one section a week.
