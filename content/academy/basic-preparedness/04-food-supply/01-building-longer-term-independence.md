What would you do if the grocery store shelves were bare?

**Severe hunger can cause even the nicest people to turn into desperate criminals.** Your average everyday middle-class neighbor may seem like a nice guy, but when his children are starving, he'll do anything to help get food in their stomachs.

One of the best things you can do, besides making sure you have enough food, is to make sure everyone in your group or neighborhood also has extra food prepared.

A great benefit of preparing as a group is that everyone becomes self-reliant, so they don't come to you for your supplies when things get really desperate.

We are going to teach you a system that will **ensure your family does not go hungry** during small to medium disasters and emergencies.

Depending on your means, this may take some time and discipline on your part.  Eventually you will reach the goal and experience a confidence based upon diligence!

We previously talked about food in the context of your 3 day bag; ensuring you have quick access to the basics right at the moment of emergency. Here we are looking beyond.

## Three elements of the Food Supply System

- 3 month Prepared Pantry
- 2 week Portable Food Supply
- 1 year Long Term Storage Supply

Each of these three categories has its own benefits and usage cases, and they work together to complement each other.

Let's discuss each of these elements in greater depth.

## Going deeper: how the three layers fit together
- **2-week portable supply:** food you can grab and go. Lightweight, ready to eat or just add water. It serves bug out situations and short emergencies.
- **3-month pantry:** the normal food your family eats, stocked deeper. It handles job loss, shortages, storms and long outages. You use it every day and replace it, so it stays fresh.
- **1-year long-term storage:** staples such as rice, beans and oats stored for years in sealed containers. It is a deep reserve for a major disruption.

### How much is enough?
A simple rule: one pound of dry food per person per day, or about 2,000 to 2,500 calories. For a family of four, a three-month pantry is about 360 pounds of food equivalent. Do not be daunted. Build in steps.

### Order of building
1. **3 days** (the Go-Bag).
2. **2 weeks** of portable food.
3. **1 month** of regular food.
4. **3 months** in the pantry.
5. **Staples** for longer.
Each step makes the next one easier.

### What to count
- **Calories:** make sure total calories meet needs.
- **Variety:** include protein, fat, carbs, fruit and vegetables.
- **Comfort food:** coffee, tea, chocolate, spices.
- **Special diets,** allergies, baby food and pet food.
- **Cooking:** a way to cook and the fuel for it.
- **Water:** food needs water to cook (rice and beans use a lot).

### Eat what you store
The best storage is food your family already eats, because you rotate it naturally. Test unfamiliar long-term foods. A family that hates beans will not eat them in a crisis.

### Help the neighbors help themselves
You cannot feed a neighborhood. Encourage others to build their own reserves. A prepared group is stronger and calmer. Offer to share tips, bulk buys and classes.

### Safety and rotation
Use first-in, first-out, mark dates and check storage every season. See the Survival basics course for details on storing and rotating food.
