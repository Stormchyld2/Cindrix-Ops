[video] Starting your one-year supply

Long-term food storage does not have to be complicated. If you seal dry staple foods in Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers and put them inside food-grade five-gallon buckets, they can be kept safely for many years when stored cool and dry. Under good conditions, white rice and dried beans are commonly stored for 20 years or more, while oats and flour are better thought of in the 10 to 20 year range. Warmth is the biggest enemy, so the cooler the room, the longer the food lasts.

For your Tier 3 foundation, start simple. A year of food sounds enormous, but a year is a sum of buckets, and you build it a few at a time.

## The simple starter set
A good first purchase is three five-gallon buckets, one each of white rice, dried beans and rolled oats, about 25 pounds each. This basic set gives roughly 50 days of food at 2,500 calories a day for one person. That is a strong start. With a bigger household, buy two buckets of each. With the canned meats, oils and spices from your pantry, these staples become filling, varied meals.

## Materials
- **Food-grade five-gallon buckets with lids.** Use new buckets sold for food, or ones that held food, and avoid ones that held chemicals.
- **Mylar bags,** sized to fit the buckets, about 5 mil thick or more.
- **Oxygen absorbers,** of the right size for the volume of the bag, in a sealed jar until use.
- **An iron or hair straightener** for sealing the bag.
- **Labels and a marker.**
- **A scale** or a standard scoop for weighing.

## How to pack, step by step
1. **Check the food.** Use clean, dry staples with low moisture: white rice, dried beans, oats, wheat, pasta, flour, sugar and salt.
2. **Put the Mylar bag in the bucket** and fill it with the food, leaving a few inches at the top.
3. **Add oxygen absorbers** on top, working quickly because they start absorbing as soon as you open the pack. Use the right amount for the bucket volume, as stated on the package.
4. **Press out the extra air** and **seal** the bag with the iron, leaving a small gap that you seal at the end.
5. **Close the lid** firmly.
6. **Label** the contents and the date.
7. **Store** in a cool, dry, dark place and off the floor.

## Which foods
**Good for long-term storage:**
- White rice (brown rice has oils that go off sooner).
- Dried beans, lentils and split peas.
- Rolled oats and wheat berries.
- Pasta and flour (flour has a shorter life).
- Sugar, salt, and dry milk powder.

**Not for oxygen-absorber packing:**
- Brown sugar, honey and anything moist or oily, which can cause problems.
- Fresh or semi-dry foods, such as soft dried fruit, which can develop dangerous bacteria in an oxygen-free environment.
- Whole spices and nuts. Store those in jars in the pantry and rotate.
Always follow safe-storage guidance from your extension service for any food outside that list.

## How much
The three-bucket starter gives about 50 days for one adult. To build toward a year, add more buckets of rice, beans, wheat and pasta, along with salt, oil, sugar, spices and vitamins. A common figure is 300 to 400 pounds of grain and beans per adult per year, with extra for fats and variety. Every family differs, so work out your own.

## Make it complete
Staples alone become boring and nutritionally thin. Pair the buckets with:
- Cooking oil or shortening.
- Salt, sugar and baking supplies.
- Spices, bouillon and sauces for flavor.
- Canned meat, fruit and vegetables for variety.
- Multivitamins for adults and children, on advice from a clinician.
- Water and a way to cook it (Modules 5 and 6).

## Learn to cook it
Test rice and beans, bake bread from flour and cook oats now. Learn how long beans soak and how much fuel they take. Beans need long cooking and plenty of water, so plan fuel. A family who has never cooked from scratch will struggle in a crisis, so practice monthly.

## Safety
- Never store food buckets near chemicals, fuel or fertilizer.
- Keep them off concrete floors, on pallets or shelves, because moisture wicks up.
- Check buckets once or twice a year for pests, dampness and bulging.
- Do not eat food that smells off, looks moldy or comes from a bulging bag.
- Rotate older stock into your pantry every few years.

## Five principles
The year supply is **preparedness** at its deepest. Packing safely is **mitigation.** Cooking from staples is **response** to shortage, and it carries a household through **recovery** from job loss or long disruptions.
