Increasing your personal readiness does not have to be expensive!

Throughout the rest of this course, there are several checklists of things that you need - but you don't need top-of-the-line gear (or even new gear) in order to meet the baseline standard for you and your family. Often, when it comes to preparedness, perfect is the enemy of good.

If you go to the store with a blank checklist, you'll likely drop several hundred dollars. If you're looking to approach this frugally, here are some tips for getting your baseline established on a budget.

## Tip #1: Use what you already have
Don't need a new backpack or a fancy special tub in order to get your Go-Bag together (You'll learn more on Go-Bags in the next lesson). Most people have at least half of what they need without even realizing it - it's a matter of being organized and intentional. For example, you likely already have:
- Kitchen items (containers, knives, lighters)
- Old backpacks or duffel bags
- Clothing layers instead of buying "specialized" gear

## Tip #2: Check your local thrift store or "Buy Nothing" Group
Most areas have what's called a "Buy Nothing" group on Facebook where people in your neighborhood will post things that they don't need anymore to see if anyone in their community could benefit. You also can post "in search of" or "ISO" and ask for a specific item. If you don't have a spare backpack lying around, chances are somebody in your neighborhood does. Thrift stores or charity shops are also a gold mine for outdoor gear and quality clothing for a fraction of the price. Pro tip: If you donate something to a thrift store, they'll typically give you a coupon for your next purchase, saving you even more money.

## Tip #3: Collect items in stages
Trying to complete a checklist in one shopping trip can get expensive fast. Instead, you can:

- Set a small weekly or monthly budget (even $20–$50)
- Add a few items each time

The great part about MAP's task list is it can help you track progress towards a goal over time. You can create a new goal task for yourself or even a weekly challenge to add one item to your Go-Bag per week, or save a set amount of money each month towards your preparedness goals.

## Tip #4: DIY where possible
Some items are cheaper (and just as effective) if you make them:

- First aid kits → build your own instead of buying pre-made
- Emergency kits → assemble with dollar store items
- Organizers → reuse containers or zip bags

Pre-packaged Go-Bags or emergency kits almost always charge extra for convenience. In the following lessons, we'll share details on all the items you'll need to be prepared so you can build it yourself.
