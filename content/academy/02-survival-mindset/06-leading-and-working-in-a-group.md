Most survival stories involve groups: families, neighbors, coworkers, strangers. Groups survive better when they work together.

## Pick a leader, quickly
- **Choose someone** to make final calls. It may be the most experienced person, the oldest, or the best at staying calm.
- **Leaders listen** first, decide, and explain.
- **Everyone** can speak up about safety.
- **Change leaders** if the task changes. A person who knows first aid leads the medical task.

## Roles
Assign roles, so tasks do not fall through:
- **Leader and deputy**
- **Safety:** watch for hazards
- **Medical**
- **Water and food**
- **Shelter and fire**
- **Communications and navigation**
- **Care for children and the elderly**
- **Security and watch**

Even a family of four can use this.

## Communicate
- **Hold short briefings** at set times.
- **Use plain words.**
- **Repeat back** instructions.
- **Share bad news** carefully, truthfully.
- **Keep a log** of decisions and events.

## Building trust
- **Be honest.**
- **Do your share.**
- **Share resources fairly.**
- **Respect differences.**
- **Resolve conflict quickly,** privately and kindly.
- **Thank people.**

## Common problems
- **One person takes over** and ignores others.
- **No one decides.**
- **People hoard** or hide supplies.
- **Blame** after a mistake.
- **Panic spreads.**
- **A member wants to leave alone.** Talk about it, and plan a safe way for them.
- **Strangers join.** Be kind and careful. Share what is safe to share.

## Keep the group safe
- **Never leave anyone alone** if they are hurt, ill or distressed.
- **Use the buddy system** for any task away from camp.
- **Count heads** at every stop.
- **Set a rally point** and a time for return.
- **Keep the pace** of the slowest.

## Teach the team
- **Cross-train:** everyone learns basic first aid, radio use and fire safety.
- **Practice** together.
- **Review** after exercises.

## Decisions
- **Urgent:** the leader decides.
- **Important but not urgent:** discuss as a group, then decide.
- **Everyday:** the person in charge of the task decides.

> Clear roles, honest talk and kindness hold a group together.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
