Good decisions in a crisis are a skill. You can practice it before you need it.

## The decision loop: Observe, Orient, Decide, Act
Military pilot John Boyd described this loop. Many responders use it.
1. **Observe:** gather facts about what is happening.
2. **Orient:** understand what the facts mean, given what you know, your training and your resources.
3. **Decide:** choose an action. Pick one, not ten.
4. **Act:** do it.
Then **observe again** to see what changed. The person who loops faster and more accurately has the advantage.

## Common traps
- **Tunnel vision:** focusing on one thing and missing others.
- **Plan continuation:** sticking with the plan after the situation changes. ("We have come this far.")
- **Group think:** no one speaks up.
- **Optimism bias:** "It will not happen to me."
- **Sunk cost:** staying because of what you have already put in.
- **Information overload:** too much news. Choose a few sources.
- **Decision fatigue:** too many choices make you worse.
- **Peer pressure and summit fever:** pushing on because others are, or because of a goal.

## Better habits
- **Use checklists.** They work under stress.
- **Pick triggers in advance.** "If the water reaches the second step, we leave."
- **Ask "what if I am wrong?"** and prefer reversible choices.
- **Hear the quiet person.** Ask each member what they see.
- **Take a short pause** before big decisions, unless there is immediate danger.
- **Write it down:** the decision, the reason and the time.
- **Decide who decides.** In a group, one person makes the call, after listening.
- **Review:** after, ask what worked.

## Quick tests for risky choices
- **Is it reversible?**
- **What is the worst case? Can we survive it?**
- **What would an experienced person do?**
- **Am I tired, hungry, cold or scared?** Those states hurt judgment. Fix them first.
- **Does everyone agree, or is someone uneasy?**

## A simple decision card
Keep this on paper:
1. What do I know for sure?
2. What do I not know?
3. What are my options? (Max 3)
4. What is the risk of each?
5. What is the first step of the best option?
6. When will I re-check?

## Practice
Run **scenario drills** with your group. Read a short story of a problem, and have each person answer the card. Compare answers.

## Checklist
- Know the OODA loop
- Know common traps
- Have triggers written for your household
- Used a decision card in a drill
