[video] Fatigue, sleep and routine

Tired people make poor choices, get hurt and get cold. Managing rest is part of survival.

## What tiredness does
- **Slows thinking** and reaction time.
- **Hurts judgment,** and raises risk-taking.
- **Weakens the immune system.**
- **Worsens mood** and increases conflict.
- **Raises the risk of accidents,** especially driving.
- **Makes cold and heat injuries more likely.**

After 24 hours with no sleep, performance is like being legally drunk.

## Rest in a crisis
- **Sleep when you can,** even in short blocks. Short naps of 20 to 30 minutes help.
- **Take turns** keeping watch or doing tasks so each person gets a long stretch.
- **Make a rest schedule.** In a group, plan **shifts** (for example, 4 hours on and 8 off).
- **Rest before you are exhausted.**
- **Create a good sleep spot:** warm, dry, off the ground, quiet, away from hazards.
- **Use earplugs and an eye mask** if you have them.
- **Keep a bedtime routine** to help sleep even when stressed.

## Routine
A routine saves energy and builds morale:
- **Wake and sleep times**
- **Meals**
- **Chores:** water, sanitation, cleaning, firewood
- **Check-ins:** radio, news and meetings
- **Exercise:** walking and stretching
- **Free time:** play, reading, talking

## Children and older adults
They tire and chill faster. Make sure they rest, eat and drink. Keep bedtimes.

## Food and water support energy
- **Eat regular meals,** with carbs and protein.
- **Drink water** often. Dehydration feels like fatigue.
- **Limit caffeine** and alcohol, which disturb sleep and hydration.

## Driving
**Do not drive when exhausted.** If you must travel, share driving, take breaks and stop to nap.

## Signs you are too tired
- Microsleeps (nodding off).
- Forgetting simple things.
- Irritability.
- Poor coordination.
- Mistakes in tasks you know well.
- Hallucinations (after long wakefulness).
If you see these, **stop and rest.** Tell others.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.

## Fatigue is a hazard
Tired people make more mistakes, misjudge risks, get irritable and become accident-prone. Many survival failures begin with exhaustion. Treat rest as equipment, not as a reward.

## Sleep is part of the plan
Adults generally need seven or more hours, but in a crisis even short naps help. A twenty-minute nap can restore alertness. When possible, split watches so that nobody goes more than a day without proper sleep. Give people with jobs that need alertness, such as driving, priority for rest.

## Build a routine
A simple daily rhythm gives structure: wake and check, breakfast, chores and work, a midday rest, afternoon tasks, an evening meal, a group check-in and a set bedtime. Routines reduce decision fatigue and stabilize mood.

## Create a sleep space
Quiet, dark, cool and warm enough. Use earplugs and an eye mask. Insulate from the ground. Keep light low in the evening. Avoid heavy meals, alcohol and caffeine late in the day.

## Warning signs of fatigue
Yawning, drifting, slow reactions, forgetting simple things, irritability and microsleeps. If you notice them while driving, pull over and rest. Do not push on.

## Support the body
Food, water and gentle movement help. Dehydration and hunger make fatigue worse.
