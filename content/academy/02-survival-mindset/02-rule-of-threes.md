The **rule of threes** is a rough guide to how long a person lasts without the basics. It helps you decide what to do first.

## The rule of threes
- **3 minutes** without air (or in icy water)
- **3 hours** without shelter in harsh weather (very hot or very cold)
- **3 days** without water
- **3 weeks** without food
- **3 months** without hope or company is not a rule, but morale decides how people cope. It is the unspoken fourth item.

These are guides, not promises. A cold, wet, windy night can kill in much less than three hours, and a hot day can kill in less than three days without water.

## Priorities in order
1. **Safety and air:** get out of immediate danger. Check breathing. Stop severe bleeding (the first treatment priority).
2. **Shelter and warmth:** protect yourself from wind, rain, sun, cold and heat. This is usually the first survival task outdoors.
3. **Water:** find and treat it. Dehydration weakens judgment fast.
4. **Signal and contact:** let people find you or know you are safe.
5. **Fire:** it gives warmth, light, signaling, water treatment and morale.
6. **Food:** needed, but you can wait longer than you think.
7. **Rest, morale and plan.**

The order changes with the situation. In a cold wet storm, shelter and fire come first. In a desert, shade and water do. At home in a power outage, water, warmth and light come first. Adapt.

## Weigh the risks
- **What is the most likely thing to hurt me in the next hour?** Deal with that first.
- **What is the most severe thing that could happen?** Avoid it.
- **What can I fix right now?**

## Do not waste energy
- **Move slowly,** and rest before you are tired.
- **Do not sweat** in the cold. Remove layers before you heat up and put them back on when you rest.
- **Do not skip water** to save it. Drink when thirsty.
- **Do not take risks** you do not need, such as crossing a river or climbing in poor light.

## Prioritize in a group
- **Assign tasks:** shelter, water, fire, first aid and signaling.
- **Look after the weakest first.**
- **Use everyone's skills.**
- **Share the load fairly.**

## Review as you go
Every hour or two, stop and ask: "What is working? What is not? What is next?"

> Air, warmth, water, signal, food. In that order, unless the situation says otherwise.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
