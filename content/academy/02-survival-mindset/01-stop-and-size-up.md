The first minutes decide whether a bad situation stays manageable or becomes a disaster. The most important tool is a calm mind.

## STOP
When something goes wrong, remember the word **STOP:**
- **S: Stop.** Do not rush. Do not run. Sit down if you need to. Stop moving unless you are in immediate danger.
- **T: Think.** Take slow breaths. What happened? What is the danger right now? What do you have?
- **O: Observe.** Look around. Check yourself, your companions and the area. Check your gear and supplies. Note the weather and how much daylight is left.
- **P: Plan.** Decide what to do first, then second. Act on the plan. Revise it as things change.

Many people who died in survival situations made things worse by panicking, moving fast and making poor choices in the first hour.

## Size up the situation
Ask five questions:
1. **Hazards:** what could hurt me right now? (Fire, water, traffic, weather, falling objects, people)
2. **Injuries:** is anyone hurt? Treat life threats first.
3. **Resources:** what do I have? (People, gear, food, water, a phone, a vehicle)
4. **Surroundings:** where am I? What is the terrain, the weather and the time?
5. **Options:** stay, go, signal, or shelter?

## The survival acronym from the Army
The Army's survival manual uses the word **SURVIVAL:**
- **S**ize up the situation
- **U**se all your senses
- **R**emember where you are
- **V**anquish fear and panic
- **I**mprovise
- **V**alue living
- **A**ct like the natives (use what local people do to adapt)
- **L**ive by your wits, but for now, **L**earn basic skills

You do not need to memorize it. The idea: calm first, think, use what you have, and keep going.

## Fear is normal
Fear is not weakness. It is the body preparing to act. The goal is to **use** it, not let it take over. Slow breathing, a simple task and a clear next step bring it down.

## Box breathing
Breathe in for four counts, hold for four, out for four, hold for four. Repeat four times. It slows the heart and clears the mind.

## First small win
Do one small useful thing right away: put on a layer, drink water, mark where you are. Action that helps builds confidence.

> Stop. Think. Observe. Plan. A calm five minutes can save the next five days.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
