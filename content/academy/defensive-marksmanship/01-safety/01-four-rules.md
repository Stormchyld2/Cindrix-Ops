Every firearm accident is a broken rule. These four are the base of all training.

1. **Treat every firearm as if it is loaded.**
2. **Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy.**
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on the target and you have decided to shoot.**
4. **Be sure of your target, what is around it and what is behind it.**

## In practice
- Always check the chamber yourself. Do not trust someone's word.
- Keep the muzzle in a safe direction, usually down range, or down and away.
- Rest the finger along the frame.
- Know what a bullet can pass through. Walls, doors and vehicles do not stop most rounds.

## Never rely on one rule
Use all four, all the time. If you break one, the others still protect you.

> Someone who follows the four rules cannot have a negligent accident.

## Check yourself
? What should you assume about every firearm?
+ That it is loaded
- That it is empty
- That the safety is on
- That it is a toy
> Treat it as loaded until you have checked it yourself.
? Where should your finger be until you decide to shoot?
+ Along the frame, off the trigger
- On the trigger
- Under the trigger guard
- On the safety
> The trigger finger touches the trigger only when you shoot.
? What does 'be sure of your target' include?
+ What is around it and behind it
- Only the target's colour
- Only its distance
- Only the lighting
> A bullet can pass through targets.
