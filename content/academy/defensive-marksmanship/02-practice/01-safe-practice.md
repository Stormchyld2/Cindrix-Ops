## Dry practice at home
- Make sure there is **no ammunition in the room** and the firearm is unloaded, checked twice.
- Pick a safe backstop, such as a wall that could stop a bullet.
- Practise stance, grip, drawing, sight picture and a smooth trigger press.
- If you must load, stop, leave the room and reset.

## At the range
- Know the range rules. Listen to the range officer.
- Wear **eye and ear protection**.
- Keep the firearm pointed down range. Keep it unloaded and the action open when not shooting.
- Never shoot at anything but the target.

## Keep notes
Record your groups and what you changed. Progress you can see keeps you honest.

> Good practice is quiet, slow and boring. That is why it works.

## Check yourself
? What must be true before dry practice?
+ No ammunition in the room and the firearm checked unloaded
- Ammunition in your pocket
- A friend loading it
- The safety off
> Dry practice means dry.
? What protection should you wear at the range?
+ Eye and ear protection
- Sunglasses only
- A hat only
- Nothing
> Eyes and ears can be damaged in one shot.
? Why keep notes of your practice?
+ To see what is improving
- To please the range officer
- To count rounds only
- It is not useful
> Records show real progress.
