Physical skills matter, but the mind decides who keeps going. People with modest gear and a strong will often outlast people with the best equipment and no hope.

## What stress does
Under stress your body releases adrenaline. Your heart beats faster, your breathing quickens, your attention narrows, your hands may shake and your memory and fine motor skills can get worse. You may feel afraid, angry, numb or confused. These are normal.

## Common reactions in a survival situation
- **Fear and anxiety.** Use it as energy.
- **Anger and frustration.** Do not direct it at partners.
- **Sadness and guilt.** Grief for loss, and guilt for survival.
- **Boredom and loneliness,** in long waits.
- **Panic.** A sudden urge to run or do something rash.
- **Denial.** "This is not happening." Face the facts, calmly.
- **Fatigue.** Mental and physical, which makes everything worse.

## What you can do
### Control your body first
- **Slow breathing** (box breathing, or in through the nose and out slowly).
- **Name what you feel:** "I am scared." Naming reduces the grip.
- **Move** a little, and stretch.
- **Drink and eat.**
- **Rest** when you can.

### Give your mind a job
- **Make a list** and tick off tasks.
- **Set small goals:** "Shelter by dark." "Water by noon."
- **Keep a routine:** wake times, meals, chores, check-ins.
- **Keep a log or journal.** It organizes thinking.
- **Stay busy,** but not frantic.

### Keep hope
- **Remember why:** the people you love, the things you want to do.
- **Focus on what you can control.**
- **Celebrate small wins:** a fire lit, water found, a good meal.
- **Use humor,** carefully.
- **Think about rescue or recovery.** Most emergencies end.
- **Use faith, prayer or meditation** if they help you.

## In a group
- **Talk.** Share worries and plans.
- **Assign jobs** to everyone, including children.
- **Listen** without judging.
- **Look for signs of trouble:** withdrawal, anger, not eating, refusing to talk.
- **Do not argue** about blame. Fix first, review later.
- **Support leaders and let them rest.**
- **Be kind.** Kindness is practical.

## Children
- **Be honest and calm,** at their level.
- **Keep routines,** such as bedtime stories.
- **Give them tasks** and ways to help.
- **Hold them,** and let them ask questions.
- **Limit** scary news.

## After the event
Stress can last for weeks. Common signs: trouble sleeping, nightmares, irritability, difficulty concentrating, feeling numb. Most ease with time, rest and support. **Seek professional help** if it does not, or if you have thoughts of harming yourself. In the US, call or text **988** (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or the **Disaster Distress Helpline at 1-800-985-5990.**

## Checklist
- Know how to do box breathing
- Have small goals and a routine
- Know how to support others
- Know where to get help afterward

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
