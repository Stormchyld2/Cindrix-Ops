The basics you need to survive when you need to move, and move ASAP. The purpose of this bag is to help you travel away from danger and to a place of safety. It holds 3 days of supplies so you can leave your home immediately and stay safe, mobile, and self-sufficient. Each member of the family has their own bag.

It's taught first because it's the fastest win with the biggest impact. Some simple survival supplies and a means to carry them is all you need to get started.

1. Done beats perfect: Start now, don't wait til the emergency happens
2. Mobility Matters: Keep it to 15–20% of your body weight (175 lb person ≈ 35 lb max). If you can't carry it, you don't need it. Even young children can have a small bag with a few items in it. Parents will obviously carry heavier things for their children.
3. Essentials Only: Water, food, shelter, first aid, communication, basic tools. Would this item make a life-or-death difference? If not, leave it.
4. Redundancy, Redundancy, Redundancy: If it's mission-critical, assume it will fail once. Pack 2–3 ways to make fire and 3 light sources.
5. Sustainability: Carry ways to make more: a water filter, not just water.

## Do it on a budget
Most people already own half of this list. Raid the kitchen and closet first, then check thrift stores and your local Buy Nothing group, then buy in stages ($20–50 a month). Build your own first aid kit instead of buying pre-made.

## Going deeper: filling the bag, item by item
The checklist names sixteen categories. Here is what each one means in practice.
- **Backpack:** a comfortable pack of 30 to 45 liters with a padded hip belt. Fit matters more than brand. Try it on with weight in it.
- **3 days of food:** about 2,000 calories a day that needs no cooking or very little: bars, nut butter packets, tuna pouches, crackers, dried fruit, instant oatmeal. Pick food you like, because morale matters.
- **Warm, waterproof clothing:** a rain shell, a warm layer (fleece or wool), a hat, gloves, and two pairs of wool or synthetic socks. No cotton.
- **3 sources of light:** a headlamp, a small flashlight and a backup or lantern, with spare batteries or a power bank.
- **Fire kit:** a lighter, a ferro rod, waterproof matches and tinder, in a waterproof container.
- **Copies of personal documents:** ID, insurance, medical information and contacts, in a zip bag or on an encrypted drive.
- **Emergency cash:** small bills (see the Financial Strength lesson).
- **Shelter tarp:** a lightweight tarp or emergency bivy, and cord.
- **Water system:** a filter such as a Sawyer, purification tablets, and a bottle or two.
- **Cutting tools:** a knife and a multi-tool.
- **First aid kit:** the Medical course lists what goes in.
- **Personal hygiene:** toothbrush, soap, a small towel, toilet paper, sanitary products.
- **Electronic items:** a power bank, charging cables, a crank or battery radio, and a spare phone battery if possible.
- **Medical necessities:** prescription medicines for at least three days, glasses, and a list of conditions and allergies.
- **Navigation:** a paper map of your area and a compass.
- **Self defense items:** see the Self-Defense course and local laws.

## Weight check
Weigh the loaded bag. If it is over 15 to 20 percent of your body weight, remove the least essential item and weigh again. Walk a mile with it. If it hurts, trim.

## Children and pets
Each child has a small bag with a change of clothes, a favorite comfort item, a snack, a whistle and a card with contact details. Pets need food, water, a leash, bags and vaccination records.

## Maintain it
- **Check the bag** twice a year, when the clocks change. Replace food, batteries and medicine, and swap clothes for the season.
- **Test everything.** Light the stove, run the filter, and charge the power bank.
- **Keep it by the door** or in the car, where you can grab it in seconds.
