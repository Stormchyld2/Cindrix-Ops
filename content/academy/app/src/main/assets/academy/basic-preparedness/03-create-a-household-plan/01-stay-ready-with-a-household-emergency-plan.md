When trouble hits, you need to be able to process it, bounce back, and continue functioning. A written plan will help you by documenting where you will go, how you will regroup, and how you will sustain the household.

Imagine this: wife is at home with the kids, you're far away off at work. Suddenly, internet and cell towers are down. Freeway is jammed. What do you do? What does your family do?

This is why you need A Household Emergency Plan:
- Written plans drive follow-through and better outcomes
- Keeps your household prepared, even if you're not there
- Your contact and support systems are already defined

## In this section, we'll help you build out each of the required plans:
- Emergency Preparedness Binder
- Family Disaster Plan
- Group Contact Plan
- Emergency Out-of-Area Contact Plan
- Family Bug Out Plan

## Going deeper: what a good household plan contains
A plan is only useful if every person in the home can find it and follow it.

### The five parts
1. **Emergency Preparedness Binder:** documents, contacts and the plans below.
2. **Family Disaster Plan:** where to shelter and how to decide between bug in and bug out.
3. **Group Contact Plan:** how your family and your group check in.
4. **Out-of-Area Contact Plan:** one distant relative or friend who relays news.
5. **Family Bug Out Plan:** where you go, how you get there and what you take.

### Questions every plan answers
- **Who?** Every person and pet in the home, and who is responsible for whom.
- **What could happen?** Your top two or three hazards.
- **Where?** Meeting places, shelter spots, destinations and routes.
- **How?** Communication methods and backups (PACE).
- **When?** Triggers for staying or going.
- **With what?** Supplies and where they are.
- **Who helps?** Neighbors, relatives and your group.

### Talk through the scenarios
Sit down with your family and run through these:
- A house fire at night.
- A winter storm with no power for a week.
- A wildfire evacuation order.
- A flood.
- Parents at work and the kids at school when cell service goes down.
- A medical emergency while the roads are closed.
Write down what each person does.

### Include children
Children as young as four can learn their phone number, the meeting place and a simple rule such as "stay with the grown-up." Practice makes it calm.

### Update it
Review the plan every six months and after any major change: a move, a new baby, a new job, a new medical condition.

### Keep it simple
A short plan that is used beats a long one that is not. One page per topic is enough.
