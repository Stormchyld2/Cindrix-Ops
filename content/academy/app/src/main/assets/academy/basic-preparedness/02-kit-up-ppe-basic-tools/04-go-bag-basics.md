In your Go-Bag, also known as a 72-hour bag or a bug-out-bag, you'll need 3 days' worth of supplies for each family member in case you ever need to flee your home.

Start simple. Don't worry about having "top of the line" gear right now - that's the whole point of this being one of your first lessons. If your Go-Bag isn't built yet, focus your energy on getting it done ASAP. You can upgrade to lighter or higher-quality gear later.

Some of you are already familiar with the concept of a Go-Bag. It doesn't sound complicated… but for some reason, it keeps getting put off. Then, in a real moment, you actually need it. That's when a finished Go-Bag becomes the difference between scrambling in panic and moving with control. We emphasize doing this first for one simple reason: emergencies don't give you prep time.

Use the Go-Bag checklist in the first lesson. First, use what you have, then fill the gaps. As you acquire these items, put them in your bag and check them off the list.

## Going deeper: a build plan you can finish this weekend
**Saturday morning (1 hour):** Gather. Walk through the house with the checklist from the first lesson and set aside everything you already own: bag, clothes, flashlight, lighter, bottles, cash, documents.
**Saturday afternoon (1 hour):** Fill the gaps. Make a short shopping list of what is missing. Buy the cheapest items first: tarp, cord, batteries, hygiene items, a few days of food.
**Sunday (1 hour):** Pack and test. Pack in zip bags by category (water and food, shelter and warmth, light and fire, first aid, hygiene, tools, documents). Weigh it. Light the lighter. Turn on every light. Try the filter on a bucket of water.

## Organize by category
Use clear zip bags or pouches, labeled with a marker.
- **Top:** rain shell, hat and gloves.
- **Easy reach:** light, knife, fire kit, snacks, first aid.
- **Middle:** food and water gear.
- **Bottom:** sleeping gear and spare clothes.
- **Outside pockets:** water bottle, map and radio.

## Seasonal swap
Every spring and fall, swap clothes, check food and water dates, and add or remove cold or hot weather items.

## Cars, work and school
- **Work:** keep a small bag or a "get home" kit: walking shoes, water, snacks, a light, a radio, a charger and a jacket.
- **Car:** a smaller kit (see the Mobility course).
- **School and daycare:** a card with contacts.

## When is a Go-Bag useful?
- A house fire, a flood or a wildfire evacuation.
- A gas leak or chemical spill.
- A power outage with a need to move.
- Any short-notice move.

## Keep going
After the bag is done, move on to the plan and supplies. The bag is the beginning, not the end.
