Recovery starts as the response ends. The first days after an event decide how smoothly the rest goes.

## Return only when it is safe
- **Wait for the all-clear** from officials before returning to an evacuated area.
- **Expect hazards:** downed lines, damaged structures, gas leaks, contaminated water, mold, debris, wildlife and unstable ground.
- **Use caution** with roads, bridges and flooded areas.

## Safety checks at home
- **Structure:** look for cracks, sagging, leaning, and damaged foundations. If unsure, have an inspector check before going in.
- **Gas:** if you smell gas, leave, and call from outside. Do not turn lights on or off.
- **Electricity:** if there is water in the house, do not touch electrical items. Turn off power at the main from a dry place if safe, or have the utility do it.
- **Water:** use only safe water until the utility says it is safe.
- **Fire:** check for smoldering or hidden fire.
- **Carbon monoxide:** never run generators or grills indoors.
- **Mold:** it starts in 24 to 48 hours in wet homes. Dry out fast, throw away soaked porous items, and wear a respirator, gloves and boots when cleaning.
- **Hazardous materials:** do not handle chemicals, asbestos or unknown containers. Call authorities.
- **Food:** discard food touched by flood or warmed by outage (see the food course).

## Personal protection while cleaning
Wear sturdy boots, gloves, eye protection and an N95 respirator. Drink water, take breaks, avoid heat exhaustion, and lift with the legs. Keep children and pets away.

## Document everything
Photograph or film damage before cleaning. Keep receipts. Make a list of lost items. Contact insurers, and ask about advances for living expenses.

## Get help
- **FEMA:** register for assistance at disasterassistance.gov or by phone if your area has a declaration.
- **Red Cross, local groups and faith organizations** provide shelter, food and supplies.
- **Beware of scams and unlicensed contractors.** Get written estimates, check licenses and do not pay up front.

## Care for people
Check on neighbors. Watch your own stress. Keep children's routines. Seek counseling if needed. The Disaster Distress Helpline is 1-800-985-5990 in the US.

## Restart essentials
Water, power, food, communications, work, school, banking and medicine. Make a list in order.
