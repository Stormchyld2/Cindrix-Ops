When something goes wrong, the first problem is often confusion. The Incident Command System (ICS) is the standard way that firefighters, police, and emergency managers organize. Its ideas scale down to a household or a small group.

## Principles of ICS
- **Common terms:** plain language, no codes, so everyone understands.
- **Unity of command:** each person has one boss.
- **Chain of command:** a clear line of who reports to whom.
- **Manageable span of control:** three to seven people per leader.
- **Modular organization:** the structure grows or shrinks to fit the event.
- **Management by objectives:** the leader sets clear goals and checks progress.
- **Incident action plan (IAP):** a short written plan for the next period.
- **Resource management:** keep track of who and what you have.
- **Information sharing:** everyone knows what they need to.

## The first minutes: a checklist
1. **Stop and assess.** Is anyone hurt? Is there an immediate danger?
2. **Make it safe.** Move people out of danger.
3. **Take command.** Someone says, "I'm taking charge."
4. **Call for help** (911) if needed.
5. **Account for everyone.** Who is here? Who is missing?
6. **Set priorities:** life safety, then stabilizing the incident, then protecting property.
7. **Assign tasks.** Give each person a clear job.
8. **Brief** everyone in a few sentences.
9. **Keep a log.**

## The sections
Keep it simple: a **commander** and as needed **operations, planning, logistics** and **finance/admin.** One person can fill several. For a family, the commander assigns jobs.

## The briefing: SMEAC
A short briefing format used by militaries and responders:
- **S**ituation: what is happening.
- **M**ission: what we will do.
- **E**xecution: who does what, in what order.
- **A**dmin and logistics: supplies, medical, communications.
- **C**ommand and communications: who leads, how we signal, what to do if we lose contact.
Practice giving one in under two minutes.

## Operational periods
Break the response into periods (an hour, four hours, a day). At the start of each, set objectives and review. At the end, report and hand over.

## Transfer of command
When a more qualified person or official arrives, hand over with a briefing: what happened, what has been done, what is needed.

## Keep control
- Keep calm, speak clearly, and do not assign blame.
- Do not freelance.
- Take care of your people first.
