The cheapest disaster is the one that never happens. Prevention removes the hazard or keeps you out of its way.

## What prevention is
**Prevention** stops an incident from happening. **Avoidance** keeps you from being exposed. **Mitigation** (module four) reduces harm when you cannot avoid the event. The lines blur, but the idea is the same: act early.

## Prevent accidents and fires
- Working smoke and CO alarms, tested monthly.
- Safe heating, cooking and wiring. Check the chimney, furnace and dryer vent each year.
- Safe storage of fuel and chemicals.
- Cord and outlet checks.
- Fire extinguishers and fire-safe habits.
Home fires kill more people than any other disaster in the United States.

## Prevent illness and injury
- Vaccines, hygiene, safe water and food.
- Falls prevention and safe lifting.
- Health checks and medicine supplies.
- Mental health care.

## Avoid hazard zones
- **Do not build or live in the path** of floods, fire, landslides or surge if you have a choice. If you already do, plan to leave early.
- **Keep clear** of dangerous people, places and situations.
- **Do not travel** into storms, floods and fire.
- **Choose where you store** supplies: above flood level, away from wildfire fuel, with ways out.

## Defend against deliberate harm
- Layers of security (see Home and Site Security).
- A low profile.
- Neighbors who know each other.
- Good digital habits.

## Prevent supply failure
- Do not rely on one source: stock supplies, grow some food, store water, keep a second power source.
- Keep cash, fuel and a full tank.
- Keep medicine in reserve.

## Prevent the slow hazards
- **Money:** an emergency fund, low debt, insurance, copies of papers.
- **Skills and income:** more than one way to earn.
- **Relationships:** a network that would help.

## Look for early warning
Prevention depends on seeing trouble coming:
- Weather alerts and radios.
- Local news and officials.
- Community groups.
- Signs in your own home: a damp wall, a smell of gas, a failing appliance.
Make a habit of acting on early warnings.

## Cost and benefit
FEMA studies find that every dollar spent on mitigation saves several dollars in later disaster costs. Prevention is not only safer, it is cheaper.

## Exercise
Walk through your property with the hazard list. For each top hazard, write one thing you can do **this month** to prevent or avoid it.
