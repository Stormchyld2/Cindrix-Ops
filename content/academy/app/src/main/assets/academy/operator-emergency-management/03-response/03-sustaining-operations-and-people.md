Response in a long emergency becomes a marathon. People wear out long before supplies do.

## Operational rhythm
- **Set a daily schedule:** wake-up, sanitation, water, meals, work shifts, security watches, rest, a evening meeting and lights-out.
- **Shift work:** no one stands a watch or works more than they can safely handle. A common pattern is 4 hours on and 8 off, or 2 on and 4 off.
- **Rest is a mission requirement.** Sleep loss cuts judgment, mood and immunity.
- **Rotate jobs** so no one carries the same burden.
- **Handover** each shift: brief the next team on what happened and what to watch.
- **Keep a log.**

## Logistics
- **Count supplies daily or weekly.** Estimate days remaining for water, food, fuel, medicine.
- **Ration early,** while you still have options. Fair rules prevent resentment.
- **Cache and protect.**
- **Plan resupply or substitutes.**

## Care for people
- **Basics first:** water, food, warmth, sleep, hygiene, safety.
- **Check in** daily with each person: how are you?
- **Watch for strain:** irritability, withdrawal, poor sleep, heavy drinking, hopelessness, risky behavior.
- **Psychological first aid:** be calm, listen, offer practical help, connect people, give hope, and avoid pushing people to talk. Most reactions are normal and settle.
- **Children** need routine, simple truth, comfort and chances to play.
- **Elders and the sick** need medicine, dignity, and company.
- **Seek professional help** for thoughts of self-harm. In the US, call or text **988.**

## Morale
Small things matter: hot food, a story, music, prayer, games, humor, a clean shirt, a task that gives purpose. Mark birthdays and milestones. Recognize effort.

## Conflict
Tension rises in crowded, tired groups. Set rules early. Hear complaints fairly and quickly. Separate people if needed. Decide and move on. Do not let small grievances grow.

## Leadership under strain
Be visible, calm and honest. Share what you know, admit what you do not. Share the hard work. Say thanks. Take your own rest so you can lead.

## Hand off to professionals
When responders arrive, brief them and help where asked. Do not resist evacuation orders.

## Watch yourself
Leaders hide stress. Eat, sleep, breathe and ask for help. A burned-out leader is a risk.
