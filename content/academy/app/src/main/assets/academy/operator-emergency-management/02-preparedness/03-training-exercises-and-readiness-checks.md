A plan that has never been tested is a guess. Exercises show what works.

## The training cycle
1. **Assess** your skills against your target capabilities.
2. **Plan** training: topics, who, how often.
3. **Train:** teach and practice the skill.
4. **Evaluate:** can people do it to standard?
5. **Improve:** fix the plan or the skill, and repeat.
FEMA's HSEEP (Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program) uses this cycle.

## Types of exercises
- **Orientation or seminar:** talk about the plan.
- **Tabletop:** talk through a scenario.
- **Drill:** practice one skill, such as a fire exit or a tourniquet.
- **Functional exercise:** act out a response in real time with roles and time pressure.
- **Full-scale exercise:** a realistic, large test, such as a night of living without power, water and the internet.

## Run a good exercise
- **State the objective.** "We will test whether we can leave in 15 minutes."
- **Write a short scenario.** Keep it realistic and a little uncomfortable.
- **Assign an evaluator** who watches and takes notes and does not take part.
- **Set safety rules:** stop words, real emergencies take priority, no real danger.
- **Run it.** Keep it moving and let people make mistakes.
- **Hold a hot wash** immediately: what went well, what did not.
- **Write an after-action report** within a few days: strengths, areas to improve, actions, who and when.

## Standards
Measure what matters: time to leave, time to purify water, time to stop bleeding, time to make contact on the radio, number of tasks completed. Compare with your target. Repeat to see progress.

## Readiness check: a quarterly list
- Plan current and everyone has read it
- Contacts, radios and phones work
- Supplies counted and in date
- Vehicle tanks, tires and kits ready
- Shutoffs and exits clear
- Medicines and documents current
- Next drill scheduled

## Build progressively
Start with a seminar, then a tabletop, then drills, then a functional exercise, then a full-scale one over a weekend. Increase stress slowly.

## Teach others
Training others deepens your own skill. Teach children, neighbors and new members.

## Keep the habit
Put dates on the calendar and treat them as appointments.

## Check yourself
? What is a hot wash?
+ An immediate discussion after an exercise
- A fire
- A kind of laundry
- A cooking method
> Capture lessons while fresh.

? What should an evaluator do?
+ Watch and take notes without taking part
- Lead the drill
- Ignore it
- Fix every error
> Observers see what participants miss.

? What is an after-action report?
- A bill
- A prize
- A map
+ A written review of strengths, gaps and actions
> It turns an exercise into improvement.
