GMRS has no exam. You apply online with the FCC, pay a fee, and wait for the license.

## Before you start
- You must be **18 or older.**
- You need an email address, a mailing address and a way to pay by card.
- You will be asked for a Social Security number or taxpayer number when you register. It is used for FCC records.
- You need to be eligible: a person who is not a representative of a foreign government. Businesses and groups are not eligible for new GMRS licenses today. Individuals are.

## The steps
1. **Register with the FCC.** Go to the FCC's Commission Registration System (CORES) and create a login. You will receive an **FRN** (FCC Registration Number). Keep it safe.
2. **Sign in to the Universal Licensing System (ULS).** Use the FCC's license manager and choose to apply for a new license in the GMRS service.
3. **Fill in the application.** It asks for your name, address and contact details. Check each field.
4. **Pay the application fee.** The fee at the time of writing is **$35,** and covers 10 years. Fees can change, so check the FCC site. There is no exam.
5. **Wait for approval.** It can take from minutes to a few weeks. Check your email and the ULS for your **call sign,** which usually starts with **K** or **W** and has letters and numbers (for example, WQAB123).
6. **Print or save your license.** Keep a copy where your family can find it.
7. **Put the renewal date on your calendar.** You may renew starting 90 days before it expires. After expiry, there is a grace period, but you cannot transmit until renewed.

## Use only the official FCC site
Many websites charge extra to "help" with GMRS licenses. You never need to pay anyone but the FCC. The official fee goes to the FCC through its site. Check the address ends in **.gov.**

## Who is covered
One GMRS license covers the licensee and their **immediate family:** spouse, children, parents, grandparents, grandchildren, siblings, in-laws, and similar relatives who live in the licensee's household or are related as the rules list. Friends and neighbors who are not family need their **own** licenses. If your group has several households, each household needs its own license.

## After you are licensed
- **Identify your station** with your call sign at the end of a transmission or conversation, and every **15 minutes** during a long one. You may use your call sign in plain voice. Family members use the same call sign.
- **Follow the rules for each channel:** power and antenna limits, repeaters and the permitted uses.
- **Keep your information current** in the ULS (address and email changes).
- **Keep records:** your license, your FRN login and your call sign. Write them in the Family page of this program.

## What GMRS allows
- Communication with family members and other GMRS users.
- **Repeaters,** if you have access to one: some are open to anyone with a license, and some are private and need permission.
- Power up to the legal limit, and outside antennas.
- Short data messages on certain channels, within the rules.

## What it does not allow
- **No business or commercial use.** GMRS is for personal and family communication.
- **No music or broadcasting.**
- **No encrypted or secret codes** meant to hide meaning.
- **No use of radios not certified for GMRS** on GMRS channels.
- **No false distress calls.**

## Checklist
- Created an FCC login and noted the FRN
- Applied for a GMRS license on the official FCC site
- Wrote down the call sign and expiry date
- Taught family members how to identify
- Put the renewal date on the calendar

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
