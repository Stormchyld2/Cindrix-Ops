Amateur radio has three license classes. You can start at the bottom and move up as you learn, or go straight to a higher class if you pass the exam.

## Technician
- **Entry level.** The first step for nearly everyone.
- Exam: **35 multiple-choice questions;** you need **26 right** (about 74 percent).
- Covers basic rules, simple electronics, safe operating and how radio waves behave.
- Privileges: **all amateur frequencies above 30 MHz** (including the popular VHF 2-meter and UHF 70-centimeter bands where handhelds and repeaters live), plus some limited **HF** privileges.
- Good for: local and regional communication, repeaters, emergency volunteer groups, and satellite contacts.

## General
- **Intermediate.** The most useful license for long-distance and emergency messages.
- Exam: **35 questions;** you need **26 right.**
- Privileges: **parts of every HF band,** so you can talk across the country and around the world with the right station.
- Good for: emergency traffic between regions when local systems are down.
- You must hold Technician first, or pass that exam as well on the same day.

## Amateur Extra
- **Top class.** The hardest exam.
- Exam: **50 questions;** you need **37 right.**
- Privileges: **every frequency allotted to amateurs.**
- Good for: serious hobbyists and those who want every option.
- You must hold General first, or pass those exams as well.

## No Morse code test
Morse code is no longer required for any license in the US. Many hams enjoy learning it, and it can get through when voice cannot, but it is optional.

## How the exam works
The questions come from a **public pool.** Every question and answer is published, and the pool is replaced on a schedule (roughly every four years). Studying the pool works, because the real exam draws from it. Check which pool is current before you study, since older materials can be out of date.

## Exam fees and the license
- An exam session charges a **small fee** (often around $15 at the time of writing), set by the volunteer examiner group. Some groups run free sessions for students and others.
- After you pass, the FCC issues the license. The FCC has discussed charging an application fee, so check the current rules.
- Licenses last **10 years,** with a two-year grace period to renew after they expire.

## Call signs
Each license has a unique **call sign.** You pick from a list when you apply, or accept the next one in line. "Vanity" call signs are available for a fee.

## What hams do that others cannot
- Use **high power and good antennas.**
- Build and adjust equipment.
- Use **digital modes** and email over radio, such as Winlink.
- Take part in **emergency communications** groups (ARES, RACES and others).
- Contact people around the world, and even astronauts on the space station at times.
- Talk through **repeaters** and **satellites.**

## What hams cannot do
- No business use.
- No encryption meant to hide meaning (some digital modes use codes that are public).
- No broadcasting.
- No false or deceptive signals.
- Identify with your call sign.

## Recommended plan for a group
- **Everyone** in the household who is old enough gets a GMRS license (one license covers the family).
- **At least two people** in the group study for the Technician exam, and one goes on to General.
- Use ham gear for longer range and for relays between groups.

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
