Phones are the first thing most people reach for, and often the first thing to fail.

## What goes wrong with phones
- **Cell towers lose power.** Most towers have batteries that last hours, and some have generators, but long outages outlast them.
- **Networks jam.** After a big event everyone calls at once, and calls fail even where towers still work. Text messages often get through when calls do not.
- **Cables and links break.** Storms, fires, floods and earthquakes cut the fiber and power lines that towers depend on.
- **Home internet and landlines** (including voice-over-internet phones) stop when the power or the modem stops.

## What radio gives you
- **It does not need a tower.** Two handheld radios talk directly to each other, with nothing in between.
- **It runs on batteries,** and you can recharge from a car, a solar panel or a power bank.
- **It reaches your group at once.** One person speaks and everyone listening hears.
- **You own the system.** No monthly bill, and no company can turn it off.

## What radio will not do
- It will not reach 911. Radio is for your group, not for emergency dispatch. Use a phone for that if one works.
- Handhelds have limited range. Trees, hills and buildings shorten it a lot. A few miles is a good result.
- Anyone can listen. Do not say anything on the radio you would not say in a public place.
- It takes practice. A radio nobody has used is not a plan.

## Four jobs for radio in a preparedness group
1. **Checking in** with family and neighbors when phones are down.
2. **Coordinating** a group on an errand, an evacuation or a work party.
3. **Listening** for weather, public safety and community information.
4. **Passing messages** to people farther away, which is where a ham license helps.

## Layers, not one tool
Use more than one way to reach people, the same as the PACE plan in this program: **Primary** (phone), **Alternate** (text or a messaging app), **Contingency** (handheld radio on GMRS) and **Emergency** (ham radio and a runner). See the Communications course.

## Checklist
- Know why phones fail in a disaster
- Know what radio can and cannot do
- Decide which of the four jobs your group needs first

> Rules and fees change. Check fcc.gov, your exam team and the radio's own manual before you rely on any number here.
