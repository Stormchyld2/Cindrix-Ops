> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Most births take hours. Sometimes a baby comes fast, or help is cut off. If it happens, **the body does the work. Your job is to keep things clean, calm and warm.**

## Call for help first
Call emergency services if you can. They will talk you through it by phone.

## Get ready
- Wash hands. Put clean towels or sheets under the mother.
- Have warm towels, a blanket, a hat for the baby, and clean string or ties and clean scissors if you must (see below).
- The mother chooses the most comfortable position. Do not tell her to hold back.

## When the baby comes
1. **Let the baby come on its own.** Do not pull.
2. If the head is out and a loop of cord is around the neck, gently slip it over the head if you can.
3. Support the head and body as they come. The baby will be slippery.
4. Rub the baby gently with a towel, dry it, clear the face. Most babies cry quickly.
5. Place the baby **on the mother's bare chest**, skin to skin, and cover both with a warm blanket. Cover the baby's head.

## After
- **Do not cut the cord in a hurry.** Waiting is safe. If help will not come, tie it in two places and cut between with clean scissors.
- The **placenta** comes out on its own, often within half an hour. Keep it in a bag to show the doctor. Do not pull.
- Gently massage the lower belly to help the womb tighten. Let the baby feed, which also helps.
- **Heavy bleeding** that soaks pads quickly is an emergency: massage, feed the baby, keep her warm and get help.
- If the baby is not breathing, rub firmly, then follow infant rescue breaths as taught in a first aid class.

> Dry, warm and skin to skin is the core of newborn care. Everything else can wait.

## Going deeper: a calm, clear order of events
Most births are normal, and a healthy baby and mother usually need you only to keep things clean, warm and calm. Call 911 first if you can, and stay on the line, because the dispatcher can coach you.

## Prepare
- Clean towels, blankets, a clean sheet, gloves, soap, a plastic sheet, a bulb syringe, string or clamps, scissors, a bowl, sanitary pads and a hat for the baby.
- Wash hands, put on gloves, and make the mother comfortable, in the position she chooses.

## Delivery
1. Let the mother push as her body leads. Do not pull the baby.
2. Support the head as it emerges. Check the neck for a cord, and if there is one, slip it over the head gently.
3. The baby usually turns, and the shoulders follow, one at a time. Support the body and catch the baby, who will be slippery.
4. Wipe the face, and gently clear the mouth and nose with a cloth.
5. Dry the baby, rub the back, and keep the head down a little. Most babies cry quickly.
6. Place the baby **skin to skin** on the mother's chest, cover both with blankets and put a hat on the baby.

## The cord and the afterbirth
You do not need to cut the cord at once. If help is far away, tie it in two places and cut between with clean scissors. The placenta usually comes out within half an hour. Do not pull it. Keep it in a bag to show the medical team.

## Bleeding afterwards
Some bleeding is normal. If the mother bleeds heavily, massage the lower belly firmly, just below the navel, until it feels like a hard ball. Putting the baby to the breast helps. Call 911, keep her warm and watch for shock.

## When it is not normal
Baby not breathing, a foot or the cord comes first, heavy bleeding, or no progress: call 911 and follow the dispatcher. Begin infant rescue breaths and CPR if the baby is not breathing.

## Afterward
Keep both warm, feed the baby when ready, and get both checked by a medical professional as soon as you can.
