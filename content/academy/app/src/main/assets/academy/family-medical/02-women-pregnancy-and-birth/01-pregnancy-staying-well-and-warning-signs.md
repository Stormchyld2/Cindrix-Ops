> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

If someone in your family is pregnant, plan early. Records, supplies and a plan to reach care matter more than anything you do in the moment.

## Keep ready
- Copies of prenatal records and the due date
- Any medicines the doctor prescribed, with a supply for weeks. Take them only as prescribed.
- Prenatal vitamins
- A plan for where the nearest birth help is, by more than one route

## Eating and drinking
- Steady meals with protein, fruit, vegetables and plenty of safe water
- Avoid raw or undercooked meat, eggs and unpasteurised dairy
- No alcohol. Ask the doctor before any new medicine or herbal remedy.

## Warning signs: get help urgently
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Severe belly pain
- A bad headache that will not go, with blurred vision or swelling of the face and hands (could be pre-eclampsia)
- Fever
- Fluid leaking before the due date
- The baby moving much less than usual
- Seizure

> Most pregnancies go well. The aim is to spot trouble early and get to help early.

## Going deeper: staying well in a long emergency
Pregnant women need more fluid, regular food, rest and their usual prenatal care. Before trouble comes, gather her records, a list of her medicines and supplements, her due date and provider's number.

## Eating and drinking
Small, frequent meals, plenty of safe water, and foods with protein, iron and folate help. Avoid raw or undercooked meat, eggs and fish, unpasteurized milk and soft cheeses, and keep to food that is stored safely. Continue prenatal vitamins as her provider has told her to.

## Safety in a disaster
- Avoid heat, long exertion, hazardous fumes, strong chemicals and contaminated water.
- Wear seat belts with the lap belt below the belly.
- Keep doing gentle activity, such as walking.
- Rest on her left side later in pregnancy.
- Look after her mental health. Stress is common, and support helps.

## Warning signs: get urgent help for
Heavy vaginal bleeding, leaking fluid, severe belly pain, a severe headache that will not go, blurred vision or seeing spots, sudden swelling of the face and hands, chest pain, trouble breathing, a fever, a seizure, thoughts of harming herself or the baby, or the baby moving much less than usual. The CDC's "Hear Her" campaign says: listen to a pregnant or recently pregnant woman who says something feels wrong.

## After birth, too
Warning signs continue for weeks after delivery: heavy bleeding, fever, severe headaches, leg swelling, chest pain, and feeling hopeless. Seek help.

## Plan
Know your nearest hospital with maternity care and two routes. Tell your doctor about your emergency plan, and ask what to do if you cannot reach them. If she is near her due date, consider staying near care.
