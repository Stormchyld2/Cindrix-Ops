> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## Dehydration in older adults
Thirst fades with age. Offer a drink every hour or two. Dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness and sudden confusion are warning signs.

## Sudden confusion
A sudden change in thinking is **a medical warning**, not just old age. Common causes: dehydration, infection (often urine), a fall, a medicine problem, low blood sugar. Check safety, give a drink if they can swallow, take their temperature, look for pain and get help.

## Skin and pressure sores
People who sit or lie for long stretches can get pressure sores.
- Change position at least every two hours
- Keep skin clean and dry, and look at heels, hips, tail bone and elbows every day
- Red skin that does not fade when pressed needs relief from pressure at once

## Dignity and company
- Ask before helping. Explain what you are about to do.
- Keep routines, light and contact. Loneliness harms health.
- For people with dementia: keep it calm and simple, same place, same schedule, a name tag or ID bracelet in case of wandering.

## Looking after the carer
Carers burn out. Share the work. Sleep, eat, and ask for help. A tired carer makes mistakes.

> Sudden confusion is never "just age". Look for the cause.

## Going deeper: the quiet dangers
Older adults often drink less, feel thirst less and dehydrate faster. Heat, illness and some medicines raise the risk.

## Dehydration signs
A dry mouth, dark urine or little urine, dizziness, tiredness, headache, constipation and confusion. Offer drinks often, soups, and water-rich foods. Do not wait for thirst. Seek help if the person is very drowsy, confused or cannot drink.

## Sudden confusion is an emergency sign
Sudden confusion (delirium) can come from infection (especially urinary or chest), dehydration, low blood sugar, pain, new medicines, a head knock, a stroke or cold and heat. It is different from slow memory loss. Check temperature, fluids, medicines, pain and the bladder, and get medical help promptly. Keep the person calm, in familiar surroundings with light, glasses and hearing aids.

## Skin and pressure sores
People who stay in bed or a chair can develop sores in hours to days over bony areas such as the tailbone, heels and hips.
- Change position at least every two hours.
- Keep skin clean and dry, and check it daily.
- Use pillows or padding to protect bony places and keep heels off the bed.
- Keep bedding smooth and dry.
- Report red patches that do not fade, blisters or open sores.

## Dignity and company
Keep a routine, speak to the person directly, explain what you are doing and keep them involved. Isolation and boredom worsen confusion and low mood.

## Looking after the carer
Caring is tiring. Rest, eat, ask for help, share jobs, take breaks and keep your own health appointments. Watch for burnout, irritability and low mood. A cared-for person is safer when the carer is well.

## Be ready to move
Plan how to move a person with limited mobility, including a wheelchair, a transfer method and a place that can take them. Tell neighbors and local officials, who may keep a list of people who need help.
