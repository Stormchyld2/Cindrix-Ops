> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

Falls are the leading cause of injury in older people. A broken hip can be life-changing, and in a crisis it is worse.

## Make the home safer
- Clear walkways of cords, rugs and clutter
- Good lighting, especially at night, with a torch by the bed
- Non-slip mats, grab bars in the bathroom
- Sturdy shoes with grip, not slippers or socks

## Stay strong
- Walk daily and do gentle balance and leg-strengthening exercises
- Stand up slowly from lying or sitting. A dizzy spell can cause a fall.
- Keep eating and drinking. Weakness makes falls likelier.

## If someone falls
- Ask them not to jump up. Check for pain, bleeding and head injury first.
- If they hit their head, are confused, very sleepy or vomit: get help.
- If the hip, back or neck hurts badly, or the leg looks twisted: **do not move them**. Keep them warm and get help.
- If unhurt, help them get up slowly: roll to the side, onto hands and knees, to a sturdy chair.

> Prevention beats treatment. Fifteen minutes of walking a day is a real medicine.

## Going deeper: why falls matter
One in four adults over 65 falls each year, and falls are a leading cause of injury and death in older adults. In a disaster, a fall can end independence, so prevention is a core part of the plan.

## Why people fall
Weak legs, poor balance, bad vision, low blood pressure on standing, some medicines, dehydration, poor lighting, loose rugs, clutter, bad footwear and new surroundings.

## Prevent
- Remove rugs and cords, fix loose steps, and clear paths.
- Add lighting, night-lights and flashlights by the bed.
- Install grab bars and handrails, and use non-slip mats.
- Keep needed items within easy reach.
- Wear sturdy shoes with non-slip soles. No socks alone on slick floors.
- Check eyesight and hearing yearly.
- Review medicines with a doctor or pharmacist for those that cause dizziness.
- Stand up slowly and sit a moment first.
- Do balance and strength exercises: sit-to-stand, heel raises, gentle walking, tai chi. The CDC STEADI program has simple exercises.

## If someone falls
1. Stay calm. Do not rush to lift them.
2. Ask if they are hurt. Look for bleeding, a deformed limb, head injury, severe pain and confusion.
3. If they are unhurt, help them roll to the side, to hands and knees, up to a chair and then to stand.
4. If they have a head injury, severe pain, a suspected hip or spine injury, or cannot move, do not move them. Keep them warm and call 911.
5. Keep watching for delayed signs after a head knock, especially if they take blood thinners.

## Plan for being alone
Keep a phone, whistle or alert button within reach, agree on a daily check-in, and teach them how to get up from a fall.
