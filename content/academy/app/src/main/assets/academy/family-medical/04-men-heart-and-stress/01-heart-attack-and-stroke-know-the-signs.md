> Basic awareness only. It does not replace a doctor or a hands-on class. Call emergency services first whenever you can. This course never gives medicine amounts: for those, read the package or ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## Heart attack signs
- Pressure, squeezing or tightness in the chest, lasting more than a few minutes or coming and going
- Pain or discomfort spreading to the arm, back, neck or jaw
- Shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, light-headedness
- In women, older people and people with diabetes: tiredness, breathlessness or stomach discomfort with no chest pain

**Call emergency services at once. Do not drive yourself.** Sit them down, loosen tight clothing. The operator can tell you whether to chew an aspirin. They will say the amount.

If they collapse and are not breathing normally: start CPR (hard, fast chest compressions in the centre of the chest) and use an AED if one is available.

## Stroke: BE-FAST
- **B** Balance lost
- **E** Eyes: sudden vision loss
- **F** Face drooping
- **A** Arm weakness
- **S** Speech slurred
- **T** Time to call emergency services

Note the **time** it began. Treatment works only in the first hours. Do not give food, drink or medicine.

## Lower the risk
- Do not smoke
- Walk, move, keep weight healthy
- Know your blood pressure and keep taking prescribed medicine
- Eat less salt and processed food

> With heart attack and stroke, minutes matter. Do not wait to see if it passes.

## Going deeper: act within minutes
For heart attack and stroke, every minute of delay costs heart or brain tissue. Calling 911 is the right first move, because ambulance crews can begin treatment and warn the hospital.

## Heart attack signs
Pressure, tightness or squeezing in the chest, pain spreading to the arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or back, shortness of breath, a cold sweat, nausea, light-headedness or sudden unusual tiredness. Women, older adults and people with diabetes may have milder or different signs, such as breathlessness, nausea or back pain without chest pain.
- Call 911. Do not drive yourself.
- Sit the person down and keep them calm and still.
- If a clinician has told them to take aspirin for the heart, or has prescribed medicine for chest pain, follow that plan and what the dispatcher says.
- If the person collapses and does not breathe normally, start CPR and use an AED.

## Stroke: BE-FAST
**B**alance loss, **E**yes (sudden vision change), **F**ace drooping, **A**rm weakness, **S**peech trouble, **T**ime: call 911 at once. Note when symptoms began, because treatment depends on that time. Do not give food, drink or medicine. Lay the person safely with the head slightly raised.

## Lower the risk
- Do not smoke, and get help to quit.
- Know your blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar, and treat them.
- Be active, keep a healthy weight, eat plenty of vegetables, beans, whole grains and fish, and cut salt and heavily processed food.
- Limit alcohol, manage stress and get enough sleep.
- Take prescribed medicines every day.

## In your plan
Keep an AED where possible, learn CPR, keep medicine lists, and know the route to the nearest hospital with cardiac care.
