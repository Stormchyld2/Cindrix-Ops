Crises wear people down. Men often hide stress behind anger, drinking or silence.

## Signs of strain
- Not sleeping, or sleeping too much
- Short temper, withdrawing, losing interest
- Drinking more
- Constant worry, a racing heart, feeling numb
- Feeling hopeless

## What helps
- **Routine:** wake, eat and sleep at set times
- **Move:** a walk or work with your hands
- **Talk:** one honest conversation with someone you trust
- **Purpose:** a job to do for the family
- Cut down on alcohol. It worsens sleep and mood.
- Limit news if it makes you tense.

## When to get help
If low mood, hopelessness or anger last more than a couple of weeks, or you cannot do daily tasks, speak to a doctor or counsellor.

**If you or someone you know is thinking of suicide, call or text 988 in the United States, any time.** If there is immediate danger, call emergency services. Stay with the person, listen without judging, and remove things they could use to hurt themselves.

> Asking for help is the strong move.

## Going deeper: stress is a health problem
Long stress raises blood pressure, weakens sleep and can lead to anxiety, depression and substance misuse. In a long crisis, mental health is as important as a first aid kit.

## Signs of strain
Irritability or anger, withdrawing, trouble sleeping, being on edge, difficulty concentrating, drinking more, changes in appetite, loss of interest, hopelessness, or aches without a clear cause.

## What helps
- **Routine:** regular meals, waking and sleeping times, jobs and breaks.
- **Sleep:** aim for a regular schedule, a dark, quiet space and avoid screens and caffeine late in the day. Share night watches so everyone gets rest.
- **Movement:** walking and exercise ease tension.
- **Connection:** talk with family and friends. Listen without fixing.
- **Purpose:** useful tasks give a sense of control.
- **Breathing and grounding:** slow breathing, noticing five things you can see, prayer or quiet time.
- **Limit news and rumors,** and use trusted sources at set times.
- **Avoid alcohol and drugs** as a way to cope.

## When to get help
Seek help from a doctor, counselor, faith leader or crisis line if low mood or anxiety lasts more than a couple of weeks, if you cannot function, if you use more alcohol or drugs, or if you have thoughts of harming yourself or others. In the US, call or text **988** for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, any time. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

## Check on each other
Men often hide distress. Ask directly and calmly: "How are you really doing?" If someone talks about suicide, stay with them, take it seriously, remove means of harm if you can safely do so and get help.

## Children
Children show stress through clinginess, tantrums, bad dreams and stomach aches. Keep routines, give simple facts, hugs and time to play.
