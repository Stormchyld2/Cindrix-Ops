Outdoors, exposure is the biggest early threat. A shelter keeps off wind, rain, snow and sun, and holds in heat.

## Choose the site
- **Safe from hazards:** avoid dead trees or heavy branches overhead ("widow-makers"), the bottoms of gullies and dry stream beds (flash floods), the base of steep slopes (rock fall and avalanche) and areas of lightning risk such as hilltops.
- **Near resources,** but not too near: close to water, firewood and building materials, but at least 200 feet from water so you do not disturb wildlife and are above flood level.
- **Drainage:** pick flat, slightly raised ground.
- **Wind:** use natural windbreaks, such as a rock face, or dense trees.
- **Sun:** shade in heat, and morning sun in cold.
- **Visible for rescue,** if you are waiting to be found.
- **Insects and animals:** avoid ant nests, game trails and swampy ground.

## Principles
1. **Insulate from the ground.** The ground steals heat faster than the air. Use a thick bed of leaves, grass, evergreen boughs, a pad or a pack.
2. **Make it small.** A snug space is easier to warm.
3. **Block wind and rain.** Overlap layers like shingles.
4. **Keep it dry.** Make the roof slope.
5. **Keep a way to vent** if you use fire or a stove inside, and never run one in a closed shelter.
6. **Do it before dark,** and before you are exhausted.

## Quick shelters
### A tarp or poncho
- **A-frame:** tie a ridgeline between two trees, drape the tarp over it, stake or weigh the sides.
- **Lean-to:** tie one edge high and stake the other to the ground, facing away from the wind.
### Debris hut (no gear)
1. Make a **frame:** a ridge pole leaned on a stump or tree, with ribs along both sides.
2. **Pile** leaves, grass, bark or branches at least **2 to 3 feet thick** on the frame.
3. **Fill** the inside with dry leaves for a mattress and insulation.
4. **Make the entrance small** and plug it with a pack or a pile of leaves.
### Lean-to
A sloped roof of branches, covered with leaves, with a fire in front (reflector wall behind the fire) if conditions are safe.
### Snow shelters
- **Snow trench or snow cave:** dig into a drift, keep the entrance below the sleeping platform, make a vent hole, and keep a shovel inside. Do not seal yourself in without ventilation.
- **Tree well:** use with caution. Snow around tree trunks can trap people.

## In a car
If you are stranded in a vehicle in winter, **stay with it,** run the engine about 10 minutes each hour with the tailpipe clear of snow and a window cracked, and keep warm with blankets. See the Mobility course.

## In a building
A damaged building may be unsafe. If it is sound, use an interior room, and be careful with heat and gas.

## Care of your shelter
- **Check the roof** and ties after rain or wind.
- **Keep the floor dry.**
- **Keep gear inside.**
- **Keep it clean,** so no food attracts animals.
- **Leave no trace** when you leave.

> Insulate from the ground, block the wind, keep it small and dry, and build before dark.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Practice skills close to home before you depend on them.
