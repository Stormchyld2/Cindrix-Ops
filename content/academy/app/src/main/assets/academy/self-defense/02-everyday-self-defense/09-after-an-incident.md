What you do in the first minutes and hours after an incident matters. The danger may not be over, you or others may be hurt, and the police will want to understand what happened. Know the steps before you need them.

## Step 1: Get safe
1. **Move away** from the threat and to a safe place if you can.
2. **Check that the threat has stopped.** Keep watching your surroundings.
3. **Keep your hands visible** if police arrive, and follow their commands.

## Step 2: Call for help
1. **Call emergency services.** Give your location, say that you need police and, if needed, an ambulance.
2. **Give a short, clear description:** who you are, where you are, what happened in one sentence, and whether anyone is hurt.
3. **Describe yourself** so police can recognize you (clothing, where you are standing) and tell them if you are holding a weapon.
4. **Stay on the line** and follow instructions.

## Step 3: Treat injuries
- Check yourself and others for injuries. Adrenaline can hide pain.
- Control severe bleeding at once with direct pressure, packing and a tourniquet if you are trained.
- Keep injured people warm and still, and call for an ambulance.
- Start CPR if someone is not breathing and you are trained.
- See the first aid and casualty care courses.

## Step 4: Protect the scene and yourself
- **Do not touch** or move items unless needed for safety or medical care.
- **Do not clean up.**
- **Stay at the scene** unless you need to leave to be safe or get help.
- **Do not talk to others** about what happened beyond the basics.

## Step 5: Talking to police
- **Be calm, polite and brief.** Stay in control of your speech.
- **Say the essentials:** that you were attacked, that you feared for your life, and that you will cooperate.
- **Point out evidence and witnesses.**
- **Do not give a long statement while you are shaken, hurt or exhausted.** It is reasonable to say, "I want to cooperate fully. I need to speak to a lawyer first. I will give a full statement once I have." Many legal experts advise this after any serious incident.
- **Do not lie, guess or exaggerate.** Say only what you know.

## Step 6: Get legal help
- **Call a lawyer** as soon as you can, ideally one who knows self-defense law. Save a name and number before you need it.
- **Notify your insurance** or self-defense legal protection provider if you have one.
- **Write down your account** as soon as you can, and keep it private for your lawyer.
- **Expect an investigation.** It is normal after a use of force.

## Step 7: Look after your health and mind
- Rest, eat and drink.
- **Expect strong reactions.** Shaking, nightmares, anger, sadness, guilt, trouble sleeping and numbness are common after a frightening event.
- **Talk to someone you trust.** A friend, family member, counselor or chaplain.
- **See a doctor** for injuries and for stress that lasts.
- **Seek professional help** if symptoms last more than a few weeks or get worse. Many people recover well with support.
- In the United States, call or text **988** if you feel you may hurt yourself.

## Step 8: Look after your family
- Tell children what happened in simple, honest, age-appropriate terms.
- Keep routines as normal as you can.
- Watch for changes in behavior and get help if you need it.

## Step 9: Learn from it
- Hold an after-action review with your family or group when things are calmer.
- Fix the gaps in your security, training and plans.

## Make it ready now
- Save emergency numbers and a lawyer's number in your phone and on paper.
- Know your insurance details.
- Talk through these steps with your household.
- Take a first aid and trauma course.

## Checklist
- Write down the steps above on a card and store it with your emergency documents.
- Find and save the contact of a lawyer who handles self-defense.
- Review your insurance.
- Take a trauma first aid course.
- Know who you would call for emotional support.

> After an incident: get safe, call for help, treat injuries, be brief with police, get a lawyer and look after your mind.
