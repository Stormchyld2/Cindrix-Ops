Once you learn the basics, you must train regularly to improve your skill. **Skill fades quickly without use.** Confidence comes from repetition, not just knowledge. The goal is to perform under stress, not just to understand what to do.

You do not rise to the occasion. **You fall back on your training.**

## Principles of good training
1. **Train regularly.** A little practice, done consistently, beats occasional intense sessions. Fifteen minutes a week beats one long day a year.
2. **Start slowly, then speed up.** Learn the movement correctly at a slow pace, then add speed. Speed with errors builds bad habits.
3. **Keep it simple.** Pick a few skills and master them.
4. **Practice under mild stress.** Add time limits, noise or fatigue a little at a time.
5. **Get feedback.** An instructor, a partner or a video can show you what you cannot see yourself.
6. **Train for failure.** Practice what to do when the tool fails, you are hurt, or the plan goes wrong.
7. **Rest and recover.** Too much training without rest leads to injury.

## Find the right training
Everyone starts somewhere. Look for reputable instructors and beginner-friendly courses. Choose a **structured progression**, not just one-off classes.

- **Awareness and de-escalation.** Community self-defense and personal safety classes.
- **Empty-hand skills.** A reputable martial arts or self-defense school with realistic training and good instructors. Visit a class first.
- **Firearm training.** Certified courses, such as those offered through the NRA or other qualified instructors, starting with safety and fundamentals. Add courses in use of force and law.
- **First aid and trauma care.** American Red Cross, a Stop the Bleed class, CPR and CERT.
- **Medical, navigation, communication and survival skills** through the Academy.

There are groups and classes for women only, for beginners, for older adults and for those with disabilities. Choose what suits you.

## Where to get reps in a safe environment
- Shooting ranges in most cities, with clubs and practical events.
- Martial arts schools and community centers.
- Local CERT programs, search and rescue volunteers and preparedness groups.
- Your own family or group, running drills together.

## Make a training plan
1. **List your priority skills:** for example, first aid, radio, fitness and defensive skills.
2. **Pick one or two** to focus on for the next three months.
3. **Schedule them** on the Calendar with a fixed time.
4. **Track your progress** in a log.
5. **Review** each quarter and adjust.

## Sample monthly plan
- **Week 1:** Fitness test and a first aid skill drill.
- **Week 2:** Radio check and a home safety drill.
- **Week 3:** A class or range session.
- **Week 4:** Family scenario drill and after-action review.

## Avoid
- Training without proper instruction in tools that can cause harm.
- Copying techniques from videos without qualified supervision.
- Overconfidence after one class.
- Training only the part that is fun.

## Checklist
- Choose a first class: self-defense, first aid or firearm safety.
- Put a monthly training slot on the Calendar.
- Start a training log.
- Find a training partner.
- Review progress every quarter.
