This lesson is for households that own or plan to own firearms. If you do not, you still need to know the rules, because you may be around firearms at a relative's home, at a range or in an emergency. Firearms are tools with serious consequences. **Safe handling and safe storage are non-negotiable.**

## The four rules of firearm safety
These rules, first written by Colonel Jeff Cooper, are taught almost everywhere. Follow them **every time.**

1. **Treat every firearm as if it is loaded.** Always. Check it each time you pick it up, and never rely on someone else saying it is unloaded.
2. **Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy.** Keep the muzzle in a safe direction, which usually means down or at a backstop.
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on the target and you have decided to shoot.** Rest your finger along the frame.
4. **Be sure of your target, and what is beyond it.** Bullets pass through walls and travel far. Know who is behind the target.

The National Rifle Association uses a similar set of rules: always keep the gun pointed in a safe direction, keep your finger off the trigger until ready to shoot, and keep the gun unloaded until ready to use.

## Safe storage
Most firearm accidents, thefts and suicides in the home could be prevented by safe storage. The safe choice is a firearm that **cannot be reached by anyone who should not have it.**

Good storage methods:

- **A gun safe or lockbox** with a good lock, bolted down if possible.
- **A trigger lock or cable lock** on every firearm not in a safe.
- **Store ammunition separately,** locked away.
- **Keep keys and codes secure.** Do not leave them where children can find them. Teenagers can find almost anything.
- **A quick-access safe** (biometric or keypad) can balance readiness and safety for a home-defense firearm. Practice opening it.
- **Never leave a loaded firearm unattended** and unsecured.

Where there are children, teenagers, visitors, or anyone at risk of harming themselves, store firearms **locked and unloaded,** with ammunition locked separately. Many states have laws about storage and child access, so learn yours.

## Talk to children
- Teach young children: **"Stop. Don't touch. Run away. Tell a grown-up."**
- Teach older children the four safety rules and take them to a class.
- Do not rely on children to obey. Rely on locks.

## At the range
- Follow the range rules and listen to the range officers.
- Wear eye and ear protection.
- Keep the firearm pointed downrange.
- Do not handle firearms when others are downrange.
- Use the right ammunition for your firearm.

## Handling and cleaning
- Clean in a well-ventilated space, with the firearm **unloaded** and the ammunition **in another room.**
- Check it twice.
- Use the proper tools and solvents.
- Inspect it for wear and damage.

## Transporting a firearm
- Know the law for transport in your state and any state you travel through.
- Unload, and lock it in a case, with the ammunition locked separately.
- Do not leave it in an unattended vehicle.

## If a firearm is found
If a child finds a firearm, tell them to stop, not touch, and leave the area, then call an adult. If you find a firearm, do not pick it up. Call the police.

## Training
Take a certified safety course. Learn the law of self-defense. Practice at a range regularly. Take a first aid and trauma course so you can treat an injury if one happens.

## Mental health and safety
Firearms and suicide are closely linked. If someone in your household is in crisis, store firearms away from the home or locked where the person cannot reach them, and call or text **988** (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline in the United States).

## Checklist
- Learn and teach the four rules.
- Store every firearm locked, with ammunition locked separately.
- Teach children the rules and rely on locks.
- Take a certified safety class.
- Learn the laws on storage, transport and carrying.
- Keep a first aid kit and trauma training.

> Safe storage and safe handling are not optional. Every time. Every gun.
