There are many ways to defend yourself, and no single one is enough. A sound approach is **layered**:

- With the right **mindset and awareness**, you can **avoid** the problem.
- With **empty-hand skills**, you can handle **close contact**. This is generally a last resort.
- With the proper **tools**, you can **extend** your ability and increase your effectiveness.

**Relying on only one is a mistake.** Tools can fail, be forgotten or be taken. Skills take time to develop and may not be enough alone.

## Tools
Tools (weapons and equipment) are external items you carry to increase your ability to stop a threat. Owning a tool without understanding it can give you a **false sense of security.**

To be effective, you must know:

- **What your tools can and cannot do.**
- **Their effective range** and limits.
- **When and how to use them safely,** and when not to.
- **The law.**

Examples of limits:

- Pepper spray can fail in wind and needs good aim.
- Stun devices have a limited range and can fail.
- Firearms demand strict safety handling and awareness of the target and beyond.
- An alarm that is not heard does nothing.

The goal is not possession. It is **competence.** That takes time and practice.

## Empty-hand skills
In a real, stressful encounter, everything you think you know can disappear. Only a lot of practice builds the confidence and the reflexes to use empty-hand skills when it counts.

The goal of self-defense is to **avoid** a fight. There are postures, movements and stances that help you do that:

- **A neutral, stable stance,** feet shoulder width apart, hands up and open.
- **Distance.** Keep more than an arm's length.
- **A calm voice** and a firm "stop."
- **Moving off the line,** stepping to the side so you are not in the direct path.
- **Footwork** to get away.

As a last resort, you can learn basic movements to **escape a grip** or **break contact,** such as:

- Striking to a vulnerable area (eyes, nose, groin, knee) only to create an opening.
- Breaking a wrist grab by rotating against the thumb.
- Getting up from the ground and protecting the head.
- Moving to safety and calling for help.

Your goal is not to fight. **Your goal is to avoid, create distance and get to safety.**

## Learn from qualified teachers
Look for instructors with real experience and a good safety record. One of our preferred partners, Mike Agoff, worked in law enforcement for decades. He trained the San Francisco Police Department in self-defense and de-escalation, and holds black belts in several martial arts including hapkido and Japanese jujitsu. Instructors like him can show you what books cannot.

When you choose a school or instructor:

1. **Visit a class** and watch.
2. **Ask about experience** and how they teach beginners.
3. **Look for realistic training** in which partners resist a little and the instructor corrects form.
4. **Avoid** schools that promise instant results or encourage aggression.

## Practice the basics
1. **Stance and movement.** Five minutes a day.
2. **Verbal boundary and disengagement.** With a partner.
3. **Escapes from grabs.** Slowly and carefully, with a trained partner.
4. **Fall and get-up.** Learn to fall safely and stand up quickly.
5. **Breathing.** Box breathing.

## Checklist
- Learn basic self-defense from a qualified instructor.
- Practice a neutral stance and verbal boundary with a partner.
- Take a firearm safety course near you if you own or plan to own one.
- Learn what each of your tools can and cannot do.
- Train regularly and keep a log.

> Your goal is not to fight. It is to avoid, create distance and get to safety.
