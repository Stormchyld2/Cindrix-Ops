Most of your travel is in a car, on foot or on public transport. Each has its own risks. The same principles apply: awareness, preparation and leaving early.

## Before you drive
- **Keep the tank at least half full.** It gives you range to leave and fuel to wait out a closed road.
- **Keep the car in good order.** Tires, oil, brakes, lights and wipers.
- **Carry a vehicle kit.** Water, food, a blanket, a first aid kit, a flashlight, jumper cables, a tire repair kit, a paper map, a charged power bank, a radio and a whistle.
- **Plan your route** and tell someone when you will arrive.
- **Check the back seat** before you get in.

## In the car
- **Keep doors locked** and windows up, particularly in slow traffic.
- **Leave room** at stops: you should see the tires of the car in front where they touch the road. That gives you space to drive around.
- **Keep valuables out of sight.**
- **Stay aware at lights** and in traffic. Look at mirrors.
- **If you think you are followed,** do not go home. Drive to a busy, lit place such as a police station, fire station or open shop, and call for help.
- **Never get out** if someone is acting aggressively toward your car. Stay locked in, and drive away if you can.

## Parking
- Park in well-lit, busy areas.
- Have your keys out before you reach the car.
- Look around and under the car.
- Back into spaces when you can, so you can leave quickly.
- Do not sit in a parked car on your phone with the doors unlocked.

## Carjacking
A carjacking is a violent threat. The guidance from law enforcement is the same in nearly every case:

1. **Your life is worth more than the car.** Give up the vehicle if confronted.
2. **Do not argue or resist.**
3. **Hand over keys** and move away from the car.
4. **If a child is in the car,** say it clearly and calmly so you can get the child out.
5. **Remember details** (descriptions, direction) and **call emergency services** once you are safe.

## Flat tires and breakdowns
- Pull over safely, to the right and away from traffic.
- Turn on your hazard lights.
- Stay in the car with the doors locked if you do not feel safe, and call for help.
- Only accept help from marked official vehicles, or call for help yourself.
- Keep a **high-visibility vest** and **reflective triangles** in the car.

## On foot and on transit
- Walk facing traffic and use well-lit streets.
- Keep one ear free of headphones.
- Do not display valuables.
- On transit, sit near the driver or in a busy car. Know where the emergency button is.
- Wait for transport in well-lit, busy places.
- If someone makes you feel unsafe, move away, change cars or tell the driver.

## Ride shares and taxis
- Check the car, the plate and the driver match your app before you get in.
- Share the trip with a trusted person.
- Sit behind the passenger seat if you are alone.

## Traveling away from home
- Learn the exits and the layout of your hotel.
- Do not announce your room number.
- Use the door latch and a door wedge alarm.
- Learn about local risks, scams and emergency numbers.
- Carry a copy of your documents separate from the originals.
- Tell family about your plans and check in on schedule.

## Checklist
- Fill and check the vehicle kit.
- Keep the tank at least half full.
- Add a high-visibility vest and reflective triangles.
- Practice checking the back seat and looking around the car.
- Decide in advance: if followed, I drive to a police or fire station.
- Review your travel safety habits before every trip.

> A car can be replaced. You cannot.
