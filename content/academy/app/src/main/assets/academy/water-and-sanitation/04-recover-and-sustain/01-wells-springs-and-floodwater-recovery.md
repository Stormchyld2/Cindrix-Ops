A well or spring gives you your own water, if you protect it and know what to do after a flood or damage.

## Wells
- **Know your well:** depth, type (drilled, dug, driven), cap, location and last test date.
- **Protect:** keep the cap sealed, keep chemicals, fuel, animals and septic systems well away, and slope the ground away from the casing.
- **Test** every year for bacteria, nitrate and other local contaminants.
- **No power?** A hand pump or a solar pump can keep a drilled well working. Some wells will take a small bucket-pump on a rope.

## Springs
- **Protect** a spring with a box or collection chamber, a lid, a diversion ditch uphill and a fence against animals.
- **Treat** spring water as you would any other, since springs can still carry germs.

## After a flood: wells
Floodwater carries sewage and chemicals. CDC advice, in outline:
1. **Do not drink** from the well until it has been tested and disinfected.
2. **Remove** debris from around the well. Wait for floodwater to drop and the area to drain.
3. **Have the pump and electrical parts checked** by a qualified person before turning power on.
4. **Disinfect** with chlorine following your health department's instructions, then flush the system until the chlorine smell is gone.
5. **Test** the water after a week or two, and again at intervals. Use bottled or treated water until a lab says it is clean.

## After a flood: tanks and pipes
- Discard water in tanks that touched floodwater. Clean and disinfect tanks.
- Flush household pipes. Run each tap for several minutes.
- Replace filters and water-heater anodes if needed.

## Boil-water and do-not-use notices
- **Boil water notice:** boil for a rolling minute before drinking, brushing teeth, making ice or washing food. You can bathe if you avoid swallowing water.
- **Do not use:** the water may have chemicals. Use only bottled or an alternate safe source.

## When water comes back
Run cold taps until clear. Flush hot water heaters. Discard ice. Clean water filters. Do not assume safety until the utility says so.

## Record
Write down what failed and what you would change. That is the recovery step.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
