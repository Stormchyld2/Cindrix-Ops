A number you have worked out yourself is worth more than a rule you only remember.

## The baseline
FEMA and the Red Cross: **one gallon per person per day,** for drinking and basic sanitation, for at least three days and ideally two weeks. That is the survival floor. The Sphere humanitarian standard says people in emergencies need at least about 15 liters (4 gallons) a day for drinking, cooking and hygiene combined.

## What people really use
- **Drinking:** about half a gallon from all fluids for an adult in mild weather, more in heat.
- **Cooking and cleaning food:** a quarter to half a gallon.
- **Washing hands and basic hygiene:** a quarter to a gallon.
- **Dishes, laundry, flushing:** several gallons if you do them normally, so cut these back first.
- **Pets, a garden, animals:** add for each.

## Adjust for people and weather
Hot weather, heavy work, illness, pregnancy and nursing all raise the need. Children need less in total but dehydrate faster. Cold weather still needs water. Plan on up to double in a heat wave.

## Work it out
1. Count people. Count pets and animals.
2. Choose a number of gallons per person per day for each tier of your plan, such as 1 for survival, 2 for comfortable and 4 or more for a decent hygiene standard.
3. Multiply by days: 14 days is a good first target, 30 days is better.
4. Add cooking water for dry foods such as beans and rice.
5. Write down the result.

**Example:** four people, 14 days, 1 gallon each per day, is 56 gallons. At 2 gallons each, it is 112.

## Rationing sensibly
- **Never ration the drinking water** to the point of dehydration. Dark urine, headache and tiredness are early signs. Save water on other uses first.
- Use **gray water** (from washing) for flushing, and wash-water for cleaning.
- Cook with less water: use pressure cookers, steam, and one-pot meals.
- Save the dishwater rinse for plants.
- Wash in a basin, not under a running tap.

## Count your sources, not only your storage
Add the water you could get each day from rain, a well, a spring or from treating a stream. A household with a good second source can store less. A household with no second source should store more.

## Put it on the inventory
Record your water in the program's inventory by container, location and date, so the gap shows.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
