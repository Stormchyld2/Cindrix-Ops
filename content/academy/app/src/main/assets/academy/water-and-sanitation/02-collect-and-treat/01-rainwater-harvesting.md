Rain is free, falls where you live and is usually the cleanest water around, if you catch it cleanly and treat it before you drink.

## The parts of a system
1. **Catchment:** the roof or tarp that catches rain.
2. **Gutters and downspouts:** carry it.
3. **First-flush diverter:** throws away the first dirty rain.
4. **Screen:** keeps out leaves and insects.
5. **Storage:** a barrel, tank or cistern, closed and dark.
6. **Outlet:** a tap or a way to draw water with a clean container.
7. **Overflow:** sends extra water away from your house.

## Choose the catchment well
- **Good:** metal roofs, clay or concrete tile, and clean plastic tarps.
- **Poor:** wood shingles, treated shingles, tar and gravel roofs, roofs under overhanging trees, and roofs with lead flashing.
- **Avoid** collecting from roofs that carry bird droppings, or that have been hit by smoke, ash or fallout.
How much: one inch of rain on a 1,000 square foot roof gives about 600 gallons, minus some loss. Even a small shed roof fills barrels fast.

## First flush
The first part of a rain washes dust, droppings and ash off the roof. Divert it:
- Use a simple standpipe diverter that fills with the first gallon or two, then lets clean water flow on.
- Or move the downspout by hand for the first ten minutes.

## Storing
- Use **food-grade, opaque** barrels or tanks with a tight lid, a screen on every opening, and no light, so algae cannot grow.
- Raise them on a firm base. Water weighs about 8 pounds a gallon.
- Keep mosquitoes out with a fine mesh.
- Keep the tank clean: drain and scrub it once a year.

## Treat before drinking
Rainwater from a roof is **not** safe to drink untreated. Filter, then disinfect with boiling, chlorine or another method from the next lessons.

## Use it
- **Drinking and cooking:** only after treatment.
- **Washing, flushing, garden:** usually fine untreated, although edible parts that touch the soil should be washed with clean water.

## Rules
Some places limit or regulate rainwater collection. Check with your local authority.

## Emergency catchment
If a storm is coming, spread a clean tarp or plastic sheet with edges lifted into a bucket, or tie a sheet to catch drips. Keep it clean of dirt and cover containers.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
