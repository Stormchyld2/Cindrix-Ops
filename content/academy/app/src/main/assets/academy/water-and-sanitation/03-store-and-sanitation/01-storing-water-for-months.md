Stored water is your most reliable source. Done well, it will last for years.

## Containers
- **Commercial bottles** sealed by the maker: simplest. Replace by the date.
- **Food-grade water containers,** from 1 to 55 gallons, BPA-free, with a secure cap and a spigot.
- **Cleaned soda bottles** for small amounts. No milk or juice jugs.
- **Water barrels** with a hand pump, kept off the ground and out of the sun.
- **Water bricks and bladders** for tight spaces.

## Filling
1. Clean and sanitize the container (a teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart of water, swished and rinsed).
2. Fill with safe tap water. Municipal water is already treated. Private wells need testing first.
3. Add nothing if the water is municipal. If the source is untreated, treat it before storage.
4. Seal tight, label with the date and store cool and dark.

## Where
Cool, dark and level, such as an interior closet, a basement, under beds, in a garage out of freezing and sun. Keep water away from fuel and chemicals, since plastic can pass on smells and vapors. Raise containers off bare concrete.

## How long
Treated water in clean, sealed, food-grade containers can last a year or more. Replace it every year, or test with chlorine strips. If it tastes flat, aerate it by pouring between containers.

## Freezing
Water expands as it freezes. Leave space in the container, and do not expect plastic to survive a deep freeze when full.

## Hot water tank and pipes
A standard tank holds 30 to 80 gallons of drinkable water. Know how to isolate it (turn off power or gas and the cold supply) and draw it from the bottom drain valve. Open a hot tap upstairs to let air in.

## Keep rotating
Mark a day each year to empty a few containers into the garden, refill and recheck. Use a log or the program's inventory.

## Large systems
Tanks of hundreds or thousands of gallons need a lid, screens, a clean source and an outlet. Chlorinate the tank when you fill it, and check chlorine levels each month.

## Protect from contamination
Never dip hands or cups into the main container. Use a spigot or a clean pouring container with a lid.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
