If you come across a crash, spill or leak, signs on the vehicle or building can tell you what it is and how far to stay away.

## Stay back and call
- **Never walk into a spill or cloud.** Stay upwind, uphill and upstream.
- Stay far away: at least about 330 feet (100 meters) for an unknown spill. Greater for large fires or leaks, as the guidebook advises.
- Call 911 and give the location, what you see, and the numbers on the placard.
- Do not touch containers or spilled material.
- Warn others and keep them back.

## Vehicle placards
Trucks and rail cars carry diamond-shaped signs. Look for:
- **Color and symbol:** red for flammable, orange for explosives, green for non-flammable gas, yellow for oxidizers, white for poison and some others, and so on.
- **Four-digit UN number:** identifies the specific material.
- **Class number** at the bottom.

## The Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG)
The US Department of Transportation publishes the ERG free. It is for first responders but useful for informed citizens.
1. Use the four-digit **UN/NA ID number** or the **material name** to find a **guide number.**
2. The guide lists hazards (fire, explosion, health), public safety distances, and basic response.
3. The **Table of Initial Isolation and Protective Action Distances** covers toxic gases that are especially dangerous. It gives distances for day and night.
Keep a copy or app on your phone, and a printed copy in the car if you travel often by tanker routes.

## NFPA 704 diamond
Fixed buildings may show a four-color diamond:
- **Blue:** health hazard (0 to 4).
- **Red:** flammability.
- **Yellow:** instability or reactivity.
- **White:** special hazards, such as "W" for reacts with water.
Higher numbers mean greater danger.

## Safety data sheets
Workplaces must keep Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for each chemical. Know where they are at your job, and read the labels at home.

## Household chemicals
Never mix bleach with ammonia or acids: it makes toxic gases. Store chemicals in original containers, away from children, and ventilate when using them.

## Know the signs of exposure
Burning eyes, nose or throat, cough, trouble breathing, dizziness, nausea, headache, skin irritation, or a strange smell. Move to fresh air, away and upwind, and call for help.

## Reporting
Call 911 and the National Response Center (1-800-424-8802 in the US) for significant spills, as the situation requires.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
