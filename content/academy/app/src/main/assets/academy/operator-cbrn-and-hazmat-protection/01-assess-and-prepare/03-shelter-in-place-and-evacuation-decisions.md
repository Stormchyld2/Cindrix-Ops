Officials will tell you which to do. If you must decide on your own, use this guide.

## When to shelter in place
- Officials say so.
- The hazard is outside, short-lived or too dangerous to travel through, such as a toxic cloud or fallout.
- You cannot leave safely.
- Fast action indoors gives better protection than being caught outside.

## When to evacuate
- Officials order it.
- The hazard is inside or very close, such as a fire or gas leak.
- You are in a flood or other area told to leave.
- The shelter cannot protect you, or the event will last long.

## Shelter in place: steps
1. **Go inside immediately.** Bring pets and people in.
2. **Close and lock** doors and windows. Close fireplace dampers.
3. **Turn off** fans, air conditioners, heaters and vents that bring in outside air. Close air intakes.
4. **Go to an interior room,** above ground level if the hazard is a heavy gas that sinks (such as chlorine), and **below ground or in the center of the building** if the hazard is fallout. Choose a room with few windows.
5. **Seal** the room if instructed: plastic sheeting cut to size over windows, doors and vents, held with duct tape. Seal gaps with wet towels.
6. **Bring** your emergency kit, water, radio, phone, flashlight, and medicines.
7. **Listen** to the radio or alerts for updates.
8. **Stay** until officials say it is safe. Do not leave early.
9. **Limit** use of the toilet and drinking to reduce trips, and keep a bucket.

## Air in a sealed room
A sealed room has a limited air supply, but typical rooms provide enough air for several hours, and sealing is meant to be short term. Do not seal tightly for a long time without guidance.

## Evacuating: steps
1. **Move** early. Roads clog quickly.
2. **Take** the go-bag, medicines, documents, pets and supplies.
3. **Lock** the house. Turn off utilities if told to.
4. **Use** the officials' routes.
5. **Drive** with windows up and vents closed, and the air set to recirculate.
6. **Go** crosswind, away from the plume, if you are given a choice.
7. **Tell** someone your route and destination.
8. **Check in** at a shelter or reception center.

## In a car when something happens
Close windows and vents, recirculate air, drive out of the area crosswind if it is safe, or park inside a building.

## Special needs
People who use oxygen, medical devices, or have limited mobility need plans made early. Register with local emergency management if a program exists.

## Practice
Practice sealing a room once, so you know how long it takes and what you need.

> This course is protective only. Follow instructions from emergency officials and call 911 for any real incident. Medicines for radiation or chemical exposure are given only on the direction of public health officials or a doctor.
