Bad weather is the commonest reason for an emergency on the road. The best choice is often to delay the trip.

## Decide whether to go
- **Check forecasts** before leaving, and heed warnings. If the road is closed, advisories say stay home, or conditions are getting worse, wait.
- If you must go, **go early**, tell someone your route and take your kit.

## Snow and ice
- **Slow down;** ice can double or triple stopping distance.
- **Gentle** steering, braking and acceleration.
- **Increase following distance** to eight to ten seconds.
- **Start in a higher gear** (or "snow" mode) to avoid wheel spin.
- **Use low beams;** keep lights, windows and mirrors clear. Clear snow from the roof and hood.
- Bridges and shaded spots freeze first. "Black ice" looks like wet pavement.
- **Winter tires and chains** make a big difference.
- **Stuck:** straighten the wheels, clear snow from around the tires, add sand or traction mats under the drive wheels and rock gently between forward and reverse.

## Stranded in snow
- **Stay with the vehicle.** It is a shelter and easier to find.
- Tie a bright cloth to the antenna, turn on hazard lights, and call for help.
- **Run the engine only about 10 minutes each hour** for heat, with the tailpipe clear of snow and a window cracked, to avoid carbon monoxide. Better, use blankets and layers.
- Move your arms and legs to keep warm, but do not overexert in snow.
- Keep hydrated and eat.

## Fog
- **Slow down,** use low beams and fog lights, never high beams.
- Use road markings to guide you.
- If visibility is near zero, **pull off the road** as far as you can, switch off all lights except hazards, and wait.
- Do not stop on the road.

## Rain
- Roads are slickest in the first minutes of rain.
- Slow down, keep distance, turn on headlights.
- Beware **hydroplaning:** ease off the throttle, do not brake hard, and steer gently.

## Wind, dust and wildfire smoke
Slow down, grip the wheel, watch for debris, and watch trucks. In smoke or dust, pull off the road, turn off lights and wait.

## Heat
Hot cars kill. **Never leave children or pets in a parked car.** Carry water, and keep the engine cool by watching the gauge.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.

## Check yourself
? Where should you stay if stranded in snow?
+ With the vehicle
- Leave the road
- Walk away immediately
- Sleep outside
> It shelters you and helps rescuers find you.

? Which lights do you use in fog?
- High beams
- Hazards only while moving
- No lights
+ Low beams and fog lights, not high beams
> High beams reflect off fog.

? How do you avoid carbon monoxide when idling in snow?
- Block the tailpipe
+ Keep the tailpipe clear and run the engine only briefly
- Idle all night
- Seal the windows
> Check the tailpipe often.
