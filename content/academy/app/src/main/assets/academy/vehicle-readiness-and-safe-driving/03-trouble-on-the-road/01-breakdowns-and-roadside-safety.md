Roadside breakdowns are dangerous, since passing vehicles kill people every year.

## If the car fails
1. **Stay calm,** and steer to a safe place. Signal and slow down.
2. **Get completely off the road,** if you can: shoulder, a parking lot, an exit ramp. Move far from traffic.
3. **Turn on hazard lights.**
4. **Park** on level ground, with wheels turned away from the road, handbrake on.
5. **Decide: stay in the car or get out?**
   - **On a busy road or highway:** usually stay in the car with the **seat belt on,** until help arrives, **unless** it is not safe (fire, smoke, risk of being hit).
   - If you get out, go **well off the road,** behind a guardrail or up an embankment, away from traffic.
6. **Call for help:** roadside assistance, 911 if hurt or in danger, or the highway patrol.
7. **Make yourself visible:** raise the hood, tie a bright cloth, and put out triangles or flares only if you can do so safely and well behind the car.

## Strangers
- If someone stops, stay in the car with the doors locked, and speak through a cracked window. Ask them to call for help rather than getting out.
- Do not accept rides from strangers.
- If you feel unsafe, call 911.

## Flat tire
- Pull off on firm, level ground away from traffic.
- If it is not safe to change a tire, call for help or drive slowly on the rim only a short distance to a safer place.
- Loosen lug nuts a half turn before jacking up the car, jack at the correct points, and never go under a car supported only by a jack.

## Overheating
- Turn off the air conditioning and turn on the heater to pull heat from the engine.
- Pull over, switch off, and let it cool. Do **not** open the radiator cap when hot.

## Dead battery
Use jumper cables in the right order, or a jump pack. Follow the manual.

## Out of fuel
Do not walk along highways. Call for help.

## Walk or wait?
In bad weather or remote places, staying with the car is usually safer. If you must walk, take water, layers, and your go-bag, leave a note in the car with your time and direction and walk on the side facing traffic.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
