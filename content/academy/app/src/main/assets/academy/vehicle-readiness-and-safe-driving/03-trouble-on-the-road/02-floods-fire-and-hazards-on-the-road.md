Some hazards turn a drive into a life-threatening emergency. Know them before you meet them.

## Flooded roads: turn around, don't drown
- **Six inches** of moving water can knock a person down. **A foot** can float many cars. **Two feet** can carry away most vehicles, including SUVs and trucks.
- **Never drive into flooded roads.** You cannot tell how deep the water is, or whether the road beneath is intact.
- **Turn around** and find another route, even if it is long.
- Flash floods can strike far from where it rained, and at night.
- **If your car stalls in rising water:** abandon it at once if you can safely do so, and move to higher ground. If water is rising inside, **unbuckle, open a window** (electric windows may work for a short time) and climb out. Water pressure may keep the door shut. A window-breaking tool helps.
- **Never drive around barricades.**

## Wildfire
- Leave early when evacuation is ordered.
- If caught on the road: **close windows and vents,** turn on headlights and hazard lights, drive slowly with caution, and avoid areas with heavy smoke.
- If you must stop, park in a clear area, away from vegetation, lie on the floor under blankets or a coat, with the engine off and the doors closed, until the fire passes. The vehicle offers some protection, but the situation is very dangerous.

## Vehicle fire
- Pull over, switch off the engine and get everyone out.
- Move at least 100 feet away and upwind. Call 911.
- Do not open the hood. Do not go back for belongings.

## Downed power lines
- Treat every line as live.
- **Stay in the car** if a line lands on it. Warn others to stay away, and call 911.
- If you must get out because of fire, **jump clear** with your feet together and **shuffle or hop** away without touching the car and the ground at once.

## Tornado and storms
- Never try to outrun a tornado in a car on a crowded road. If you can reach a sturdy building, go in. If not, a low ditch is better than a car or overpass. Cover your head.

## Earthquake
If driving, slow down and stop in a clear area away from bridges, buildings and wires. Stay inside the car with your seat belt on until the shaking stops.

## Avalanche, landslide and rockfall
Heed warnings, avoid steep slopes and watch for debris on the road after heavy rain.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
