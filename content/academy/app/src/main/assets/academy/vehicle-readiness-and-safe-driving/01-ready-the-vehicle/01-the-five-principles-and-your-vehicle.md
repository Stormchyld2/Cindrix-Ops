Your vehicle may be your shelter, your way out and your lifeline. It is also where most people get hurt in an emergency, on the road.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- What could strand you: a dead battery, a flat tire, no fuel, a storm, a flood, a snowbound road, a wildfire, a blocked road, a breakdown at night?
- What could harm you on the road: crashes, aggressive drivers, road crime, floodwater, ice, fire?
- Is your vehicle in good condition and right for your needs?

## 2. Preparedness
- Maintain it: oil, tires, brakes, battery, lights, wipers, fluids.
- Keep the **fuel tank at least half full.**
- Carry a **vehicle kit** and a **go-bag.**
- Know your routes, with alternates, and have paper maps.
- Keep documents, spare keys and a phone charger.

## 3. Response
When trouble comes: **stay calm, get safe, make yourself seen, call for help,** and decide whether to stay with the vehicle or go on foot.

## 4. Mitigation
- Do not drive in dangerous conditions when you can wait.
- Keep your vehicle out of flood zones and away from falling trees.
- Keep tires, brakes and lights in good order.
- Have insurance and roadside assistance.

## 5. Recovery
After a crash or breakdown: care for people, report, repair, review. After a disaster: clean, dry and inspect the vehicle before heavy use, especially after floods.

## The big idea
Most vehicle emergencies are predictable. A **half tank, good tires, a kit and a plan** prevent most of the trouble.

## This course
1. Ready the vehicle: maintenance, kits, fuel.
2. Drive smart: awareness, routes, defensive driving, bad weather.
3. Trouble on the road: breakdowns, floods, fire, road crime, blocked roads.
4. Recover and plan.

> The best driving skill is not being there when danger arrives. Leave early, leave rested and leave with fuel.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
