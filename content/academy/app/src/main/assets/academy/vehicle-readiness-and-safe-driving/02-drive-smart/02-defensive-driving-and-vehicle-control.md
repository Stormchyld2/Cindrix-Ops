Defensive driving means giving yourself time and space so that other people's mistakes do not become your crash. Control skills help when conditions are poor.

## The basics
- **Seating:** sit upright, with seat and mirrors set so you see well and reach the pedals. Keep hands at 9 and 3, or 8 and 4.
- **Seat belt on,** always.
- **Speed:** match speed to conditions, not the sign. Slow down early.
- **Look where you want to go,** not at the hazard. Eyes steer the car.
- **Smooth inputs:** gentle braking, steering and throttle keep the tires gripping.

## Braking
- **Anti-lock brakes (ABS):** if you must stop hard, press the brake firmly and keep pressing, and **steer.** You may feel a pulsing pedal. That is normal.
- **Without ABS:** press as hard as you can without locking the wheels, and release slightly if they lock.
- **Stopping distance** grows with speed. At highway speed it is several hundred feet on dry roads, and several times more on ice.

## Skids
- **Front-wheel skid** (the car plows straight ahead): ease off the throttle, let the tires regain grip, then steer.
- **Rear-wheel skid** (the back swings out): ease off the throttle, and steer gently in the direction you want the front to go, then straighten as the car recovers. Avoid hard braking.
- Practice in a **safe, empty parking lot** or a skid-control class, with an instructor.

## Avoiding collisions
- If a crash is unavoidable, **brake as much as you can and steer** to hit something soft or at an angle rather than head-on, if you can.
- Keep an escape route.
- Use your horn and lights to warn others.

## Head-on and wrong-way
Brake, move right, and sound the horn. Move off the road if needed.

## Highway
- Use the right lane for travel and the left to pass.
- Do not tailgate or weave.
- Use turn signals early.
- Keep out of other drivers' blind spots, especially trucks.

## Night
Slow down. Use high beams when appropriate. Do not outdrive your headlights. Keep windshield clean.

## Training
Take a defensive driving class. Real skill comes from guided practice, not from reading.

## Not covered
Pursuit, ramming and high-speed escape are specialized skills for trained professionals and carry grave legal and safety risks. They are not part of this course.

> Follow traffic laws and local rules. Evasive or high-speed driving techniques need a certified professional course. This course teaches avoidance, preparation and safe response.
