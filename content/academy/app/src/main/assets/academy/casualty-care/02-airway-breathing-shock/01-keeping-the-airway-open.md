A person who cannot get air through their throat can die in minutes. Check this after bleeding is under control, or straight away for someone who is unconscious.

## Signs of a blocked airway
- Snoring, gurgling or noisy breathing.
- No breathing movement at all.
- Blue or grey lips and face.

## What to do
1. **Talk to them.** A person who can talk has an open airway.
2. If they are unconscious and breathing, **roll them onto their side** (the recovery position) so the tongue and any vomit fall away from the throat. Bend the upper knee for stability.
3. If you suspect a neck or back injury and they are not breathing, use the **jaw thrust** (lift the jaw forward with your fingers at the corners) rather than tilting the head back. If the airway still does not open, tilt the head, because breathing matters most.
4. Clear anything you can **see** in the mouth. Do not sweep blindly with a finger.
5. If they are not breathing, start CPR and send someone for help (see your CPR training).

## Choking
A choking adult who cannot cough, speak or breathe needs firm back blows and abdominal thrusts. This is taught best in a class. Take one.

> Never put anything in the mouth of a person who is unconscious or having a fit. Never give them food or drink.

## Going deeper: opening and protecting an airway
An unconscious person's tongue can fall back and block the throat. Positioning is the first fix.

- **Head tilt and chin lift:** if no neck injury is likely, tilt the forehead back and lift the chin.
- **Jaw thrust:** if a neck or spine injury is possible, place your fingers behind the angles of the jaw and lift it forward without tilting the neck.
- **Recovery position:** for a breathing, unresponsive person with no spinal concern, roll them onto their side with the top leg bent, the head tilted back a little and the mouth angled down so fluids drain.
- **Suction:** do not put fingers into the mouth blindly. Sweep only if you can see a solid object.
- **Children and babies:** use a neutral position for infants, and do not over-tilt the head.

## Signs it is working
You see the chest rise, hear air and feel breath on your cheek.

## Choking: quick review
- **Adult or child, can cough:** encourage coughing.
- **Cannot cough, speak or breathe:** give five back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand, then five abdominal thrusts. Repeat until the object clears or the person becomes unresponsive.
- **Unresponsive:** call 911, lower them to the ground and start CPR, looking in the mouth each time you open it for breaths.
- **Infants:** five back blows face-down on your forearm, then five chest thrusts with two fingers, never abdominal thrusts.

## Burns and swelling
A burned face, soot in the nose or mouth, a hoarse voice or noisy breathing suggest airway swelling. This is an emergency: call 911 and keep the person upright if they are conscious.
