When there are more injured people than helpers, you help the **most people** by treating those you can save first. This is called **triage**.

## The START method (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)
Go from person to person. Spend under a minute on each.
1. **Can they walk?** Ask everyone who can walk to move to a safe place. They are **minor (green)**. Check them later.
2. **Are they breathing?** If not, open the airway. If still not breathing, they are **expectant (black)**. Move on. If breathing starts, they are **immediate (red)**.
3. **Breathing too fast (more than 30 breaths a minute)?** They are **immediate (red)**.
4. **Is the pulse at the wrist missing, or does the nail bed take more than two seconds to turn pink again?** **Immediate (red)**. Stop the bleeding if there is any.
5. **Can they follow a simple command, such as "squeeze my hand"?** If not, **immediate (red)**. If yes, **delayed (yellow)**.

## What each colour means
- **Red (immediate):** needs help now.
- **Yellow (delayed):** serious but can wait.
- **Green (minor):** walking wounded.
- **Black (expectant):** dead or not likely to survive with the help you have.

## Do two things on the way
While you triage, stop severe bleeding and open airways. Do not stop for anything longer.

## Re-check
People change. Go round again and move people between colours if they get better or worse.

> Triage is hard. You will not help everyone. Doing the most good for the most people is the right aim.

## Going deeper: why triage exists
When there are more injured than helpers, the goal changes: do the most good for the most people. Triage sorts people quickly so those who can be saved by quick care get it first. It is not about who deserves it. It is difficult, and requires a calm mind.

## START step by step
1. **Walking wounded:** call "if you can walk, go to that spot." They are marked minor (green) for now.
2. For each remaining person, check **breathing.** If not breathing, open the airway once. Still not breathing: **black** (dead or expectant). If breathing starts, **red.**
3. If breathing over 30 a minute: **red.**
4. Check **circulation:** no radial pulse or capillary refill over two seconds: **red.** Stop massive bleeding as you go.
5. Check **mental status:** if the person cannot follow simple commands: **red.** Otherwise: **yellow** (delayed).

## The colors
- **Red (immediate):** life-threatening but treatable.
- **Yellow (delayed):** serious, can wait for a time.
- **Green (minor):** walking wounded.
- **Black (deceased or expectant):** dead, or too injured to survive with available resources.

## Rules
- Spend less than a minute on each person.
- Stop only to open an airway and stop massive bleeding.
- Use tags, tape or a marker on the forehead to mark categories.
- Retriage as conditions change.
- Children and infants have different breathing rates, and there is a pediatric version called JumpSTART.

## Be honest with yourself
Triage is a skill that needs training. Ask your local Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) about courses.
