Some wounds are too deep or in the wrong place for a pad on top to work, for example the groin, the armpit, the neck, or a deep gash. Here you **pack** the wound.

## What you need
Plain gauze that unrolls, or hemostatic gauze (gauze treated to help blood clot), or in an emergency a strip of clean cloth.

## Steps
1. Open the wound with your fingers and find where the blood is coming from.
2. **Push the gauze into the wound** with your fingers, right down to the source of bleeding. Keep feeding it in until the wound is full.
3. Press **hard** on the packed wound with both hands for **at least three minutes** without letting up.
4. Hold the packing in place with a pressure bandage.
5. Do not remove the packing. Medical staff will take it out.

## Notes
- It hurts. Tell the person it is needed and that it will be over soon.
- Pack down to the bottom of the wound. Gauze laid on the surface does not stop deep bleeding.
- A wound in the neck: pack and press, but never press hard enough to block breathing.
- A tourniquet cannot be used on the groin, armpit or neck, which is why packing matters.

> Count the three minutes on a watch or phone. It feels much longer than it is, and people stop too soon.

## Going deeper: the technique in detail
Packing is for deep wounds that a tourniquet cannot reach, such as the groin, armpit, neck or the base of a limb. Hemostatic gauze (which contains a clotting agent) works better than plain gauze, but plain gauze or clean cloth works too.

1. Expose the wound. Cut or tear clothing away so you can see where the blood is coming from.
2. Wipe away pooled blood only enough to find the source.
3. Push the gauze **into the wound** with your fingers, aiming at the bleeding point. Keep pushing until the wound is full, feed more gauze if needed.
4. Pack **tight.** Think of stuffing a bag firmly.
5. **Press hard** straight on top with both hands for **at least three minutes**, or longer if your product's instructions say so.
6. Keep pressure on while you wrap a pressure bandage over it.
7. **Leave the packing in.** Do not pull it out to look. Only a medical team should remove it.
8. Note the time and tell the next responders.

## Common errors
- Only putting gauze on top of the wound without pushing it in.
- Packing too lightly or too short a time.
- Letting go too soon to check.
- Using tissues, cotton wool or fluffy material that leaves fibers in the wound.

## Combine with other steps
If the limb allows it, place a tourniquet high above the wound first for severe limb bleeding, then pack if bleeding persists. Put a second tourniquet next to the first if needed.

## Practice
Practice on a training pad, a thick sponge or a dummy with a pre-made wound, so your hands know the motion. Time yourself: aim to start packing in under a minute.
