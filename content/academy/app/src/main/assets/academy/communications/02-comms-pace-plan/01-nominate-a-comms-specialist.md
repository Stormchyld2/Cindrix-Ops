Every preparedness group needs one designated radio lead. In this course that person is the **Comms Specialist (CS)**.

If you are in a preparedness group, nominate a Comms Specialist. This should be your most knowledgeable radio person. All fallback communication plans route through the CS when normal channels fail, and the CS keeps the group's communication chains working at every level of the PACE plan.

## What the Comms Specialist does
1. **Owns the PACE plan.** Writes it, keeps it current, and makes sure every member has a copy.
2. **Keeps the contact list.** Names, phone numbers, callsigns, radio models and where each person lives or works.
3. **Starts the chains.** When the normal way to reach people fails, the CS starts the next method: a group text, an email chain, a radio call.
4. **Runs radio checks.** Calls regular checks so every radio is tested and every person practices.
5. **Trains.** Teaches new members how to program, operate and troubleshoot.
6. **Tracks equipment.** Knows who has which radio, which batteries are charged, and which spares are on hand.
7. **Keeps a log.** Writes down who was reached, when, on what channel, and how clearly.
8. **Trains a backup.** Chooses and teaches a deputy who can take over if the CS is away or cannot be reached.

## Why one person
When everyone tries to organize at once, messages cross and people get confused. One person who knows the plan can act quickly and clearly. Government emergency planners say the same: an emergency communication plan works best when someone is assigned to it and the steps to move from one method to the next are written down.

## Choosing the right person
Look for someone who:

- Is calm and clear when stressed.
- Is willing to practice and teach.
- Knows the radios and the rules, and is licensed or willing to earn a license.
- Can be reached by most of the group.
- Lives or works somewhere with a good radio position.

## Name a deputy
The CS can be away, hurt or out of range. Name a deputy, give them the same training and the same copy of the plan, and make sure they have their own radios and batteries.

## What the group owes the CS
- Show up for radio checks.
- Keep your radios charged and your information current.
- Report problems early.
- Follow the plan, even when you think of something better. Change the plan together afterward.

## Checklist
- Choose a Comms Specialist and a deputy.
- Write both names at the top of the PACE plan.
- Give every member a copy of the CS and deputy contact details.
- Set a weekly radio check time.
- Give the CS a notebook to log calls.

> The CS does not have to do everything. The CS makes sure everything gets done.
