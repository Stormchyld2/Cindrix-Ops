A **net** is an organized group conversation on one channel, run by one person called **net control**. Nets stop a busy channel from turning into noise, and they get every message through in order.

## Radio checks
A **radio check** is a quick test: "How do you hear me?" Use it to learn how well you are heard.

1. **Call.** "Base, this is Fox. Radio check. Over."
2. **Answer.** Give a short, clear report: "Fox, this is Base. You are loud and clear. Over." Or "weak but readable," "readable with noise," "unreadable."
3. **Acknowledge.** "Copy. Fox, out."

Keep radio checks short and do them at agreed times, not all day.

## Reading a signal
- **Loud and clear.** Perfect.
- **Good.** Clear, with little noise.
- **Readable, with noise.** Copyable, with effort.
- **Weak.** Hard to copy.
- **Unreadable.** Cannot be understood.

If you are weak, move to a higher place, change the antenna position or try another channel.

## Running a net
Net control opens the net, takes check-ins, handles messages and closes the net.

1. **Open.** "This is [callsign], net control for the [group] net. This net is for [purpose]. Stations with traffic, call now. Over."
2. **Take check-ins.** Stations call in one at a time. Net control writes down each one, with callsign, name and location.
3. **Handle traffic.** Stations with messages call, and net control tells them when to send. One message at a time.
4. **Priority.** Emergency traffic goes first. Then urgent traffic. Then routine.
5. **Close.** "This is net control. The [group] net is now closed. [Callsign], out."

## Net discipline for everyone
- **Check in** when asked. If you cannot be heard, say so on the next call.
- **Do not talk** unless net control says to, or you have emergency traffic.
- **Stay on the frequency.** If you need to leave, ask net control first and say when you return.
- **Listen.** Keep the volume up and the squelch set so you hear net control the first time.
- **Net control has final authority.** Follow its instructions.
- **Do not relay** a message unless net control asks you to.

## Emergency traffic
If you have an emergency, say your callsign, then "**emergency traffic**." Net control stops everything else and takes your message first.

## Keep a log
Net control writes down who checked in, when, any messages and any problems. The log shows who may need help and what to fix.

## Checklist
- Practice a radio check with a partner every week.
- Learn the signal reports.
- Practice running a short net with three or four stations.
- Write a net script and keep it with the radio.
- Keep a net log.

> A net works because everyone listens first and talks only when it is their turn.

- Your name only
- "Mayday" for every problem
- Nothing until the end
+ Your callsign and "emergency traffic"

- Change your callsign
- Turn off the radio
- Just leave
+ Ask net control
