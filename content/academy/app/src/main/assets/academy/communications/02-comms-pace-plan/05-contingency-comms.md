**Contingency** is when the cell towers are down, the internet is down and the power grid is down. Your radio is now your lifeline.

## Use agreed times and a calling channel
When nothing else works, the best way to find each other is to listen at the same times on the same channel. Agree on:

- A **calling channel** where everyone listens.
- **Check-in times**, so everyone is listening together.
- A **move-to channel**, where you go to talk once you have made contact.

## The Wilderness Protocol
The **Wilderness Protocol** is a widely known way for people in remote places to call for help or information. It is an agreement among radio operators:

- Listen for **5 minutes at the top of every third hour**, starting at **7:00 a.m.** local time. That gives you windows at 7:00 a.m., 10:00 a.m., 1:00 p.m., 4:00 p.m., 7:00 p.m. and 10:00 p.m.
- The primary frequency is **146.520 MHz**, the national calling frequency on the 2-meter VHF band.
- Secondary frequencies include **446.000 MHz** on the 70-centimeter band, and other amateur bands.
- It is for **emergency** use and for **calling out for help or information**. Once you connect with someone, move to another channel so the calling frequency stays free.

For a family or group this is a good fallback. Everyone with a radio that can receive 146.520 MHz can listen. If you are a licensed ham, you can answer and call. If you are not, remember that the amateur service requires a license, and the rules for unlicensed people transmitting are narrow: they apply to a real emergency involving life or property, not to practice.

## GMRS windows
For your own group, set your own windows on your GMRS channels:

- **Primary GMRS simplex channel.** The radio-to-radio channel your group uses first.
- **A repeater channel.** The repeater your group can reach from most places.
- **A backup simplex channel** if the first is busy.

## How to make contact
1. At the agreed time, turn on the radio and listen for the full five minutes.
2. If you hear nothing, call: "[Your callsign or code name], calling for [name or group], this is [your callsign]. Over."
3. Wait for an answer and call again once.
4. Once you connect, move to the agreed working channel, check in and exchange your short messages.
5. End with your callsign. Go back to listening.

## Program the channels ahead of time
- Program your **local repeaters** into channels.
- Program the **146.520 MHz** and **446.000 MHz** calling frequencies if your radio supports them (and if you are licensed to transmit on them).
- Program several **simplex** channels for direct radio-to-radio talk.
- Put the channels in the same order on every radio.
- **Print a copy** of the channel list and **laminate** it.

## Write it into the plan
- The calling channel and the times.
- The repeaters, with tones.
- The working channels.
- The trigger to move to Emergency: "no contact on any channel in two full windows."

## Checklist
- Find GMRS repeaters near home and program them.
- Find ham VHF and UHF repeaters near home and program them for listening.
- Program simplex channels for direct talk.
- Program 146.520 MHz and 446.000 MHz for listening (and for use if licensed).
- Print and laminate your channel list.
- Practice the check-in times with your group.
- Write the trigger for moving to Emergency.

> Listening costs nothing and needs no license. Even if you cannot transmit, you can still hear what is happening and who is looking for you.
