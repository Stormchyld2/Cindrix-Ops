For any critical information, such as locations, frequencies, times and instructions, the receiver reads the message back word for word. The sender then confirms "Correct" or corrects any errors before anyone acts.

## Why it works
Radios are noisy. People hear what they expect to hear, and small mistakes in numbers and names can send a team to the wrong place. A read-back catches the mistake while it is still cheap to fix. Emergency radio operators, pilots and military units all use it for this reason.

## The pattern
1. The sender gives the message.
2. The receiver repeats it word for word.
3. The sender says "Correct" if it matches, or says the right part again.
4. Only after a "Correct" does anyone act.

Example:

Sender: "Meet at checkpoint Alpha at one four zero zero. Over."

Receiver: "Meet at checkpoint Alpha at one four zero zero. Correct? Over."

Sender: "Correct. Out."

## What to read back
- **Locations and directions.**
- **Times and dates.**
- **Frequencies and channels.**
- **Names and callsigns.**
- **Numbers of any kind.**
- **Instructions that change what people do.**
- **Medical information.**

## What not to read back
Routine and low-risk talk such as "radio check" or "stand by" does not need a read-back. Over-using it slows the channel.

## When you relay a message
When you pass a message from one person to another, repeat it **word for word**. Do not change it, shorten it or fix it. If something does not make sense, ask for clarification before you send it on.

## When the read-back is wrong
If the receiver reads it back wrong, do not say "no." Say: "Negative. I say again: [the right words]." Then ask for another read-back.

## Make it automatic
Practice until the read-back happens without thinking. Add it to every drill, and add it to your quarterly exercise.

## Checklist
- Practice read-back with a partner using locations and times.
- Practice correcting a wrong read-back.
- Add read-back to every drill.
- Teach every family member.

> A read-back takes five seconds. A wrong turn can take five hours.
