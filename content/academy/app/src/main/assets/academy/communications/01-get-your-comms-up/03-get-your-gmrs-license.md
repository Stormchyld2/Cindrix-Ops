A GMRS license is one of the easiest and most valuable upgrades to your family communications plan. There is no test. You apply online, pay the fee, and the license is good for 10 years.

## What the license gives you
- Legal use of higher-power GMRS radios and removable antennas.
- Access to **repeaters** that can extend your range to tens of miles. Some repeaters are open to all licensed users. Others need the owner's permission.
- Coverage for your **immediate family**. Once you are licensed, family members of any age may use GMRS radios within your license. The FCC counts as immediate family a spouse, children, grandchildren, stepchildren, parents, grandparents, stepparents, siblings, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews and in-laws.

## What it costs and how long it lasts
The FCC application fee is currently $35. The license lasts 10 years. Fees can change, so always check the FCC site when you apply. You can renew from 90 days before it expires up to the expiration date. If you miss the date, you apply for a new license.

## Who may apply
You must be at least 18 years old. You must not be a representative of a foreign government. Businesses cannot get new GMRS licenses. A business cannot use one employee's family license for company work.

## Apply in order
1. **Get an FCC Registration Number (FRN).** Go to the FCC's CORES system, register with your name, address and email, and you will receive a 10-digit FRN. Save it somewhere safe. It is your login for all FCC filings.
2. **Open the License Manager.** Use the FCC Universal Licensing System (ULS) and sign in with your FRN.
3. **Start a new GMRS application.** Choose the GMRS radio service, enter your details, and answer the eligibility questions.
4. **Pay the fee.** You pay online with a card. Keep the receipt.
5. **Wait for your callsign.** It usually arrives within a few days, by email and in your ULS account. Your callsign looks like three or four letters followed by three numbers, such as WRxx123.
6. **Print the grant and store it.** Keep a paper copy in the radio kit and a copy on your drive.

## Using your callsign
- You must **identify yourself with your callsign** at least every 15 minutes while you are talking, and at the end of a conversation. Say it plainly: "This is [callsign]."
- Family members using your license identify with your callsign. They do not get their own.
- On a repeater, ID the same way.

## Repeaters: find, ask, and program
A repeater is a tall radio on a hill or tower that hears you and re-sends your message on a different frequency, so you reach much farther than a handheld could by itself.

1. **Find repeaters near you.** Look up the lists kept by GMRS clubs and repeater directories. Note each repeater's channel, location and owner.
2. **Ask permission if needed.** Many are open, but some owners require you to join a club or ask first.
3. **Record the tone.** Most repeaters require a **CTCSS** (or DCS) tone to open. Write it down exactly.
4. **Program the repeater** into your radio with the right frequency, offset and tone, then test it with a friend.

## What GMRS does not allow
- You may not link repeaters over the internet or phone lines to extend range.
- You may not connect GMRS to the phone network to carry your communications.
- You may not use the radio for business without the right license.
- The radio itself must be FCC-certified for GMRS (Part 95E).

## Checklist
- Register with the FCC and write down your FRN.
- Apply for your GMRS license and pay the fee.
- Save your callsign and the grant on paper and on your drive.
- Find two or three repeaters near home and one near your work or bug-out route.
- Record each repeater's tone and who owns it.
- Program the repeaters into your radio and test them.
- Teach every family member how to say the callsign.

> Fees and rules can change. Before you apply, check the FCC's GMRS page for the current fee and the current rules.
