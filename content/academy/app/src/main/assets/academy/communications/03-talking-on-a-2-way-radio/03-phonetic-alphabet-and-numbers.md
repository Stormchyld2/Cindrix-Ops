On a radio, letters that sound alike get mixed up. "B," "D," "P," "T" and "V" are almost impossible to tell apart in static. The **phonetic alphabet** gives each letter a clear word, and it is the same for everyone, anywhere.

## The alphabet
Use the international (ICAO) alphabet. Do not make up your own words.

- A: **Alfa**
- B: **Bravo**
- C: **Charlie**
- D: **Delta**
- E: **Echo**
- F: **Foxtrot**
- G: **Golf**
- H: **Hotel**
- I: **India**
- J: **Juliett**
- K: **Kilo**
- L: **Lima**
- M: **Mike**
- N: **November**
- O: **Oscar**
- P: **Papa**
- Q: **Quebec**
- R: **Romeo**
- S: **Sierra**
- T: **Tango**
- U: **Uniform**
- V: **Victor**
- W: **Whiskey**
- X: **X-ray**
- Y: **Yankee**
- Z: **Zulu**

## Numbers
Say each digit by itself. Never say a number as a group. For 1400, say "one four zero zero," not "fourteen hundred." The only change from normal speech is that **nine** is often said "**niner**" so it is not confused with the German word "nein."

- 0: Zero
- 1: One
- 2: Two
- 3: Three
- 4: Four
- 5: Five
- 6: Six
- 7: Seven
- 8: Eight
- 9: Niner

For a frequency, say each digit and say "point" for the decimal: 146.520 is "one four six point five two zero."

## When to use it
- **Spell names and unusual words.** "Fox, F-O-X: Foxtrot Oscar X-ray."
- **Spell your callsign** when you give it.
- **Spell street names** and any word the other person might not copy correctly.
- **Say numbers digit by digit,** including times, addresses, frequencies and grid coordinates.

## Say the time the same way
Use the 24-hour clock. 2:30 p.m. is "one four three zero." State a time zone if the group is spread out. Say "hours" only if you want to; it is not required.

## Practice
1. Spell your full name in the phonetic alphabet.
2. Spell your street name.
3. Say your phone number digit by digit.
4. Spell the names of your family members.
5. Read the license plates of passing cars out loud, with phonetics.

## Common mistakes
- Making up words, such as "apple" for A or "Mary" for M. People may not know them.
- Saying numbers as groups.
- Rushing the word. Say each phonetic word slowly and clearly.
- Using phonetics for ordinary words that do not need it.

## Checklist
- Memorize the phonetic alphabet.
- Print a copy and keep it in your radio kit.
- Practice spelling your name and callsign.
- Practice saying numbers digit by digit.
- Practice 24-hour time.

> If a letter might be misunderstood, spell it. If it will not be misunderstood, do not spend the time.
