Good radio procedure is a habit. If you follow the same five steps every time, you will be clear even when you are scared or tired.

## Before you key the microphone
1. **Listen first.** Make sure nobody else is talking. Talking over others blocks them and may block an emergency call.
2. **Plan your words.** Decide what you will say in a few short sentences.
3. **Know your location.** Be ready to say exactly where you are, particularly if you are mobile or searching.

## The five parts of a call
1. **Call the station.** Say the name or call of the person or group you want. "Base."
2. **Say "this is."**
3. **Say who you are.** "Fox."
4. **Say your message.** Clearly and slowly, in short phrases.
5. **End with "over" or "out."**

Example: "Base, this is Fox. I am at the north gate. All clear. Over."

## How to speak
- Press the push-to-talk button, wait a **second**, then speak. If you speak at once, the start of your first word may be cut off.
- Hold the microphone or radio **across your face**, a couple of inches from your mouth, not right in front of it.
- Speak **slowly and distinctly**, with equal weight on each word. Do not let your voice trail off at the end.
- In poor signal, **exaggerate** your words slightly and slow down.
- **Pause** between phrases by letting go of the button. Do not say "um" with the button down.
- Be **brief**. Short messages leave the channel open and are easier to copy.

## When to drop words
Once you have made contact and nobody will be confused, you can shorten the exchange:

- You can drop the called station's name.
- You can drop "this is."
- In quick back-and-forth, you can drop your own name if confusion is unlikely.
- Never shorten anything that could be misunderstood.

## Identification
Identify yourself as the rules require. For GMRS, give your callsign at least every 15 minutes and at the end of a conversation. For amateur radio, give your callsign at least every 10 minutes and at the end of a conversation. If you use a tactical call, you still need your legal callsign at those times.

## Receiving a call
- **Always acknowledge** a call that is for you, even if only to say "stand by."
- **Never acknowledge** a message you did not fully understand. Say "say again" instead.
- Write down what you hear, especially names, numbers and places.

## Good habits
- Tell the truth. Do not guess, and label guesses as guesses.
- Stay calm. Anger, jokes and complaints waste airtime.
- Do not eat or chew gum while talking.
- If you are tired, hand the radio to someone else.
- If you have nothing to add, stay quiet.

## Checklist
- Practice the five-part call in pairs.
- Practice waiting one second after pressing the button.
- Memorize your callsign and say it clearly.
- Learn your own location description, such as an address, a crossing or a landmark.
- Write the call format on your channel card.
