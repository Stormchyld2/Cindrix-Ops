**Emergency** is the last level. Every other method has failed, and the radios are down too, or no one answers. It is the level most people forget, and the one you will be glad you planned.

## What Emergency looks like
At this level there is no technology to rely on. The plan is simple, low-tech and written down. Government planners give examples such as a written message carried by hand, or a runner who delivers a message to the recipient's location. In a family, it is usually a **meeting place** and a **waiting rule**.

## The pieces of an Emergency plan
1. **Primary and backup meeting places.** Choose a place close to home, such as a neighbor's house or a landmark, and a second place in another direction or farther away. People should be able to reach both on foot.
2. **A time rule.** Decide when you go to the meeting place: "If we cannot reach each other by [time], go to the meeting place." Decide how long you wait there before you move on.
3. **A written note.** If you must leave, leave a note where the others will find it. It should say your name, the date and time, where you are going and who you are with. Use code words if there is any risk of the note being read by strangers.
4. **A runner.** In a group, name who carries messages on foot or by bike, and what route they use.
5. **Signals.** Agree on simple signals, such as a particular cloth in a window, chalk marks at a gate, or three whistle blasts, that mean "here" or "need help." Keep them simple and tell no one outside the group.
6. **An out-of-area contact.** If you can reach any phone at any time, call the person outside the region and leave your status.

## Safety and sense
- Do not walk through danger to reach a meeting place. If the route is blocked or unsafe, use the backup or wait.
- Children should know the meeting places and have them written on a card in their pocket, with a trusted adult's name and number.
- Tell your children's school or your workplace where your family will go.
- Do not post your meeting places or notes in public.

## Practice the Emergency level
Practice makes it real. Once a year, walk or drive each route to each meeting place with the whole family and time it. Find the problems on a calm day.

## Checklist
- Choose two meeting places and write them down.
- Write the time and waiting rule.
- Prepare a note card for each family member with names, numbers and meeting places.
- Choose and test signals.
- Name a runner if you are in a group.
- Walk the routes once a year.

> The Emergency level is the plan that works with no power, no signal and no gear. Keep it simple enough that a child can follow it.
