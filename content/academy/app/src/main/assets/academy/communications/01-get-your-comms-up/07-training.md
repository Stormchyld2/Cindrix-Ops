Owning a radio does not make you a radio operator. Skills fade without practice, and an emergency is the worst time to learn which button is which. Take a course, read, and watch, in that order, then practice.

## The order that works
1. **Take a course.** A hands-on class beats any video. A good class teaches you your radio's menu, how to program it, and how to talk on the air, with an instructor who can fix your mistakes. Look for a local amateur radio club class, a preparedness group class, or a community emergency response team (CERT) class.
2. **Read.** A short book gives you a base to build on. Read the manual for your own radio first, from cover to cover. Then read one or two practical field guides on your type of radio.
3. **Watch.** Videos are best for seeing a procedure once you already know the idea. They are not a substitute for practice.
4. **Practice every month.** A short check-in each week beats a long session once a year.

## What a good course covers
- How to turn on, change channels and set power.
- How to program frequencies, offsets and tones.
- How to talk on the air, with prowords and the phonetic alphabet.
- How to join a net and how to run one.
- The rules for your license and your service.
- How to troubleshoot: weak signal, no audio, no repeater.

## A monthly practice plan
- **Week 1:** Radio check with a partner. Rotate who calls.
- **Week 2:** Practice a written message, send it, and read it back.
- **Week 3:** Try a different location, like a hill or a car, and note the range.
- **Week 4:** Run through your PACE plan. Try the next method when the first one fails.

## Where to find training
- Local amateur radio clubs, many of which hold classes for new GMRS and ham operators.
- Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) programs.
- Your preparedness group or a local radio class that teaches handhelds, such as the Project 22:3 Baofeng 101 and 201 classes.
- Free online material from the ARRL, FEMA and other public sources.

## Suggested reading list
- Your radio's own manual.
- *The Guerrilla's Guide to the Baofeng Radio* by NC Scout.
- *Basic SHTF Radio* by Don Shift.
- The AmRRON basic prepper communications videos.

When you have internet, find the current links. Save the files to the drive so you have them without it.

## Checklist
- Sign up for a hands-on radio class near you.
- Read your own radio's manual from start to finish.
- Read at least one practical field guide.
- Schedule a weekly practice call.
- Run your PACE plan once a quarter.
- Keep a log of who you called, when, and how well you were heard.

> Practice is the plan. A short call every week keeps the skill fresh.
