The shared vocabulary of radio makes communication clear and unambiguous. Sloppy procedure creates confusion, which is exactly what you cannot afford in an emergency. Each word below has one meaning. Use it that way.

## The core words
- **"This is."** Separates the station you are calling from your own. "Wren, this is Fox."
- **"Over."** You have finished speaking and expect a reply.
- **"Out."** You have finished and expect no reply. The conversation is over. **Never say "over and out"**, because the two words contradict each other.
- **"Copy."** I have received and understood your message. "Copy" is preferred over "received" and "acknowledged." Do not use the old Morse code "QSL."
- **"Roger."** The quickest way to say you understood. It comes from the Morse code letter R, meaning received.
- **"Say again."** Please repeat all or part of that message. Do not say "please repeat," which can be confused with an order to repeat a firing command in military use.
- **"Stand by."** Wait. I cannot respond right now, but I will.
- **"Wilco."** Short for "will comply." I understand and I will do it. Do not combine it with "roger," because "wilco" already means you understood.
- **"Affirmative" and "Negative."** Yes and no. "Yes" and "no" are easy to lose in static.
- **"Do you copy?" or "Do you read me?"** A question asking if you are being understood.
- **"Break."** A short pause between parts of a long message. It is used sparingly. For a pause, just unkey the microphone and wait.
- **"Radio check."** Asks how clearly your signal is being received.
- **"Go ahead."** I am ready to receive. Send your message.

## Emergency words
- **"Emergency traffic."** Said after your identification when you call to report an emergency. Everyone else stops talking and listens.
- **"Mayday."** An international call for help from a vessel, vehicle or person in immediate danger of death. Said three times: "Mayday, Mayday, Mayday." Use it only when a life is at stake. It comes from the French "m'aidez," meaning "help me."
- **"Pan-pan."** An urgent message that is not life-threatening, such as a stranded vehicle. Said three times.
- **"Silence."** Stop transmitting. Used by net control to clear a channel for emergency traffic.

## Words and habits to avoid
- Avoid "Q" signals such as QSL and QTH on public service nets. Plain words are clearer.
- Avoid slang, jokes and unclear shorthand.
- Do not say "please" or "thank you" over a busy channel. They add time and no meaning.
- Do not say "over and out."

## Station roles
- **Net control.** The person who runs a group conversation (a net) and has the final say on who talks.
- **Tactical call.** A name for a job or place, such as "Base," "Medical" or "Checkpoint One," rather than your own callsign.
- **Simplex.** Radio-to-radio talk, with no repeater.
- **Repeater.** A tall radio that re-sends your message and extends your range.
- **Squelch.** The setting that mutes the radio when no signal is there.
- **PTT.** Push-to-talk. The button you hold to transmit.

## Checklist
- Learn the 12 core words so you can say them without thinking.
- Practice in pairs, switching roles each time.
- Write the emergency words on your channel card.
- Agree on tactical calls for your group.

> Never say "over and out." Choose one or the other.
