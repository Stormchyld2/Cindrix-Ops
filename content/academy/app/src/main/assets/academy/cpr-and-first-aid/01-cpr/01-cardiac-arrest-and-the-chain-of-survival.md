Cardiac arrest is when the heart suddenly stops pumping effectively. It is not the same as a heart attack, but a heart attack can cause one.

## Heart attack vs cardiac arrest
- **Heart attack:** a blocked artery cuts blood to part of the heart muscle. The person is usually awake, with chest pain or pressure, and the heart keeps beating.
- **Cardiac arrest:** the heart's electrical system fails, and the heart quivers or stops. The person **collapses, does not respond and is not breathing normally.** Without help, death follows in minutes.

## Why bystanders matter
Most cardiac arrests happen at home. In the United States, survival is much higher when a bystander starts CPR quickly. CPR can **double or triple** a person's chance of survival, and an AED shock within minutes can raise it much more. Each minute without CPR and a shock lowers the chance of survival.

## The adult chain of survival
1. **Recognize** the emergency and **call 911.**
2. **Start CPR** immediately. High-quality chest compressions keep blood flowing to the brain.
3. **Use an AED** as soon as it arrives.
4. **Advanced care** from emergency medical services.
5. **Hospital care** and treatment.
6. **Recovery:** rehabilitation, emotional support and long-term care.

Steps 1 to 3 are done by people like you.

## Who gets cardiac arrest?
Anyone: adults of all ages, children, athletes, people with no known heart problem. Causes include heart disease, electrical problems, drowning, drug overdose, choking, electrocution and severe injury.

## Signs
- **Collapse:** sudden, with no warning.
- **No response** to tapping and shouting.
- **No breathing, or only gasping.** Gasping is a sign of cardiac arrest and does not count as normal breathing. People may gasp for the first few minutes.
- **No pulse** (but do not waste time checking, since untrained people often cannot find one).
- **Possible seizure-like movements** at the start.

## What to do: the short version
1. **Check the scene** is safe.
2. **Tap and shout:** "Are you okay?"
3. **If no response and not breathing normally,** shout for help.
4. **Call 911** or have someone call and get an AED. If alone, put the phone on speaker.
5. **Start chest compressions** and don't stop.
6. **Use the AED** when it arrives.
7. **Continue until** help arrives, the person moves, or you are too tired to go on.

## If you are unsure
**Do CPR.** The risk of hurting someone who does not need it is small, and the risk of not acting is death. Many states' Good Samaritan laws protect you.

## Checklist
- Know the difference between heart attack and cardiac arrest
- Know the signs of cardiac arrest, including gasping
- Know the first steps: check, call, compress, shock

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
