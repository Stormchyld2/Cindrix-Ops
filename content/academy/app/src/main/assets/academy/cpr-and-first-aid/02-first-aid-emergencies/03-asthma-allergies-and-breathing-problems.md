Trouble breathing is always serious. Learn the common causes and what to do.

## Asthma
Asthma narrows the airways. Triggers include allergens, smoke, cold air, exercise, infection and stress.

### Signs of an attack
- **Wheezing** (a whistling sound), coughing, **chest tightness.**
- **Shortness of breath,** fast breathing.
- **Trouble speaking** in full sentences.
- **Pulling in** at the neck, ribs or belly with each breath.
- **Blue or gray lips** (severe).

### What to do
1. **Stop and sit up.** Stay calm. Panic makes it worse.
2. **Help them use their prescribed quick-relief inhaler** (rescue inhaler) as their doctor's **asthma action plan** says. Use a **spacer** if they have one. **Never use someone else's inhaler without authorization,** and never guess at the number of puffs. Follow the plan or the label.
3. **Move away from the trigger.**
4. **Breathe slowly** with them.
5. **Call 911 if** they are struggling to speak, blue, not improving after the rescue inhaler, or it is their first attack. In a severe attack, do not wait.
6. **Stay with them** and be ready for CPR.

### Be prepared
- **Have an asthma action plan** from a doctor.
- **Keep a rescue inhaler** in every bag, car and kit. Check expiration dates.
- **Avoid triggers,** and keep air clean (see the Wildfire course for smoke).
- **Teach** family and friends.

## Anaphylaxis
A severe allergic reaction with breathing trouble, throat swelling, hives, vomiting and dizziness. **Epinephrine** is the treatment, and it must be given early. **Call 911.** See the Medical course.

## Choking
A blocked airway. **Back blows and abdominal thrusts.** See the Medical course.

## Croup (children)
A barking cough, hoarse voice and noisy breathing, often at night. **Stay calm,** hold the child upright, and breathe cool or moist air (the outdoors or a steamy bathroom). **Call 911** if they struggle to breathe, drool, turn blue or are too tired to cry.

## Smoke and carbon monoxide
- **Smoke inhalation:** get to fresh air and call 911.
- **Carbon monoxide poisoning:** headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, with no fever, especially if several people are affected or a heater or generator is running. **Get everyone out into fresh air and call 911.** Do not go back in.

## When breathing stops
Begin **rescue breaths and CPR.**

## Panic attacks
A panic attack can feel like a heart attack, with rapid breathing, chest tightness and fear. If you are not sure, **treat it as serious.** For a known panic attack, help the person slow their breathing, speak calmly and reassure them.

## Checklist
- Know the signs of an asthma attack
- Rescue inhalers in kits, and everyone knows the plan
- Know the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning
- Know when to call 911 for breathing trouble

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
