## Heart attack signs
- **Chest pain or pressure,** like squeezing, fullness or heaviness. It lasts more than a few minutes, or goes away and comes back.
- **Pain spreading** to the arm (often left), shoulder, back, neck, jaw or stomach.
- **Shortness of breath,** with or without chest discomfort.
- **Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness.**
- **Women** may have more subtle signs: unusual fatigue, shortness of breath, nausea or vomiting, back or jaw pain, with or without chest pain.
- Older adults and people with diabetes may have **mild or no pain.**

### What to do
1. **Call 911.** Say "possible heart attack."
2. **Have the person rest** in a comfortable position, often sitting with knees bent.
3. **Loosen tight clothing.**
4. **Aspirin:** the dispatcher may tell you to have the person chew a regular aspirin if they are not allergic and have no bleeding problem. **Follow the dispatcher and the label.** Do not give it on your own judgment if unsure.
5. **If they have prescribed heart medicine** (such as nitroglycerin), help them take it as directed by their doctor.
6. **Stay with them,** keep them calm and watch them.
7. **Be ready to start CPR** and use an AED if they collapse.
8. **Do not let them** walk around, drive or eat.

## Stroke signs: BE FAST
A stroke is a blockage or bleed in the brain. Remember **BE FAST:**
- **B:** **Balance.** Sudden loss of balance or coordination.
- **E:** **Eyes.** Sudden vision trouble in one or both eyes.
- **F:** **Face.** One side of the face droops. Ask them to smile.
- **A:** **Arm.** One arm is weak or drifts down when raised.
- **S:** **Speech.** Slurred or strange speech, or confusion. Ask them to repeat a simple sentence.
- **T:** **Time.** If you see any of these, **call 911 right away** and **note the time** the symptoms began, or when the person was last seen normal.

Other signs: sudden severe headache, numbness (especially on one side), confusion, trouble walking.

### What to do
1. **Call 911.** Say "possible stroke."
2. **Note the time** symptoms started. Treatment depends on it.
3. **Keep the person calm,** lying down with the head and shoulders slightly raised.
4. **Do not give** food, drink or medicines, including aspirin. A stroke caused by bleeding can be made worse.
5. **Check breathing** and be ready to start CPR.
6. **If they vomit,** turn them on their side.
7. **Stay with them** until help arrives.

## TIA (mini-stroke)
Symptoms that **go away** within minutes are still an emergency. A TIA is a warning. **Call 911.**

## Prevention
- Control blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes.
- Do not smoke.
- Stay active and eat well.
- Take medicines as prescribed.
- Have regular checkups.

## Checklist
- Know the signs of a heart attack and stroke
- Know BE FAST
- Know to call 911 and note the time
- Know not to give a stroke patient food, drink or aspirin

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
