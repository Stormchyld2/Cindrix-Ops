An **AED (automated external defibrillator)** is a small, portable device that checks the heart rhythm and, if needed, delivers an electric shock to restart a normal rhythm. They are made for ordinary people. The device talks you through each step. **You cannot hurt someone who needs a shock by using one.** It will not shock if a shock is not needed.

## Where to find AEDs
- Airports, malls, gyms, schools, offices, hotels, churches, casinos and stadiums.
- Some police cars, fire trucks and buses.
- Many are in **wall cabinets** that sound an alarm when opened.
- Use a phone app or the program's **map** of AED locations if available, and note those near you.
- **911 dispatchers** often know where the nearest AED is.

## Using an AED step by step
1. **Start CPR** while someone fetches the AED.
2. **Turn on** the AED (press the power button or open the lid). Listen to the prompts.
3. **Expose the chest.** Cut or remove clothing. **Dry** the skin if wet. **Remove** medication patches, wiping any residue. **Shave** a very hairy chest quickly if the kit has a razor.
4. **Peel and apply the pads** as shown in the pictures on the pads.
   - **Adult:** one pad on the **upper right chest,** just below the collarbone. The other on the **lower left side,** under the armpit.
   - **Child or infant:** use **child pads.** If the chest is small, put one pad in the **center of the chest** and one on the **back,** between the shoulder blades.
5. **Plug in the connector** if the device needs it.
6. **Stand clear.** Say, "Clear!" and make sure no one is touching the person. **The AED analyzes the rhythm.**
7. **If a shock is advised,** the AED will charge. When it says, **make sure everyone is clear,** then press the **shock button** (some devices shock on their own).
8. **Resume CPR immediately** after the shock, beginning with compressions, for **2 minutes,** until the AED prompts again.
9. **Follow the prompts** and repeat until help arrives or the person recovers.
10. **Keep the pads on** even if the person wakes. Leave the AED on.

## Cautions
- **Do not place pads** over an **implanted device** (a hard lump under the skin, usually near the collarbone). Place the pad at least an inch away.
- **Do not place pads on a medication patch.**
- **Do not use it in standing water.** Move the person to a dry spot and dry the chest.
- **Do not touch the person** during analysis and shock.
- **Do not use on a person who is awake and breathing,** since the machine will not shock, and you should not apply pads.
- **Metal surfaces** are okay for use. Keep the pads dry.
- **If the pads will not stick,** dry the chest, remove hair, and press firmly.

## What AEDs say
Different models differ, but they all give clear voice prompts. Some also give **CPR coaching** (a metronome, depth feedback). Follow their directions.

## Maintenance (if you own one)
- Check the **status light** monthly.
- **Replace pads** and the battery before they expire (usually every 2 to 5 years).
- **Keep pads** for adults and children.
- **Store** at the recommended temperature.
- **Register** the AED with your local EMS so dispatchers know.
- **Train** the people nearby.

## Practice
Take a class and use a trainer AED. Find the AEDs where you live, work and shop, so you can run to them.

## Checklist
- Know how to use an AED
- Know where the nearest AEDs are
- Know the pad positions, adult and child
- Know the cautions: water, implants and patches
- If you own one, check it monthly

> This is education, not a substitute for a hands-on CPR and first aid class from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or similar. It never gives medicine amounts. Follow product labels and your doctor, and call 911 in an emergency.
