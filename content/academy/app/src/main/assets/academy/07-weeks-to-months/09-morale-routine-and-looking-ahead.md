Long emergencies test patience. Routine, purpose and connection keep people well.

## Routine
- **Set** regular times for waking, meals, chores and bed.
- **Assign** daily chores to everyone.
- **Keep** school, reading and learning for children.
- **Plan** something to look forward to each week.
- **Make** the home as clean and orderly as you can.

## Purpose
- **Give** everyone a role.
- **Set** goals: the garden, a repair, a skill.
- **Help** others.

## Connection
- **Eat** together.
- **Talk** every day.
- **Check** on neighbors.
- **Share** stories, music and games.
- **Keep in touch** with family at a distance (by radio, letters or whatever works).

## Care for yourself
- **Sleep, eat, drink** and move.
- **Spend time** outdoors in daylight.
- **Limit** news. Check at set times.
- **Use** relaxation, prayer, meditation or hobbies.
- **Take breaks** from caregiving.
- **Forgive** yourself and others. Everyone is under strain.

## Children
- **Keep** routines.
- **Be honest** at their level.
- **Give** them jobs and play time.
- **Watch** for changes: nightmares, clinging, anger. Give extra attention.

## Watch for trouble
Signs of depression or serious distress: loss of interest, withdrawal, hopelessness, sleep or appetite changes, or thoughts of harming oneself. **Reach out.** In the US, call or text **988,** or the **Disaster Distress Helpline at 1-800-985-5990.**

## Looking ahead
- **Plan** the transition back to normal.
- **Keep** records of what you learn.
- **Hold** an after action review.
- **Rebuild** your supplies and your plan.
- **Share** what you learned with neighbors.
- **Practice** and keep learning.
