Days without power are hard. Weeks need a real plan.

## Heating safely
- **Use only equipment made for indoor heating** and installed correctly: a wood stove or fireplace with a clean chimney and a tight flue; a vented gas or propane heater; or an indoor-safe propane heater with an oxygen depletion sensor, used as the manual says.
- **Clean chimneys** each year.
- **Keep flammables** at least 3 feet away.
- **Use carbon monoxide detectors** with battery backup on each floor, and test them.
- **Never** use a charcoal grill, outdoor propane heater, camp stove or generator inside.
- **Never** use an oven to heat the home.
- **Keep a fire extinguisher** handy.
- **Fuel:** store firewood dry, seasoned for 6 to 12 months. Know how much you burn.
- **Close off** rooms, and insulate windows with plastic and blankets.
- **Dress in layers** and use sleeping bags.
- **Use a small room.**

## Cooling
- **Use fans** powered by battery or a small solar panel.
- **Close** blinds and curtains on the sunny side.
- **Open** windows at night.
- **Go to** a cooling center.
- **Wet** cloths and a spray bottle.
- **Stay hydrated.**

## Power
- **Generators:** outdoors only, on a dry level surface, 20 feet from the house, with the exhaust pointing away. **Do not backfeed** into the house wiring. Use a transfer switch installed by an electrician, or run extension cords. Let it cool before refueling. Store fuel safely in approved containers, away from the house.
- **Fuel stability:** treat gasoline with stabilizer and rotate. Propane keeps for years.
- **Solar panels and power stations:** quiet and safe indoors for charging phones, radios and lights, and sometimes small appliances. Plan to match your needs to their capacity.
- **Batteries:** rechargeable AA and AAA with a charger, a car battery, a deep-cycle battery.
- **Prioritize:** medical devices, communications, light, refrigeration for medicine, then comfort.
- See the **Power Outage Preparedness** course for detailed guidance.

## Food and refrigeration
- **Eat** perishables first.
- **Use** ice and coolers.
- **Plan** meals from storage.

## Mental and physical comfort
- **Keep** a routine.
- **Use** light in the evening for activities.
- **Play** games and read.

## Checklist
- A safe heat plan and carbon monoxide alarms
- A cooling plan for hot spells
- A generator or solar plan, safely used
- A list of power priorities

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
