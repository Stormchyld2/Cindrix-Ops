Longer disruptions can raise crime and tension, but cooperation is the strongest protection. This lesson is general safety information, not legal advice. Follow your local laws.

## Cooperation first
- **Neighbors who know each other** deter crime and help in emergencies. A watchful, friendly street is hard for outsiders to exploit.
- **Organize a neighborhood watch** and patrol, in coordination with local police where possible. Watch and report. Do not confront.
- **Share** information.
- **Look out** for the vulnerable.

## Layers of protection for your home
1. **Deterrence:** good lighting, trimmed shrubs, visible locks and neighbors who know you.
2. **Delay:** strong doors (solid core, long screws in the strike plate), deadbolts, window locks and pins, security bars on sliding doors.
3. **Detection:** door and window alarms, motion lights, a dog, and neighbors.
4. **Response:** a plan to call for help, leave, or shelter in a safe room.

## Keep a low profile
See the low-profile lesson. Do not advertise supplies.

## Fire safety first
Fire is far more likely than a break-in. Have **smoke alarms, extinguishers** and an **exit plan.** See your fire department for advice.

## Keep records and documents safe
- **Store** copies of documents in a secure place.
- **Use** a safe or a hidden place.

## If someone comes to the door
- **Do not open** it for strangers. Speak through the door.
- **Ask** for identification from claimed officials. Call to verify.
- **Be polite and firm.**
- **Do not** let anyone in.

## If someone enters your home
- **Get your family to a safe room,** and call 911.
- **Do not confront** if you can avoid it. Property can be replaced.
- **Follow** the dispatcher's instructions.

## Self-defense and firearms
- **Know the law** on self-defense, firearms and use of force where you live. Laws differ widely.
- **Training** in de-escalation, first aid, and, if you own firearms, safe handling and storage is essential. Lock them away from children. Use certified courses. See the Self-Defense course.
- **Avoidance** and de-escalation come first. The best fight is the one you avoid.
- **Using force** has serious legal and moral consequences.

## Scams and fraud
Disasters bring scams: fake contractors, charity scams, price-gouging, and fake officials. **Verify** before paying. **Do not pay upfront** for repairs. **Report** fraud to authorities.

## Dealing with outsiders
- **Be kind,** and share when you can, but protect your family.
- **Avoid** inviting strangers into your home.
- **Direct** people to official aid, shelters and distribution points.

## Check your emotions
Fear can make people act poorly. **Stay calm, talk with your group, and keep to the plan.**

## Checklist
- A friendly relationship with neighbors
- Good locks and lighting, and a safe room plan
- Smoke alarms and extinguishers
- A good understanding of local laws

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
