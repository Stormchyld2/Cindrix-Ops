Having a route is not the same as being able to drive it. This lesson covers how to choose routes and how to behave on the road when everyone else is leaving too.

## Choose routes before the day
- Plan **at least two routes** in different directions.
- Use **paper maps** and mark them. Highlight highways, secondary roads and back roads.
- Note **choke points:** bridges, tunnels, underpasses, railroad crossings and narrow canyons. Any of these can fail or jam. Think about alternates around each.
- Note **fuel, water, restrooms and shelter** along each route.
- Note **hazards:** low-water crossings, steep or unpaved sections, rockfall or burn areas.
- **Drive each route once** in daylight so you know it.
- Record the **distance and time** with and without traffic.

## On evacuation day
1. **Leave early,** before severe weather arrives or traffic builds.
2. **Fuel up first** if it is safe, or leave with at least half a tank, which should be your habit anyway.
3. **Go one car per family.**
4. **Follow recommended routes.** Official routes are cleared and staffed. Shortcuts may be blocked.
5. **Keep your radio on** for road and shelter updates.
6. **Keep spacing** and follow officers and signs. In some events, officials reverse lanes to speed traffic out.
7. **Share your plan** with your out-of-area contact before you lose signal.

## Traffic jams
- Stay calm and keep your distance.
- Do not drive on the shoulder unless directed. You may block responders.
- Keep fuel in mind. In long jams, shut off the engine when stopped for long periods if it is safe to do so.
- Keep water, snacks and a bathroom plan (see the vehicle kit lesson).
- Do not stop to help strangers at the roadside if it puts your family in danger. Call it in instead.

## Water on the road
The National Weather Service teaches **Turn Around, Don't Drown.**
- **Six inches** of fast-moving floodwater can knock over an adult.
- **Two feet** of rushing water can carry away a vehicle, pickups and SUVs included.
- You cannot tell how deep water is or what lies beneath it, especially at night.
- Never try to cross a low-water crossing if you cannot see the bottom.
- Turn around and find another way.
- Watch for roads weakened by receded floodwater. They can collapse under a vehicle's weight.

## Smoke and fire
- Close windows and vents, and set the air to recirculate.
- Drive with headlights on. Slow down.
- Do not drive through heavy smoke or flames. If trapped, park in a clear area away from vegetation, close windows, lie on the floor under blankets and call 911 (see the Wildfire course).

## Downed power lines
If a power line falls on your car, **stay inside** until trained responders arrive, because of shock risk. If you must leave because of fire, jump clear with both feet together and shuffle away without touching the car and the ground at once.

## Avoid hazards in some emergencies
In events that can affect road stability, such as earthquakes, avoid overpasses, bridges, power lines, signs and other structures that could fall.

## Control cruise
- Turn off cruise control on wet, icy or snowy roads. It can increase wheel spin during a skid and set speeds too high for standing water.

## If the car is hard to control
Pull over, stop and set the parking brake.

## Checklist
- Two routes marked on paper maps
- Choke points and alternates noted
- Fuel, water and rest stops listed
- Routes driven once in daylight
- Family agreed on turning around at floodwater

> It is better to arrive late than not at all.
