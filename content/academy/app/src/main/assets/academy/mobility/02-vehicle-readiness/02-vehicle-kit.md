Extra gear you keep in your vehicle.

Some emergencies force you to leave home on foot, but there are many, such as a wildfire approaching, where you can leave by vehicle. Also consider emergencies that happen while you are away from home, at work or at the grocery store, where your car is all you have. Think of your car or truck as a giant **go bag.** The vehicle kit is a perfect supplement to your personal go bag.

## Hide it
Do not leave a huge bag of gear on the back seat, visible to a passer-by. That is an easy way to get a window smashed. Use the trunk, under the seats, the glovebox, the center console and the spare-tire well.

## Core kit (always in the vehicle)
Ready.gov names these as basic:
- Jumper cables (8 to 12 feet) and, better, a **battery jump box**
- Flares or a reflective triangle
- Ice scraper
- Car phone charger
- Blanket
- Map
- Cat litter or sand for tire traction

## Safety and signalling
- Fire extinguisher (rated for vehicle fires)
- Flashlight with spare batteries, or a hand-crank or solar lantern, and a headlamp
- Whistle
- Road flares, a small mirror for signalling, a white or bright distress flag or light sticks
- Bright cloth or sign to display in a window
- High-visibility vest

## Car-related items
- A quart of motor oil and a gallon of 50/50 antifreeze (where used)
- Funnel and clean rags
- Windshield washer fluid
- Tow strap or rope, bungee cords
- Tire repair kit, a tire inflator that plugs into the vehicle and a pressure gauge
- Tire chains or traction aids where required
- Small shovel
- Basic tools: pliers, flat and Phillips screwdrivers, adjustable wrench
- Pocket knife or multi-tool
- Duct tape and electrical tape
- Penetrating oil
- Work gloves
- Spare fuses and bulbs
- **Extra fuel** in approved containers, stored safely (see the next lesson)

## Medical and personal
- A basic first aid kit (see the Medical courses), plus a tourniquet and bleeding supplies if you have been trained
- A copy of your medical emergency wallet card
- Personal medicines for a day or two, rotated
- Disposable gloves
- Hand sanitizer, wipes, toilet paper
- Sunscreen and insect repellent

## Water, food and warmth
- **Water.** At least a gallon per person for a day, more in hot climates. Choose containers that tolerate heat and freezing. Check supplies in extreme weather.
- **Food.** Non-perishable, high-calorie, ready to eat: bars, nuts, canned food with a pull tab.
- **Warmth.** Emergency blankets, wool blanket or sleeping bag, spare socks, hat, gloves, rain poncho.
- **Shelter.** A tarp and cord.
- **Fire.** Lighters, waterproof matches, a metal can for heating.

## Communication and navigation
- Paper road atlas and local maps, with your routes marked
- Battery or hand-crank radio with NOAA weather band
- A handheld radio (see the Communications course)
- Notebook and pencil
- A printed card of contacts and rendezvous points

## Papers
- Copies of ID, registration and insurance in a waterproof bag
- Cash in small bills
- A list of key phone numbers

## Other helpful items
- Garbage bags and zip bags
- Water filter or purification tablets
- A mess kit
- Plastic sheeting and tape
- Waterproof boots and a change of clothes

## Care for the kit
- **Review it when the clocks change.** Replace food, water, batteries and medicines. Add winter or summer gear.
- **Write the date** of your last update on the checklist.
- Use the vehicle kit with your go bag, choosing items for different events and weather.
- Check water and food during extreme heat and freezing.

## Checklist
- Jumper cables and jump box
- Fire extinguisher
- Safety lights and flares
- Small shovel
- Basic first aid kit
- Get-home bag
- Maps
- Water and food
- Blankets and shelter tarp
- Kit checked when the clocks change

> A kit you cannot find in the dark is not a kit. Pack it so you can reach it.
