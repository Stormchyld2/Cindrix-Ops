Sooner or later someone in your group will be stuck in a car. This lesson tells you what to do.

## First moves after a breakdown
1. **Get off the road** if you can. Pull as far onto the shoulder as possible.
2. **Turn on hazard lights.**
3. **Set the parking brake** and make everyone aware of traffic.
4. **Decide: stay or go.** Most of the time, stay in the vehicle.
5. **Call for help.** Describe your location (road, mile marker, direction), your problem and the condition of anyone with you. Stay on the line until you know who you spoke with and what happens next.
6. **Make yourself visible:** reflective triangle, flares, a bright cloth on the antenna or door handle.
7. **Stay out of traffic.** Do not stand between the car and traffic. If you must leave the car, move well off the road, behind a barrier.

## Stranded in winter
- **Stay in your vehicle.** Walking in a storm is dangerous, especially if you could lose your way.
- **Conserve energy.** Avoid pushing the car or shoveling snow, which can cause a heart attack or injury. Sweaty clothes lose insulation and raise the risk of hypothermia.
- **Stay calm** and think clearly.
- **Protect against carbon monoxide.** Run the engine only when the exhaust pipe is clear of snow and objects. Wind-driven or freezing snow can block the exhaust and push deadly carbon monoxide into the car. Crack a window slightly for fresh air.
- **Prevent overheating.** Keep the radiator clear of snow.
- **Conserve fuel and heat.** Run the engine in short intervals, about **10 minutes at a time.** Use the dome light only at night and only while the engine is running.
- **Stay warm and keep circulating.** Loosen tight clothing, change positions often, huddle with others, rub your hands together or tuck them under your armpits, and periodically take off your shoes and rub your feet.
- **Signal rescuers.** Tie a bright cloth to your antenna or door handle.

Warning signs of **hypothermia:** shivering, confusion, slurred speech, drowsiness and clumsiness. Warm the person gradually and get medical help. See the medical courses.

## Stranded in summer heat
- Never leave anyone, especially a child or pet, in a parked car.
- Run the air conditioning sparingly. Keep the shade up. Drink water.
- Watch for heat illness: headache, dizziness, nausea, heavy sweating then dry skin. Move to shade, cool the person and call for help.

## Fire in the vehicle
1. Pull over, switch off the engine.
2. Get everyone out and away, upwind, at least 100 feet.
3. Call 911.
4. Do not open the hood. A small fire can flare up.
5. Use an extinguisher only on a small fire and only if safe.

## Getting home on foot
If you must walk, use your **get-home bag.** A get-home bag is smaller than a go bag and holds what you need to cover the distance from work or town to home.

Contents:
- Comfortable walking shoes or boots and socks (keep them at work or in the car).
- Water and a filter, and food for one or two days.
- Rain jacket, hat, gloves.
- Flashlight or headlamp.
- Paper map with routes marked.
- Phone power bank and cable.
- Small first aid kit and blister care.
- Whistle, mirror and a bright item.
- Cash.
- Copy of your contact and rendezvous card.

Plan your walk:
1. **Know the distance** from work or regular places to home. Most adults walk about 3 miles an hour on flat ground, slower with a load.
2. **Pick routes** that avoid choke points, bridges that may be damaged and areas of crowds.
3. **Travel in daylight** when possible, with a buddy.
4. **Tell your contact** your route and expected arrival.
5. **Avoid confrontation.** Blend in, keep calm, keep moving.
6. **Rest and drink** regularly.

## Checklist
- Get-home bag at work and in the car
- Spare shoes and socks
- Distances and routes from regular places written down
- Hazard lights, triangle and flares in the car
- Everyone knows to keep the exhaust clear of snow

> Stay with the vehicle unless you are certain of a safe place and the way to it.
