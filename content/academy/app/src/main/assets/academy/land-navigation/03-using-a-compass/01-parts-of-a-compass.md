There are two common hand compasses. Learn the parts of both and you can use almost any.

## The baseplate compass (the clear plastic one)
- **Baseplate.** The clear plastic body. It has a ruler on the edge and lines that you can lay along a map.
- **Direction-of-travel arrow.** Printed on the baseplate. It points the way you will walk.
- **Rotating housing (bezel).** The dial you turn. It has degree marks.
- **Orienting arrow.** A drawn arrow inside the housing that turns with the dial.
- **Orienting lines.** Lines inside the housing, parallel to the orienting arrow.
- **Magnetic needle.** Floats in the liquid. The red end points to magnetic north.
- **Index line.** The mark where you read the bearing, at the base of the direction-of-travel arrow.

## The lensatic compass (the military one)
A metal case with a lid, a sighting wire and a small lens. You hold it up to your eye, line up the wire with a far-off object, and read the dial through the lens. It is sturdy and quick for sighting, but it takes more practice than a baseplate compass for map work.

## What to buy
A baseplate compass with a **declination adjustment**, a **luminous** needle or marks, and a **ruler in the scale of your maps** is a good choice. A simple one costs little. Buy two and keep one in a bag, never on the same ring as your keys.

## Care
- Keep it away from steel and magnets. An arm's length from vehicles, power lines and large metal objects. A hand's width from phones, radios, knives and belt buckles.
- Check that the needle swings freely and does not have a bubble that blocks it.
- A needle that points the wrong way after a lightning strike or a strong magnet is **reversed**. Test it against a trusted one.

> Tie a lanyard to your compass so you do not drop it in water.

## Going deeper: choose and test your compass
A reliable compass is among the best-value pieces of kit you can own.

## What to look for
- A **liquid-filled capsule** that settles quickly.
- A **rotating bezel** marked in 2-degree steps.
- **Luminous marks** for night use.
- **Declination adjustment,** which saves arithmetic.
- A **clear baseplate** with rulers and a scale that matches your maps.
- A **sighting mirror** for accuracy, if you can afford it.
- A **lanyard,** so it does not get lost.

## Brands and cost
Established makers such as Suunto, Silva and Brunton make dependable models, often between $20 and $60. Avoid toy compasses.

## Carry two
Keep one in your pack and a spare in a pocket or on your bag strap. Keep a simple compass in each vehicle and kit.

## Learn its parts
Name each part on your compass: baseplate, direction-of-travel arrow, bezel, orienting arrow, orienting lines, needle and index line. The next lessons use them.

## Care
Keep it away from magnets, steel and electronics, and avoid heat, which can make bubbles appear in the liquid.
