Seed is alive. Heat, moisture and light shorten its life. Cool, dry and dark storage makes it last.

## The rule of 100
A handy rule: **the temperature in degrees Fahrenheit plus the percent relative humidity should add up to 100 or less** for good storage. A room at 60 °F and 40 percent humidity (a sum of 100) is good. 70 °F and 50 percent is not.

## What you need
- **Airtight containers:** glass jars with tight lids, or heavy foil or Mylar bags, or thick zip-top freezer bags inside a jar.
- **Desiccant** to absorb moisture: silica gel packets, or dried rice or powdered milk wrapped in a paper towel or coffee filter.
- **Labels** and a pen that does not smudge.
- **A cool, dark place:** a closet in the coolest part of the home, a basement, or a refrigerator.
- **A notebook or the Seed vault screen** to log the contents.

## Steps
1. **Dry the seed completely.** Seed must be thoroughly dry before storage. Moist seed molds or rots. Spread seed on a screen or paper plate in a warm, airy place away from direct sun for a week or two. Seeds should snap, not bend.
2. **Clean** the seed: remove chaff, bits of fruit and weak seed.
3. **Put each kind** in its own labeled envelope or packet.
4. **Label** with the crop, variety, year and where it came from.
5. **Place** the packets in an airtight jar with a desiccant packet.
6. **Seal** the jar, and keep it cool and dark.
7. **Check** every few months for moisture, mold or pests, and refresh the desiccant.

## Refrigerator and freezer
- **Refrigerator:** a good choice for most seed. Keep it in a sealed jar with desiccant, so condensation does not wet it. Let the jar warm to room temperature before opening, so moisture does not form on the seed.
- **Freezer:** works very well for long-term storage if the seed is **very dry** and sealed in airtight containers. Many seed banks use it. Moist seed can be damaged by ice crystals. Warm to room temperature before opening.
- **Do not** store seed in a damp basement, a hot attic or a garage.

## Pests
- **Weevils and moths** can infest beans, peas and corn. Freeze seed for a few days before storing to kill eggs.
- **Mice** love seeds. Use metal or glass containers.
- **Check** stored seed regularly.

## Protect from disaster
- **Keep a second copy** of the most important seed in another location.
- **Keep** some seed in a waterproof container in your go-bag or bug out location.
- **Keep** a list of what you have.

## What not to do
- Do not store seed in the heat.
- Do not store seed in plastic bags in a humid place.
- Do not mix varieties.
- Do not forget to label. An unlabeled packet is nearly useless.

## Checklist
- Jars with desiccant and labels
- A cool, dark storage spot
- A seed inventory in the program
- A second location for key seed

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
