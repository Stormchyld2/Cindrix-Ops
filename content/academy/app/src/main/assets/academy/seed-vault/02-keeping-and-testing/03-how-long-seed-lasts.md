Different crops stay alive for different lengths of time. These are typical ranges when seed is stored cool and dry. Test older seed.

## Short-lived (about 1 year)
- **Onions,** leeks, chives and parsnips
- **Parsley** and **salsify**
- **Spinach** can be short, 1 to 2 years
Buy fresh seed for these each year, or test before sowing.

## Medium (2 to 3 years)
- **Corn** (sweet corn is shorter-lived than field corn)
- **Peppers**
- **Carrots**
- **Beans and peas,** often 3 years or more with good storage
- **Okra**
- **Lettuce** can be 3 or more
- **Sweet potato slips** do not store as seed.

## Longer (3 to 5 years)
- **Tomatoes**
- **Cabbage family:** cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, kale, collards and Brussels sprouts
- **Beets** and **chard**
- **Eggplant**
- **Radishes** and **turnips**
- **Peas** and **beans**
- **Lettuce**

## Long (5 years or more)
- **Cucumbers**
- **Melons**
- **Squash and pumpkin**
- **Watermelon**
- **Endive** and **celery**
- **Many herbs** such as basil

These charts are guides. Seed quality, how it was grown and how it was stored decide the real lifespan. A seed stored in a freezer may last far longer.

## Factors that matter
- **Temperature and humidity:** the biggest ones. Cooler and drier means longer life.
- **Initial quality:** seed from healthy plants, harvested ripe, lasts longer.
- **Species and variety.**
- **Pests and mold.**
- **Light.**

## Planning around it
- **Buy short-lived seed** (onions, parsnips, parsley, spinach) fresh each year, or save your own.
- **Use medium-lived seed** within three years, and test.
- **Treat long-lived seed** as your reserve, but still test every couple of years.
- **Keep a rotation schedule** so you plant and replace on time.

## If seed is old
Old seed is not useless. Test it. Sow it thicker. Combine a few seeds per hill. Expect fewer plants and plan accordingly.

## Seed banks and bigger scales
Seed banks keep seed for decades in freezers with controlled humidity. You can do the same at home on a small scale using a freezer and airtight containers.

## Checklist
- Know the life of each crop in your vault
- Buy short-lived seed fresh
- Test older seed before planting

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
