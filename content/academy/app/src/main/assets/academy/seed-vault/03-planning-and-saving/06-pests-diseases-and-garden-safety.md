Every garden has bugs, weeds and weather. Prevention is easier than rescue.

## Prevent first
- **Healthy soil** grows healthy plants.
- **Crop rotation** breaks pest and disease cycles.
- **Resistant varieties.**
- **Clean tools and containers.**
- **Good spacing and airflow.**
- **Water at the base,** in the morning.
- **Remove** diseased plants and debris. Do not compost badly diseased plants.
- **Mulch** to reduce weeds and soil splash.
- **Attract beneficial insects** such as ladybugs, lacewings and bees, with flowers like alyssum, dill, cilantro and marigolds.
- **Use row covers** to block insects, and netting to keep out birds.

## Common pests and simple answers
- **Aphids:** a strong spray of water, insecticidal soap, ladybugs.
- **Cabbage worms:** row cover, hand-picking.
- **Tomato hornworms:** hand-pick.
- **Squash bugs and vine borers:** hand-pick, row cover until flowering, plant resistant varieties.
- **Potato beetles:** hand-pick, mulch, rotate.
- **Slugs and snails:** traps, hand-pick at night, copper tape, remove hiding places.
- **Birds:** netting or scare devices.
- **Deer and rabbits:** fencing (a deer fence needs to be 8 feet high or use two lower fences), repellents.
- **Gophers and moles:** wire baskets under beds, traps.
- **Weeds:** mulch and hand-pull when small. A hoe on a dry day works.

## Diseases
- **Fungal diseases:** powdery mildew, blight, rust. Space plants, water the base, remove infected leaves, and rotate.
- **Bacterial and viral diseases:** remove and destroy infected plants. Control pests that spread them.
- **Blossom end rot:** a calcium and watering issue in tomatoes. Water evenly.
- **Late blight** in tomatoes and potatoes can destroy a crop in days. Watch for dark patches on leaves, and remove infected plants. Do not save seed or tubers from infected plants.

## Use pesticides carefully
- **Start with the least toxic options.**
- **Read and follow** the label.
- **Wear** protection.
- **Do not spray** when bees are active, or when it is windy.
- **Observe** the waiting time before harvest.
- **Store** safely, away from children.
- **Never reuse** pesticide containers.

## Garden safety
- **Wash produce** before eating. Soil can carry germs.
- **Wash hands** after gardening.
- **Keep** raw manure away from crops you will eat raw. Use composted manure, and apply it at least 90 to 120 days before harvest, as guidance advises.
- **Tetanus:** keep your shots current. Gardens expose you to soil and cuts.
- **Wear gloves, sun protection** and sturdy shoes.
- **Lift** with your legs.
- **Heat:** garden early and drink water.
- **Tools:** store them safely.
- **Pets and children:** keep them away from compost, chemicals and poisonous plants.
- **Poisonous plants:** do not eat anything you cannot identify. Some garden plants (rhubarb leaves, tomato leaves, green potatoes, castor beans, foxglove) are toxic.

## Wildlife and neighbors
- **Be a good neighbor.** Discuss gardens, share the harvest and keep it tidy.
- **Check** local rules about fences, livestock, water and compost.

## Checklist
- A rotation plan
- Row cover and netting
- A habit of checking plants weekly
- Gloves, a hat and clean tools

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
