A garden that feeds a family needs a plan based on calories and nutrition, not only on what looks nice.

## How many calories do you need
A rough figure is **2,000 calories per adult per day,** or about 730,000 calories a year. Children need less, and hard-working adults more. Pregnant and nursing women need extra.

## Which crops fill the gap
Here are rough yields per 100 square feet in good conditions, with the calories they give:
- **Potatoes:** 100 to 150 pounds. About 350 calories per pound. A very high number.
- **Sweet potatoes:** 50 to 100 pounds.
- **Winter squash:** 50 to 100 pounds, lasting for months in storage.
- **Dry beans:** 5 to 10 pounds of dry beans, protein-rich.
- **Corn (grain):** 10 to 20 pounds of dry grain.
- **Carrots and beets:** 100 to 150 pounds. Lower calories, but good nutrition.
- **Cabbage:** 100 to 150 pounds.
- **Tomatoes:** 100 to 200 pounds. Few calories, but great for flavor and vitamins.
These are estimates. Your yield depends on soil, water, variety, pests and skill.

## A rough sizing
- **Potatoes alone:** about 2,000 square feet could supply a lot of the calories for a family of four in a good year.
- **A mixed garden** of 1,000 to 2,000 square feet with potatoes, beans, corn, squash and greens can give a family of four a major part of its food.
- **Start much smaller** (100 to 400 square feet) and expand as skills grow.

## Nutrition: eat the rainbow
A diet of only potatoes or only beans is not enough. Aim for variety:
- **Carbohydrates and calories:** potatoes, corn, squash, grains.
- **Protein:** beans, peas, lentils, nuts, eggs and meat.
- **Fat:** oil, nuts, seeds, sunflowers, and animal fats.
- **Vitamin C:** peppers, tomatoes, cabbage, kale, potatoes, citrus.
- **Vitamin A:** carrots, sweet potatoes, squash, dark leafy greens.
- **Iron and calcium:** beans, leafy greens.
- **B vitamins:** whole grains, beans.
Greens such as kale, collards and chard are very nutrient-dense and easy to grow.

## Plan by season
- **Spring:** peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes, onions, potatoes.
- **Summer:** beans, corn, squash, tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers.
- **Fall:** carrots, beets, kale, cabbage, garlic (plant in fall for next year), and a second planting of greens.
- **Winter** (mild climates): kale, collards, leeks, and cover crops.
Use **succession planting:** sow small amounts every two weeks so food comes over a longer time.

## Storage crops
Choose crops that store: potatoes, winter squash, onions, garlic, carrots, beets, cabbage, apples, dry beans and grains. They carry food into winter.

## Make a plan
1. **List** the people you feed and their calorie needs.
2. **Choose** crops to cover calories, protein and vitamins.
3. **Estimate** space from the yields above.
4. **Draw** a garden map with rows or beds.
5. **Schedule** planting dates from your frost dates.
6. **Count** the seed you need.
Keep it in the binder, and use the program's Seed vault screen to track your seed.

## Do not forget
- **Water:** vegetables need about 1 inch of water per week.
- **Soil:** feed it with compost.
- **Labor:** a large garden takes many hours. Grow what you can care for.

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
