Stored food runs out. **Seed can make food again,** if you keep it well and plant it.

## The idea
- A packet of seed is small, light and cheap. It can become many pounds of food.
- Seed gives you food after your stored food is gone.
- Seed you save yourself costs nothing, and it keeps getting better suited to your soil and climate.
- Seed can also be shared and traded with neighbors, which builds community.

## What a seed vault is
A seed vault is a **planned, protected, labeled collection of seeds** that you can plant over several seasons. It is not a single packet in a drawer. It is a small system:
1. **A plan:** what you will grow, and how many calories and nutrients it should give.
2. **The seed:** the right varieties, bought or saved.
3. **Storage:** cool, dry, dark and sealed, protected from pests.
4. **Testing and rotating:** you know the seed still grows.
5. **Skill:** you know how to grow it and save more.

## Be honest about the limits
- **Growing food takes a season.** A seed vault is not a first-week plan. Stored food and water come first. The Survival basics and Basic Preparedness courses cover those.
- **Practice now.** A first garden often fails. Learn while you still have a store-bought backup.
- **Weather and pests** can wipe out a crop. Plant more than one kind of crop and more than once.
- **Land, water and time** are needed. Even a few containers help.
- **Calories take space.** A salad garden will not feed a family. Potatoes, beans, squash, corn and grains do.

## What a small garden can do
A well-planned garden of 200 to 400 square feet can produce a large share of a family's vegetables, and a good deal of calories if it includes potatoes, beans and winter squash. A bigger plot is needed for grains.

## The five principles
- **Prevention and hazard assessment:** know your climate, frost dates, soil and pests.
- **Preparedness:** keep seed, tools, water and a plan.
- **Response:** be able to plant quickly after a disruption.
- **Mitigation:** diversify crops and sites, and protect against drought and cold.
- **Recovery:** save seed, rebuild soil and share.

> Plant a little every year. A vault you never grow from is only a box of seed.

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.

## Check yourself
? What is a seed vault best used for?
- Replacing water storage
- Feeding a family in the first three days
+ Making food again after stored food runs out
- Replacing medicine
> Seed gives you food over time. Stored food and water cover the first days.

? Why practise growing now?
- Gardens need no skill
- Seed spoils if it is not planted this year
- It is required by law
+ A first garden often fails, and you can still eat from your stores
> Learning early costs little when you have a backup.

? Why is saved seed useful?
- It never needs testing
+ It costs nothing and suits your soil
- It always grows faster
- It cannot fail
> Saved seed from your own plants adapts to your conditions.

? Which crops give the most calories per space?
- Lettuce and herbs only
- Flowers
+ Potatoes, beans, squash and corn
- Radishes only
> Plan for calories, not only salad.
