Buy seed that fits **your climate, your skill and your appetite.**

## Start with your place
- **Find your USDA hardiness zone** (planthardiness.ars.usda.gov) and your **average last spring frost** and **first fall frost** dates. Your local Cooperative Extension office has these.
- **Know your soil** (sand, loam, clay), sun, water and wind.
- **Check your season length:** the days between last and first frost. Choose varieties whose "days to maturity" fit inside it.

## Buy from the right places
- **Local garden centers,** seed libraries and co-ops: seed that has done well in your area.
- **Reputable seed companies** that test their seed and list germination rates and the "packed for" year.
- **Seed swaps** and local growers.
- **Avoid** unknown sellers, "miracle seed" offers, bulk "survival seed" cans of unknown mixed quality (you may get a lot of crops you will not eat or that do not suit your climate), and seed more than a couple of years old without a test.

## What to buy first (calories and nutrition)
Think in four groups.
1. **Calorie crops:** potatoes (seed potatoes or small tubers), sweet potatoes (slips), corn (flour or dent corn), winter squash, and dry beans.
2. **Protein crops:** dry beans, peas, lentils, peanuts (in warm regions) and soybeans.
3. **Nutrient crops:** greens (kale, collards, chard, spinach), carrots, beets, tomatoes, peppers, onions, garlic, cabbage and broccoli.
4. **Easy, fast, and morale crops:** radishes, lettuce, green onions, herbs, zucchini and sunflowers.

## A starter list for a family of four
- Beans (bush and pole): 1 pound
- Peas: 1/2 pound
- Corn (dent, flint or flour): 1 pound
- Squash (winter and summer): 2 ounces each
- Tomatoes, peppers: 1 packet each of 2 or 3 varieties
- Carrots, beets, onions: 1 packet or more of each
- Kale, collards, chard, spinach, lettuce: 1 packet each
- Cabbage, broccoli: 1 packet each
- Herbs: basil, cilantro, parsley, oregano
- Cucumbers, melons: 1 packet each, if your season is long enough
Add **potatoes** from certified seed potatoes (not grocery store potatoes, which may carry disease or be treated to prevent sprouting).

## Quantity
A packet usually holds 25 to 1,000 seeds. A larger "bulk" amount costs less per seed. For long storage, buy more than a year's worth, but not more than you can plant over a few years.

## Choose varieties wisely
- **Pick** varieties listed as good for your region and your season length.
- **Choose** a mix of early, mid and late varieties to spread risk.
- **Include** disease-resistant varieties.
- **Prefer** open-pollinated varieties if you want to save seed (see the next lesson).
- **Try** a few new things each year, and keep notes on what worked.

## Check the packet
- **Variety name** and crop.
- **Year packed** and the germination rate.
- **Days to maturity,** spacing and planting depth.
- **Organic or untreated** if you prefer. Some seed is treated with fungicide, and the label says so. **Do not eat treated seed.**

## Tools and supplies
Keep a trowel, hand fork, hoe, spade, rake, a bucket, pruners, a watering can, twine, labels, row cover and a notebook with the seed.

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
