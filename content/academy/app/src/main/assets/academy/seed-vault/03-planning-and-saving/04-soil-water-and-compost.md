Seed is only half of a garden. Healthy soil and enough water grow food.

## Know your soil
- **Texture:** sand drains fast and holds little. Clay holds water and nutrients but compacts. Loam is a balanced mix. Do the **jar test:** fill a jar one-third with soil, add water, shake and let it settle for a day. Sand settles first, silt next, clay last. The layers show the mix.
- **pH:** most vegetables like 6.0 to 7.0. A soil test from your Extension office or a kit tells you pH and nutrients, and what to add.
- **Organic matter:** the dark, crumbly part. More is better.
- **Contamination:** if your site is near old buildings, roads or industry, test for lead and other contaminants before growing food. Raised beds with clean soil help.

## Improve soil
- **Add compost** (about 1 to 2 inches worked into the top few inches each year).
- **Add organic matter:** leaves, straw, grass clippings (untreated), well-rotted manure.
- **Avoid compaction:** do not walk on beds. Use paths.
- **Do not overwork** wet soil.
- **Use cover crops** (clover, rye, vetch) to protect and feed soil in the off season.
- **Mulch** with straw, leaves or wood chips to hold moisture, stop weeds and feed the soil.

## Compost
Compost turns kitchen and yard waste into rich soil food.
1. **Pick a spot** (a pile, bin or tumbler).
2. **Layer** "browns" (dry leaves, straw, cardboard, paper), which supply carbon, and "greens" (vegetable scraps, grass clippings, coffee grounds), which supply nitrogen. Aim for about 2 or 3 parts browns to 1 part greens by volume.
3. **Keep it moist** like a wrung-out sponge.
4. **Turn** it every week or two to add air.
5. **Wait:** compost is ready in 2 to 6 months, dark, crumbly and earthy-smelling.
- **Do not add** meat, dairy, oils, pet waste, or diseased plants (unless it gets very hot).
- **Human waste** is not for the garden unless composted through safe, long-term processes, and never for crops eaten raw. Follow local rules and public health guidance.

## Water
- **Vegetables need about 1 inch of water per week,** from rain or watering.
- **Water deeply and less often** to encourage deep roots. Shallow, frequent watering makes weak roots.
- **Water in the morning** so leaves dry, which reduces disease.
- **Water at the base,** not on the leaves.
- **Mulch** to reduce evaporation.
- **Drip lines or soaker hoses** are efficient.
- **Collect rainwater** in barrels. It is great for the garden.
- **Check** your local rules on rainwater collection.
- **Conserve:** use greywater only as local rules allow, and keep it off edible parts.

## Fertilizer
- **Compost and mulch** supply most needs.
- **Nitrogen:** from legumes, manure and compost, helps leafy growth.
- **Phosphorus and potassium** support roots, flowers and fruit.
- **Follow a soil test** rather than guessing.
- **Organic options:** bone meal, blood meal, fish emulsion, wood ash (in moderation, only on acidic soils).

## Checklist
- A soil test
- A compost pile or bin
- A mulch supply
- A watering plan and water source

> Growing seasons, frost dates and pests vary by place. Ask your local Cooperative Extension office (find yours at nifa.usda.gov) for advice that fits your area.
