Heat and cold injuries are some of the most common emergencies in a disaster and in the outdoors. This lesson ties together the signs and first aid steps. The Power Outage course covers prevention in the home.

## Dehydration
The body needs fluids to work. Dehydration can build over hours and can follow heat, illness (vomiting or diarrhea), poor access to water and exertion.

**Signs**
- Thirst, dry mouth.
- Dark, low urine, or none.
- Headache, tiredness, dizziness.
- In severe cases: confusion, a fast heartbeat, sunken eyes and fainting.
- **Children and older adults** dehydrate faster. In infants look for no wet diapers, a sunken soft spot and no tears.

**First aid**
- **Move to a cool place** and rest.
- **Give small, frequent sips** of water or an oral rehydration solution (oral rehydration salts mixed with clean water as directed on the package).
- Avoid alcohol and very sugary drinks.
- **Seek care** for severe dehydration, persistent vomiting or diarrhea, confusion, or a person who cannot drink.
- See the Family Medical course for children and older adults.

## Heat illness
**Heat cramps:** painful muscle spasms in heat. Rest in the shade, stretch gently and sip fluids.

**Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, cold, pale, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea, muscle cramps, tiredness, dizziness, headache, fainting.
- **Move** to a cool place, **lie down** and **loosen clothing.**
- **Apply cool, wet cloths** or take a cool shower.
- **Sip water.**
- **Get medical help** if symptoms worsen or last longer than an hour.

**Heat stroke:** a medical emergency. Body temperature of **103°F (39.4°C) or higher,** hot red dry or damp skin, a fast strong pulse, throbbing headache, confusion, loss of consciousness, seizures. Temperature can reach 106°F within 10 to 15 minutes.
- **Call 911.**
- **Move the person to a cool, shaded place.**
- **Cool them fast:** immerse in cool or cold water if possible, or wet the skin and fan, and place ice packs wrapped in cloth on the neck, armpits and groin.
- **Do not give fluids** if they are confused or unconscious.
- **Keep cooling** until help arrives.

**Prevention:** drink water regularly, wear light clothing, rest in the shade, avoid hard exercise at midday, never leave anyone in a parked car, and watch the vulnerable.

## Cold injuries
**Hypothermia** is a dangerously low body temperature (below 95°F, 35°C), from cold, wet and wind.

**Signs**
- Shivering (it may stop in severe cases), clumsiness, slurred speech, confusion, drowsiness.
- Slow breathing and a weak pulse.
- Cold, pale skin.
- Infants: cold skin, very low energy.

**First aid**
1. **Call 911.**
2. **Move to a warm, dry place,** handling gently.
3. **Remove wet clothing,** replace with dry layers and wrap in blankets, including the head and neck.
4. **Insulate from the ground.**
5. **Warm the core** (chest, neck, head and groin) with warm (not hot) packs wrapped in cloth, and skin-to-skin contact.
6. **Give warm, sweet drinks** if the person is awake and can swallow. No alcohol.
7. **Do not rub** the limbs, use hot water, or heating pads directly.
8. **Handle gently.** A cold heart is irritable.
9. **Begin CPR** if there is no breathing or pulse, and continue until help arrives. Do not stop.

**Frostbite:** freezing of skin and tissue, usually on fingers, toes, nose, ears and cheeks.
- **Signs:** cold, numb, hard, white or gray-yellow skin, waxy, with tingling and loss of feeling. Later blisters.
- **First aid:** move to warmth, remove wet or tight clothing and jewelry, **warm the area gently in warm (not hot) water,** or with body heat (for example, fingers in armpits), for about 20 to 30 minutes, until the skin turns red and feeling returns. **Do not rub.** Do not use direct heat. **Do not rewarm if there is a chance it will freeze again.** Cover with loose dry dressings and keep fingers and toes apart. Seek medical care.

**Trench foot and immersion foot:** from prolonged wet and cold. Keep feet dry, change socks often and dry feet.

## Sun
- **Sunburn:** cool, moisturize, drink water. Seek care for blisters over large areas, fever or confusion.
- **Prevention:** sunscreen, hats, shade, protective clothing.

## Altitude and others
Know the signs of altitude sickness (headache, nausea, dizziness) and descend if they worsen.

## Checklist
- Know the signs of heat exhaustion and heat stroke
- Know the signs of hypothermia and frostbite
- Know how to cool and warm a person safely
- Oral rehydration salts, emergency blankets and cold and heat packs in the kit
- Know when to call 911

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
