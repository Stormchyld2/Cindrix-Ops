The foundation of home, vehicle and everyday medical readiness.

Basic first aid kits treat the most common everyday injuries and illnesses: minor cuts, scrapes, burns, headaches, blisters, sprains and routine problems at home, work, school or while traveling.

## Contents of a basic home first aid kit
**Wound care**
- Adhesive bandages in multiple sizes
- Sterile gauze pads in several sizes
- Roller gauze
- Medical tape (cloth or paper)
- Non-stick pads
- Antibiotic ointment
- Antiseptic wipes
- Butterfly closures or tape strips
- Saline or clean water for rinsing
- Burn dressings or gel

**Protection**
- **Disposable gloves** (nitrile, and extra in case of latex allergy)
- **CPR face shield or mask**
- Hand sanitizer
- Safety glasses and masks

**Tools**
- Tweezers
- Scissors or trauma shears
- Safety pins
- Digital thermometer
- Instant cold packs
- Emergency blanket

**Medicines (over the counter)**
- Pain and fever relievers (ibuprofen, acetaminophen), used as the label directs
- Antihistamines
- Anti-diarrheal medicine and oral rehydration salts
- Antacids
- Hydrocortisone cream
- Any prescription medicines and **epinephrine** if prescribed for a family member
- **Naloxone** (see the overdose lesson)

**Information**
- A **first aid guide**
- A **list of emergency numbers** and Poison Control (1-800-222-1222)
- **Medical information** for each family member: allergies, medicines, conditions, blood type, doctor numbers.
- A pen and notepad.

A kit like this works for homes, vehicles, workplaces and backpacks. Make versions in different sizes.

## About medicines
- **Follow the label** for every product and **ask a pharmacist** if unsure. This course does not give medicine amounts. Doses depend on age, weight, health and other medicines.
- **Check expiration dates** twice a year and replace.
- **Store in a cool, dry place,** not in a hot car.
- **Keep medicines away from children,** in a locked place if needed.
- **Note allergies** and interactions.

## Where to get supplies
- **Pre-built kits:** a good start for beginners. Quality kits are available at pharmacies, big-box stores and online retailers.
- **Build-your-own kits:** often better, because you can choose higher-quality components, customize for your family's needs and avoid low-quality filler. Local pharmacies and outdoor retailers carry reasonable items. Look for kits and parts that meet **ANSI/ISEA Z308.1** if you want a workplace standard.
- **Avoid** unknown brands for critical items like tourniquets (see the next lesson).

## Where to keep kits
- **Home:** one in the kitchen or a central place, and one near bedrooms. Make sure everyone knows where.
- **Vehicles:** in each car, out of direct heat.
- **Workplace:** where you spend your day.
- **Go bags** and backpacks: a small version.
- **Boats, RVs, camp.**

## Check and restock
- Check the kit **when the clocks change.**
- **Replace** what you use, and anything expired or damaged.
- **Keep an inventory list** inside the lid and in the program's Inventory screen.
- **Know your kit.** Practice finding things in the dark.

## Checklist
- A basic kit at home, in each vehicle and in each go bag
- Gloves and CPR mask in every kit
- Medicines checked for expiration
- Emergency numbers and medical information inside
- Kit checked when the clocks change

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
