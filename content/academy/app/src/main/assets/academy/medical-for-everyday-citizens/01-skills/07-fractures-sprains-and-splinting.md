Sprains, strains and broken bones are among the most common injuries in a disaster, from falls on stairs, debris and cleanup work.

## What is injured
- **Sprain:** a stretched or torn **ligament** (connects bone to bone), often at the ankle, wrist or knee.
- **Strain:** a stretched or torn **muscle or tendon.**
- **Fracture (broken bone):** a crack or break in a bone. Can be closed (skin intact) or **open** (bone or wound through the skin).
- **Dislocation:** a bone pushed out of its joint.

You often cannot tell them apart without an X-ray. **If in doubt, treat it as a fracture.**

## Signs of a fracture
- Severe pain, especially with movement or touch.
- **Swelling** and bruising.
- **Deformity:** a limb at a wrong angle, a bump or a gap.
- Inability to use or bear weight on the limb.
- A grinding or snapping feeling or sound.
- Bone visible through the skin (open fracture).
- Numbness, tingling, cold or pale skin below the injury, which can mean blood flow or nerve problems.

## First steps
1. **Check the scene and the person.** Treat life threats first: bleeding, breathing, shock.
2. **Do not move** the person unless they are in danger. If you suspect a **neck or back injury** (a fall from height, a car crash, numbness, tingling, severe neck or back pain), keep the head and neck in line and **do not move them.** Call 911.
3. **Control bleeding.** Cover an open fracture with a clean dressing. **Do not push bone back in** and do not try to straighten the limb.
4. **Immobilize** the injured part in the position you found it (see below).
5. **Check circulation, sensation and movement** below the injury, before and after splinting: pulse, skin color and warmth, feeling and movement of fingers or toes.
6. **Apply cold** (an ice pack wrapped in cloth) for 15 to 20 minutes at a time to reduce swelling and pain. Never put ice directly on the skin.
7. **Treat for shock** if needed.
8. **Get medical care.** All suspected fractures need a doctor.

## Splinting: how to immobilize
A splint prevents movement of an injury. It reduces pain, prevents more damage and makes transport easier.

**Principles**
- **Splint the joints above and below** the injury (for a forearm, include the wrist and elbow).
- **Splint in the position found.** Do not force a limb straight. If there is no pulse below the injury, a trained responder may gently realign. Otherwise leave it.
- **Pad the splint** so it does not dig in, especially at bony points.
- **Fasten firmly but not too tight.** Use bandages, cloth strips or tape above and below the injury, never directly over the injury.
- **Leave fingers or toes visible** so you can check circulation.
- **Re-check** circulation every 15 minutes or so, and loosen if the skin turns pale, blue or cold, or becomes numb.

**Commercial splints**
- **SAM splint:** a padded, moldable aluminum splint. Bend it into a curve for strength, shape it to the limb, and secure with a wrap.
- **Padded board splints** and **traction splints** (for trained users).
- **Triangular bandages and slings.**
- **Elastic wraps.**

**Improvised splints**
Items like sticks, rolled newspaper or magazines, cardboard, a rolled blanket or a pillow can work. Pad them with clothing.
- **Buddy splinting:** tape an injured finger to the next one, or an injured leg to the other leg, with padding in between.
- **Sling and swathe:** for an arm or shoulder. A triangular bandage or a shirt can make a sling to hold the arm across the chest, with a swathe (a band around the chest) to stop it swinging.
- **Ankle:** a pillow or rolled blanket wrapped around the foot and ankle and tied.

## Sprains and strains: R.I.C.E.
For minor injuries with no sign of a fracture:
- **Rest:** stop using the injured part.
- **Ice:** a cold pack wrapped in cloth for 15 to 20 minutes, several times a day for the first couple of days.
- **Compression:** an elastic wrap, snug but not tight.
- **Elevation:** raise it above the heart if possible.

If the pain is severe, you cannot bear weight, or the joint looks wrong, treat it as a fracture.

## Head and spine
- **Head injury** warning signs: loss of consciousness, vomiting, severe headache, confusion, drowsiness, unequal pupils, clear fluid from nose or ears, seizure. **Call 911.**
- **Spine injury:** hold the head still, do not move the person, and call 911. If you must move them to save life, use many helpers to keep head, neck and back in line.

## Practice
Practice splinting on a partner with a SAM splint and with improvised materials.

## Checklist
- Know signs of a fracture
- Can splint an arm, a leg and an ankle with commercial and improvised materials
- Can make a sling and swathe
- SAM splint, triangular bandages and elastic wrap in the kit
- Know how to check circulation, sensation and movement
