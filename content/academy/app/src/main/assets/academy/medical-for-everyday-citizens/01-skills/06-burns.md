Burns are common in disasters: cooking fires, stoves, generators, candles, hot water, chemicals and electricity.

## Stop the burning
- **Thermal burn (heat or fire):** move away from the source. If clothing is on fire, **stop, drop and roll.** Smother flames with a blanket. Remove smoldering clothing if it is not stuck to the skin.
- **Chemical burn:** brush off dry powder, then **flush with lots of running water for at least 20 minutes.** Remove contaminated clothing and jewelry. Protect your own skin and eyes. Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) and 911.
- **Electrical burn:** **turn off the power or move the person with a dry non-conducting object (wood, plastic).** Do not touch them while they are in contact with power. Check breathing and pulse. **All electrical burns need a doctor,** even if small, because of hidden damage.
- **Sunburn:** cool, moisturize and drink water.

## Cool the burn
- **Cool (not cold) running water for at least 10 minutes,** and up to 20. This reduces pain and damage.
- **Do not use ice,** which can cause further damage.
- **Remove rings, watches and tight items** before swelling starts, unless they are stuck.
- **Do not break blisters.**
- **Do not apply** butter, oil, toothpaste, egg white or ointments to a fresh burn.

## Cover the burn
- Cover loosely with a **clean, non-stick dressing** or a clean, non-fluffy cloth. **Cling film** (plastic wrap) laid loosely over the burn works well in the short term. Do not wrap it around a limb.
- **Keep the person warm,** because large burns cause heat loss, but do not cover the burn with a blanket that sticks.
- **Do not apply** creams to severe burns.

## How bad is it?
- **First-degree:** red, painful, no blisters (like sunburn). Cool and soothe.
- **Second-degree:** blisters, red and wet, very painful. Cool and cover. Larger ones need care.
- **Third-degree:** white, leathery, charred or numb. A medical emergency.

## Call 911 or get urgent care for
- Burns larger than the person's palm.
- Burns on the **face, hands, feet, genitals, or over joints,** or that go all the way around a limb.
- **Third-degree burns,** whatever the size.
- **Chemical** and **electrical** burns.
- **Inhalation injury:** burns to the face, singed nose hairs, soot around the nose or mouth, coughing, hoarseness or trouble breathing.
- Burns in **children, older adults,** or people with other medical conditions.
- Signs of **shock.**

## Treat for shock
Lay the person down, keep them warm, and watch for changes. See the shock lesson.

## After the first day
- Keep the burn **clean and dressed.** Change dressings daily and keep them dry.
- **Watch for infection:** more pain, redness, swelling, pus, fever.
- **Pain:** follow package directions for over-the-counter pain relievers, or ask a pharmacist.
- **Keep out of the sun** as it heals.
- **Drink plenty of fluids.**

## Preventing burns in an outage
- Use cooking fires outdoors.
- Keep children away from stoves, fires and hot water.
- Turn pot handles inward.
- Never leave flames unattended.
- Keep a fire extinguisher handy.
- Set water heaters to a safe temperature.

## Checklist
- Know how to cool and cover a burn
- Know which burns need urgent care
- Burn dressings and cooling gel in the kit
- Know how to treat chemical and electrical burns
- Fire extinguisher where you can reach it

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
