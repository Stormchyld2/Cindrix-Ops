Uncontrolled bleeding is a leading cause of preventable death after an injury. A person can bleed to death in as little as three to five minutes. You can learn to stop it.

## The three steps: Alert, Bleeding, Compress
1. **Alert:** call 911. Make sure the scene is safe first. If there is a threat, move yourself and the person to safety if you can. **Wear gloves** if you have them.
2. **Bleeding:** find the source. Expose the wound by cutting or removing clothing. Look for blood pooling under the person, soaked clothing and wounds that are hidden.
3. **Compress:** use one of three methods, in order of what the wound needs.

## Method 1: Direct pressure
For most bleeding.
1. **Cover the wound** with a clean cloth, gauze or the cleanest thing you have. If you have nothing, use your hand.
2. **Push down hard** with both hands, using your body weight, directly on the wound.
3. **Keep pushing.** Do not lift to check. Do not stop.
4. **If blood soaks through,** add more cloth on top and keep pressing. Do not remove the soaked layers.
5. **Bandage firmly** (a pressure dressing or an elastic wrap) once bleeding slows or stops, and keep it on.
6. **Elevation** of a limb above the heart may help in some injuries, but never delay pressure for it, and do not elevate a suspected fracture.
7. **Object in the wound:** do not pull it out. Apply pressure around it and pad it so it does not move.

## Method 2: Wound packing
For deep wounds where pressure alone is not enough, and in areas where a tourniquet cannot go: the **groin, shoulder, armpit and neck.** (Use caution in the neck, and keep the airway clear.)
1. **Find the bleeding source** deep in the wound.
2. **Pack gauze firmly into the wound,** all the way to the bottom, with your fingers. Plain gauze works. **Hemostatic gauze** (impregnated with a clotting agent) works better, and is made for this.
3. **Keep packing** until the wound is full.
4. **Apply firm pressure** with both hands for **at least three minutes** (follow your training for the specific product).
5. **Bandage** over the packing with a pressure dressing.
6. **Do not remove** the packing at the scene.

## Method 3: Tourniquet
A tourniquet is for **severe bleeding from an arm or leg** that is life-threatening, that direct pressure does not control, in a situation with multiple victims, or in an unsafe environment where you must move fast.

**Use only on an arm or leg.** Never on the neck, chest, abdomen or head.

**How to apply a commercial tourniquet** (such as the CAT)
1. **Place it** 2 to 3 inches above the wound, **between the wound and the heart.** If you cannot tell where the wound is, or in an emergency, place it **high and tight** near the armpit or groin. **Never place it over a joint** (knee or elbow), or over an object in a pocket.
2. **You can put it on bare skin or over clothing.**
3. **Pull the strap tight,** pulling the free end through the buckle, so it is snug.
4. **Twist the windlass rod** until the bleeding stops.
5. **Lock the rod** in its clip to hold it.
6. **Secure** the strap over the rod.
7. **Write the time** on the tourniquet (many have a white tab) or on the person's forehead, with a marker.
8. **Tell the person** that it will hurt, because it must be tight to work.
9. **If bleeding continues,** apply a second tourniquet next to and above the first. You can also pack the wound.
10. **Do not remove or loosen** the tourniquet. Leave that for hospital staff. **Do not cover it** so responders can see it.
11. **Tell the responders** the time it was placed.

**Common mistakes**
- Placing it too loosely.
- Placing it over a joint.
- Waiting too long to use it for severe limb bleeding.
- Forgetting to note the time.
- Taking it off.
- Using string, wire or a belt that cuts the skin and does not tighten enough. Improvised tourniquets often fail, so carry a real one.

**Improvised tourniquet** (if you have no commercial one)
Use a wide strip of cloth (at least 2 inches wide, so it does not cut), tied around the limb above the wound, with a rigid stick or rod as a windlass. Twist until bleeding stops, then secure the stick. Note the time. This is a last resort.

## After the bleeding stops
- **Look for other injuries** and bleeding.
- **Treat for shock:** lay the person down, keep them warm, and do not give food or drink (see the shock lesson).
- **Keep watching** the dressing and tourniquet.
- **Stay with the person** until help arrives.
- **Clean up safely:** wash your hands and any skin exposed to blood with soap and water, and dispose of gloves and soiled material properly. Tell a doctor if you were exposed.

## Special cases
- **Nosebleed:** sit up, lean forward, and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 to 15 minutes. Do not tilt the head back.
- **Scalp wounds** bleed a lot. Use firm pressure.
- **Amputations:** control bleeding with a tourniquet or pressure. Wrap the amputated part in clean, moist gauze, place it in a plastic bag, and put the bag on ice (not directly on ice). Take it with the patient.
- **Internal bleeding:** signs include pain, swelling, bruising, a rigid belly and shock. You cannot stop it with first aid. Call 911 and treat for shock.

## Practice
- Practice applying a tourniquet to yourself (one-handed) and to a partner, timed.
- Practice packing a wound on a training pad.
- Check the tourniquet and gauze in your kits each season.

## Checklist
- Know the three steps: Alert, Bleeding, Compress
- Can apply direct pressure, packing and a tourniquet
- Tourniquet and hemostatic gauze in each kit
- Practiced tourniquet application under time
- Attended a Stop the Bleed class
