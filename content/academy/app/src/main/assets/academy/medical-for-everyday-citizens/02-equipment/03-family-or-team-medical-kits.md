Larger kits built to care for several people during prolonged emergencies.

Family or team kits are larger medical kits designed to care for multiple people during prolonged emergencies, or when professional care may be delayed. They bridge the gap between basic first aid, emergency trauma care, short-term sick care and disaster medical preparedness. They are intended for families, preparedness groups and community aid.

## What goes in
**Bleeding and wounds**
- Large quantities of gauze (rolled, pads and packing)
- Compression wraps
- Multiple tourniquets
- Hemostatic gauze
- Trauma dressings
- Chest seals
- Burn dressings
- Irrigation supplies (a large syringe and saline)
- Steri-strips and wound closure tape
- Sterile wound cleaning supplies
- Tape in several widths

**Splinting and support**
- SAM splints
- Elastic wraps (several sizes)
- Slings and triangular bandages
- Cold packs and heat packs
- Cervical collar (for trained use)

**Medicines** (follow labels and ask a pharmacist)
- Pain relievers
- Antihistamines
- Anti-diarrheals
- Oral rehydration salts and electrolytes
- Antacids
- Prescription medicines (if applicable), with a rotation plan
- Naloxone and epinephrine (if available and trained)
- Eye wash

**Diagnostics**
- Thermometer
- **Pulse oximeter**
- **Blood pressure cuff**
- **Stethoscope**
- Penlight
- A notebook and patient record forms

**Personal protection and hygiene**
- Extra gloves in all sizes
- Masks (N95 and surgical), eye protection and gowns
- Hygiene supplies, soap, wipes, sanitizer
- Biohazard bags
- Sharps container if you use needles
- Trash bags and disinfectant (diluted per label)

**Reference**
- A good field first aid book (such as the American Red Cross First Aid manual or a wilderness medicine guide)
- Emergency contact lists
- Patient information sheets

## Organize it
> **Pro tip:** a large kit becomes useless if poorly organized. Use labeled pouches, clear containers, color coding and inventory sheets.

Organize by category:
- **Bleeding** (red)
- **Airway and breathing** (blue)
- **Medications** (green)
- **Diagnostics** (yellow)
- **Splinting and support** (orange)
- **Hygiene and protection** (white)

Keep a **laminated inventory** on the lid with quantities and expiration dates. Put the most urgent items on top.

## Scale to your needs
- **Count people** and add for children, older adults, pets and people with medical conditions.
- Plan for **two to four weeks** of minor care, and more for high-risk members.
- **Build a family list** of conditions and medicines and make sure supplies match.
- **Split** supplies among locations, so one loss does not remove everything.
- Balance **weight and portability.** A large, heavy kit stays home. A grab-and-go version travels.

## Medication notes
- **Do not stockpile** antibiotics or other prescription medicines without a doctor. Work with your doctor on a plan.
- **Rotate** medicines before they expire.
- **Store** them cool and dry.
- **Know allergies** and interactions.
- **Never share** prescription medicines.

## Keep it up
- **Inventory** every season.
- **Replace** used and expired items.
- **Practice** with the kit during drills. Make sure several people know where things are.
- **Train** with your group and update the kit as skills grow.

## Checklist
- A family or team kit stocked and labeled
- Inventory sheet on the lid
- Organized by category with color coding
- Medicines checked and rotated
- Group members know where it is and how to use it

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
