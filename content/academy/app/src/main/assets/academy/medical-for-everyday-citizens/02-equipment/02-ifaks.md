Individual first aid kits: compact, trauma-focused and built to keep someone alive until help arrives.

An **IFAK** is a compact kit designed to treat severe, life-threatening injuries where immediate medical care may not be available. Unlike basic first aid kits, IFAKs focus primarily on:
- **Massive bleeding**
- **Penetrating trauma**
- **Airway and chest injuries**
- **Immediate life-saving interventions**

IFAKs are **not** designed for convenience or minor injuries. They are built to keep someone alive long enough to reach higher medical care.

## Where IFAKs are carried
Commonly carried during tactical operations, hunting trips, off-roading, remote travel, firearms training, disaster response and high-risk occupations. Many people also keep IFAKs in vehicles, hiking packs, range bags and plate carriers. Mount it where you can reach it with either hand, and tell people where it is.

## What goes in an IFAK
- **Tourniquet** (at least one, and preferably two). Choose a model with evidence of effectiveness, such as the C-A-T (Combat Application Tourniquet) or SOF-TT.
- **Hemostatic gauze** (a clotting-agent gauze such as QuikClot Combat Gauze or Celox).
- **Compression bandage** (such as the Israeli bandage or an emergency trauma dressing).
- **Rolled gauze or packing gauze.**
- **Vented chest seals (two)**, so you can seal an entry and exit wound.
- **Nitrile gloves.**
- **CPR face shield or mask.**
- **Trauma shears.**
- **Permanent marker** for recording times.
- **Light source** (a small headlamp or flashlight).
- **Emergency blanket** to prevent heat loss.
- **Nasopharyngeal airway (NPA)** and lubricant, for trained users.
- **Tape.**
- **A card** with the steps and a tourniquet time log.

## Care and packing
- **Keep it simple and organized.** Pack in the order you will use it: tourniquet and gauze on top.
- **Use vacuum or compact packaging** where recommended, and keep the kit **in a visible, accessible place.**
- **Protect from heat and sun,** which damage tourniquet plastics and gauze. Do not leave it in a hot car trunk for long.
- **Inspect** for damage and expiration every few months. **Practice** with a trainer tourniquet (do not use your live one for practice, because repeated use wears it).
- **Replace** items after use.

## Where to buy
- Recommended sources: **reputable medical suppliers, tactical medical companies, EMS supply companies** and preparedness retailers.
- **Avoid mystery-brand medical gear** from unknown online sellers. **Counterfeit tourniquets** have been found online and fail under load.
- Check for the **Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care** recommended device list, and buy from authorized dealers.

## Training matters
Owning trauma gear without training creates false confidence. Take a **Stop the Bleed** or equivalent course before you need this equipment. Practice often.

## Laws and responsibility
- Know your state's **Good Samaritan law.**
- Understand **scope:** you provide first aid until professionals arrive. Do not exceed your training.
- Keep your kit **accessible** to those who may need it, and tell people where it is.

## Checklist
- IFAK in each vehicle, range bag and go bag
- Two tourniquets, hemostatic gauze, compression bandage, two vented chest seals
- Purchased from a reputable source
- Trained with a Stop the Bleed class
- Kit inspected every few months

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
