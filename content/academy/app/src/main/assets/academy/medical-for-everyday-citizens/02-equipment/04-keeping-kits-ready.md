A kit is only useful if it is complete, in date and where you can find it.

## A kit maintenance routine
**Every time the clocks change (twice a year):**
1. **Open every kit.** Check the contents against the inventory.
2. **Check expiration dates** on medicines, ointments, saline, gauze with clotting agents and batteries.
3. **Replace** anything expired, damaged, missing or used.
4. **Check seals and packaging.** Damp or sun-damaged packages should be replaced.
5. **Check batteries** in lights and devices.
6. **Update personal information:** allergies, medicines, contacts and photos of the family.
7. **Update the inventory** and write the date.
8. **Practice:** put on a tourniquet, apply a chest seal, splint an arm. Use a trainer.

**After every use:** restock the same day.

## Storage
- **Cool, dry and dark.** Heat and sunlight spoil medicines and degrade plastics and adhesives. Do not leave kits in a parked car in summer or winter for long periods.
- **Keep out of reach of children,** but known to adults.
- **Keep it accessible.** A kit buried in a closet is useless.
- **Keep it clean** and protected from water. Use waterproof bags.
- **Label it** clearly.
- **Mark** where kits are on your home plan.

## Rotating medicines
- **Use the oldest first.**
- **Write expiration dates** on the outside.
- **Check with a pharmacist** about safe use after the date. Do not rely on expired emergency medicines such as epinephrine.
- **Dispose** of expired medicines at a take-back site.

## Track it in the program
Use the **Inventory** screen: record each medical item with its expiration date and location. It will help you spot what is due. Set reminders on the **Calendar.**

## Know your kit
- **Learn what every item is for.**
- **Practice finding things** quickly and in the dark.
- **Teach** family members.
- **Make a one-page quick reference card** for the lid.

## Build in layers
- **On your body:** a tourniquet and gloves, or a small pouch.
- **In your bag or vehicle:** an IFAK.
- **At home and with the group:** a family or team kit.

## Checklist
- Kit check on the calendar twice a year
- Inventory sheet current
- Expiration dates written on the outside
- Kits stored cool and dry
- Used items restocked the same day

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
