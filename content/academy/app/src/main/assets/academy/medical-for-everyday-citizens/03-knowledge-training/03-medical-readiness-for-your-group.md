Medical readiness is stronger when it is shared. A group with a plan and a few trained members can handle far more than a single household.

## Medical roles in the group
- **Medical lead:** keeps the training and kit plan, and leads in a medical emergency.
- **Trained members:** hold current CPR, first aid and Stop the Bleed certifications.
- **Kit keeper:** inventories and restocks the kits.
- **Records keeper:** keeps medical information cards, with consent, stored securely.
- **Backup** for each role.

## A medical information card for each member
Collect, with consent:
- Name, age, emergency contacts
- Allergies and medicines
- Medical conditions
- Blood type
- Doctor and pharmacy
- Special needs

Keep it **secure and private.** Share it only with the people who need it. Store the cards in a sealed envelope or in an encrypted vault.

## A plan for a medical emergency
1. **Who calls 911** and how, if phones fail?
2. **Who gets the kit and AED?**
3. **Who leads care?**
4. **Where is the nearest hospital and the alternate?**
5. **How do we transport** someone, and who drives?
6. **What if EMS cannot come?** Where do we take the patient, and who do we contact?
7. **Who looks after the rest of the group?**
8. **How do we record** what we did?

Write the plan and put it in the group binder. Practice it.

## Training and drills
- Hold a **medical skills night** each quarter.
- Run **scenarios:** a cut, a fall, a burn, a collapse, an allergic reaction, a vehicle crash.
- **Rotate** who leads.
- **Time** the response.
- **Hold an after action review** and fix gaps.

## Know your community resources
- Nearest hospitals, urgent care and clinics.
- Local EMS response time.
- Doctors, nurses, EMTs, paramedics and veterinarians in the group or area.
- Pharmacies.
- **Poison Control:** 1-800-222-1222.
- **988** for crisis support.
- **Local CERT** and **Medical Reserve Corps** programs.

## Care for the carers
Giving first aid can be stressful. Make time to **debrief,** talk and rest. Seek help if someone is struggling afterward.

## Checklist
- A medical lead and backup named
- Medical information cards collected with consent
- A written medical emergency plan
- A quarterly medical skills night scheduled
- Local resources list in the binder

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
