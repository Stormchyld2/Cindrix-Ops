Cardiac arrest is when the heart stops pumping effectively. The person collapses, does not respond, and is not breathing normally. Without CPR and a shock, death follows in minutes. This lesson gives you the overview. Take a class for hands-on practice, and see the CPR and First Aid course for the full detail.

## The chain of survival
1. **Recognize** and **call 911** early.
2. **Start CPR** right away.
3. **Use an AED** as soon as one is available.
4. **Advanced care** by paramedics.
5. **Hospital care** and recovery.

Everyone can do steps 1 to 3.

## Adult CPR in brief
1. **Check the scene** and **tap and shout:** "Are you okay?"
2. **Call 911** and **send someone for an AED.** If alone, put your phone on speaker.
3. **Check breathing** for no more than 10 seconds. **Gasping** is not normal breathing.
4. If **not breathing normally,** begin CPR.

**High-quality compressions (adult)**
- **Position:** heel of one hand on the **lower half of the breastbone** (center of the chest), other hand on top, arms straight, shoulders over hands.
- **Rate:** **100 to 120 compressions per minute.**
- **Depth:** **at least 2 inches** (5 cm), no more than about 2.4 inches (6 cm).
- **Recoil:** let the chest **come all the way back up** after each compression.
- **Minimize interruptions:** never pause compressions for more than **10 seconds.**
- **Switch** with another rescuer every 2 minutes if possible, to stay strong.

**If trained to give breaths:** after **30 compressions,** give **2 breaths** (each about 1 second, enough to see the chest rise), with the head tilted back and chin lifted, nose pinched. Return to compressions quickly.

**If untrained or unwilling to give breaths:** do **hands-only CPR,** continuous compressions at the same rate and depth until help or an AED arrives.

## Children and infants
- **Child (1 year to puberty):** rate 100 to 120. Depth about 2 inches (or at least one-third of the chest depth). Use one or two hands. For one rescuer, **30:2.** With two rescuers, many courses teach 15:2.
- **Infant (under 1):** rate 100 to 120. Depth about 1.5 inches (or one-third of the chest). Use **two fingers** (one rescuer) or **two thumbs encircling the chest** (two rescuers). **30:2** with one rescuer.
- For children and infants who collapse, **call and give 2 minutes of CPR first** if you are alone, then call, since many pediatric arrests are breathing-related.

## AED: use it as soon as it arrives
An **AED** analyzes the heart rhythm and delivers a shock if needed. They are safe for lay people to use, and give voice prompts.
1. **Turn it on.**
2. **Expose the chest** and dry it if wet. Remove medication patches. Shave a very hairy chest if a razor is in the kit.
3. **Apply the pads** as shown in the pictures on the pads. For adults: one on the **upper right chest** below the collarbone, one on the **left side** below the armpit. For children and infants, use pediatric pads if available, placed front and back if they are too large to fit on the chest.
4. **Plug in** the pads if needed.
5. **Stand clear:** make sure no one is touching the person. Let the AED analyze.
6. **If advised, press the shock button** after making sure everyone is clear.
7. **Immediately resume CPR** starting with compressions, for 2 minutes, then the AED will analyze again.
8. **Keep going** until the person moves, EMS takes over or you are too exhausted.

Notes:
- **Do not place pads** over an implanted device (a bulge under the skin), over a medication patch, or in water.
- **Move the person** out of puddles and dry the chest.
- **If the AED fails** or you do not have one, **keep doing CPR.**

## Find AEDs now
Find where AEDs are in your area: workplaces, schools, gyms, stores, churches, fire stations. Consider buying one for your group or home.

## If the person wakes up
Stop CPR when they move, breathe and respond. Place them on their side (recovery position) and stay with them until help arrives. Cardiac arrest can recur.

## Compressions hurt
CPR may break ribs. That is common and acceptable. Do not stop.

## Practice
Take an AHA or Red Cross class. Practice on a manikin. Use a metronome or the song "Stayin' Alive" for rate. Refresh regularly.

## Checklist
- Know the rate (100 to 120), depth (at least 2 inches) and ratio (30:2)
- Know how to use an AED
- Know where the nearest AEDs are
- Have taken a CPR and AED class
- Recertification date on the calendar

> This lesson is first aid education, not medical advice or a substitute for hands-on training from the American Red Cross, American Heart Association or a Stop the Bleed class. Call 911 for any life-threatening emergency.
