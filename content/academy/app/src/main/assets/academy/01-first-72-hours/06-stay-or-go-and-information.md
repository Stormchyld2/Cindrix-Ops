One of the first decisions in an emergency: do you stay or do you leave? Reliable information makes the difference.

## Where to get information
- **Emergency alerts** on your phone (Wireless Emergency Alerts), and your city or county alert system. Sign up.
- **NOAA Weather Radio,** which broadcasts alerts and forecasts on a battery or crank radio.
- **Local radio and TV,** and the **Emergency Alert System.**
- **Official sources:** your county emergency management, police and fire department pages, and the National Weather Service.
- **Neighbors,** and your own eyes.
- **Be careful with social media.** Rumors spread fast. Check against official sources.

## Shelter in place: when to stay
Stay put when:
- The danger is outside and **leaving would expose you,** such as a chemical release, a severe storm, a tornado or an active threat nearby.
- **Officials tell you to.**
- You are in a safe, sturdy place, and you have supplies.
- Roads are blocked or dangerous.

For a chemical or hazardous release, go indoors, close and lock doors and windows, turn off fans and air conditioning, and go to an interior room (above ground for some chemicals). Listen for updates.

## Evacuate: when to go
Leave when:
- **Officials order an evacuation,** or you are in the path of a wildfire, flood or storm surge.
- **Your home is unsafe:** damaged, flooding, or with a gas leak.
- **You are told it is the safest option.**
- **You have a safe place to go.**

Go **early,** because roads fill and conditions get worse. See the Mobility course for planning routes, vehicle readiness and destinations.

## Decide with a short checklist
1. **What is the threat, and how fast is it coming?**
2. **What do officials say?**
3. **Is my home safe, and do I have supplies?**
4. **Can I leave safely, and where would I go?**
5. **Who depends on me?**
6. **What happens if I am wrong?** Prefer the choice you can undo.

## If you go
- **Take your go bag,** documents, medicine, phone and charger, pets and their supplies.
- **Tell someone** where you are going and your route.
- **Leave a note** at home.
- **Lock up,** turn off utilities if told to, and unplug appliances.
- **Follow evacuation routes,** and do not take shortcuts.
- **Keep your fuel tank at least half full** at all times.

## If you stay
- **Keep your radio on.**
- **Keep the phone charged** and save the battery.
- **Check on neighbors.**
- **Do not go out** in dangerous conditions.
- **Be ready to leave** if conditions change.

## Practice the decision
Talk through scenarios as a household: a fire, a storm, a chemical spill, a long power cut. Write down your triggers, such as "We leave if the water reaches the porch."

> Get reliable information, decide early, and prefer the choice you can reverse.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
