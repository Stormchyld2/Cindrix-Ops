You can go many days without food, but you will feel better, think better and stay warmer if you eat regularly.

## What to eat first
1. **Perishables in the refrigerator,** such as milk, meat, eggs and leftovers, within about four hours of the power going out, then cook or discard.
2. **Frozen food,** while it still has ice crystals. Cook thawed meat and fish first.
3. **Fresh produce** that will spoil.
4. **Canned and dry goods** last.

## Food that needs no cooking
- Canned meat, fish, beans, fruits, vegetables and soups (eat them straight from the can if needed)
- Peanut butter, nuts and trail mix
- Crackers, granola bars, cereal and dried fruit
- Shelf-stable milk and juice
- Ready-to-eat meals
- Honey and sugar for energy

## Cooking safely when the power is off
- **Camp stove, charcoal grill or fire pit:** **outdoors only,** well away from the house, never in a garage, tent or porch with walls.
- **Canned heat (such as Sterno)** is for use with good ventilation only.
- **Keep a fire extinguisher** nearby.
- **Wash hands** before you cook and eat.
- **Use a manual can opener,** and keep one in your kit.
- **Cook meat to a safe temperature** with a food thermometer. Ground meat should reach 160 °F, and poultry 165 °F.

## Food safety rules
- **Two-hour rule:** perishable food that has been above 40 °F for more than two hours (one hour if over 90 °F) should be thrown out.
- **Cans that are bulging, leaking or rusty** go in the trash.
- **Flood water:** discard food that touched flood water, including cans. Clean and sanitize sealed cans that are undamaged before opening.
- **Do not taste to test** if something is safe.

## Special diets
- **Infants:** keep formula, bottles and safe water on hand. Do not dilute formula.
- **People with diabetes or other conditions:** keep suitable food, and talk to a doctor about emergency plans.
- **Pets:** keep their food and water.

## Appetite and morale
Warm food lifts spirits. Plan simple hot meals, and have comfort foods on hand, such as coffee, tea, chocolate and hard candy.

## Build up to a month
Start with three days, then add a week, then two weeks. See the Weeks to Months module for rotating food.

> Eat the perishables first, keep the cans for later, and cook outdoors.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for hands-on training. Rules and local conditions vary, so follow official guidance first.
