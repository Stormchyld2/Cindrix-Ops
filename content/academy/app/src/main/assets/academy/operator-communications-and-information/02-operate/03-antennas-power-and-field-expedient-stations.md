A good field antenna and reliable power are worth more than an expensive radio.

## Simple antennas
- **Roll-up J-pole or slim-jim** for VHF and UHF: cheap, light, can hang from a branch.
- **Ground plane vertical:** a quarter-wave whip with radials: easy and effective.
- **Magnetic mount** on a car roof for mobile radios.
- **Dipole for HF:** two wires, each a quarter wavelength, fed from the center with coax, hung between trees. The length in feet is about 468 divided by the frequency in MHz, for the full length. Adjust to the best match with an SWR meter.
- **End-fed wire** with a matching unit: easy to deploy.
- **Yagi:** a directional antenna for weak signals.

## Placement
- **High and clear** of metal, buildings and trees.
- **Away from power lines** by at least 1.5 times the height of the mast, in case it falls.
- **Vertical** antennas for local coverage and **horizontal** for HF ones such as the dipole.
- **Ground:** use a good ground for HF.
- **Keep coax** away from sharp edges and weight on the connector.

## SWR
Standing wave ratio shows how well the antenna matches the radio. A low SWR (under 2:1) is good. A high SWR can damage a transmitter. Use a meter and tune.

## Safety
- **Never** raise an antenna near power lines.
- **RF exposure:** keep antennas away from people when transmitting, especially at higher power. Follow FCC guidance.
- **Lightning:** disconnect antennas and stay inside when storms approach. Install lightning protection for permanent stations.
- **Cables and tripping:** mark and secure.

## Power
- **Battery:** a deep-cycle 12-volt battery, such as a LiFePO4 pack, runs a 50-watt radio for many hours.
- **Solar:** a panel with a charge controller keeps batteries topped up.
- **Vehicle:** engine on to avoid draining the battery.
- **Handhelds:** carry spare batteries, and a battery case for AA cells.
- **Power budget:** list each radio's current draw in receive and transmit. Estimate hours.

## A go-kit station
- Radio, antenna, coax, mounting
- Power and spare power
- Headphones, notepad, pencils, log sheets, maps
- Flashlight, rain cover
- Cheat sheets: channels, frequencies, call signs, procedures
- Tools: pliers, tape, zip ties, SWR meter, multimeter

## Practice
Build and test a station outside, in daylight and in the dark. Time how long it takes.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
