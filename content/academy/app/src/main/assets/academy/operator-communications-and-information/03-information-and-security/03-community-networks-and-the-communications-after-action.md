Your best network is the one you share with others.

## Link into community networks
- **ARES and RACES:** volunteer ham groups that support local government and the Red Cross in emergencies.
- **SKYWARN:** a weather spotter network with the National Weather Service.
- **CERT:** FEMA's Community Emergency Response Teams.
- **Neighborhood networks:** radios and phone trees.
- **GMRS repeater groups** in many areas.
Join one. Learn the local nets, frequencies and procedures. Offer your skills.

## Build a neighborhood network
1. **Survey** who has radios and licenses.
2. **Agree** on channels, a check-in time, a net control rotation and call names.
3. **Create** a one-page list and distribute it.
4. **Hold** monthly practice nets.
5. **Map** coverage and use relay points.
6. **Plan** for the vulnerable and who checks on them.

## Digital tools
- **Offline messaging** over local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth for short range.
- **Mesh radios** that relay short texts.
- **Winlink:** email over radio, run by ham operators, handles messages and forms.
- **Packet and digital modes.**
These add capability but need power and skill.

## Written messages
Keep a message pad and a log for each net. Use standard forms.

## Responder liaison
In a disaster, local officials often need reliable ground information. Offer reports through proper channels. Do not self-deploy into danger. Wait for requests from authorities.

## Documentation
Keep records for later learning: logs, maps, message copies.

## After-action review
After each exercise or event, ask:
- Did messages get through? How fast?
- Which methods worked and failed?
- Did people know the procedure?
- Was the log complete?
- What do we need: gear, training, people, practice?
Update the plan and train.

## Your communications binder
- PACE plan
- Contacts and call signs
- Channel list and frequencies
- Net procedures and scripts
- Message forms
- Station checklists
- Power budget
- Training and exercise schedule
- After-action reports

> When the network fails, people who know each other and have practiced together still find a way to talk.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
