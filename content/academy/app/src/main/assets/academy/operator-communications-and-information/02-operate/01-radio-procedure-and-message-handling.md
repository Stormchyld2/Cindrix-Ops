Under stress, messy radio talk wastes time and causes errors. Standard procedure keeps it clean.

## Rules of the air
- **Listen first.** Make sure the channel is clear before you transmit.
- **Think, then press** the talk button, wait a second, then speak. Release at the end.
- **Speak clearly and slowly,** in a normal voice, close to the microphone.
- **Be brief.** Say only what is needed.
- **Use plain language.** No jargon, no "10-codes" that others may not know. Use clear words.
- **Identify.** Use the call signs as the law requires. For GMRS, give your call sign at the end of a transmission and every 15 minutes during. For ham, identify every 10 minutes and at the end.
- **No profanity,** and no private talk on emergency channels.
- **Do not interrupt** unless it is an emergency.

## Calling
**Who you are calling, then who you are:** "Base, this is Scout One, over."
**Reply:** "Scout One, this is Base, go ahead."
**Ending:** "Base, out," or "Scout One, clear."

## Useful words
- **Over:** I finished, your turn.
- **Out:** conversation finished.
- **Roger / copy:** I received your message. (Not "I agree.")
- **Say again:** repeat.
- **Stand by:** wait.
- **Break, break:** I have an emergency message, make room.
- **Affirmative / negative:** yes / no. Use these, not "yeah."
- **Wilco:** I will do it (use rarely, plain words are better).
- **Read back:** you repeat the message to confirm.
- **Spell** with the **phonetic alphabet** when names or numbers matter: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie... Say numbers as digits: "one two three," "niner" for nine if desired.

## Message format
For anything important, use a written form, like the FEMA ICS 213:
- **To, From, Date and time**
- **Subject**
- **Message** (short and clear)
- **Reply**
Write **before** you speak: it makes the call short, and it leaves a record.

## A standard structure for reports
**Who, what, where, when, how many, what needed.**
Example: "Base, this is Scout One. Medical emergency at the north bridge, one adult male with a leg injury, conscious, need transport. Time 1405. Over."

## Read back
For critical messages (locations, orders, medical, numbers), ask the receiver to read back. "Read back, over."

## Emergency traffic
Say "Emergency, emergency, emergency" (or "Mayday" for life-threatening distress, in the same repeated way) and give your location, the problem and what you need. All other stations stop.

## Log
Every message in and out goes in the log: time, from, to, and a brief summary.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
