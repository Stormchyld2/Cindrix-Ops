In a crisis, a flood of information, much of it wrong, spreads faster than the event itself. An operator keeps a clear picture and helps others do the same.

## The information cycle
1. **Collect:** gather facts from reliable sources.
2. **Verify:** check them against others.
3. **Analyze:** work out what they mean for you.
4. **Decide:** use them to plan.
5. **Share:** tell those who need to know.
6. **Review:** check results and update.

## Reliable sources
- **Official:** NOAA Weather Radio and the NWS, local emergency management, police and fire, public health, utility companies, FEMA, the Red Cross.
- **Local media:** radio and TV stations that are in the area.
- **Your own observation** and reports from trusted neighbors and group members.
- **Radio:** AM/FM radio, NOAA weather, public safety scanners (where legal), and amateur radio nets.

## Weak sources
- Anonymous posts, forwarded chain messages, screenshots with no source.
- Posts that cause strong emotion, request urgent sharing, or lack details.
- Sources with a motive to mislead or sell.

## Verify before you share
Ask:
1. **Who** said it, and do they have a reason to know?
2. **When?** Is it still current?
3. **Where?** Does it apply to us?
4. **Can I confirm it** with a second, independent source?
5. **Does it make sense?**
If you cannot verify, say "unconfirmed."

## Rumor control
- **Do not pass on** what you cannot verify.
- **Correct** false information politely and with sources.
- **Use an official rumor page** when one exists (FEMA runs rumor control during disasters).
- **Provide regular updates** to your group at set times, so people do not make up their own.
- **Avoid panic wording.** Give facts and what to do.

## Keep a situation board
A board or notebook that shows: time, what we know, what we do not know, what we are doing, and what is next. Update it at each shift. Use a map with pins.

## Track the situation
- Hazards and where they are.
- Roads open and closed.
- Weather and forecast.
- Resource status.
- People and their conditions.
- Official instructions.
- Rumors and their status.

## Briefings
Give short daily briefings: situation, plan, tasks, safety. Use the **SMEAC** structure.

## Disinformation and scams
After disasters, scams and false messages spike. Do not give money or personal details to unsolicited callers. Check charities and contractors.

## Overload
Limit news time. Constant alerts raise stress. Set times to check, and turn off the rest.

> Follow FCC rules. GMRS needs a license, ham radio needs an amateur license, and encrypting messages is not allowed on amateur and GMRS bands. Use plain language and legal methods only.
