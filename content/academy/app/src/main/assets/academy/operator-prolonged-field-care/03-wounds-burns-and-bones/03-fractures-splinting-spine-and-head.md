Broken bones are common in disasters, falls and crashes. Good splinting reduces pain, blood loss and further damage.

## Recognize a fracture
Pain, swelling, deformity, bruising, loss of use, grating, an open wound with bone, and a limb that looks wrong. When in doubt, splint.

## Principles of splinting
1. **Check circulation, sensation, and movement** below the injury (fingers or toes: color, warmth, feeling, wiggle) **before and after** splinting.
2. **Control bleeding** first.
3. **Cover open wounds** with a sterile dressing.
4. **Splint the limb as found,** padding gaps. Immobilize **the joint above and below** the break.
5. **Do not push bone back in.**
6. **Realign gently only if** the limb has no pulse or is severely deformed and you are trained, and stop if resistance or severe pain.
7. **Elevate** and, if available and appropriate, apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes at a time.
8. **Recheck** circulation every 15 minutes.

## Common splints
- **SAM-type splints,** cardboard, rolled newspapers, branches, trekking poles, or rolled blankets, tied with bandages or tape.
- **Sling and swathe** for the arm.
- **Buddy taping** of fingers and toes.
- **Pelvic binder** for hip and pelvis injuries.
- **Traction splints** for a broken thigh bone need training. A broken thigh can bleed over a liter into the leg.

## Dislocations
A joint that is out of place is painful and deformed. Splint it as found. Reduction (putting it back) is for trained people.

## Spinal injury
Suspect a spinal injury after a hard fall, a crash, a blow to the head or neck, or if the person has neck pain, tingling, numbness, weakness or cannot move.
- **Keep the head, neck and back in line.**
- **Hold the head steady** with your hands, and ask the person not to move.
- **Move only if** the airway is blocked, or the scene is dangerous. Then log roll with at least three helpers, keeping the spine straight.
- Improvised collars: a rolled towel may help, but hand support is best.
- **Pad** under the body and head to keep it aligned.

## Head injury
- Danger signs: loss of consciousness, repeated vomiting, severe headache, confusion, unequal pupils, weakness, clear fluid from the nose or ears, seizure, worsening drowsiness.
- Keep the head and neck in line, the head slightly raised if no spinal injury is suspected.
- Do not give food or drink if drowsy.
- **Watch** for changes, and wake and check every hour. Record.
- **Evacuate urgently** if danger signs appear or worsen.

## Pain and comfort
Support, positioning, reassurance and warmth reduce pain. Pain medicines are the business of clinicians, and your own prescriptions.

## Pressure sores and swelling
Pad, change position and check regularly.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.
