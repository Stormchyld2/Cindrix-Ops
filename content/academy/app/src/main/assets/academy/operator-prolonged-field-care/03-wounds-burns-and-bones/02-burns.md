Burns hurt, cause shock and invite infection. In the field, good first steps and simple care save skin and lives.

## Stop the burning
- **Remove** the person from the heat, flames, hot liquid or chemical.
- **Stop, drop and roll** for flames.
- **Remove** burned clothing and jewelry near the burn, unless stuck to the skin, before swelling.
- **Cool** the burn with cool (not ice cold) running water for 10 to 20 minutes. Do not use ice, butter, oils or toothpaste.
- **Chemical burns:** flush with large amounts of water for 20 minutes or more, brush off dry powder first. **Electrical burns** need urgent medical care since heart and internal damage can occur.

## Depth and size
- **Superficial (first degree):** red, dry, painful, like a sunburn.
- **Partial thickness (second degree):** red, blistered, wet and very painful.
- **Full thickness (third degree):** white, leathery or charred, and may be painless because nerves are destroyed.
- **Size:** the person's palm including fingers is about 1 percent of body surface. Large burns cause fluid loss and shock.

## Urgent burns (get help or evacuate)
- Burns to the **face, hands, feet, genitals, or over joints.**
- Burns that go **all the way around** a limb or the chest.
- **Large** burns: more than a few palms in an adult, and any significant burn in a child, an elder or a sick person.
- **Chemical, electrical** and **inhalation** burns.
- **Full-thickness burns** of any size.
- Signs of airway burns: hoarse voice, soot around the nose or mouth, singed hair, trouble breathing.

## Care in the field
- **Keep the person warm,** because burned skin cannot hold heat. Cool the burn only briefly, then cover and warm the person.
- **Cover** the burn with a clean, dry, non-stick dressing or plastic wrap (cling film) laid loosely, not wrapped tightly around.
- **Do not** break blisters. Do not apply ointments, which complicate care.
- **Elevate** burned limbs.
- **Fluids:** large burns cause big fluid loss. Offer frequent sips if the person is awake and able, as burn teams advise.
- **Pain** can be severe. Positioning, covering the burn from air, comfort and calm help. A clinician may direct medicines.
- **Check circulation** in burned limbs, as swelling can cut it off.
- **Remove rings and bracelets** early.

## Infection
Watch for pus, spreading redness, foul smell and fever. Change dressings gently with clean hands.

## Sunburn and heat
Treat with shade, cool water, fluids and soothing care. Severe, blistering sunburn on large areas needs care as a burn.

## Prevent
Fire safety, safe cooking, hot water at 120 °F or lower, smoke alarms, care with fuel and chemicals.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.
