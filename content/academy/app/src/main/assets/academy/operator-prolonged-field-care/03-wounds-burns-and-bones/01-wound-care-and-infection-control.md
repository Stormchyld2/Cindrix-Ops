After the bleeding is controlled, the next threat is infection. Good wound care over the following days is what keeps a survivable injury survivable.

## Principles
- **Stop the bleeding first,** then clean.
- **Clean hands, clean gloves, clean tools.**
- **Irrigate** with plenty of **clean, safe water** (treated if needed). Pressure irrigation from a syringe or a bag with a small hole flushes out dirt and germs. A liter or more for a dirty wound is common wilderness practice.
- **Do not scrub** deep wounds or pour in harsh chemicals. Avoid hydrogen peroxide and strong iodine in deep wounds because they damage tissue.
- **Remove** loose dirt and debris you can see. Leave embedded objects (a knife, a stick) in place: stabilize them with bulky dressings, since removal can cause heavy bleeding.
- **Dress the wound:** cover with clean, non-stick gauze, then bulky padding, held with a bandage that is snug but not tight.
- **Keep it dry and clean.** Change dressings daily or when soaked or dirty.

## To close or not to close
- Do not close dirty, deep, bite or puncture wounds, which are likely to become infected. Leave them open and covered.
- Clean, shallow cuts may be closed with strips, if edges come together easily and within a few hours.
- Do not sew wounds unless you are trained and the wound is clean.
- Heavily contaminated or deep wounds need a clinician.

## Signs of infection
- Spreading redness, warmth, swelling and growing pain
- Pus or foul smell
- Red streaks running up the limb
- Fever and chills
- Swollen glands
These need medical help. Antibiotics are prescribed by a clinician; do not guess.

## Tetanus
Tetanus spores live in soil. Keep tetanus shots up to date (about every ten years, sooner for a dirty wound).

## Bites
- Wash well with soap and water.
- Do not close.
- **Animal bites** risk rabies and infection: seek medical care promptly.
- **Human bites** are very prone to infection.
- **Snakebite:** keep calm, keep still, remove rings, immobilize the limb at heart level, and get to care. Do not cut, suck, tourniquet or ice the bite.

## Special wounds
- **Eye injuries:** do not press. Protect the eye with a rigid shield. Do not remove objects stuck in the eye.
- **Amputations:** control bleeding. Wrap the part in a clean damp cloth in a bag, and keep it cool, not on ice directly.
- **Abdominal wounds with exposed organs:** cover with a damp, clean dressing, do not push back, and keep knees bent.
- **Chest, neck and head wounds** need urgent help.

## Hygiene around the casualty
Wash hands before and after, dispose of waste safely, and keep wounds covered.

> This course teaches the thinking and the hands-on steps a layperson can do. Medicines, needles, fluids and surgery belong to trained clinicians. Take an in-person Wilderness First Responder, Wilderness EMT or Tactical Casualty Care course. Always call for help if you can.

- A clean scab
- Cold skin
+ Spreading redness, warmth, pus and red streaks
- Itch only

? What do you avoid with snakebite?
+ Cutting, sucking, tourniquets or ice
- Getting help
- Staying calm
