A casualty who is stable now may not be in an hour. Reassessment catches the change.

## The loop
Every 15 minutes (more often if unstable), run **MARCH** again:
- **M:** Is any bleeding soaking through? Is a tourniquet still working?
- **A:** Can the person speak? Is the airway clear?
- **R:** How fast is breathing? Is the chest moving evenly?
- **C:** Check pulse, color, warmth, capillary refill, and mental state.
- **H:** Head injury changes? Is the person staying warm?

## Vital signs, in plain terms
- **Pulse:** feel at the wrist (radial) or neck (carotid). Count for 30 seconds and double. Adults at rest: about 60 to 100 beats per minute. A fast, weak pulse signals shock. If the wrist pulse is missing, the situation is serious.
- **Breathing rate:** count breaths for 30 seconds and double. Adults at rest: about 12 to 20. Over 30 or under 10 is dangerous.
- **Skin:** pale, cool, clammy skin suggests shock. Blue lips suggest poor oxygen.
- **Capillary refill:** press a fingernail and release. Color should return in about two seconds.
- **Level of alertness:** use **AVPU:** Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive. A drop is a warning.
- **Temperature:** check by feel and thermometer. Cold is a danger.
- **Oxygen saturation** with an oximeter, if you have one. Normal is about 95 percent or higher. Readings under about 90 percent are a concern. Cold fingers and nail polish give false readings.
- **Pain:** ask for a 0 to 10 rating.

## Trends beat single numbers
Write down each set of numbers with the time. A rising pulse and a falling alertness suggest the person is getting worse, even if the numbers look okay. Look for **the direction.**

## Signs of serious trouble
- Breathing slow and noisy, or very fast.
- Weak or no pulse at the wrist.
- Confusion or drowsiness that grows.
- Skin turning gray or blue.
- A belly that becomes hard and swollen.
- Vomiting blood, or passing black or bloody stool.
- A seizure.
- A headache with vomiting and drowsiness after a head injury.
Respond: re-check MARCH, call for help, prepare to move.

## Talk to the casualty
A person who can talk to you is breathing, and has blood flowing to the brain. Keep asking questions, and keep them awake if possible.

## Head-to-toe again
Repeat a careful head-to-toe check every hour or so, since new injuries appear as shock fades and pain returns.

## Record it
A simple table in a notebook: time, pulse, breathing rate, skin, alertness, temperature, oxygen reading, notes and actions. See the documentation lesson.
