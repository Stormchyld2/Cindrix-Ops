After the first hour, the work shifts to living safely while the area is disrupted.

## Aftershocks
- They **decrease** in number and size over time, but some can be strong.
- Each time, **drop, cover and hold on.**
- **Damaged buildings** can fail in an aftershock. Stay out.
- **Sleep somewhere safe,** such as a tent or car outside, if your home is damaged.
- USGS publishes aftershock forecasts after large events. Check them when you can.

## Take care of basics
- **Water:** one gallon per person per day. Ration but do not skimp.
- **Food:** eat perishable food first, then stored food. Use a camp stove or grill **outdoors only.**
- **Shelter:** keep warm and dry. Use a tent or tarp if the home is unsafe.
- **Sanitation:** use a bucket toilet with a bag and some absorbent material. Wash hands often. See the Basic Preparedness course.
- **Medicine:** keep taking prescriptions, and plan refills.
- **Rest.**

## Information
- **Battery radio** for official updates.
- **Phone:** keep text messages short. Save the battery.
- **Neighbors:** share news and resources.
- **Beware rumors.** Use official sources.

## Community
- **Check on neighbors,** especially those who need help.
- **Share tools and skills.**
- **Do not interfere with rescue crews.** Let trained teams enter damaged buildings.
- **Join a CERT** or help in organized efforts.
- **Do not go sightseeing** in damaged areas.

## Travel
- **Avoid driving** unless needed. Roads may be damaged and responders need space.
- **Watch for damaged bridges,** road cracks and downed lines.
- **Keep fuel** in the car.

## Mental health
- **It is normal** to feel anxious, tired and shaky. Talk with others.
- **Children** may regress or have nightmares. Give them attention and routine.
- **If you feel overwhelmed,** seek help. The Disaster Distress Helpline is 1-800-985-5990 (call or text) in the US. 988 is the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

## Hazards in cleanup
- **Dust, glass and sharp debris:** wear boots, gloves, long sleeves and a mask.
- **Do not lift** heavy things alone.
- **Asbestos and lead:** older buildings may contain them. Avoid disturbing damaged materials, and ask local authorities for guidance.
- **Chemical spills:** keep away, ventilate, and call authorities.
- **Standing water** and **mold:** avoid.
- **Generators and carbon monoxide:** run generators outdoors, away from windows and doors. See the Power Outage course.

## Checklist
- Know what to do in an aftershock
- Safe place to sleep chosen
- Water, food and sanitation plan active
- A battery radio on
- Neighbors checked on

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
