Fastening things down is cheap and works. It is the best way to prevent injuries and damage.

## Walk each room and ask: what could fall, slide or break?
Look up, look at walls, look at the floor. Imagine strong shaking from side to side.

### Quick, free fixes
- **Move heavy items to low shelves.** Books, dishes, jars, tools and electronics should not sit high.
- **Move heavy pictures and mirrors** away from beds, couches and desks where people sit or sleep.
- **Move beds away from windows** and heavy objects overhead.
- **Keep exits clear** and do not place tall furniture across doorways.
- **Do not store flammable liquids** on high shelves or near an ignition source.
- **Use closed containers** for hazardous products and keep them low.

### Low-cost fixes
- **Anchor tall furniture** (bookcases, dressers, china cabinets, entertainment centers) to wall studs with brackets or straps.
- **Strap the water heater** to wall studs with two metal straps, one in the upper third and one in the lower third. A tipped water heater can break gas and water lines and start a fire.
- **Secure the refrigerator** and large appliances with straps where possible.
- **Hang pictures with closed hooks.**
- **Use museum putty or wax** on small items on shelves, and lips or cords on open shelves.
- **Fit cabinet latches** that lock on closing (child locks that hold doors shut during shaking).
- **Use flexible connectors** on gas and water lines to appliances. A plumber can install them. Do not DIY gas work.
- **Secure computers, TVs and monitors** with straps or non-slip pads.
- **Lock wheels** on rolling furniture and appliances.
- **Fasten lighting fixtures,** especially heavy ones over beds.
- **Strap down propane tanks** and gas grills.

### Bigger projects: retrofit
- **Bolt the house to its foundation** if it is an older wood-frame home.
- **Brace cripple walls** (short walls between the foundation and first floor).
- **Retrofit a soft-story building,** such as one over a garage.
- **Brace or remove unreinforced chimneys,** which can fall through roofs.
- **Hire a licensed structural engineer or contractor.** Many areas have retrofit programs and grants. Check with your state or county.

## Know where to shut off utilities
- **Gas:** know where the meter and shutoff valve are and keep a **wrench** near it. Shut off gas **only** if you smell gas, hear hissing or see damage. Once off, only the gas company should turn it back on.
- **Water:** know the main shutoff, to stop flooding from broken pipes.
- **Electricity:** know the main breaker.
- Mark each shutoff and show every adult in the home.

## Secure your workplace and school
- Walk the building. Ask your safety officer or manager about fixes.
- Know the evacuation and shelter plan.
- Keep desks and walkways clear.

## Make it a project
1. **Walk through** with a notebook, room by room.
2. **List fixes** from cheap to costly.
3. **Do the cheap ones this weekend.**
4. **Schedule** the others.
5. **Photograph** your work for insurance.
6. **Check again** each year.

## Checklist
- Heavy items moved to low shelves
- Tall furniture anchored
- Water heater strapped
- Gas shutoff location known with a wrench nearby
- Beds away from windows and heavy objects
- Retrofit questions asked about the home

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
