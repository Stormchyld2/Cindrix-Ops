Everyone in your household needs to know what to do, including the smallest and the most vulnerable.

## Children
- **Make it a game.** Practice "Drop, Cover, Hold On" like a drill. Praise them.
- **Keep instructions simple:** "Get down. Cover your head. Hold on."
- **Practice where they sleep, play and study.**
- **Explain** that earthquakes are natural and the shaking will stop.
- **Teach** them not to run outside, and where to meet afterward.
- **Make sure** their school or daycare has a plan, and know who may pick them up.
- **Put a card** in their backpack with contact details and the out-of-area contact.
- **After a quake,** children may be scared, clingy or have trouble sleeping. Be patient, offer comfort and keep routines.

## Babies and toddlers
- **Keep cribs away from windows** and heavy objects, with nothing hanging over them.
- **If an earthquake happens,** cover the baby with your body, and a blanket.
- **Keep supplies:** diapers, formula, spare clothing, comfort items.

## Older adults and people with limited mobility
- **Plan with them,** and ask what they need.
- **Wheelchair users:** lock your wheels, bend forward to protect your head, hold on.
- **People who use walkers or canes:** get low, cover your head.
- **Keep** a flashlight, whistle and shoes **by the bed,** and a charged phone.
- **Arrange helpers:** neighbors who can check on them.
- **Keep medicines** and equipment (oxygen, hearing aids, glasses) in a grab bag.
- **Know evacuation help:** elevators will not work. Plan for stairs and for carrying techniques that trained people use.

## People who are blind, deaf or have other disabilities
- **Blind or low vision:** keep a cane by the bed, and learn the layout of exits.
- **Deaf or hard of hearing:** keep a flashlight, written plans, and a vibrating alert or bed shaker if possible, and a pen and paper to communicate.
- **Cognitive or developmental disabilities:** use clear, simple instructions, visual cards, and practice.
- **Service animals:** plan for their care and carry supplies.
- **Medical equipment:** know back-up power plans.

## Pets
- Keep **carriers,** leashes, food, water and records ready.
- **Pets may bolt** or hide after a quake. Keep them secured.
- **Know** which shelters allow pets.

## Check on neighbors
After shaking and your own safety, **check on those who may need help:** older neighbors, those living alone, those with disabilities and families with children.

## Checklist
- Practiced drop, cover and hold on with every child
- A plan for each person with special needs
- Medicines and equipment ready in a grab bag
- Pets included in the plan
- A neighbor buddy system

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
