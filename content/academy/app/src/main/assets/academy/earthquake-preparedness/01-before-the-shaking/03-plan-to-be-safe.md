Shaking is only the start. A plan decides how your household reunites and gets through the next days.

## Make a written plan
1. **Out-of-area contact:** pick one person who lives far away. After a disaster, local calls jam but a call or text to a distant relative may get through. Everyone calls or texts that person to say they are safe.
2. **Meeting places:** pick one near your home (a street corner or park) and one outside your neighborhood in case you cannot get home.
3. **Where each person will be:** school, work, daycare. Know their plans for an earthquake. Know who is allowed to pick up the children.
4. **Special needs:** older adults, people with disabilities, babies, pets and anyone who needs medicine or equipment.
5. **Who does what:** assign jobs: grab the kit, check the gas, get the pets, check neighbors.
6. **Practice:** every adult and child knows what to do.

## Practice drop, cover and hold on
- Do it at home, at work and at school. **Practice at least once a year** (many places hold the Great ShakeOut each October).
- **Practice in different rooms** and imagine different situations: in bed, in the kitchen, in the car.
- **Time yourself.** The goal is to take cover in seconds.

## Know the safe spots
In each room, find the **best cover:** a sturdy table or desk to get under, or an inside wall to crouch against and cover your head and neck. Know the **danger spots:** windows, mirrors, tall furniture, hanging lights and exterior walls.

## Talk about the after
- **Check for injuries,** give first aid.
- **Check for damage and hazards,** such as gas leaks, fires and broken glass.
- **If your home is unsafe,** where will you go?
- **Text instead of calling,** to keep phone lines open.
- **Listen to the radio** and official alerts for information.
- **Expect aftershocks.**
- **Care for pets** and plan where they can stay.

## Plan for tsunamis, if on the coast
- If you feel **strong or long shaking,** or the ground shakes for **more than 20 seconds,** or you are told to, **go to high ground or inland at once on foot.**
- **Do not wait for an official warning.**
- Know your zone, routes and assembly areas. Watch for signs.

## Communications
- **Text** if calls fail.
- **Use social media** or the "safe and well" tools of the Red Cross.
- **Keep your phone charged.**
- **Use a battery or crank radio** to hear alerts.
- **Add a GMRS or ham radio plan** for your group (see the Radio Licenses course).

## Neighbors
- **Get to know your neighbors.** Know who has skills, equipment, children or needs.
- **Plan a neighborhood meeting spot** and a check-in system.
- **Take a CERT class.**

## Write it down
Fill in the household plan in this program (Plan screen) and print a copy for each adult, the go bag and the car. Include the contact numbers on paper, because phones may die.

## Checklist
- Out-of-area contact chosen
- Two meeting places named
- Everyone has practiced drop, cover and hold on
- Plan includes children, pets and people with needs
- Plan printed and kept in the go bag

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
