A damaged home can cost tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars to fix. Planning ahead softens the blow. This lesson is general information, not financial or legal advice.

## Insurance
- **Standard homeowners and renters policies usually do not cover earthquake damage.** Earthquake coverage is a separate policy or endorsement, in most of the US.
- **Ask your agent:** What is covered? What is the **deductible** (often a percentage of the home's value, such as 10 to 20 percent)? Are **contents** and **additional living expenses** covered? What is excluded (often fire is covered by the standard policy, but ground failure and flood are separate)?
- **Compare** cost against your risk and ability to pay.
- **Flood** is also not covered by standard policies. Earthquakes in some areas cause floods or tsunamis.
- **Renters:** ask about contents coverage.
- **Review** each year.

## Retrofit may lower the cost
- Some programs offer **grants** or **discounts** for retrofitting.
- Check your state earthquake authority and local programs.

## Documents
- **Store copies** of important documents in a waterproof bag and in a secure online or off-site location: IDs, passports, insurance policies, bank and credit card details, deeds, titles, wills, medical records, tax records.
- **Take photos or video** of every room and valuables for insurance claims, and keep the files off-site.
- **Keep a list** of contact numbers for your insurer, bank and mortgage holder.

## Emergency money
- **Cash:** keep a stash of small bills in your kit.
- **Savings:** build an **emergency fund** for deductibles and temporary housing.
- **Know your mortgage terms.** Damage to your home does not usually pause your payment, so ask your lender about options.

## After the quake
- **Contact your insurer** quickly and **document damage** with photos before cleaning up.
- **Keep receipts** for emergency repairs and living expenses.
- **Watch for scams** and contractors who ask for money upfront. Verify licenses.
- **Apply for FEMA assistance** if a disaster is declared. It does not replace insurance.
- **Ask your lenders** about hardship options.

## Checklist
- Know whether you have earthquake coverage and its deductible
- Photos and videos of your home and belongings stored off-site
- Copies of key documents in a waterproof bag and off-site
- Cash in the kit
- Know who to call at your insurer and bank

> Earthquake guidance is the same across the US, but local hazards differ. Check your state geological survey and emergency management office.
