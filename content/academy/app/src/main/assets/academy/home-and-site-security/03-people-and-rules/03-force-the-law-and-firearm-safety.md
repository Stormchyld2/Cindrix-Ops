If you choose to keep a firearm for home defense, safety and the law are your first responsibilities. A gun in the house raises the stakes for everyone, so training and storage matter more than the weapon.

## The law, in outline
- **Self-defense** generally allows reasonable force to protect yourself or others from an imminent threat of death or serious harm. The force must be **proportionate** and **necessary.**
- **Duty to retreat:** some states require you to retreat if you safely can. Others, with "stand your ground," do not. Many have a "castle doctrine" inside your home. Rules differ widely.
- **Property alone** usually does not justify deadly force.
- **After the threat ends,** the right to use force ends.
- **Consequences:** even a lawful shooting leads to investigation, legal costs and trauma.
- **Know your state.** Talk to a local attorney or a certified instructor. Join a legal defense program if you wish.

## The four rules of firearm safety
1. **Treat every firearm as loaded.**
2. **Never point it at anything you are not willing to destroy.**
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on target and you have decided to shoot.**
4. **Know your target, what is around it and what is behind it.**

## Safe storage
Most gun deaths in the home are accidents, suicides and thefts, not defensive uses.
- **Lock** every firearm unloaded in a safe or with a lock when not in your control, and store ammunition separately, if children or at-risk people are around.
- A **quick-access safe** (biometric or keypad) can keep a home-defense gun secure and reachable for you.
- **Never** leave a gun where children, visitors or people in crisis can reach it.
- Talk to children about gun safety: stop, don't touch, leave, tell an adult.
- If anyone in the home is in mental health crisis, consider storing guns elsewhere for a time.

## Training
- Take a certified safety and basic skills class, and then a class on the legal and practical issues of defense.
- Practice safe handling, cleaning and storage.
- Practice with a **unloaded or training firearm** and a trusted instructor.
- Know that stress changes how well you shoot and judge.

## Alternatives
Many people choose non-lethal options (alarms, lighting, dogs, reinforced doors, a safe room, pepper spray where legal). They carry less legal and moral risk. Whatever you choose, do not rely on one thing.

## If you do use force
Call 911 at once, say you were attacked and need help, put the weapon down when police arrive, show empty hands, say little, and ask for a lawyer before giving a statement.

## Mental health
Take care of yourself and your family if an event occurs. Seek counseling.

> Laws on self-defense, firearms and force differ by state and country. This course is general safety education, not legal advice. Talk to a local attorney and take a certified class.
