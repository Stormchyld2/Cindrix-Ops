Early warning gives you time. Time is the thing you need most.

## Lighting
- **Motion lights** at doors, the yard and the driveway.
- **Dusk-to-dawn lights** at entrances.
- **Low-level lights** along paths. Avoid glare that blinds you.
- **Keep bushes trimmed** below windows and away from doors.
- Battery or solar lights give light in a power cut.

## Alarms
- **Smoke alarms** on every level and in every bedroom. Test monthly, replace batteries yearly, and replace the unit after ten years.
- **Carbon monoxide alarms** on every level and near bedrooms.
- **Door and window alarms:** small battery-powered chimes that sound when opened. Cheap, useful at night, and portable.
- **Monitored alarm systems** call a service. They need power or batteries and a phone or internet link.
- **Panic buttons** and wearable alert devices.

## Cameras
- Visible cameras deter, and give a record.
- Put them where they see doors, driveways and yards, not windows into neighbors' homes.
- Know local privacy rules.
- Many need power and the internet. Have a way to save recordings, and a backup.

## Dogs
A dog is an alarm that barks. A trained dog can help, but needs care, training and responsibility.

## Neighbors
The best alarm is a neighbor who notices. Share phone numbers, watch each other's homes and agree on a signal for trouble.

## Simple, low-tech warnings
- Gravel paths that crunch.
- Tin cans on a cord across a gap (only for camps or remote property).
- Squeaky gates left unoiled.
- Bells on doors.

## Power cuts
Alarms and cameras may fail when power does. Keep batteries, a battery backup, flashlights and a plan for manual watches.

## Test
Walk in with a door open at night. How long before someone would know? Fix gaps.
