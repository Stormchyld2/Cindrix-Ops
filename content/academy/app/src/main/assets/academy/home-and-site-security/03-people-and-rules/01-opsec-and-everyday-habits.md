Operational security (OPSEC) means not giving people information they could use against you. Most of it is quiet common sense.

## What to protect
- That you have extra supplies, tools, fuel or cash.
- Your schedule: when the house is empty.
- Your address, vehicle, plans and the people in your group.
- Your children's names, schools and routines.
- Passwords, account numbers, and documents.

## Social media
- Do not post pictures of your stock, locations, license plates or maps.
- Do not announce trips while you are away, or share live locations.
- Turn off location tagging on photos.
- Tell family not to post details about your group or plans.
- Use privacy settings. Remove personal data from people-search sites where you can.

## Talk
- Avoid bragging about preparations.
- Keep conversations about supplies and plans with those who need to know.
- Use a cover story if asked: "We keep some food for storms."

## Mail and trash
- Shred papers with names and numbers.
- Do not leave boxes of supplies or empty packaging of expensive gear at the curb.
- Hold mail while you travel.

## Routines
- Vary your routes and times where possible.
- Do not leave the same time and way every day.
- Park in well-lit places and lock doors and valuables out of sight.
- Make the house look lived in when away: lights on timers, a neighbor to collect packages.

## Strangers and approaches
- Be polite but careful. You do not have to open the door. Speak through it.
- Ask for identification from "workers" and verify with their company.
- Beware of pretexts: someone "needs help," "checking the gas," or "selling" something.
- Trust your instincts and leave if something feels wrong.

## Digital habits
Use strong passwords, two-step sign-in, updates and backups. Be careful with scams and phishing, especially after disasters, when fraud rises.

## The balance
You do not need to be secretive about everything. A calm, friendly, low-profile family is hard to target.

> Laws on self-defense, firearms and force differ by state and country. This course is general safety education, not legal advice. Talk to a local attorney and take a certified class.
