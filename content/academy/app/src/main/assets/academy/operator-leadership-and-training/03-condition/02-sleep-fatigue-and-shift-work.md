Tired people make mistakes, get angry, and get hurt. In long events, sleep is a resource to manage, like water.

## What lack of sleep does
- Slower thinking and reactions.
- Poor memory and judgment.
- More risk-taking, or more caution that slows things down.
- Mood changes and conflict.
- More illness and accidents.
After about 17 to 19 hours awake, performance is like having a blood alcohol level of 0.05 percent. After 24 hours it is worse.

## How much
Most adults need seven to nine hours. Teenagers eight to ten. Children more. In an emergency you may get less, so protect what you can.

## Plan sleep
- **Make a sleep schedule** as part of every plan.
- **Share the watch** so that each person gets a long block of sleep when possible, 4 hours at least and ideally 6 to 8.
- **Do not make one person do all the night work.**
- **Protect the sleeping place:** quiet, dark, cool, comfortable.
- **Use earplugs and eye covers.**
- **Naps** of 20 to 30 minutes help, and a nap before a long night shift helps more.

## Shift work design
- Watches of **two to four hours** at night keep people alert.
- **Rotate** the least popular hours fairly.
- Allow time to hand over carefully.
- Avoid changing schedules abruptly.
- Keep at least one rested decision-maker on call.

## Fight fatigue
- **Food and water:** regular meals and drink.
- **Light:** bright light in the day. Darkness at sleep time.
- **Movement:** stand, stretch, walk.
- **Talk and tasks** that keep the mind busy, in safe jobs.
- **Caffeine** can help in moderate amounts, early in the shift. Avoid late use that blocks sleep.
- **Do not rely** on energy drinks and other stimulants.

## Signs of dangerous fatigue
Micro-sleeps, nodding, missing words, forgetting what you just did, drifting while driving, irritability. If you see these:
- Stop risky tasks, especially driving, machinery and weapons handling.
- Swap with a rested person.
- Sleep.

## Driving
Do not drive when drowsy. Pull over safely, nap 20 minutes, and share driving.

## Recovery
After a long event, sleep as much as needed and return slowly to routine.

## Mental health
Poor sleep worsens anxiety and mood. Keep routines and talk with others.
