Skills fade without practice. A simple training program keeps your group ready.

## Principles
- **Train as you will fight.** Practice in real conditions: dark, rain, tired, with gear on.
- **Crawl, walk, run.** Learn the basics, then add complexity, then add stress and time pressure.
- **Repeat.** Short, frequent sessions beat one long day.
- **Make it practical.** Hands-on, not just reading.
- **Test and fix.** Find weak points in a safe setting.
- **Cross-train** so every key task has more than one person.

## Step 1: Assess
List the hazards from your risk assessment and the tasks needed to deal with them. Mark each person's current skill: none, basic, good, can teach.

## Step 2: Set standards
For each task, define what "good" looks like and how long it should take.
- Put out a small fire with an extinguisher: in under 10 seconds from the call.
- Treat a severe leg bleed with a tourniquet: in under one minute.
- Set up shelter: in 10 minutes.
- Set up the radio station: in 15 minutes.

## Step 3: Make the schedule
- **Weekly:** a 15-minute skill drill.
- **Monthly:** a 2-hour practical.
- **Quarterly:** a scenario drill.
- **Yearly:** a full exercise and review of the plan and supplies.
Put these on the calendar.

## Step 4: Build the sessions
Each session has:
1. **Objective:** one skill.
2. **Explain and show:** short demo.
3. **Practice:** hands-on with feedback.
4. **Test:** do it to the standard.
5. **Review:** what to fix.

## Step 5: Record
Use the sign-off page in the Academy to record who has passed what. Aim to bring every person to Tier 3, then Tier 2, then Tier 1 in their area.

## Sample yearly cycle
- **Spring:** water, food, garden, vehicle checks.
- **Summer:** navigation, fitness, field skills, fire.
- **Fall:** security, communications, heating, winter prep.
- **Winter:** medical, planning, indoor skills, review.

## Include children and elders
Give every person a role matched to their ability. Children can learn to call for help, use a whistle, know the rally point and basic first aid.

## Keep it positive
Make it fun, with games and small challenges. People who enjoy training do more of it.
