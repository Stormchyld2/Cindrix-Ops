Exercises test plans. After-action reviews turn experience into improvement. Together they are the best teachers.

## Kinds of exercises
- **Discussion (tabletop):** talk through a scenario at a table. Good for plans and decisions.
- **Drill:** practice one skill or action, such as an evacuation.
- **Functional:** test a function such as communications or first aid with realistic conditions.
- **Full-scale:** the whole plan, as close to real as is safe.

## Plan an exercise
1. **Purpose:** what do we want to learn?
2. **Scenario:** a realistic event: a winter storm, a house fire at night, a chemical spill nearby.
3. **Objectives:** two or three things to test.
4. **Roles:** players, a controller who runs the scenario, and an observer who takes notes.
5. **Safety:** a safety officer and a stop word, like "real world," for a real emergency.
6. **Materials:** maps, props, gear, a timeline of events (injects).
7. **Schedule and notice:** tell everyone and keep it safe.

## Run it
- Brief the group, including safety.
- Start the scenario.
- Add surprises: a blocked road, a failed radio, an injured person.
- Observers record what happened and when.
- End when objectives are tested or safety requires it.

## The after-action review (AAR)
Hold it **soon,** while memories are fresh. Everyone takes part. No blame. The goal is learning.

Ask four questions:
1. **What did we plan to do?**
2. **What actually happened?**
3. **Why was there a difference?**
4. **What will we sustain, and what will we improve?**

## Running the AAR
- Start with the plan, then the timeline.
- Let the people who did things speak first.
- Look at causes, not people: gear, training, communication, planning.
- Write the lessons down.
- For each improvement, assign an **owner and a date.**
- Follow up at the next meeting.

## Improvement plan
Put actions on the to-do list in the Cindrix Ops program with an owner and due date. Update the plan, buy gear, schedule training. Repeat the exercise to confirm the fix.

## Real events
Hold an AAR after every real incident, big or small. A near miss is a free lesson.

> The goal is not to be perfect. It is to be a bit better every time.
