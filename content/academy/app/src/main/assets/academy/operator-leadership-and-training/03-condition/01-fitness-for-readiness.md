In a crisis you may need to walk miles, carry loads, climb, lift, dig and stay on your feet for long hours. Fitness makes all of that safer.

## Before you start
- See a doctor if you have a health condition, are over 40 and inactive, or have symptoms such as chest pain or dizziness.
- Start gently. Build over weeks.
- Stop if you feel sharp pain, chest pain or dizziness.

## What to train
1. **Aerobic endurance:** walking, hiking, cycling, swimming. The CDC suggests at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity for adults.
2. **Strength:** two or more days a week for all major muscles. Squats, lunges, push-ups, rows, carries and deadlift patterns.
3. **Carrying:** loaded walks (rucking) start light and increase gradually.
4. **Mobility and balance:** stretching, yoga, single-leg balance work.
5. **Core:** planks and carries protect the back.

## Practical tests
Pick tasks that match your needs, and test every few months:
- Walk 5 miles with a light pack.
- Carry a bag of groceries or a child safely for a quarter mile.
- Climb stairs for five minutes.
- Lift and carry an item of about a quarter of your body weight a short way, with good form.
- Get up from the floor without using hands.
Measure progress, not comparison.

## A simple weekly plan
- **Day 1:** 30 to 45 minutes brisk walk.
- **Day 2:** strength: squats, push-ups, rows, planks.
- **Day 3:** rest or gentle stretching.
- **Day 4:** loaded walk or hike.
- **Day 5:** strength again.
- **Day 6:** longer easy activity.
- **Day 7:** rest.

## Protect your body
- Warm up, cool down.
- Use good form.
- Take rest days.
- Wear proper shoes and care for your feet.
- Drink water and eat enough protein and carbohydrate.
- Progress by about 10 percent a week in load or distance.

## For different groups
- **Older adults:** balance, leg strength and walking.
- **Children:** active play, an hour a day.
- **People with limits:** adapt, and use what you can do. Seated exercise counts.

## Heat and cold
Train in different weather in a safe way, and learn the signs of heat illness and hypothermia.

## Motivation
Exercise with others. Track progress. Fitness is part of preparedness.
