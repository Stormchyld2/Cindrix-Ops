Stress narrows attention, hurts memory and speeds up bad decisions. A leader who understands this can protect themselves and the group.

## What stress does
- Tunnel vision, poor listening, forgetting simple steps.
- Fine motor skills drop. Large movements still work.
- Time seems to speed up or slow down.
- Some people freeze, some flee, some fight, some help. All are normal.

## Control yourself first
1. **Breathe:** in for four counts, hold four, out for four, hold four. Repeat a few times.
2. **Slow down your voice.** A calm voice calms others.
3. **Name the problem** in one sentence.
4. **Pick the first small action.**
5. **Do it,** then reassess.

## The decision loop
**Observe, orient, decide, act, then observe again.** Short loops beat perfect plans.
- Gather the facts you have, not the ones you wish you had.
- Decide with 70 percent of the information when time is short.
- State the decision and the reason in a sentence.
- Check results and adjust.

## Giving orders in stress
- Use short, simple, direct sentences.
- Use the person's name, then the task, then the time or standard: "Sam, take the children to the car now."
- Ask for a repeat of important orders.
- Do not shout unless you need to be heard.

## Keeping the group steady
- **Give a job to everyone.** Work reduces fear.
- **Give information,** even when it is bad, and say what is being done.
- **Be visible and calm.**
- **Show respect.** Praise specifically and correct privately when you can.
- **Do not promise** what you cannot deliver.

## Spotting stress in others
Signs include: shaking, snapping at others, going silent, repeating questions, rapid breathing, poor judgment, or not eating or drinking.

## Psychological first aid
Safety first, then:
1. Check on the person and listen without pressure.
2. Meet basic needs: warmth, water, food, rest.
3. Give clear, honest information.
4. Link them with family and friends.
5. Give them a simple task if they are able.
6. Do not force them to talk about the event.
Get professional help for severe or lasting distress, such as thoughts of self-harm. In the United States, the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline can be reached by call or text to 988.

## Your own limits
Eat, drink, rest and hand over when you can. A tired leader makes mistakes.
