Small animals turn kitchen scraps and garden waste into eggs, meat and manure. They also need care every day, so decide whether you can give it.

## Check the rules first
City and county rules may limit or ban chickens and rabbits, and may limit roosters. Check zoning, the homeowners' association and any permits.

## Chickens
- **Eggs:** a good hen lays about 4 to 6 eggs a week in her first couple of years, fewer in winter and as she ages. Four to six hens feed a family's eggs.
- **Coop:** about 3 to 4 square feet per bird inside, and about 10 in a run. Provide roosts, nest boxes (one per 3 or 4 hens), ventilation without drafts, and dry bedding.
- **Predators:** raccoons, foxes, hawks, dogs, snakes and rats. Use **hardware cloth** (not chicken wire) with half-inch openings, buried or skirted at the base, and lock the coop at night.
- **Feed and water:** a balanced layer feed, grit and oyster shell, and clean water every day. In an emergency, add scraps, garden waste, grain, bugs, and a forage.
- **Health:** watch for lethargy, pale combs, mites, lice and respiratory illness. Keep new birds apart for 30 days. Wash hands after handling.
- **Biosecurity:** avian influenza spreads by wild birds. Keep feed covered, limit contact with wild birds and report sick or dying birds to your state veterinarian.
- **Meat birds:** fast-growing breeds reach butchering weight in 8 to 10 weeks. Learn humane slaughter and clean processing before you need it.

## Rabbits
- Quiet, small, and produce a lot of meat for little feed. A doe can have several litters a year.
- **Hutches:** wire-floored hutches off the ground, in shade, with shelter from wind, and nest boxes for litters.
- **Feed:** pellets, hay (the main food), greens and clean water.
- **Care:** keep out of heat, which is dangerous, protect from predators, and handle gently.
- Rabbit meat is very lean, so it should be balanced with fat in the diet.

## Waste is a resource
Manure makes compost. Compost chicken manure for several months before using it on food crops, and follow food safety guidance on timing before harvest.

## Other animals
Goats, sheep, pigs, cattle, bees and fish need more land, knowledge and fencing. Start small. Learn from a local farmer or a Cooperative Extension office first.

## Plan for feed and vet care
Store feed in sealed metal bins away from rodents. Know a local vet. Have a plan for a sick animal, and for what happens if you cannot feed them.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
