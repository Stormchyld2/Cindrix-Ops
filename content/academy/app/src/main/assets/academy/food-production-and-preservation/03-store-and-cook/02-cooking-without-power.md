Stored food is no use if you cannot cook it. Plan stoves, fuel and recipes before you need them.

## Safe fire rules first
- **Never burn charcoal, run a camp stove or use a gas grill indoors.** Carbon monoxide kills in minutes.
- Cook outdoors or under a well-ventilated cover, away from walls, eaves and flammable materials.
- Keep water or a fire extinguisher nearby, and keep children and pets away.
- Fuel must be stored safely (see Building your stash).

## Stove choices
- **Propane camp stove:** easy and clean. Store extra cylinders outdoors.
- **Butane stove:** compact but poor in cold.
- **Liquid-fuel stoves** (white gas, multi-fuel): rugged and good in cold.
- **Rocket stove:** burns small sticks efficiently. Cheap and can be homemade.
- **Wood or charcoal grill:** outdoors, with plenty of fuel.
- **Solar oven:** free fuel, slow, needs sun.
- **Sterno and alcohol stoves:** small and light for backup.
- **Dutch oven:** cast iron for baking and stewing on coals.

## Fuel planning
Estimate: a family cooking two hot meals a day may use about a pound of propane every couple of days, or a bag of charcoal every few days. Test for yourself, then multiply for a month or three.

## Fuel-saving cooking
- Soak beans and grains before cooking.
- Use a lid and a pot that fits the burner.
- **Thermal cooking:** bring food to a boil, then insulate the pot in blankets or a box to finish cooking without fuel.
- Cut food small.
- Cook one-pot meals.
- Use a pressure cooker.

## Foods that need little cooking
Instant oats, couscous, dehydrated meals, canned foods, tortillas, nut butters, jerky, and hard cheeses.

## Practice
Cook a full week of meals on your emergency stove with stored food. Note fuel used, time taken and what you missed. Write your findings on a card and put it with your gear.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
