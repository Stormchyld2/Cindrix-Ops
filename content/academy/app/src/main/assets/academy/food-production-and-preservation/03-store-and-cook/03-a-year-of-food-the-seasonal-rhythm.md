Put everything together in a yearly cycle.

## Winter: plan and tend
- Review last year's records. Order seeds, test germination and plan beds.
- Tend livestock and keep them warm, fed and watered.
- Check stored food, rotate, repair.
- Start onions, leeks and early seedlings indoors toward late winter.
- Use dried and canned food, ferments and roots from the cellar.

## Spring: sow and prepare
- Prepare beds with compost.
- Sow peas, spinach, lettuce, radishes, potatoes and onions as soon as the soil is workable.
- Move hardened seedlings outdoors after the last frost.
- Raise chicks and rabbits.
- Forage young greens safely.

## Summer: grow and begin to preserve
- Weed, water and mulch.
- Harvest daily. Preserve what you cannot eat: dry herbs, freeze, can tomatoes and jam, make pickles.
- Plant fall crops.
- Save seed from early crops.
- Keep animals cool.

## Fall: harvest and store
- Harvest potatoes, squash, onions, carrots and beans. Cure them.
- Can, dry and ferment in bulk.
- Plant garlic and cover crops.
- Fill the cellar and woodpile.
- Butcher what you will not winter, using safe methods.

## Keep a calendar
A simple wall calendar listing jobs per month turns a vague plan into habit. Record dates of first and last frost, planting and harvest.

## Track the numbers
How many pounds of food did you grow? How many jars did you can? How much did you eat? Compare to your family's needs. Add one skill or project each year.

## Share and trade
Swap seed, surplus and skills with neighbors. A community that shares food is more resilient than one household alone.

## The goal
Move steadily from "I have food stored" to "I can keep making food." Do it in steps, and keep a store as a safety net while you learn.

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
