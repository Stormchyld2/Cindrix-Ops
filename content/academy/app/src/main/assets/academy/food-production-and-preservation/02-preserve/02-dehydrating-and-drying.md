Drying removes water so germs cannot grow. It is simple, light, and uses little equipment.

## Methods
- **Electric dehydrator:** controlled heat and airflow. Fast and reliable.
- **Oven:** set low (about 140 °F) with the door propped open.
- **Sun drying:** only in hot, dry, breezy weather (humidity under 60 percent and temperatures above about 85 °F). Fruits suit it best.
- **Air drying:** herbs, hot peppers and beans on the stalk.
- **Solar dryer:** a simple box with screens and a vent works in good sun.

## Steps
1. Choose fresh, ripe, unblemished food.
2. Wash and slice **evenly, thin** (about a quarter inch or less).
3. **Pretreat** where needed: blanch vegetables briefly to stop enzymes, and dip fruit that browns in lemon or ascorbic acid solution.
4. Spread in a single layer with space for air.
5. Dry at the right temperature until done: fruit is leathery and pliable, vegetables are brittle, and herbs crumble.
6. **Condition:** put dried fruit in loosely packed jars for several days, shaking daily, to even out moisture. Mold means it needs more drying.
7. **Pack** airtight in glass jars or Mylar bags, with an oxygen absorber if the food is dry enough, and store cool and dark.

## Jerky and meat
Raw meat needs care. Use lean meat, marinate, then **heat the meat to 160 °F (poultry 165 °F) before or during drying** to kill germs, according to USDA guidance, and dry until it cracks but does not break. Store cool and dry and eat within a couple of months, or freeze.

## What dries well
Apples, pears, bananas, berries, tomatoes, onions, peppers, carrots, green beans, peas, corn, mushrooms (from safe sources), herbs and leather made from fruit puree.

## Using it
Rehydrate in water or add to soups and stews. A pound of dried vegetables replaces many pounds of fresh.

## Safety
- Do not under-dry food.
- Keep out of humidity.
- Check stored food for mold, and throw away any with mold.
- Do not dry foods with high fat (nuts keep better whole, in a freezer).

> Always use tested recipes and current guidance for preserving food. Local laws govern livestock, hunting, fishing and selling food.
