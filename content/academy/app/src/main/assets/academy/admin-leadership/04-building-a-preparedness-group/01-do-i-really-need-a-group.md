The lone wolf does not do well when things get difficult. You need like-minded people who can watch your back and fill in what you cannot handle yourself.

## The case for a group
- **Skills multiply.** No one person can be a nurse, a mechanic, a radio operator, a cook, a gardener and a guard. A group can.
- **Time and rest.** In a long emergency, someone has to sleep. Shared work and shared watches prevent exhaustion.
- **Resources.** Shared tools, vehicles, generators and supplies go further.
- **Safety.** A group is harder to hurt than one person alone, and has more eyes to notice danger.
- **Morale.** People cope better together. Isolation is hard on mental health.
- **Recovery.** Research on disasters shows that communities with strong social ties recover faster and with fewer deaths.

A well-known saying: **"If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together."**

## Why most people do not build one
Few people have the skill to create a group, the drive to follow through and the focus to make the group a priority.

- **Normalcy bias.** People assume tomorrow will be like today, so building a group does not feel urgent.
- **Busy lives.** Jobs, families, hobbies and bills crowd everything out.
- **Fear of looking strange.** Many people do not want to be seen as "a prepper."
- **Not knowing how.** Starting is the hardest step.

## The best members are busy
The people you want in a group are generally those with lots of **important and urgent** priorities already: they work, have families, have skills and perhaps money. They will not join something vague or disorganized.

## You be the driving force
Someone has to get things moving. If you wait for others, nothing will happen.

1. **Have a plan.** Know what you want to do and why.
2. **Do the steps one by one.** This course lays them out.
3. **Be patient.** Groups take months to form and years to mature.
4. **Make it easy to say yes.** Keep the first events short, friendly and useful.
5. **Show, do not preach.** Let people see what you do and why it helps.

## If a group is not possible yet
- Start with your **household.** A prepared family is a group.
- Build **one friendship** at a time.
- Join an **existing group:** CERT, a church team, a ham radio club, a neighborhood association.
- Keep your own plan strong. A prepared member is an asset to any group.

## Checklist
- Write down the reasons you want a group.
- List five people you know who might be interested.
- Decide the first small event you could hold.
- Look up your local CERT team and radio club.

> The best time to build a group is before you need one.
