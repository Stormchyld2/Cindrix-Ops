Every group has disagreements. A group that handles them well becomes stronger. A group that ignores them falls apart. Teams of any kind go through predictable stages, and understanding them helps you lead through them.

## The stages of a group
Researchers describe four stages that most groups pass through.

1. **Forming.** People are polite and unsure. They look for direction and ask what the group is about. *The leader's job:* give clear purpose, structure and a warm welcome.
2. **Storming.** Differences appear. People disagree about plans, roles and leadership. It can feel uncomfortable. *The leader's job:* stay calm, listen, help people talk it out, and keep to the group's purpose and agreement.
3. **Norming.** The group settles into ways of working. Trust grows and roles become clear. *The leader's job:* reinforce good habits and share the lead.
4. **Performing.** The group works well together and gets things done. *The leader's job:* delegate, support and keep improving.

A group may slip back when things change, such as a new member or a crisis. That is normal.

## Build trust
Trust is the foundation. It comes from small things done reliably.

- **Keep your word.** Do what you say, or say early when you cannot.
- **Be honest,** including about mistakes.
- **Show up,** on time and prepared.
- **Respect privacy** and confidences.
- **Give credit,** and take blame when it is yours.
- **Treat everyone fairly,** whatever their status or skill level.
- **Train together.** Shared effort builds bonds.

## Common sources of conflict
- **Unclear roles,** so people step on each other.
- **Different expectations** about commitment.
- **Personality clashes.**
- **Ego and status,** from strong or domineering personalities.
- **Money and shared resources.**
- **Strong disagreement** on politics, religion or values. Keep these out of group business, and agree on what the group is about.
- **Stress,** which magnifies everything.

## How to handle a conflict
1. **Act early.** Small problems get bigger when ignored.
2. **Talk privately first.** Praise in public, correct in private.
3. **Cool down.** Wait until the anger has passed.
4. **Listen.** Let each person say what they see and feel without interruption.
5. **Focus on the issue,** not the person. Describe the behavior and its effect: "When the radio checks are skipped, I do not know if you are OK."
6. **Find the interests.** What does each person really need?
7. **Look for options** that meet both needs.
8. **Agree on a solution** and write it down.
9. **Follow up.** Check in after a week or two.
10. **Use a neutral third person** if you cannot resolve it between yourselves, such as another leader.

## Dealing with difficult personalities
- **The dominator.** Thank them and invite others to speak: "Let us hear from someone who has not spoken."
- **The silent member.** Ask them directly, kindly, for their view.
- **The complainer.** Ask for a specific proposal.
- **The know-it-all.** Give them a way to teach, within the group's rules.
- **The unreliable member.** Be clear about expectations, and if it continues, follow the process in your group agreement.

## When someone must leave
Sometimes a member cannot meet the agreement, or poses a risk. Follow your written process: private conversation, written warning, and a decision by the leaders or the group. Be fair and respectful. Protect the group's information and equipment, and keep the matter confidential.

## Practice teamwork
- Run drills with mixed teams.
- Rotate who leads an exercise.
- Debrief with AARs.
- Share meals and social time.

## Checklist
- Note which stage your group is in.
- Write a short conflict process in your group agreement.
- Practice giving feedback with the "behavior and effect" method.
- Arrange a neutral person to help in disputes.
- Plan a social event for the group.

> Conflict handled well builds trust. Conflict ignored breaks groups.
