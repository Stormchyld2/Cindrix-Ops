Growing a group takes patience and respect. Recruit people who will stay, and make them feel welcome from the first day.

## Find the right people
Look first among people you already know and trust:

- Family and close friends who live nearby.
- Neighbors you already get along with.
- Coworkers, church or club members.
- People from the training classes you attend: CERT, first aid, radio.
- Local volunteers: fire department, search and rescue, emergency management.

## Start the conversation
You do not need to start with "disaster."

- "Our power has gone out twice this year. Have you thought about what we would do if it went out for a week?"
- "I am taking a free first aid class. Would you like to come?"
- "A few of us are getting together to learn about emergency radios. Interested?"

Listen to their concerns. Do not push or scare. Offer a small, easy first step.

## Make the first events easy
Hold a short, friendly **meet-up** with a clear agenda. See the next lesson. Offer something useful, such as a first aid skill, a radio demonstration or building a vehicle kit. Serve food. Keep it to about two hours.

## Be open, but careful
Be welcoming, and also sensible about who you let into the trust of the group.

- **Meet in public or at your home** with other trusted members, not alone with strangers.
- **Get to know people** over several meetings before inviting them to sensitive information.
- **Take references** from people who know them.
- **Do not share** the details of your supplies or plans in the first meetings.
- **Listen** to your gut and to the concerns of other members.

## A membership path
Set clear steps so people know what to expect:

1. **Guest.** Attends a meet-up or training session.
2. **Associate member.** Attends regularly for a few months, completes the basic training, and agrees to the group agreement.
3. **Full member.** Is voted in or agreed by the group, receives the full contact list and plans, and takes on a role.

Describe it in your group agreement.

## Welcome new members
- Assign a **buddy** to every new member.
- Give them a **welcome pack:** the group agreement, the contact list, the meeting schedule and the basic training plan.
- Introduce them to everyone.
- Invite them to take part in a role or project.
- Check in after the first month.

## Keep people engaged
- Make meetings useful and short.
- Recognize contributions.
- Give people a say.
- Rotate roles so no one is stuck.
- Handle conflicts early.
- Celebrate milestones and have fun.

## When someone leaves
People move, change jobs and change interests. Thank them, update the roster and records, and retrieve any shared equipment. Take care that information about the group remains protected.

## Checklist
- Write a list of ten people to invite.
- Plan a first friendly meet-up.
- Write down the steps of your membership path.
- Prepare a welcome pack.
- Assign a buddy to every new member.

> Welcome people warmly and trust them gradually.
