An AAR only matters if something changes afterward. This lesson shows how to turn lessons into real improvement.

## Close the loop
1. **Write the improvement action** clearly: what will change.
2. **Assign one owner.**
3. **Set a date.**
4. **Decide how you will check** that it worked.
5. **Review at the next meeting.**
6. **Test the change** at the next drill.

If the fix works, add it to the plan. If not, try again.

## Sort lessons into types
Not all fixes are the same. Sorting them helps you decide what to do.

- **People:** training, roles, communication, fatigue. Fix with training, practice, clearer roles.
- **Plans:** missing steps, unclear triggers. Fix by rewriting the plan.
- **Equipment:** failures, missing gear, wrong gear. Fix by repair, replacement, new gear and checks.
- **Information:** wrong, late or missing. Fix with better sources and communication.
- **Environment:** conditions you cannot change. Fix by adapting the plan.

## Find the root cause
A symptom is what you see. A **root cause** is why it happened. Ask "why?" up to five times.

Example:
- The radio did not work. **Why?** The battery was dead.
- **Why?** It was not charged.
- **Why?** No one checks batteries regularly.
- **Why?** There is no checklist or owner.
- **Fix:** Add a monthly battery check to the Comms Specialist's duties, with a checklist.

## Make it stick
- **Update the written plan** and the checklists.
- **Train the change.** Do not just announce it.
- **Add it to the Calendar** and To-do list.
- **Tell everyone** what changed and why.
- **Celebrate** the improvement.

## Track lessons over time
Keep an **improvement log:**

- Date and event.
- The lesson.
- The change made.
- Whether it was tested.
- Whether it worked.

Review it at the annual review. Patterns show you the deeper problems.

## Share lessons
- Share your AARs with neighboring groups, if appropriate, with names removed.
- Read others' reports. Many fire and emergency agencies publish public after-action reports. Learn from their mistakes without paying for your own.

## Keep a learning culture
Groups that improve share some habits:

- They treat mistakes as information.
- They thank people for pointing out problems.
- They keep drills realistic and varied.
- They make fixes small and quick.

## Checklist
- Record each lesson with a type and a root cause.
- Give every improvement an owner, a date and a check.
- Update plans and checklists.
- Start an improvement log.
- Review the log at the annual review.

> A lesson is not learned until behavior changes.
