Planning and talking mean little unless things get done. This lesson shows how to track actions and how to run the yearly review that keeps your group improving.

## The action log
An **action log** is a list of everything the group has agreed to do. Keep it simple:

- **Number.** A short reference.
- **What** needs doing, in one clear sentence.
- **Who** owns it. One person, not "the team."
- **By when.** A date.
- **Status:** open, in progress, done or dropped.
- **Notes.**

Review it at the start of every meeting. Ask:

- What is done? Celebrate it.
- What is late? Why? Can we help?
- What is no longer needed? Close it.

Use the **To-do list** and **Calendar** screens in this program so everyone can see what is due. Add an item the moment it is agreed.

## Good actions
A good action is **specific, owned and dated.**

- Bad: "Look at getting more water."
- Good: "Sam will price four 55-gallon water barrels and report at the next meeting on 12 March."

## Follow up kindly
- Ask in private first: "How is that going? Do you need anything?"
- Offer help or split the task.
- Do not shame people. Reassign if needed.
- Thank people when they finish.

## The preparedness cycle
Emergency managers use a cycle that your group can also follow:

1. **Plan.** Decide what you will do.
2. **Organize and equip.** Get the people, roles and gear.
3. **Train.** Learn the skills.
4. **Exercise.** Practice the plan.
5. **Evaluate and improve.** Learn and fix. Then plan again.

Every meeting, drill and review fits in one of these steps.

## The annual readiness review
Once a year set aside a full day. Use the review to ask: are we more ready than a year ago?

1. **Review the group.**
   - Roster, roles, backups, succession.
   - Attendance and training record for each member.
2. **Review the plans.**
   - Area study and hazard list. What has changed?
   - Plan A, B and C for each priority hazard.
   - The PACE communications plan.
   - Rally points and routes.
3. **Review supplies and equipment.**
   - Inventory, expiration dates, repairs.
   - Shared equipment and who holds it.
4. **Review training.**
   - Who has which skills and certifications?
   - Which skills have faded and need refreshing?
   - What training to plan next year.
5. **Review lessons learned.**
   - AARs from the year.
   - Which fixes were made and which were not.
6. **Set next year's goals.**
   - Pick three to five clear goals.
   - Put the year's meetings, drills and training on the Calendar.
7. **Celebrate** the year.

## A readiness score
Make a simple score. For example: of 40 items on your readiness list, how many are done? Track it each year. Seeing it rise is a good motivator and shows where to focus.

## Checklist
- Start an action log and review it at every meeting.
- Make every action specific, owned and dated.
- Schedule the annual readiness review on the Calendar.
- Prepare a readiness checklist and a scoring method.
- Write next year's goals at each review.

> What gets written down with an owner and a date gets done. What does not, does not.
