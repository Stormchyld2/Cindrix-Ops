How a group makes decisions decides how well it holds together. Choose your method before you need it.

## Three ways of deciding
**1. The leader decides.** Fast and clear. Best in emergencies and for small, routine matters. The risk is that people feel unheard, and the leader may miss something.

**2. The group votes.** Fair and quick for a clear yes/no. Each person has one vote. The risk is that the losers feel ignored, and the group splits.

**3. Consensus or consent.** The group talks until everyone can live with the decision. Slower, but it builds buy-in. A useful form is **consent:** the group goes ahead unless someone has a serious, reasoned objection. This is quicker than demanding that everyone love the idea.

## Match the method to the decision
- **Emergency and safety decisions:** the leader or function lead decides. Time matters.
- **Routine and logistical matters:** the person in charge of that area decides.
- **Group direction, rules and money:** discuss, then use consent or a vote.
- **Joining or removing a member:** a defined process (see the vetting lesson).

Write this down in a short "how we decide" page.

## Commander's intent
The Army uses a simple idea called **commander's intent.** The leader says **what** needs to be achieved and **why,** and leaves the **how** to the people doing it. Then if the plan falls apart, everyone still knows the goal and can adapt.

Example: "We want every household to have 72 hours of water by the end of March, so that no one is relying on the store in the first days. How you do it is up to you."

## Running a good discussion
1. **State the question clearly,** in one sentence.
2. **Give the facts** before opinions.
3. **Go around the group** so quieter members speak.
4. **Listen without interrupting.**
5. **Summarize the options** and their trade-offs.
6. **Decide** with the method you agreed.
7. **Record the decision** and who does what.
8. **Support the decision** after it is made, even if you disagreed.

## Disagree well
- Argue about **ideas,** not people.
- Use "I think" and "I am worried that," not "You always."
- Ask questions before making accusations.
- Take a break if tempers rise.
- Accept that you will not win every time.

## Close the loop
Every decision needs:

- **Who** will do it.
- **What** exactly.
- **By when.**
- **How we will know** it is done.

Write this in the minutes and review it at the next meeting.

## Checklist
- Write a one-page "how we decide" for the group.
- Decide which decisions the leader makes alone.
- Practice a consent decision on a small matter.
- Use commander's intent in your next task.
- Record every decision with an owner and a due date.

> A group that decides how it decides in advance argues less when it matters.
