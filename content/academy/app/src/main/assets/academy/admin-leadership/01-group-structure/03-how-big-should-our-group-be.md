How big is too big? There is no single right answer. It depends on your purpose, your leadership and your community.

## What decides the size
- **Type of organization.** A family-and-friends group is small. A church preparedness team, a workplace group or a CERT team can be larger. A neighborhood group may cover many households.
- **Goals of the group.** A group that trains together and may travel together needs to be small. A group that shares information and supplies across a district can be larger.
- **Leadership structure.** More people need more layers of leadership and more time.
- **Time and resources.** Meetings, training and logistics take effort, and it grows with every member.
- **Trust.** The more people, the harder it is to keep real trust. See the vetting lesson.

## The trade-offs of bigger groups
Bigger groups bring:

- More skills, more hands and more resources.
- More coverage of your area and more people to share watches.

But also:

- **More communication to manage.**
- **More personalities and more conflicts,** with greater risk from pride, ego and domineering personalities.
- **Weaker trust** and weaker accountability. People hide in a crowd.
- **Harder logistics:** meeting places, storage, transport, food.
- **Greater risk of information leaks.**
- **Slower decisions.**

## A practical rule
Keep the size down to a manageable level and split groups that get big.

- **Core group:** four to eight people who know each other well. Start here.
- **Team:** about eight people under one team leader.
- **Larger group:** up to about 32 people, made of four teams of eight.
- **Beyond that,** start another group and link them as neighbors, with leaders who talk regularly. Several small groups that cooperate are stronger and safer than one big one.

## Think about proximity
The number matters less than who lives **near** whom. In a disaster you may be cut off from the people you planned on. Roads and bridges close. The people who can reach you in an hour on foot are worth more than the ones who live two hours away by car.

A useful saying is: **"If you cannot see someone from your front porch, they are not able to help you when trouble starts."** Build your group from people who live close to you, and keep wider relationships as a second layer.

## Start small, grow slowly
1. **Start with you and one other person.**
2. **Add one household at a time** and let each new member settle in.
3. **Grow only as fast as you can train, equip and look after new members.**
4. **Use a waiting period** (a few months) before a new member is fully trusted.
5. **Set a size limit** for each team and stick to it.

## Checklist
- Decide the purpose of your group, then decide its target size.
- List the households within walking distance who might join.
- Decide how many members each team will have, and when you will split.
- Write down your wider relationships as a second layer.

> Small teams that trust each other beat big crowds that do not.
