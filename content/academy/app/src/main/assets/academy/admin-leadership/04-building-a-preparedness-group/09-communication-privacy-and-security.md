Groups share contact details, plans and supplies. Handling that information carefully protects the group and each member. This is sometimes called **operational security (OPSEC).**

## Why it matters
- **Your supplies are attractive.** Someone who knows you have food, fuel and tools may target you or come asking in a crisis.
- **Your plans are personal.** Meeting places, routes and schedules should not be public.
- **Your members' details are private.** Names, addresses and phone numbers belong to them.
- **Your group's reputation matters.** Careless talk can bring unwanted attention.

## The basic rule
**Share information only with people who need it, only as much as they need, and only when they need it.**

## Practical habits
**Online:**
- Do not post your supplies, plans, locations, or members' names publicly.
- Use private, secure messaging apps with strong passwords for group chats. Turn on disappearing messages for sensitive talk.
- Use two-factor authentication for accounts.
- Do not tag locations or post photos of your home or kit.
- Teach family members, especially teenagers, the same rules.

**In person:**
- Do not discuss your preparations loudly in public.
- Be careful what neighbors, visitors and workers see.
- Keep supplies out of sight.
- Do not boast.

**Documents:**
- Keep the group binder in a safe place.
- Limit who has the full contact list.
- Use a numbered system or first names only in shared lists.
- Keep digital copies encrypted and in a protected folder or drive.

**Code words:**
- Agree on code words for locations, supplies and plans in open channels, as in the PACE plan.

## Control who knows what
Use levels:

1. **Public:** that the group exists, in general terms, and its public events.
2. **Members:** meeting schedules, general plans, training.
3. **Core members:** full contact lists, supply details, sensitive plans.
4. **Leaders:** group finances and personal matters.

## New members and privacy
Give new members access in stages. See the recruiting lesson.

## Handle personal data with care
Collect only what you need: names, phone numbers, skills, and emergency contacts. Ask for consent. Store it securely. Delete it when someone leaves, if they ask.

Information about people's health, medication or disabilities is sensitive. Collect it only if members choose to share it for their own safety, and keep it restricted.

## Photographs and social media
Ask before taking or posting photos. Avoid showing faces, addresses, license plates or equipment. Do not post in real time from your home or camp.

## Radio
Normal radio talk is public. Anyone with a receiver can hear it. Use code words for sensitive information, and keep messages short.

## If information leaks
- Tell the leader.
- Assess what was shared and who saw it.
- Change plans that may be compromised, such as meeting places and codes.
- Learn from it in an AAR.

## Checklist
- Write a simple information-handling rule for the group.
- Set up a private, secure group chat.
- Agree on code words for locations and supplies.
- Review what each member posts online.
- Store the contact list securely and limit access.

> Share what is needed, with whom it is needed, when it is needed.
