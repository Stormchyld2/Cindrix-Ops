A group works best when everyone knows their job. Roles prevent two problems: **nobody doing something,** and **everybody doing the same thing.**

## Borrow from emergency services
The Incident Command System (ICS), which fire, police and emergency management use, organizes every response around five functions. A preparedness group can use the same pattern in a simple form:

1. **Command:** the leader, who sets goals and makes the final decisions.
2. **Operations:** the people who do the work in the field.
3. **Planning:** the people who collect information and prepare the plans.
4. **Logistics:** the people who provide supplies, equipment, food and transport.
5. **Finance and administration:** the people who track money, records and paperwork.

In a small group one person may hold more than one job. In a larger group each job can have a team.

## Common roles
Write down who holds each of these. Titles are up to you.

- **Group leader.** Overall direction and final decisions.
- **Deputy leader.** Takes over when the leader cannot lead, and takes on special tasks.
- **Team leaders.** Lead a team of about eight.
- **Comms specialist.** Runs the PACE plan, the radios and the contact list. (See the Communications course.)
- **Medical lead.** Keeps the medical kits and training, and leads in a medical emergency. (See the medical courses.)
- **Training coordinator.** Plans classes, drills and practice.
- **Logistics lead.** Tracks shared supplies, equipment and storage.
- **Area and intelligence lead.** Keeps the area study and the hazard list. (See the Area Studies course.)
- **Security and safety lead.** Looks after home and group safety plans, safe handling rules and drills.
- **Secretary.** Keeps minutes, action items, the calendar and the contact list.
- **Treasurer.** Tracks shared money, if there is any.
- **Welcome lead.** Meets new people and helps them settle in.

## Choose by strength and willingness
- Ask people what they are good at and what they would like to do.
- Match roles to skills, but also give people a chance to learn.
- Do not give someone a role they do not want. It will not be done.
- Rotate where it helps, so no one gets burned out.

## Write a short job description for each role
For each role write:

1. **What the job is** in two sentences.
2. **What the person does** each month and in an emergency.
3. **What the person needs** (training, gear, access).
4. **Who backs them up.**
5. **How long** the role lasts before review.

## Chain of command
Decide who decides what:

- **Normal times:** decisions made by agreement or vote at meetings, within the plan.
- **Emergencies:** the leader, or the person in charge of that function, makes calls quickly. The group accepts that and reviews it afterward.
- **When the leader is not there:** the deputy leads. If the deputy is not there, the next person on the list leads.

Write the order of succession down.

## Keep roles visible
- Put a one-page **roster** in your binder: names, roles, contact details and backups.
- Review the roster every quarter.
- When someone leaves or joins, update it the same day.

## Checklist
- Write the list of roles that your group needs.
- Assign each role and name a backup.
- Write a short job description for each.
- Agree the order of succession.
- Put the roster in the binder and review it each quarter.

> Everyone should be able to say what their job is, who backs them up and who they report to.
