Every movement, real or practiced, teaches something. Capture it.

## The AAR for a movement
Ask:
1. What was the plan? (route, times, gear, roles)
2. What happened? (actual route, times, problems)
3. Why? (decisions, weather, fatigue, gear failures, navigation errors)
4. What do we change? (gear, training, plans, rules)
Write it down. Keep it for the next plan.

## Checklist for your movement plan
Build a binder or file with:
1. **Decision rules:** when to stay, when to go, who decides.
2. **Routes:** the main and two alternates from home to each destination (family, safe place, bug-out place), with distances, times, water and hazards.
3. **Rally points:** primary and alternate, with how to reach them.
4. **Gear lists:** for each person, with weights.
5. **Communications:** radio channels, schedules, signals and a PACE plan.
6. **Roles:** leader, navigator, medic, rear security, logistics.
7. **Fitness plan:** training schedule and benchmarks.
8. **Trip plan template:** to leave with someone.
9. **Medical:** kits, medicines, conditions, evacuation points.
10. **Signals:** methods and what they mean.

## Training schedule
- **Monthly:** a loaded walk with the group on a set route.
- **Quarterly:** a navigation exercise: a day trip with map and compass only.
- **Twice a year:** an overnight exercise with shelter, food and water.
- **Yearly:** a longer or harder test, in different weather.
Add each outing to the log.

## Skills to keep fresh
Map and compass, first aid, shelter, fire, water treatment, signaling, radio use, foot care and group leadership.

## Review the plan
Update it each year, after each event and when people or places change.

## A last word
Moving through danger is the last resort. Your best skills are the ones that keep you from needing it: preparing, staying informed, leaving early and having a safe place to go.

> The best evasion is leaving before trouble arrives, and the best rescue is the one you never need.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
