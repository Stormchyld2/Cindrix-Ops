Moving on foot through danger is the hardest thing in this Academy. Most people who must do it should not, if staying is safe. This course teaches you when to move, how to do it well and how to be found.

## 1. Prevention and hazard assessment
- Is moving safer than staying? Often it is not.
- What hazards lie on the route: terrain, weather, water, people, wildlife, fire, flooding, and your own condition?
- Who is in the group, and what can they manage?

## 2. Preparedness
- Fitness, boots, pack, kit, food and water for the trip.
- Navigation skills and maps.
- Communications and a trip plan left with someone.
- A rehearsed plan, with alternates.

## 3. Response
- Move smartly: steady pace, rest, security, checks, discipline.
- Adapt as the situation changes. Stop and think when needed.

## 4. Mitigation
- **Reduce your exposure:** avoid dangerous areas, move in good conditions, keep a low profile and spread risk.
- Carry the right gear and know first aid.
- Keep a backup plan and a way to call for help.

## 5. Recovery
- Reach safety, link up with rescuers, tend injuries, rest and review.
- After an event, replace gear and write lessons.

## The movement decision
Ask:
1. **Why** do we need to go?
2. **Where** are we going, and what is there?
3. **How far,** and how long will it take?
4. **What are the risks of going,** and of staying?
5. **Who can do it?** Everyone in the group must be able.
6. **When** is the best time?
7. **What is plan B?**
If the answers are not good, **stay put,** make shelter and signal for help. Many rescues begin with people who stayed where they were.

## Principles of movement
- **Plan,** do not drift.
- **Rest** before you are tired.
- **Hydrate** and eat on the move.
- **Check** your position often.
- **Keep the group together.**
- **Be flexible.**

> Most people who die in the wilderness did not die because they lacked a skill. They died because they made a poor decision early: leaving a safe place, ignoring a warning, or pushing on when tired.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
