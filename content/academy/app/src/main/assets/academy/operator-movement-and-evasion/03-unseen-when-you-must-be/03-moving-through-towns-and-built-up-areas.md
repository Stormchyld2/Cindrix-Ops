After a disaster, you may need to walk through a damaged town or city. People, hazards and rubble change everything.

## Plan
- **Know the layout** from maps. Plan routes along main roads, parks, rail lines, riverbanks or less-used streets as conditions suggest.
- **Time:** move in daylight if it is safe. Night can protect or endanger you.
- **Dress to blend:** ordinary clothing, sturdy shoes, a plain pack. Avoid tactical or military looks that draw attention.
- **Carry:** water, food, first aid, a light, a map, a radio, cash and a small tool kit. Keep valuables hidden.
- **Go in a group** of two to four if you can.

## Hazards
- **Damaged structures:** do not walk under leaning buildings, hanging facades, loose glass or wires.
- **Debris:** sharp objects, nails, rebar, broken glass. Wear thick-soled shoes and gloves.
- **Fire and smoke.**
- **Floodwater:** may be contaminated and hide holes and open manholes.
- **Hazardous materials:** unusual smells, spills or tanks. Move upwind.
- **Downed power lines:** keep at least 30 feet away. Treat all lines as live.
- **Gas leaks:** a smell of gas or a hiss means leave at once.
- **Traffic and stranded vehicles.**
- **Dogs and wildlife.**

## People
Most people are scared and willing to help. A few may be dangerous.
- **Stay calm, polite and low-key.**
- **Keep your distance** from groups you do not trust, and cross the street if needed.
- **Trust your instincts:** leave if something feels wrong.
- **Avoid** crowds, looting and confrontations.
- **Keep hands free,** and valuables out of sight.
- **Do not display** what you carry.
- **Do not offer** details about your plans.
- **If challenged,** be calm, do not argue, and give up property if needed. Your safety is worth more.
- **If you see a crime,** call 911 when it is safe, and do not intervene.
- **Cooperate** with police, firefighters and the National Guard.

## Where to rest
- Use places with a clear view, more than one exit and near help: a police or fire station, a hospital, a church or a shelter.
- Do not sleep in abandoned buildings that may be unsafe.
- Choose daylight rests in busy, safe spots.

## Check your direction
Keep a map, and note landmarks and street signs. Streets may be blocked, and signs gone. Use a compass.

## Water and food
Find water at designated points, and treat it. Use shops carefully, avoid risky food.

## Be ready for rescue
Look for shelters and reception centers where officials help. Keep papers, and report your location to your family when you can.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
