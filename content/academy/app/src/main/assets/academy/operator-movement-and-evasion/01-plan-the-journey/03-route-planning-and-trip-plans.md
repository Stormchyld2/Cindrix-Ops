A route is a series of short, safe, well-chosen legs. Plan at the table so you can walk in the field.

## Gather information
- **Maps:** topographic maps of the area and nearby areas, as well as road maps. Print and carry paper copies.
- **Weather:** forecasts and alerts. Be ready to adapt.
- **Conditions:** recent reports on trails, roads, water, fire and closures.
- **Local knowledge:** rangers, locals, guidebooks.
- **Daylight:** sunrise and sunset times.
- **Water sources and shelters** along the way.
- **Escape routes:** roads, trails and places to go if the plan fails.

## Choose the route
Use the principles from the Land Navigation course:
- **Handrails:** follow streams, ridgelines, roads and fences.
- **Checkpoints:** distinct features along the way for confirming position.
- **Attack points:** a clear feature near each target.
- **Avoid** steep slopes, cliffs, thick brush, swamp, flooded ground, bridges and busy roads where possible.
- **Choose ridgelines** for easy walking and visibility, and **valleys** for water and shelter, remembering hazards.
- **Take the easier route** over the shorter one if the time is similar.
- **Plan rests** at safe spots.

## Estimate time
Use **Naismith's rule:** about 3 miles an hour on flat ground, plus one hour for every 2,000 feet of climb. Add 10 to 20 percent for a group, rough terrain, a heavy pack, snow, or darkness. Plan to arrive well before dark.

## The route card
Write for each leg: from, to, bearing, distance, time, terrain, hazards and notes. Write the whole plan on a single page.

## Plan B, C and D
- **If the route is blocked:** alternate.
- **If someone is hurt:** the nearest help and evacuation point.
- **If the weather turns:** turn-back time and shelter spots.
- **If we are separated:** a rendezvous point and time.
- **If we are lost:** stop, think, and use the "lost" lesson.

## The trip plan to leave behind
Give a trusted person: who is going, the route and times, the vehicle and its location, your gear and communication, when to expect you, and when to call for help.

## Go or no go
Set criteria before you leave: weather, health, time, group condition. Agree on a **turn-around time:** no matter how close the goal, you turn back at that time.

## Rehearse
Walk part of the route in training. Time your pace.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
