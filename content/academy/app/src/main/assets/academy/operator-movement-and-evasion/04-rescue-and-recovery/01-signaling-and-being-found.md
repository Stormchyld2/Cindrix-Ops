Most people in trouble are found by search teams. Your job is to **stay alive and be easy to find.**

## How searches work
Search and rescue teams plan using your trip plan, last known point, the terrain and where lost people usually go. They search the most likely places first. The more information they have, the faster they find you.

## Stay put
Unless you have a good reason to move, stay near where you were last known. Move only if you are in danger, or if you are sure of a safe way out and have daylight. If you move, leave a note and a trail.

## Make a shelter and stay warm
A warm, dry person lasts longer and signals better.

## The internationally known signals
- **Three of anything:** three whistle blasts, three fires, three flashes, three shots. Repeat at intervals.
- **SOS** in Morse: three short, three long, three short.
- **Ground-to-air signals:** a large **V** means "need assistance," **X** means "need medical help," an arrow shows direction, and **N** means "no."
- **Make signs big:** at least 10 feet long, high contrast, in open ground, using rocks, branches, clothing or trampled snow.

## Signal methods
- **Whistle:** carries farther than your voice and uses less energy. Three blasts, wait, repeat.
- **Mirror:** a signal mirror or any shiny object can be seen for miles. Aim by making a V with your fingers, putting the aircraft or target in the V, and flashing sunlight on it.
- **Fire and smoke:** three fires in a triangle. Add green leaves, wet wood or rubber to make smoke by day (take care with safety and wildfire danger). Fires are easy to see by night.
- **Light:** flash a headlamp or strobe three times at intervals at night.
- **Bright cloth:** hang or spread orange, red or yellow cloth.
- **Phone and radio:** call 911 if there is any signal. Text if calls fail. Use emergency radio channels.
- **Personal locator beacon (PLB) or satellite messenger:** the best device for remote areas. Register it and test it.
- **Cell phone tip:** conserve battery, turn it off between calls, keep it warm, and move to high ground to find a signal.

## When you hear or see searchers
- **Make noise and use signals.** Do not hide.
- **Move into the open.**
- **Stay put** and let them come to you, unless told otherwise.
- **Wave both arms** over your head if you see an aircraft.
- **Cooperate** and follow instructions.

## Leave a note and marks
If you leave a spot, leave a note: who, when, where you're going, condition. Mark your route with arrows made of sticks, rocks or cut marks, and tape on branches.

## Keep your spirits up
Food, water, warmth, rest and a task keep people going.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
