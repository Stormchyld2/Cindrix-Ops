Every long move meets obstacles. A few are deadly. Most can be avoided by taking the time to look.

## Streams and rivers
Moving water is stronger than it looks. **Knee-deep fast water can knock you down,** and drowning is a leading killer of hikers.
- **Scout first.** Walk up and down to find the best spot: wide, shallow, with a slow current, a gravel bed and a safe runout below.
- **Avoid** bends, narrows, logs, rapids and any place with a waterfall or hazard downstream.
- **Cross early in the day,** especially in snowmelt country, when levels are lower.
- **Unbuckle** the hip and chest belts of your pack so you can drop it if you fall.
- **Face upstream,** shuffle sideways, and use a pole as a third leg.
- **Wear shoes,** and keep dry socks in a bag.
- **Group crossings:** hold on in a line or a triangle, or cross one at a time with a spotter and a rope from a safe place.
- **If you fall:** float on your back with feet downstream and toes up, and swim at an angle to shore. Never stand in fast, deep water, since feet can be trapped.
- **If it looks too dangerous, do not cross.** Wait, or go around.

## Roads and open areas
- **Look and listen** both ways. Cross in a dip or bend, at a narrow place, or under a bridge.
- **Cross one at a time** while others watch.
- **Move quickly** and clear the road.
- **Do not cross** a highway or a busy road if you can use a bridge or underpass.

## Slopes and rock
- **Move up and down at an angle,** with zigzags (switchbacks), rather than straight.
- **Test footholds** and keep three points of contact on rough ground.
- **Use poles** for balance.
- **Descend** with knees bent and a controlled pace.
- **Avoid** loose rock and scree, steep snow and gullies prone to rockfall.
- **Beware of cornices** and avalanches. Avoid steep, snowy slopes after storms.
- **Never** roll or throw rocks down a slope.

## Snow and ice
- **Frozen water:** ice thickness varies. Do not trust it unless you know it is thick, clear and well frozen. Stay off moving water and rivers.
- **Whiteout:** stop and shelter.
- **Cold:** keep dry and keep moving, but do not sweat.

## Thick brush and forest
Move slowly, shield your face, and follow game trails. Use handrails like streams or old roads.

## Swamps and wet ground
Avoid when you can. If you must cross, test each step with a pole, keep on tussocks, and keep a short rope between people.

## Wire and fences
Cross carefully, hold the wire down and step over. Check the ground beyond.

## Heat and desert
Move in the cool of the day, rest in shade, carry plenty of water, and do not overexert.

## Check on others
Watch for signs of fatigue, hypothermia, heat illness and injury.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
