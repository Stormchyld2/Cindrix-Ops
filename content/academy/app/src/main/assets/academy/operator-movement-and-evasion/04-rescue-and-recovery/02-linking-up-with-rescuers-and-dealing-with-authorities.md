Rescue is a handover. It goes well when you make it easy.

## When help arrives
- **Stay calm.** Show your hands and be visible.
- **Follow instructions** from the rescuers or officers. They are focused on safety.
- **Identify yourselves:** names, numbers in the group, injuries, medical conditions, and who may still be missing.
- **Give the facts:** where you have been, for how long, what happened.
- **Give the casualty record** and brief them.
- **Do not argue** about evacuation. If you disagree, say so calmly, but follow safety orders.
- **Keep your group together.**

## At a checkpoint or reception center
- Expect questions and screening.
- Have ID ready.
- Be patient and polite.
- Tell officials about medical needs and special needs.
- Ask about shelter, food, water, and how to contact family.
- **Register** with the Red Cross Safe and Well or FEMA channels, and tell your out-of-area contact.

## Search for missing members
If someone is still missing, tell the rescuers immediately. Give a description, their last known location and what they carry. Do not go back out to search on your own.

## Medical checks
After a rescue, you may be cold, hurt, dehydrated or in shock. Accept medical care. Tell medics about all injuries and exposures.

## Property and equipment
Rescue teams may ask you to leave equipment. Prioritize people. Your gear can be replaced.

## After rescue
- **Contact family.**
- **Rest, eat, drink, and warm up.**
- **Report** anything useful about hazards on the route to the authorities.
- **Return equipment** you borrowed.
- **Thank** your rescuers, and consider donating to the local search and rescue team, which is often volunteer.

## Rescue costs
Some areas charge for rescues. Check local policy. Carry insurance where useful.

## Respect the law
Do not trespass or break the law to evade rescue. Do not lie to authorities.

> This course is about safety and survival: avoiding trouble, staying unseen when danger is real, and being found. It is not about hunting people or attacking anyone. Obey the law, and when police or rescuers arrive, cooperate with them.
