Temperature is what turns a long outage dangerous, especially for babies, older adults and anyone with a health condition.

## Cold
### Stay warm
- **Pick one room** (ideally one with few windows and on the sunny side), close the doors and live in it. Hang blankets over doors and windows to hold heat.
- **Layers** of loose, dry clothing, hats, gloves and warm socks. Wool and synthetics keep you warm even when damp.
- **Sleeping bags** and wool blankets. A few people in one space keep each other warm.
- **Keep moving** a little, and eat and drink regularly. The body needs fuel to make heat.
- **Insulate from the floor** with rugs, foam or cardboard.
- **Cover windows** with blankets or plastic film at night.
- **Keep pets** warm.
- **Do not use** a gas range or oven to heat the home. Only use a fireplace or wood stove that is built and vented for heating, with dry fuel, a screen and a working chimney. Check local rules before using a portable or kerosene heater, and follow the maker's instructions strictly.
- **Never** use a charcoal grill, camp stove or propane heater not rated for indoor use inside.
- **Protect pipes:** let faucets drip, open cabinet doors, and drain the lines if the house will be cold for days.

### Hypothermia
Hypothermia is a medical emergency. Body temperature below **95°F (35°C)** needs medical help.

Warning signs:
- **Adults:** shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness.
- **Infants:** bright red, cold skin, very low energy.

If you suspect hypothermia:
1. **Call 911** or get medical help.
2. **Move the person to a warm, dry place.**
3. **Remove wet clothing** and wrap in dry blankets, including the head and neck.
4. **Warm the center of the body first** (chest, neck, head, groin). Use dry layers and, if available, warm (not hot) packs wrapped in cloth.
5. **Give warm, sweet drinks** if the person is awake and able to swallow. No alcohol.
6. **Do not rub** limbs or use direct hot water or heating pads.
7. **Keep the person still and handle gently.**
8. If the person stops breathing, begin CPR (see the CPR course).

## Heat
### Stay cool
- **Keep blinds and curtains closed** during the day, especially on the sun side.
- **Stay on the lowest floor.** Heat rises.
- **Wear light, loose, light-colored clothing.**
- **Drink water regularly,** more than you think you need, even if you are not thirsty. Avoid alcohol and very sugary drinks.
- **Take cool showers or baths,** or use wet cloths on the neck and wrists.
- **Use battery fans** to move air, but remember that in extreme heat fans alone may not prevent illness.
- **Avoid hard activity** in the hottest part of the day.
- **Open windows at night** if it is cooler outside, and close them in the morning.
- **Never leave anyone in a parked car.**
- **Go somewhere cool** if the home becomes dangerous: a library, a mall, a community cooling center, a friend's air-conditioned home. Leaving is a perfectly good plan.

### Heat illness
- **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, cold, pale, clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea, muscle cramps, tiredness, dizziness, headache, fainting. **Move to a cooler place, loosen clothing, apply cool wet cloths, sip water.** Get medical help if symptoms get worse or last over an hour.
- **Heat stroke** is the most serious: body temperature can reach **106°F or higher within 10 to 15 minutes,** with hot, red, dry or damp skin, a fast strong pulse, headache, dizziness, confusion or loss of consciousness. **Call 911.** Move the person to a cool place and cool them quickly with cold cloths or a cool bath until help arrives. Do not give them anything to drink if they are confused or unconscious.

## Who is most at risk
- Infants and young children, older adults, pregnant people.
- People with heart disease, other chronic illness or on certain medicines.
- People who live alone or are isolated.
- People who work outdoors.

Check on them often.

## Gear you might need
- Warm sleeping bags rated for your climate
- Wool blankets
- Battery-powered fans
- Electrolyte packets
- Extra warm clothing and hats

## Checklist
- Picked your warm room for winter outages
- Sleeping bags or heavy blankets for everyone in the house
- A plan to stay cool that does not need AC
- Know how to find local warming and cooling centers
- Know the signs of hypothermia, heat exhaustion and heat stroke

> In extreme cold or heat, leaving for a safer place is a good plan.
