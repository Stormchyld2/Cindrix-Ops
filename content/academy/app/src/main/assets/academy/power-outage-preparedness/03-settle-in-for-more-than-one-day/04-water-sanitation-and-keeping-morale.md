Long outages wear on people. Water, hygiene and morale are what keep a household healthy and steady.

## Water
- **Drinking:** at least one gallon per person per day, more in heat or for nursing mothers and the sick.
- **Hygiene:** extra water for hand washing and dishes.
- **If water pressure fails or you are on a well:** use stored water first. Collect rainwater for flushing and washing, not for drinking unless treated.
- **If told to boil water:** bring it to a rolling boil for **1 minute** (3 minutes above 6,500 feet elevation), cool, and store in clean covered containers.
- **If you cannot boil,** use a water filter rated for bacteria and protozoa, or treat with unscented household bleach or purification tablets as the label directs. See the Water lessons in Basic Preparedness.

## Hygiene and sanitation
- **Wash your hands** with soap and water, or use sanitizer with at least 60 percent alcohol if water is short, before eating and after the toilet.
- **Toilets:** if the water is off, pour a bucket of water into the bowl to flush. If the sewer is down, line the bowl or a bucket with a heavy bag, add kitty litter or sawdust, tie the bag and store it in a covered container away from living areas until you can dispose of it properly.
- **Trash:** bag it, tie it and keep it outside in sealed bins.
- **Wipes and a basin** can substitute for showers.
- **Dishes:** use paper plates and cups to save water, or wash with treated water.
- **Laundry:** wash a stock of clothing before storms.

## Mental well-being
A long outage means worry, boredom and stress. Both adults and children feel it.
- **Keep a routine:** set meal times, sleeping times and tasks.
- **Share the load:** give everyone a job. Even young children can sort supplies or carry water.
- **Stay informed,** but limit news. Check updates at set times.
- **Stay connected** with family, neighbors and your group.
- **Play:** board games, cards, books, drawing, puzzles. Keep a "power-out kit" for children.
- **Move:** walk, stretch, do chores.
- **Sleep and eat.**
- **Talk about feelings.** Reassure children.
- **Limit alcohol.**
- **Seek help** if someone is overwhelmed. Call your local crisis line or 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, if you or someone else is in distress.

## Security
- Keep doors and windows locked and check on neighbors.
- Do not advertise that you have supplies or a generator. Noise draws attention.
- Use lights discreetly.
- Report suspicious activity.
- Stay with your buddy.

## Keep a log
Write down the time the power went out, what you did, temperatures, what you threw away and what you need next time. It helps with insurance claims and the after action review.

## Checklist
- Water stored for drinking and hygiene
- Know how to boil or treat water
- Toilet backup plan with bags and litter
- Hand-washing supplies
- A routine and tasks for everyone
- A "power-out kit" of games and books

> A calm routine is as important as a full pantry.
