This is the lesson people want to skip to, and it is the one where people get hurt. A generator or power station is not required to get through an outage, but it makes a long one easier.

## Decide what you need to power
List what you would actually run in a long outage, and its watts:
- **Life-safety:** medical devices.
- **Health and property:** refrigerator and freezer (they cycle, so a startup surge of two to three times running watts matters), sump pump, well pump, furnace blower.
- **Comfort:** lights, fans, phone charging, a small TV or radio.

Add the **running watts,** then the largest **starting watts** of any motor. That total sets the size of the generator or battery you need. Size for what you will actually use, not the whole house.

## Portable power stations
Quiet, no fumes, safe indoors, great for phones, lights, CPAPs, laptops and small appliances. Recharge by wall, car or solar panel. They are limited by battery size (see the lighting and charging lesson).

## Gas, propane and dual-fuel generators
Can run a refrigerator, a well pump or a furnace blower, but come with rules.

### The rules
1. **Outside only,** more than **20 feet** from the house, with the exhaust pointing away from windows, doors and vents. Never in the garage, basement, crawl space or porch, even with the door open.
2. **Dry location.** Use a canopy or generator tent that lets exhaust escape, never a closed enclosure. Keep it off wet ground and away from rain, but not under a closed structure.
3. **Have a CO detector** with battery backup in the house, near sleeping areas.
4. **Let it cool before refueling.** Turn it off, wait, and fuel outside. Never refuel a running or hot generator.
5. **Store fuel safely** (see the Mobility course): approved containers, away from the house, away from ignition sources, with stabilizer, rotated.
6. **Use proper cords:** heavy-duty, outdoor-rated, the right wire size for the load and length, not damaged. Plug appliances directly into the generator.
7. **Ground it** following the manual.
8. **Keep children and pets away** from hot parts and exhaust.
9. **Run it only as needed.** Turn it off at night if you can warm the house another way, and never leave it running unattended for long.
10. **Follow the manual.**

## Never backfeed
**Never plug a generator into a wall outlet to power your home.** This is called **backfeeding.** It can send electricity back into the grid and **electrocute a lineworker** or a neighbor down the street. It also can cause fires and damage your generator and home.

If you want the generator to run house circuits:
- Have a **licensed electrician** install a **transfer switch** or an **interlock** device that isolates the house from the grid.
- Get the required permit and inspection.
- Use a generator-ready inlet and the correct cord.
- Choose the circuits you will power and label them.

## Test before you need it
A generator that has sat in the garage for two years with old gas in it probably will not start.
- Run it **at least every three months** under load for 20 to 30 minutes.
- Use **fresh fuel with stabilizer.** Drain or run the carburetor dry for long storage.
- Check the oil, air filter and spark plug, and keep spares.
- Check the cords and fuel containers.
- Keep a **log.**
- Practice the full setup, including the cord and the transfer switch, once a year.

## Solar and other options
- **Solar panels with a power station** give silent, fuel-free charging.
- **Whole-house battery systems and standby generators** are installed by professionals.
- **Vehicle power inverters** can charge small devices from a running vehicle (outdoors only).
- **Do not connect** panels or batteries to house wiring without a qualified electrician.

## Gear you might need
- Portable power station and solar panel
- Generator sized to what you actually need to run
- Heavy-duty outdoor extension cords
- Fuel stored safely, with stabilizer
- CO detector with battery backup

## Checklist
- Decided what you would actually need to power in a long outage
- Generator placed and run outside only, 20 feet from the house
- Transfer switch or interlock installed if powering house circuits
- Ran your generator or power station in the last 3 months
- Fuel stored safely and rotated
- CO detector with battery backup in the house

> Never backfeed, never run it inside and never skip the test run.
