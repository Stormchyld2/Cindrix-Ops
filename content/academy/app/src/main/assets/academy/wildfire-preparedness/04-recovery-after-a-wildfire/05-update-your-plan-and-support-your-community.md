Every wildfire teaches something a plan did not account for. Update your household plan while it is fresh, and check on your neighbors, especially anyone elderly, disabled or otherwise more vulnerable during a slow recovery.

## Hold an after action review
Within a week or two, sit down with your household (and your group) and use the four questions from the Admin and Leadership course:
1. **What did we expect to happen?**
2. **What actually happened?**
3. **Why was there a difference?**
4. **What will we do differently next time?**

Prompts for a wildfire:
- Did we get warnings? Did the alerts work?
- Did we leave early enough? What was our trigger? Was it right?
- Did the go bags have what we needed? What did we miss?
- Did the routes work? Were roads blocked? How was traffic?
- Did we reach our contacts? Did the communications plan work?
- Pets and animals: how did loading go?
- Home hardening and defensible space: what held and what failed? What did the fire do to our neighbors' homes?
- Insurance: what surprised us?
- Emotional health: how did we cope? Who needs support?

## Turn lessons into actions
For every lesson write **what, who and by when,** and add it to your **CindrixOps to-do list** and calendar. Update your plan, kit lists and inventory.

## Rebuild smarter
- Use **ember-resistant materials** and vents, and Class A roofing.
- **Expand defensible space** and landscaping with fire-resistant plants.
- **Add water capacity,** hoses and pumps.
- **Improve access:** driveways, gates and a clear address.
- **Reduce risk** with a **Firewise USA** community.

## Support your community
- **Check on neighbors,** especially elderly and disabled residents, and people who lost their homes.
- **Offer specific help:** a meal, a place to charge phones, help with paperwork, a ride, a hand with cleanup, care for pets.
- **Share information:** where to get help, which scams to avoid.
- **Join or start a Firewise USA** neighborhood program. NFPA's program exists in thousands of communities specifically to help neighbors organize mutual support before and after fires.
- **Volunteer** with your CERT, Red Cross or a local recovery group.
- **Donate** to trusted organizations and avoid scams.
- **Record your household skills and resources** on the **Family page** for future mutual aid.

## Plan for the long road
Recovery takes months or years. Expect setbacks. Take care of each other. Celebrate small steps.

## The five principles: a summary
You have now seen all five principles in action:
1. **Prevention and hazard assessment:** risk, hardening, defensible space.
2. **Preparedness:** the plan, bags, insurance, alerts and drills.
3. **Response:** warning levels, leaving early, and what to do if trapped.
4. **Mitigation:** pre-evacuation prep and active steps to limit damage.
5. **Recovery:** re-entry, claims, health and learning.

Each informs the others. A better recovery makes a better plan.

## Checklist
- Updated the household wildfire action plan based on what was learned
- Held an after action review
- Checked in on neighbors, especially elderly or disabled residents
- Wrote down household skills and resources on the Family page for future mutual aid
- Joined or started a neighborhood preparedness program

> Recovery ends when the lessons become a better plan.
