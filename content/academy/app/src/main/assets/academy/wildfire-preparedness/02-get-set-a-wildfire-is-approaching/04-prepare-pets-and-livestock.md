Animals complicate an evacuation more than almost anything else, and they are the thing people most often delay leaving for. Plan before the day.

## Pets
**Prepare in advance**
- **ID:** collar tags, a microchip with current information and a photo of you with each pet.
- **Carriers** for each pet, sized to let them stand and turn. Practice getting them in.
- **Leashes and harnesses.**
- **Food and water** for at least three days and bowls.
- **Medicines** and a list, with veterinary records and vaccination proof.
- **Litter** and a small box for cats, waste bags for dogs.
- **Comfort items** such as a blanket and a toy.
- **Contact information** for your vet and for boarding.

**Find a place to go**
- Only **service animals** are usually allowed in public shelters. Some areas open pet-friendly or co-located shelters. Ask your emergency management office.
- Identify **pet-friendly hotels** along your routes.
- Arrange to stay with **friends or family** outside the area.
- Identify **boarding facilities** and vets outside the hazard zone.
- Know your **local animal shelter's** evacuation plan.

**Act early**
- **Confine pets to one room** in advance so they are easy to find the moment you need to go. The Red Cross recommends this.
- Do not leave pets behind. If you must leave them, they may not survive.
- **Do not turn pets loose** to fend for themselves.
- In extreme cases, leave a note with the pets' details, and clear water and food, but treat this as a last resort.

## Livestock and large animals
**Plan transport before the day**
- **Trailers:** Do you have enough trailers for every animal? Do you have enough trucks and drivers? Practice loading. Keep the hitch, lights and tires ready.
- **Halters, leads and ID** for each animal, with your name and phone number on them (a leg band, a neck tag or a marker on the animal).
- **Feed and water** for several days, with buckets.
- **Veterinary records** and proof of ownership (photos and brand papers).
- **First aid kit** for animals.

**Find a destination**
- Identify **fairgrounds, equestrian centers, friends' farms and large-animal shelters** outside the area.
- Ask your **county animal services or extension office.**
- Arrange **mutual aid** with neighbors with trailers.

**If you cannot move them**
- Some agencies advise **releasing animals** into a large, cleared area, such as a plowed field or a large irrigated pasture, away from the fire and from roads. Know your local advice and the legal rules before you rely on it.
- **Do not leave animals in barns or stalls.**
- Mark animals with your phone number.
- Tell responders and neighbors what you did.

## Other animals
- **Birds:** a carrier, a cover, and water. Keep them away from smoke.
- **Reptiles and fish:** a plan to keep them warm and aerated.
- **Small mammals:** a carrier and bedding.

## Smoke and animals
Smoke harms animals too. Keep them indoors with filtered air if you stay, and watch for coughing or lethargy.

## Practice
Run a **drill** with your pets and livestock, timing loading. Hold an after action review.

## Gear you might need
- Carriers or leashes staged by the door
- Halters, leads or a trailer for larger animals

## Checklist
- Pet carriers or leashes staged and accessible
- Transport plan identified for livestock or large animals
- Pet-friendly shelter or boarding identified
- Pets have ID and a microchip with current details
- Veterinary records copied and packed

> Plan for the animals before the day, or they will cost you time you do not have.
