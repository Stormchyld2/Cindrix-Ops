National evacuation guidance is specific about what to wear if a wildfire is closing in: **long pants, a long-sleeve shirt, heavy shoes or boots, a cap, a dry bandanna for your face, and goggles or glasses.** Natural fibers like cotton and wool are preferred, since synthetic fabrics can melt against skin. A bonus if you can get **Nomex** pants or other fire-resistant clothing.

## Why this clothing
- **Radiant heat** is a major killer. Covering skin protects against burns and heat injury.
- **Embers** land on clothing and skin.
- **Smoke and ash** irritate the eyes and lungs.
- **Cotton and wool** char instead of melting. **Nylon, polyester and fleece** can melt and stick to the skin.

## The kit to stage by the door
- **Long pants** of cotton or denim (not leggings, shorts or synthetic athletic pants).
- **Long-sleeve shirt** of cotton or wool. Cover wrists and the neck.
- **Heavy leather boots or sturdy shoes** with cotton or wool socks. No sandals.
- **Cap** with a brim, or a helmet.
- **Dry cotton bandanna** or a respirator. Do not wet the bandanna in heavy smoke unless you have no other option, as wet cloth can allow steam burns at high heat. Carry a dry one and a small bottle of water.
- **Goggles or glasses** to protect your eyes. Contact lens wearers should carry glasses, since smoke and ash irritate eyes.
- **Leather gloves.**
- **Wool blanket.**
- **Water.**

## Respiratory protection
- A **NIOSH-approved N95 respirator** (or P100) keeps out smoke particles. It must fit tightly and be worn correctly. Facial hair can break the seal.
- **Cloth, surgical and dust masks do not protect** against fine smoke particles.
- Respirators do **not** filter carbon monoxide. Leave smoke areas.
- If you have heart or lung disease, ask your doctor before using a respirator.

## Personal safety on the move
- **Hydrate.** Drink water before and during.
- **Stay together.** Use the buddy system.
- **Keep calm.** Slow, steady breathing helps.
- **Protect your eyes.** Do not rub them.
- **Watch for heat illness** (see the Power Outage course for signs).
- **Do not run in the dark** without light.
- **Check on others,** the elderly and young, as heat and smoke affect them first.

## Children and the vulnerable
- Children over 2 can wear masks, but standard respirators rarely fit them. Choose a mask that fits snugly over the nose and under the chin.
- Cover children's skin and keep them close.
- People with asthma or other lung or heart conditions should keep their medicines handy and leave early.

## Gear you might need
- Long pants and long-sleeve shirt, staged for fast access
- Heavy shoes or boots, not sandals
- Goggles or glasses and a dry bandanna or mask
- Leather gloves and a cap

## Checklist
- Long pants and long-sleeve shirt staged for evacuation
- Heavy shoes or boots staged (not sandals)
- Goggles or glasses and a dry bandanna or mask available
- Gloves and cap staged
- N95 respirators fitted and practiced

> Cover your skin with natural fibers and protect your lungs.
