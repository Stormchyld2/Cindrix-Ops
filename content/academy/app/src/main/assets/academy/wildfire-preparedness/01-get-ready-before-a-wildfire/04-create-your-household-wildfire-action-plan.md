A plan you have never said out loud is not a plan. It is a hope. The national **Ready, Set, Go!** program, run by the International Association of Fire Chiefs with the USDA Forest Service and adopted by fire departments nationwide, calls this your **Personal Wildland Fire Action Plan:** evacuation routes, emergency contacts and a family meeting point, practiced until everyone knows it without being told twice.

The goal is not a perfect document. It is a plan simple enough that a stressed, frightened person can still follow it.

## What goes in the plan
1. **Triggers.** What makes us leave? Write them as "if... then...":
   - "If an evacuation **warning** is issued for our zone, we pack and stage the car."
   - "If we see flames or heavy smoke within X miles, or an **order** is issued, we leave at once."
   - "If a Red Flag Warning is in effect and a fire starts within 20 miles, we move early." Choose your own figures with your fire department.
   - "If anyone feels unsafe, we leave. Nobody needs permission."
2. **Routes.** At least **two evacuation routes** in different directions. Print them. Drive them.
3. **Destinations.** Two places, one in each direction, away from the fire-prone area (see the Mobility course).
4. **Meeting places.** One near home, one outside the neighborhood, for when you get separated.
5. **Contacts.** An out-of-area contact everyone knows. Local emergency numbers.
6. **Roles.** Who gets the children? Who gets the pets? Who grabs the go bags? Who shuts off the gas? Who checks on the neighbor? Write names.
7. **A must-bring list.** People, pets, prescriptions, papers, photos and valuables, plan and supplies.
8. **Special needs.** People who need help moving, medical equipment, medicines, animals and livestock.
9. **Communications.** Use your PACE plan (see the Communications course) in case phones fail.

## Make it visible
- Keep a printed copy on the refrigerator and in each go bag and vehicle.
- Do not rely on your phone alone.

## Practice it
Hold a drill twice a year, once at the start of fire season:
1. **Walk through the plan** out loud.
2. **Do a timed run:** can everyone be in the car with go bags in about 10 minutes?
3. **Drive the routes,** including the alternate.
4. **Practice at night,** with the power out.
5. **Hold an after action review** and fix what you find.

Include children. Let them know what to do if you are not home, and who they should go to.

## When you are not home
- Know the school's and workplace's plans.
- Name who can collect your children.
- Know how you would get home or meet if roads close.
- Share your plan with a neighbor you trust.

## Review each year
Update contacts, routes and the must-bring list. Check that all alerts still work.

## Gear you might need
- A printed paper copy (phones die and signals drop)
- A refrigerator magnet or binder to keep it visible

## Checklist
- At least two evacuation routes identified
- Out-of-area emergency contact chosen and shared
- Family meeting location set away from the fire-prone area
- Plan includes pets and livestock
- Every household member has practiced the plan at least once
- Signed up for local text and email emergency alerts
- Must-bring list made in advance
- Triggers written down

> A simple plan you can follow when frightened beats a perfect plan you cannot.
