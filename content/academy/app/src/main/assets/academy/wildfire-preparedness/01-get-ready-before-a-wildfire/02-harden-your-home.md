Most homes lost in a wildfire are not destroyed by a wall of flame. They are destroyed by embers. A single ember in a dry gutter, an open attic vent or a pile of leaves against the siding can ignite a home long before the fire front arrives. **Hardening** your home means closing the small entry points embers use.

Walk the outside of your home and look at it the way an ember would: **where would it land, and what would it land on?**

## Work from the top down
**Roof**
- Choose a **Class A fire-rated** roof (asphalt composition, metal, clay or concrete tile). Wood shake roofs are very vulnerable.
- Fill gaps under tile roofs with noncombustible material.
- **Clean the roof** of leaves, needles and debris, especially in valleys and around skylights and chimneys.
- Keep tree branches at least **10 feet** from the chimney and stovepipe, and cover the chimney outlet with a metal spark arrester of 1/2-inch or smaller mesh.

**Gutters**
- Clear them of leaves and needles often in fire season.
- Use metal gutters and noncombustible, **ember-resistant gutter guards.**

**Vents**
- Cover all vents (attic, crawl space, eave, gable, foundation) with **1/8-inch or smaller noncombustible metal mesh.** Larger openings let embers in. Consider ember- and flame-resistant vents.
- Do not use plastic or fiberglass screening.

**Eaves and soffits**
- Enclose eaves and soffits fully with noncombustible or ignition-resistant material, with no gaps.

**Siding and walls**
- Use fire-resistant materials such as fiber cement, stucco, brick, stone or fire-retardant-treated wood.
- Seal gaps where siding meets the foundation, corners and trim.
- Keep a **6-inch noncombustible gap** between the ground and the lowest wood siding.

**Windows and doors**
- **Dual-pane tempered glass** resists heat better than single-pane glass.
- Add metal screens on windows.
- Weatherstrip garage doors and exterior doors to stop embers entering.
- Know that glass fails first under radiant heat, so keep flammables away from windows inside and out.

**Decks and porches**
- Use fire-resistant decking, or at minimum clear the area under and around the deck of anything that can burn.
- Enclose the underside with 1/8-inch metal mesh, or with noncombustible material.
- Keep furniture, planters and cushions off the deck when fire danger is high.

**Fences and attachments**
- A wooden fence attached to the house can carry fire to the wall. Use a **noncombustible section (metal or masonry) for the first five feet** where it meets the house, or a metal gate.
- Keep **trellises, arbors and wood piles** away from the house.

**Utilities**
- Keep propane tanks at a safe distance, with a cleared area around them.
- Make sure your **address is visible** from the street, with reflective numbers.
- Make sure your **driveway** is wide enough (typically 12 feet) with vertical clearance of about 15 feet for fire trucks, and that the **gate** can be opened by firefighters.

## Prioritize
You do not have to do everything at once. In order:
1. **Clean** roof, gutters and the 0 to 5-foot zone, which costs only time.
2. **Cover** vents with metal mesh.
3. **Remove** flammables from decks and the house edge.
4. **Seal** gaps and fix the eaves.
5. **Replace** the roof and windows when it is time.

## The extra mile
- 1/8-inch or smaller noncombustible metal mesh for vent retrofits
- Fire-rated roofing materials for future roof replacement
- Ember-resistant gutter guards
- Tempered dual-pane windows or film

> **Expert advice from Brent Weir (25+ years firefighter):** As a fire captain I was on the scene of a large apartment fire near a residential neighborhood. Many fires started nearby from embers landing on wood shake roofs and on combustible decks and patio furniture. The danger is real. Be mindful of things around your house that could ignite, and remove them when wildfire danger is high.

## Checklist
- Roof is fire-rated or confirmed fire-resistant
- All vents covered with 1/8-inch or smaller noncombustible mesh
- Gutters cleared of leaves and debris
- Eaves and soffits fully enclosed, no gaps
- Windows are dual-pane or tempered glass where feasible
- Deck and porch checked for fire-resistant materials
- Address visible from the street
- Driveway and gate allow fire truck access
