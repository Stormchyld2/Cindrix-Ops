United Policyholders is a national nonprofit that has guided disaster survivors through recovery since 1991. It puts the first priority plainly: **take care of your family's needs first.** Once that is handled, documentation is what protects you financially. This lesson is general information and **not legal or financial advice.**

## Photograph everything before any cleanup
Once debris is cleared, that evidence is gone.
1. **Take photos and video** of every room, every damaged item and the outside, from several angles.
2. **Narrate** what you see.
3. **Photograph serial numbers** and labels.
4. **Photograph the damage** to vehicles, fences, trees and landscaping.
5. **Do not throw anything away** until the insurer has seen it or told you to, unless it is a health hazard (rotten food). Photograph first.
6. **Keep samples** of damaged building materials if asked.
7. **Make a list** of what is lost, with a rough age, value and replacement cost.

## Contact your insurer first
- **Open the claim** promptly. Call, and get the claim number and the adjuster's name and number.
- Ask for an **advance on Additional Living Expenses (ALE)** if you are displaced.
- Ask about **emergency payments** for immediate needs: clothes, food, housing.
- Ask what **documentation** is needed.
- **Make sure you talk to your insurance company and get your claim going before anyone tries to do work on your property.** Unauthorized work can cause problems with your claim.
- **Keep copies** of everything you send.

## Keep a claim diary
Write down:
- **Date and time** of every contact.
- **Who** you spoke with and their title.
- **What was said** and promised.
- **What you sent and received.**
- **Expenses** and receipts.

## Beware of scams
After a disaster there will be many well-intentioned people coming to help. There will also be scammers.
- **Do not pay in full in advance.**
- **Check contractors' licenses** with your state.
- **Get written estimates and contracts.**
- **Do not sign over** your insurance check or sign a blank form.
- **Beware of** "storm chasers," door-to-door sales and pressure to act at once.
- **Get contact information** from anyone legitimate who offers help. Down the road, once claims are made, their help may be valuable.
- **Be careful online** with donation requests, fake government sites and requests for personal information. FEMA does not charge for help.
- **Report fraud** to your state attorney general and to the FTC.

## Help from governments and nonprofits
- **FEMA and DisasterAssistance.gov** if the disaster received a federal declaration. FEMA assistance cannot duplicate what insurance pays, so a claim must be filed first.
- **State and local recovery centers.**
- **American Red Cross.**
- **Salvation Army, Team Rubicon, local churches** and other volunteer groups.
- **Tax relief:** ask the IRS or a tax professional about casualty losses and deadline extensions.
- **Legal aid:** many bar associations offer free help for disaster survivors.

## Keep records safe
Keep a **binder** or a dedicated digital folder for all claim paperwork, with photos, receipts, estimates, letters and the diary.

## Gear you might need
- A phone or camera for documentation
- A notebook or note-taking app for a claim diary

## Checklist
- Photographed or filmed all damage before any cleanup begins
- Contacted insurer to open a claim
- Requested an advance on Additional Living Expenses if displaced
- Started a claim diary: date, time, who you spoke with and what was said
- Checked FEMA and DisasterAssistance.gov if the disaster received a federal declaration
- Watching for scams

> Document first, then clean. Talk to your insurer before anyone works on your property.
