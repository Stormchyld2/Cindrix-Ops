Do not return home just because the fire looks like it has passed on the news. **Wait for official confirmation from local authorities that it is safe to re-enter.** Downed power lines, hidden hot spots and weakened trees remain dangerous long after the flames are gone.

## Before you go back
- **Wait for the all-clear.** Do not enter closed areas.
- **Check air quality** at airnow.gov or on a weather app. Smoke can linger for days.
- **Go in daylight,** especially if power is off, so hazards are easier to see.
- **Bring supplies:** sturdy boots, gloves, an N95 mask, goggles, a flashlight, water, a first aid kit, a camera and your documents.
- **Take a buddy.** Tell someone where you are going.
- **Children should not do cleanup** or return with you until it is safe.

## On the road
- **Watch for broken traffic lights, missing street signs and debris.**
- **Expect road damage,** weakened culverts and burned trees that can fall.
- **Do not drive through standing water or over damaged roads.**
- **Obey checkpoints** and instructions.

## Around the house
**Hazards**
- **Downed power lines.** Stay far away and assume they are live. Report them.
- **Hot spots and smoldering stumps.** Roots can burn underground for days. Do not step in ash pits, which can hide hot coals.
- **Weakened trees and branches** (snags) that can fall, especially in wind.
- **Unstable structures,** broken glass and sharp metal.
- **Gas leaks.** If you smell gas, leave and call from a distance.
- **Propane tanks.** Check tanks, regulators and lines before turning gas back on. Have a professional check.
- **Electrical hazards:** do not touch wet equipment, and have the system checked before turning power on.
- **Carbon monoxide** from generators and engines run indoors.
- **Hazardous materials:** damaged fuel tanks, burned batteries, chemicals, asbestos and lead in older buildings.
- **Septic tanks** may be damaged. Do not drive over them.

**Search the home carefully for hidden embers or smoldering fires.** Check the attic, the roof, the eaves, vents, closets and under the floor. Watch for smoke. **Keep watching for several hours** (the fire watch). Wet down hot spots. Call 911 if you find fire.

## Check utilities
- **Do not turn on** gas, power or water until they are checked.
- **Electric:** have an electrician check wiring that was wet or burned.
- **Gas:** call the gas company for a check.
- **Water:** see the health lesson.
- **Sewer and septic:** check for damage before using.

## What to bring out
- **Food** that was exposed to heat, smoke or no refrigeration should be thrown away. See Food and Fridge Triage in the Power Outage course.
- **Documents,** valuables and keepsakes you can safely recover.
- **Medications** exposed to heat or smoke: ask a pharmacist.

## Keep safe while you work
- **Wear the gear:** gloves, long sleeves, long pants, closed shoes and socks, goggles and a fit-tested N95.
- **Wash ash off** your skin, eyes and mouth as soon as you can.
- **Take breaks,** drink water, and stop if you feel unwell.
- **Do not work alone.**
- **Take photos** before you move things (see the next lesson).

## If your home is destroyed or unsafe
- **Do not enter** a damaged structure.
- **Contact your insurer** and the Red Cross or local recovery center.
- **Find temporary housing.**

## Gear you might need
- Sturdy boots and gloves
- An N95 mask for ash and smoke residue
- Goggles, flashlight, camera and water

## Checklist
- Confirmed officials have declared the property safe to re-enter
- Watches for hazards: downed lines, hot spots, weakened trees, structural instability
- Avoids drinking tap water until it has been cleared, if advised locally
- Checked for hidden embers and smoldering areas
- Goes in daylight with a buddy and protective gear

> Return when it is declared safe, in daylight, with a buddy and the right gear.
