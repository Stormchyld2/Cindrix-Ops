## Plugging and patching
- **Plug** (put something into the hole) or **patch** (put something over it) to cut the flow. These are **temporary emergency fixes**, not repairs.
- Soft wood plugs wrapped in cloth work for small holes. Wrap them so the cloth fills small gaps, and **caulk** what is left with rags and wedges.
- For a burst pipe: shut off the water, then use a pipe clamp, rubber and a hose clamp, or an epoxy putty made for the job.
- For a roof or wall opening: a tarp lapped over the peak and fastened to boards.

## Shoring
**Shores** are braces that prop up a damaged wall, deck or ceiling to stop it falling further.
- Use a **flat base and a flat bearing surface** so the load spreads.
- Cut the timber about **half an inch short** so wedges can tighten it into place.
- Drive **wedges in pairs** from opposite sides so the shore stays straight.
- A **strongback** (a board across several studs or joists) spreads the load.
- Shore at the same angle as the load whenever possible and never lean heavy weight on one spot.
- Under metal columns, use steel or heavy wood that will not be cut through.

## When not to
If a structure looks unstable, **do not go in to shore it**. Leave and call for professional help.
