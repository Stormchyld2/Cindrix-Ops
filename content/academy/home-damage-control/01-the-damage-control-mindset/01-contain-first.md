Navy damage control has one goal: **keep the ship afloat and fighting**. Crews do not try to fix everything at once. They:

1. **Make it safe**: protect people first.
2. **Stop it spreading**: isolate the damaged area behind doors, bulkheads and hatches.
3. **Reduce the source**: stop the leak, put out the fire, cut the power.
4. **Remove the harm**: pump out water, ventilate smoke.
5. **Repair temporarily**, then permanently when it is safe.

## At home
- Know where the **water shutoff, electrical panel and gas shutoff** are, and practice shutting each off.
- **Closed doors slow fire and water.** Shutting a door behind you is a basic containment step.
- Decide in advance who does what, and where you meet outside.
- Keep a small kit: flashlight, work gloves, tarps, duct tape, wood scraps, rags, a bucket, a wrench for shutoffs, and a fire extinguisher.

> Life first, then property. Never go back into danger for belongings.

## Check yourself
? What is the first step in damage control?
+ Protect people and make the area safe
- Fix the pipe
- Save the furniture
- Call the insurer
> People come before property.
? Why close doors behind you in a fire or flood?
+ Closed doors slow the spread
- It looks neat
- It saves energy
- It locks the damage in forever
> Containment buys time.
? Name one thing to know in your home before an emergency.
+ Where the water, power and gas shutoffs are
- The neighbor's car model
- The roofer's phone number only
- The street name only
> Fast shutoffs stop damage from growing.
