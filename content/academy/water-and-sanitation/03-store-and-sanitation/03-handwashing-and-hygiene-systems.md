Washing hands with soap prevents many diarrhea and breathing illnesses. In a disaster, it may be the most powerful health step you have.

## Why it works
Germs from waste and dirty hands reach food and mouths. Soap lifts germs off skin so water can rinse them away. Hand sanitizer with at least 60 percent alcohol helps when your hands are not visibly dirty, but it does not remove dirt, and it is **not** a substitute for soap and water after the toilet or before eating.

## When to wash
- After using the toilet or changing a diaper
- Before preparing or eating food
- After handling waste, garbage or animals
- After caring for a sick person
- After coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose
- After cleaning or treating wounds

## The 20-second method
1. Wet hands with clean water.
2. Lather with soap, including backs, between fingers and under nails.
3. Scrub for at least 20 seconds.
4. Rinse.
5. Dry on a clean cloth or air dry.

## A tippy-tap and other low-water stations
- **Tippy-tap:** hang a jug with a small hole in the cap (or a pierced side) from a rope. Tip it with a foot lever to pour a thin stream, soap hung next to it. It uses very little water and keeps hands off a tap.
- **Bucket with a tap:** a bucket on a stand with a spigot, and a basin below.
- Place stations **at the toilet, kitchen and the entrance.**

## Showers and basin baths
Use a bucket, a cup and a washcloth. Wash the face, armpits, groin, feet and hands first. Even two cups of water makes a useful wash. Solar shower bags warm water for little cost.

## Clothes and dishes
- Wash dishes with hot, soapy water, rinse in a second basin with a bleach rinse (a teaspoon of plain bleach to a gallon of water), air dry.
- Wash clothes by agitating in a bucket, wringing, rinsing and drying in the sun, which also kills germs.

## Menstrual and baby hygiene
Plan for private washing, disposal and extra water for women, babies and the sick.

## Sickness
Keep a sick person's cup, plate and towel separate. Clean surfaces that they touch with a bleach solution. Wash hands after contact.

## Soap supply
Soap is cheap, light and powerful. Store a lot, and make more only if you know how. Keep a spare for each station.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
