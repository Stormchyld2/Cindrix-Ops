When water stops, so do flush toilets. Waste kills more people in disasters than many injuries, because germs spread from it. A plan for toilets matters as much as one for food.

## A rule of thumb
Plan on about **a gallon of waste capacity per person per day** and enough bags, lime or covering material for the whole time.

## Option 1: bucket toilet (best for homes)
1. Use a sturdy 5-gallon bucket with a snug toilet seat or a seat lid.
2. Line it with a heavy plastic bag.
3. After each use, add a scoop of **carbon cover:** sawdust, dry leaves, wood shavings, peat or shredded paper. It stops smell and flies.
4. Tie off the bag when about half full.
5. Keep a **lidded second container** to hold bags, outside and away from living areas, until they can be buried or collected.
6. Wash and disinfect the bucket with a bleach solution after each emptying.

## Option 2: trench or pit latrine (when staying put)
- Site it **at least 100 to 200 feet from any well, spring, stream or lake,** downhill from water, away from living and cooking areas, and out of flood paths.
- Dig a trench about 2 feet wide and 3 feet deep for a group; a hole about 6 feet deep for a long stay.
- Cover waste with soil or ash after every use. Keep a lid to keep out flies, and a screened vent for a deep pit.
- Close the pit when waste is within a foot of the top, and cover it with a mound of soil.

## Option 3: cat hole (travel)
Dig a hole 6 to 8 inches deep, at least 200 feet from water, trails and camps. Cover it well. Pack out paper in a bag where rules require it.

## Urine
Urine is mostly clean. Separate it away from camp, away from water, and cover damp places with soil to cut smell.

## Waste in a city or apartment
Use bags in a bucket and seal them well. Many cities give instructions for double-bagging and disposal. Never dump waste in storm drains, streams or yards where children play.

## Other waste
- **Trash:** bag it, keep it dry and away from animals. Burn only where safe and allowed.
- **Medical and sharps waste:** keep sharps in a hard container.
- **Dead animals:** bury at least 200 feet from water, deep enough to prevent scavenging.

## Never
- Use human waste on garden crops you eat raw, unless it has been safely composted using a proven method.
- Dump waste in water sources.
- Use a latrine for cooking or drinking supplies.
Wash your hands after every use. That is the next lesson.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
