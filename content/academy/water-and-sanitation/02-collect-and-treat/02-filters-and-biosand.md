A filter removes particles and many germs, with no fuel and no waiting. Learn what yours does and does not do.

## What the ratings mean
- **Pore size:** 0.1 to 0.2 micron (absolute) removes bacteria and protozoa. About 0.02 microns or smaller is needed to remove viruses, so many camp filters do not stop viruses.
- **NSF/ANSI standards:** look for NSF 53 (health contaminants), NSF 58 (reverse osmosis) and NSF 42 (taste), and for "removes bacteria, protozoa and viruses" on the label of a purifier.
- **Carbon** improves taste and removes some chemicals and chlorine. It does not make dirty water safe alone.
- **Ceramic filters** can be scrubbed clean and last long. Cartridges clog and have a rated life.

## Types
- **Pump and squeeze filters:** light and good for streams.
- **Gravity filters:** hang a bag, and the water drips through. Easy for a group.
- **Ceramic and carbon countertop systems:** many gallons a day for a home, with no power.
- **Straw filters:** emergency use only.
- **Reverse osmosis and distillers:** remove salts and many chemicals, but are slower or need power.

## Using a filter well
1. **Pre-filter** cloudy water through a cloth or let it settle.
2. **Backflush or clean** as the maker says. Do not let it freeze, because ice can crack the element and you cannot see the crack.
3. **Keep clean and dirty sides separate:** wash hands, and never let the clean outlet touch the dirty bag.
4. **Replace** the cartridge by its rating or when flow drops sharply.
5. **Follow with chlorine or boiling** if viruses could be present, such as after floods or near people.
6. **Keep a spare** element.

## Build a simple filter (an emergency step, not the final one)
A layered bucket filter: a bucket with a hole near the bottom, layers (from the bottom up) of gravel, coarse sand, fine sand and charcoal, over a cloth. It clears mud and some larger particles. It does **not** make water safe, so treat the output.

## The biosand filter
A concrete or plastic column filled with fine sand forms a "biolayer" on top that eats germs. Done right, it removes most bacteria and parasites and works in homes in many countries. It is a long-term, low-cost project. Follow a proven design (such as CAWST's) and disinfect the water after.

## Backups
Keep two methods. If the filter breaks, boiling still works. If fuel runs out, chlorine tablets still work.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
