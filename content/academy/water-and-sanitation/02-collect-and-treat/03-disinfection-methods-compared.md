Clear water is made safe by killing germs. Each method has strengths and limits.

## Boiling
Bring water to a **rolling boil for 1 minute** (3 minutes above about 6,500 feet), then cool it covered. It kills bacteria, viruses and parasites. It needs fuel and a pot, and it does not remove chemicals. Boiled water tastes flat. Pour it between two clean containers to add air.

## Chlorine (household bleach)
- Use plain, unscented bleach with 5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite and no added cleaners or thickeners.
- CDC guide for clear water: with 6 percent bleach, about **8 drops (1/8 teaspoon) per gallon**; with 8.25 percent, about 6 drops. For cloudy or cold water, double the amount.
- Stir, then wait **at least 30 minutes.** It should smell faintly of chlorine afterward. If not, repeat once and wait another 15 minutes.
- Bleach loses strength with age, heat and sun. Replace it each year.
- It works on bacteria and viruses well, and less on Cryptosporidium. Filtration helps.
- Do not use bleach that has no strength label, or any with perfumes or "splash-less" additives.

## Chlorine dioxide and iodine tablets
Light, long-lasting and good for bags. Follow the package times: chlorine dioxide handles Cryptosporidium with a longer wait (up to four hours for some), and iodine is a poor choice for pregnant women and anyone with thyroid disease.

## Sunlight (SODIS)
Fill **clear PET plastic** bottles (not glass, and not colored) with water that is clear enough to read newsprint through. Lay them on a reflective surface in full sun for about **six hours** (two days if cloudy). UV-A and heat kill most germs. It works only for small amounts of clear water, and does not remove chemicals.

## UV pens and lamps
Battery-powered UV pens kill germs in clear water in under a minute a liter. They need clear water, working batteries and careful stirring. Carry a backup method.

## Distillation
Boil water and catch the steam. It removes salts, metals and many chemicals, but it is slow and takes fuel. Use it for chemically contaminated or salty water if you must.

## Choosing
- **Cloudy and germ-risk:** filter, then chlorine or boil.
- **Chemical risk:** avoid, or distill, or use a carbon and RO system.
- **Small, clear, sunny:** SODIS.
- **Group, long term:** gravity filter plus chlorine.
- **After a flood or sewage spill:** treat in layers, and prefer another source.

## Always
Wash your hands. Use clean containers. Store treated water covered.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
