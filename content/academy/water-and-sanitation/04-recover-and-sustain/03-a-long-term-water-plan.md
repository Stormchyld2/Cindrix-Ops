## Step 1: assess (prevention)
- List your sources and rank them.
- List the top three threats to your water: power, flood, drought, contamination, freezing.
- Test private wells and note the date.

## Step 2: prepare
- **Storage:** gallons per person per day, for 14 days at least. Aim for 30.
- **Second source:** rain catchment, well, spring or a nearby stream.
- **Treatment:** at least two methods (for example a filter and chlorine) plus a boiling kit.
- **Containers and carrying:** enough to move water, and a cart or wagon.
- **Sanitation:** a bucket toilet kit, bags, cover material, soap, a hand-wash station.
- **Information:** a list of phone numbers (utility, health department, plumber, well driller).

## Step 3: response
- Write short cards: "If the water stops," "If there is a boil-water notice," "If the well floods."
- Practice the cards with the whole family.

## Step 4: mitigation
- Raise water storage and well caps above flood level.
- Protect pipes and stored water from freezing.
- Fit a backflow preventer if recommended.
- Spread supplies across the house and a second site.

## Step 5: recovery
- Disinfect and test after emergencies.
- Replace supplies.
- Write an after-action note.

## A yearly rhythm
- **Spring:** check rain gutters, tanks and screens.
- **Summer:** watch for drought, test well.
- **Fall:** drain exposed pipes, check storage and rotate it.
- **Winter:** protect from freezing, check heaters and keep a thaw plan.

## Teach
Make sure at least two adults and the older children can find the shutoff, treat water, use the toilet system and wash hands properly. Skills are as important as gear.

> A household that can find, treat, store and protect water, and handle its waste, has solved the first big problem of any long emergency.

> Local rules on rainwater, wells, greywater and waste differ. Check with your health department before you build anything permanent.
