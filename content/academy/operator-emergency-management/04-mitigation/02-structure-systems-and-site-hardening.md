Make the home and its systems tougher so the next event does less harm.

## The load path
Most structural failures happen at the connections. A strong house ties the roof to the walls to the foundation. In a high wind or an earthquake, a missing connection lets a section fail.
- **Roof-to-wall:** hurricane straps or clips.
- **Wall-to-foundation:** anchor bolts, and bracing in cripple walls in earthquake country.
- **Gable ends and garage doors** are weak points in wind. Brace and reinforce them.
Have a qualified engineer or inspector advise.

## Openings
Windows and doors fail first. Shutters, impact-rated glass, or plywood covers protect them from wind and debris. A strong entry door prevents wind pressure breaking in, and adds security.

## Roof and exterior
- Use a good roof covering for your hazard: Class A fire-rated for wildfire areas, impact-resistant for hail and wind.
- Seal gaps and vents. In wildfire areas, use ember-resistant vents with small mesh.
- Clean gutters and roof of debris.
- Keep trees trimmed and away from the house.

## Defensible space (wildfire)
- **Zone 0 (first 5 feet):** non-combustible: gravel, concrete, hard surfaces. No mulch or wooden decks touching the house.
- **Zone 1 (to 30 feet):** lean, clean and green. Space plants, remove dead growth, prune trees up from the ground.
- **Zone 2 (30 to 100 feet):** reduce fuel and space trees.
Follow CAL FIRE and local fire department guidance.

## Flood
- **Elevate** utilities (furnace, water heater, electrical panel) above expected flood level.
- **Install** flood vents in enclosed foundation walls.
- **Backflow valves** keep sewage from backing up.
- **Sump pumps** with battery backup.
- **Grade** the ground away from the house, clear drains and gutters.
- **Store** valuables above the flood line.

## Utilities
- **Know the shutoffs** (water, gas, electric) and label them.
- **Flexible gas lines** and a seismic gas shutoff valve help in earthquakes.
- **Strap** water heaters.
- **Surge protectors** and a transfer switch for a generator.
- **Smoke and CO alarms** wired to batteries.

## Interior
- Anchor tall furniture, bookcases, mirrors and televisions.
- Use latches on cabinets.
- Keep heavy items low.
- Keep exits clear.

## Site
- Grade, drain, fence, light and plant for your hazards.
- Keep fuel away from the house.
- Have two ways in and out.

## Safe room
A safe room built to FEMA P-361 standard protects from tornado winds. A basement or interior room is a minimum.

## Keep records
Photograph work and keep receipts. Update your insurance.
