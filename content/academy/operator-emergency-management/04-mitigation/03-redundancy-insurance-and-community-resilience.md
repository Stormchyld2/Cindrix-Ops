Resilience is the ability to take a hit and recover. It comes from redundancy, resources and relationships.

## Redundancy
Two is one, one is none. For each critical function, have more than one way:
- **Water:** stored water, rain, a well or spring, a filter.
- **Power:** battery bank, generator, solar, a non-electric alternative.
- **Heat:** wood stove, safe heater, layers, a small room.
- **Light:** lamps, flashlights, candles.
- **Cooking:** stove, grill, solar oven.
- **Communication:** phone, radio, messages, meeting points.
- **Transport:** vehicles, bicycles, walking.
- **Medical:** kit, skills, a plan for care.
- **Food:** stored, grown, preserved.
- **Money:** cash, bank, trade goods.
Spread redundancy over **different failure modes.** A second generator that uses the same fuel does not help if fuel is gone.

## Diversity
Mix your sources and methods so one failure cannot knock out everything. Do not put all supplies in one room, or all your savings in one place.

## Buffers
Extra time, extra money, extra supplies. Maintain buffers for each system. A month of slack beats a plan that works only if everything goes right.

## Insurance and finance
- **Review policies yearly:** home, renter, auto, flood, earthquake, life, health.
- **Understand deductibles** and exclusions. Standard homeowners insurance usually does not cover floods or earthquakes.
- **Document your belongings** with photographs, receipts and a list.
- **Keep an emergency fund** of several months of expenses, partly in cash.
- **Keep copies** of key documents off-site.

## Community resilience
The strongest households are part of strong communities:
- Know your neighbors, their skills and needs.
- Join groups: CERT, Neighborhood Watch, church or civic groups, ham radio clubs.
- Plan together: shared tools, a shared meeting place, a call tree.
- Check on the vulnerable.
- Support local preparedness and mitigation efforts.

## Social capital
Studies of disasters show that communities with strong social ties recover faster and lose fewer lives. Build trust now.

## Learn from failure
Resilient systems fail gracefully and recover. After each failure, look for the cause, and fix the design, not only the symptom.

## Maintain
Resilience fades if ignored. Schedule checks, drills and reviews.
