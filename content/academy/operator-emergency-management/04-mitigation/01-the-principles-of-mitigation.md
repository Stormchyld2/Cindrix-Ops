**Mitigation** means actions that reduce the harm from hazards over the long term. It is different from preparedness (getting ready to respond) and from response. Mitigation changes the situation, so there is less to respond to.

## The payoff
The National Institute of Building Sciences found that, on average, every dollar spent on federal mitigation grants saves about six dollars in future disaster costs, and building to modern codes saves even more. For households, even small steps pay.

## Four kinds of mitigation
1. **Reduce the hazard:** clear brush, grade the land, clean gutters, trim trees, install a sump pump.
2. **Reduce exposure:** move critical items out of the flood zone, relocate a shed, store fuel away from the house.
3. **Reduce vulnerability:** strengthen the structure, add roof straps, shutters, anchor bolts, flood vents, a safe room, and strap the water heater.
4. **Increase capacity:** more water, a second power source, stored food, training, insurance, savings, community links.

## Mitigation by hazard (examples)
- **Wind:** roof-to-wall straps, impact windows or shutters, a strong garage door, tree care.
- **Flood:** elevate utilities, flood vents, sump pump with battery backup, backflow valves, sandbags, move valuables up, flood insurance.
- **Wildfire:** defensible space, fire-resistant roofing, ember-resistant vents, a clean roof and gutters.
- **Earthquake:** anchor the house to the foundation, brace cripple walls, strap the water heater, secure furniture and objects.
- **Winter:** insulation, pipe protection, a safe backup heat source, a generator transfer switch.
- **Fire:** alarms, sprinklers, safe wiring, extinguishers.
- **Heat:** shade, insulation, a cooling room.
- **Power outage:** battery backup, generator, solar, a stove, a plan for medical devices.
- **Crime:** the security layers.
- **Economic:** savings, diverse income, low debt, insurance.

## Choosing projects
Rank each action by how much risk it removes and what it costs. Do the cheap and effective first. Plan the big ones over a few years. Keep a list with dates.

## Building codes and permits
Do structural work to code and with permits where required. Hire licensed professionals for electrical, gas and structural work.

## Insurance as mitigation
Insurance does not prevent loss but spreads the cost. Review coverage each year: flood, earthquake, fire, and contents. Photograph your belongings.

## Community mitigation
Take part in local hazard mitigation planning. Support firebreaks, drainage, and codes. Work with neighbors on shared hazards.

## Do not forget the people
Mitigation also includes health, skills and relationships.
