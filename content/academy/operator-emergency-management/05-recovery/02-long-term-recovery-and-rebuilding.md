Recovery can take months or years. Plan, pace yourself, and build back better.

## Phases
1. **Stabilize:** safety, shelter, utilities, basic supplies.
2. **Restore:** repair, clean, replace essentials.
3. **Rebuild:** longer-term construction, income, school.
4. **Improve:** use the rebuild to mitigate, so the next event hurts less.

## Money and help
- **Insurance:** file early, keep records, ask questions, and follow up in writing.
- **FEMA Individual Assistance:** grants for housing and other needs, when declared.
- **SBA disaster loans:** low-interest loans for homes, businesses and renters.
- **State and local programs.**
- **Nonprofits** (VOAD members such as the Red Cross, Salvation Army, and faith-based groups) offer cleanup, supplies and casework.
- **Employers and schools** may offer flexibility.
Keep a recovery binder with applications, contacts, case numbers and receipts.

## Rebuild with mitigation
When you repair, build stronger: raise utilities, upgrade roofs, add shutters, use fire-resistant materials, improve drainage. Ask your building department about codes and incentives. Mitigation grants often become available after a disaster.

## Choose contractors with care
- Hire licensed and insured contractors.
- Get several written estimates.
- Check references.
- Never pay in full in advance.
- Get a contract with dates and scope.
- Be cautious of door-to-door offers after disasters.

## Income and work
Plan for lost income: unemployment benefits, disaster unemployment assistance, remote options, temporary work. Keep communication open with employers.

## Children and schooling
Restore routine and school soon. Watch for stress in children.

## Community recovery
Join community recovery efforts. Volunteer, share skills, support local businesses. Take part in town meetings.

## Health
Look after sleep, food and exercise. Watch for depression, anxiety and substance use. Get medical care. Many people feel low after the first rush of activity fades.

## Pace yourself
Recovery is not a sprint. Set small goals, accept help and take breaks. Celebrate progress.

## Record the lessons
Write what worked and what did not, for yourself and for others.
