Professionals do not treat a disaster as a single event. They treat it as a cycle that repeats: **prevention and hazard assessment, preparedness, response, mitigation and recovery.** Each phase feeds the next, and weakness in one shows up in the others.

## The five principles
1. **Prevention and hazard assessment:** find what can hurt you, and stop or avoid what you can.
2. **Preparedness:** plan, train, equip and exercise so you are ready.
3. **Response:** act during the event to save lives, protect property and stabilize the situation.
4. **Mitigation:** reduce the long-term risk and the harm from the next event.
5. **Recovery:** restore life, rebuild, and learn.
The cycle turns: the lessons of recovery feed the next round of hazard assessment.

## What an operator does differently
- **Thinks in systems.** Water, food, power, shelter, medical care, communication, security and morale depend on each other. A weak link in one breaks others.
- **Plans for more than one kind of disaster.** An "all-hazards" plan covers the things that most events share: loss of power, water, communication and roads; injury and illness; the need to shelter or leave.
- **Decides early, with imperfect facts.** Waiting for certainty is a decision to be late.
- **Uses simple, written plans** that others can follow.
- **Drills the plan** and fixes what the drill shows.
- **Cares for people,** including the helpers.

## Lifelines
FEMA describes seven **community lifelines** that must keep running: safety and security, food, water and shelter, health and medical, energy, communications, transportation, and hazardous materials. A household or group can adopt the same list: if one fails, what is the backup?

## Levels of readiness
- **Tier 3** gave you the foundation: a kit, a plan, basic skills.
- **Tier 2** gave you practice: water, food, security, navigation, vehicles, medical care.
- **Tier 1** asks you to **lead and integrate:** to hold the whole system together under pressure, and to teach others.

## How this course runs
Five modules, one for each principle, each with three lessons. At the end you will build a complete **all-hazards plan** for your household or group, test it and review it.

> A plan is not a binder. It is a set of decisions that people have practiced.

> This course is for people who have finished Tiers 3 and 2. It builds on them. Nothing here replaces the instructions of emergency services.
