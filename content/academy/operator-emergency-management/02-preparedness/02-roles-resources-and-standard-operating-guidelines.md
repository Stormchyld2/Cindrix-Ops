In an emergency, people look to those who seem to know what to do. A group that has agreed on roles and procedures in advance acts faster and quarrels less.

## Roles
For a household or group, define a few clear roles with a deputy for each:
- **Leader (incident lead):** sets priorities, makes the final call, keeps the big picture.
- **Operations:** does the work: shelter, water, food, repairs.
- **Medical:** assesses and treats, keeps health records.
- **Security and safety:** watches hazards and people, runs watches.
- **Communications:** radios, phones, messages, records.
- **Logistics:** supplies, inventory, transport, fuel.
- **Planning and records:** keeps notes, a task board, and looks ahead.
- **Care and morale:** children, elders, food, rest, spirits.
In a small group, one person has several roles. The point is to name who is responsible.

## Span of control
A leader can manage three to seven people well. If the group grows, divide it into teams, each with a lead.

## Standard operating guidelines (SOGs)
SOGs are short, written steps for jobs that repeat or that matter. They are **guidelines,** not rigid scripts. They give a good default and still leave room to adapt.
Write a SOG for:
- Opening and closing the day
- Handing over a watch
- Water collection and treatment
- Cooking and food safety
- Sanitation and waste
- A medical emergency
- A fire
- Strangers at the gate
- Leaving in a hurry
- Radio use and message logs
Each SOG fits on one page: **purpose, who, what you need, steps, and what to report.**

## Inventories and caches
- Keep an inventory of supplies, with locations and dates.
- Split key supplies across places so one loss does not end you.
- Check and rotate on a schedule.

## Training and cross-training
Each person learns their own role and one other. Check each person's skills: medical, mechanical, radio, cooking, gardening, security, teaching. Fill gaps by training.

## Records
Keep a log of events, decisions, and supplies. It helps when memory fails and when you review.

## Keep it humane
Rank is not about status. Respect, rest, fair work and clear thanks keep a group together longer than orders do.
