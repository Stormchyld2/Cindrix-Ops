A plan is the set of decisions you make **before** the emergency, so you do not have to invent them during it.

## The planning process (FEMA CPG 101)
1. **Form a team.** Include everyone who will carry out the plan, and some who will challenge it.
2. **Understand the situation.** Use your hazard assessment.
3. **Determine goals and objectives.** What must the plan achieve? Keep people alive, keep them warm, fed and hydrated, keep them informed.
4. **Develop the plan.** Write courses of action: who does what, when and with what.
5. **Prepare and review.** Check with others, test it on paper.
6. **Approve and share.** Everyone must know it.
7. **Implement and maintain.** Drill, update, repeat.

## A plan has layers
- **Base plan:** purpose, scope, roles, how decisions are made, how people communicate, where to meet. Short.
- **Functional annexes:** one per job, such as water, food, medical, security, communications, sheltering, evacuation, children and pets, finances and papers, morale.
- **Hazard annexes:** one per top hazard with its special steps, such as flood or wildfire evacuation.
- **Checklists and cards:** one page for each emergency job, in simple language.
- **Contact list:** phone numbers, radio channels, addresses, meeting points.
- **Maps:** routes, shutoffs, resources.

## Write good plan text
- **Plain and short.** Short sentences, bullet lists, no jargon.
- **Who, what, when, where.** "Dad shuts off the gas at the meter after the earthquake."
- **Triggers:** "If the water stops for more than 4 hours, we start the water plan."
- **Alternates:** every key step has a backup. The tiered PACE approach: primary, alternate, contingency, emergency.
- **Decision points:** when do we leave, when do we stay, when do we ask for help?

## Resource planning
For each function, list the **people, equipment, supplies, skills, time** required. Mark the gaps. Fill the gaps in order of risk.

## Special needs
Plan for the elderly, children, those with disabilities, the sick, pets and livestock. Consider medical equipment that needs electricity. Plan for people who are away from home when it happens (at work, school or on the road).

## Continuity
Plan for continuing the essentials: income, schooling, medicine, communication with family. Back up documents and photographs.

## Test with a tabletop
Read a scenario aloud. Each person says what they would do. Look for gaps. This is cheap and finds many problems.

## Review
After any exercise or event, update the plan with what you learned and the date.
