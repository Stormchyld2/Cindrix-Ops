[video] Moving with a low profile

In a crisis you often do not want to be noticed. The goal is not to hide from help. It is to avoid unwanted attention from people who might harm you or take what you have.

## Why it matters
After a disaster, desperate people may look for supplies. Criminals may take advantage of confusion. Standing out can attract trouble.

## Appear ordinary
- **Dress plainly,** in neutral colors. Avoid flashy clothing, brand logos and anything that signals wealth or prepping.
- **Carry bags** that do not look like tactical or expensive gear.
- **Do not display** supplies. Keep food and water out of sight.
- **Act normal:** walk like you belong. Confident, not rushed.
- **Avoid** drawing attention with light, noise and smoke at night.

## Be quiet
- **Talk less,** and keep your voice low.
- **Avoid** bragging or telling strangers what you have or where you live.
- **Be careful online.** Do not post your location, supplies or plans.
- **Limit** radio chatter and use plain talk without personal details (see the Radio Licenses course).

## Move smart
- **Stay aware** of your surroundings. Look up from your phone.
- **Travel in daylight** where possible.
- **Use main roads** where there are people and light, or the safest route for your situation.
- **Avoid** crowds that are agitated, and areas known for trouble.
- **Plan routes** with alternates.
- **Travel in groups** if you can.
- **Keep to shadows** only when you must avoid light, and avoid dark places where you cannot see.
- **Know the exits.**

## Light and noise discipline
- **Cover windows** at night so light does not show.
- **Keep generators** quiet and place them out of sight. They attract attention.
- **Cook** when smells will not draw attention, or cook in sheltered places.
- **Dim lights** and keep them low.

## Your home
- **Do not advertise** that you have supplies. No signs, no piles of gear outside.
- **Keep garbage** like empty food boxes out of sight.
- **Be a good neighbor** quietly. Neighbors who trust you protect you.
- **Know who is around.**

## Vehicles
- **Keep valuables out of sight.**
- **Keep tanks half full** or more.
- **Do not leave** a car unlocked.
- **Park** where you can leave quickly.

## Trust your instincts
If a place or person feels wrong, **leave.** Do not worry about being rude.

## What "low profile" really means
In a disaster or unrest, you are safest when you do not stand out. That does not mean hiding. It means looking like everyone else, drawing no attention to your supplies and avoiding places where trouble is likely. Most harm in a crisis falls on people who looked like easy or interesting targets.

## Practical habits
- **Dress plainly,** in neutral colors, without logos, slogans or military styling, and carry gear in an ordinary pack or bag.
- **Keep supplies out of sight.** Do not talk about what you have, in person or online.
- **Move at a normal pace** and with purpose, as though you know where you are going.
- **Keep your phone, valuables and cash out of sight.**
- **Travel with company** when you can, and keep to well-lit, busy routes.
- **Plan routes in advance** and avoid chokepoints, protests and crowds.
- **Be quiet,** at night especially, with lights and sound discipline: low light, covered screens and no loud talk.

## At home and in your vehicle
At home, close curtains at night and keep lighting modest. Do not advertise a generator by running it at night in view. In a vehicle, keep it locked and the fuel tank above half, park where you can leave quickly and do not leave valuables in sight.

## Trust your instincts
If a place or a person feels wrong, leave. You do not need to prove anything. Walk away, cross the road, go into a shop or call for help.

## Scenarios to think through
**Walking home after a power failure.** Streetlights are out and many people are on the road. Walk with a companion on well-used streets, keep a small light pointed down and off your face, carry your bag in front of you in crowds, and keep your hands free. Avoid shortcuts through alleys and car parks even if they save time.

**Stopping for fuel or supplies.** Lines draw crowds and tempers. Go early or off-peak, fill up when you are at half a tank, not at empty, and buy what you need without commentary. Talking about your plans or preparations to strangers marks you as someone with something to take.

**Leaving the area.** Move before the roads clog, not with them. Take the secondary route you scouted in advance. Keep the vehicle plain and unloaded in sight: gear belongs in the trunk, not strapped to the roof.

## What a low profile is not
It is not secrecy about everything. Tell your out-of-area contact and your trusted neighbors your plans. It is not rudeness. A friendly nod costs nothing and makes you part of the neighborhood, which is the best protection of all. It is not being fearful. People who move calmly and look confident attract less attention than people who look frightened.

## Practise
Take a walk around your area at dusk and see what stands out. Is your house the only one lit? Can people see into your windows? Then repeat it from the point of view of a stranger, and adjust.
