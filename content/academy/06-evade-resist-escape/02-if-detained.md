[video] If you are held or detained

If you are detained or held against your will, your job is to **stay alive, stay healthy and keep your mind clear.**

## First moments
- **Stay calm.** Panic helps no one.
- **Do what is needed to avoid violence.** Your life matters more than property.
- **Do not argue, resist physically, or make sudden moves.**
- **Be polite and plain.** Do not provoke.
- **Keep your hands visible.**
- **Follow instructions** unless they put you in immediate mortal danger.

## While held
- **Watch and remember.** Faces, voices, routines, sounds, directions.
- **Stay healthy.** Eat and drink what you can. Keep moving if possible. Rest when you can.
- **Keep your mind busy.** Count, recall songs, plan, remember people who love you.
- **Be careful what you reveal.** Share only what is needed. Do not make promises you cannot keep.
- **Build a calm relationship** with captors where you can. Being seen as a person can make harm less likely, but do not try to manipulate.
- **Keep your dignity.**
- **Do not volunteer** for risky tasks.
- **Medical needs:** state them calmly.
- **Look for signs** of help or change in routine.

## In a crowd or active threat
If there is an active violent threat (such as a shooter), the federal "Run, Hide, Fight" guidance applies:
1. **Run** if there is a safe path. Leave your belongings. Help others if you can. Call 911 when safe.
2. **Hide** if you cannot run. Lock and barricade a door, silence your phone, turn off lights and stay out of sight and quiet.
3. **Fight** only as a last resort, when your life is in imminent danger, with whatever you have, committing fully.
See your local police and the Department of Homeland Security for training.

## Law enforcement stops
If stopped by police, stay calm, keep your hands where they can see them, follow instructions, and know your rights. Do not resist, and handle disagreements later with a lawyer.

## Escape?
Only attempt escape if there is a **clear, safe chance** and you believe staying is more dangerous. Failed attempts can make things worse. **Outside help** is often a better route.

## After release
- **Get medical care** and emotional support.
- **Report** to police and write down what you remember.
- **Talk** to someone you trust. Trauma is real. **988** and the **Disaster Distress Helpline (1-800-985-5990)** can help.

> Survive first. Everything else comes after.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.

## Stay calm and keep safe
Being stopped by police, security staff or a crowd can be frightening. The goal is to stay safe, stay calm and protect your rights. Almost every stop ends peacefully when both sides stay calm.

## With law enforcement
- Keep your hands visible, move slowly and follow lawful instructions.
- Stay calm and polite. Do not argue, resist or run.
- Ask clearly, "Am I free to go?" If not, you are being detained.
- You may have the right to stay silent and ask for a lawyer. State this plainly and calmly if you choose to use it.
- Do not consent to a search, but do not physically resist one. Say "I do not consent to searches."
- Write down badge numbers, times and what happened afterward.

## If held by others
If you are held by a private person or group, stay calm, avoid provoking them and comply with reasonable instructions while you look for a safe chance to leave or call for help. Your life matters more than property.

## After release
Write down what happened while it is fresh, seek medical care if needed, contact a lawyer or a legal aid group and report abuse to the proper authorities. Look after your emotional wellbeing.

> Rights and procedures vary by country and place. Learn the laws that apply to you.

## Rehearse the first thirty seconds
Most of what matters happens at the start. Practise a short, calm script with your family, so that it is automatic: hands visible, move slowly, say your name if asked, ask whether you are free to go and state whether you wish to remain silent or want a lawyer. Rehearsing with children is wise, because young people are often the ones who panic. Teach them to stay calm, keep their hands visible and ask for a parent or lawyer.

## Know the difference between a stop and an arrest
A brief stop is a temporary detention. You can generally be asked for identification in many places, and you may be patted down if the officer believes you are armed. An arrest means you are in custody and have the right to remain silent. In the United States, you have the right to be told of these rights upon arrest, and you can ask for a lawyer at once. Laws vary widely elsewhere, so learn those that apply to you before you travel.

## Disasters and checkpoints
After a disaster, authorities may set up checkpoints, curfews and evacuation controls. Comply calmly, carry identification and documents showing your address, and ask where the nearest shelter or exit route is. These controls are usually for safety, and resistance only makes things worse.

## Document and recover
If you believe you were treated unlawfully, write down what happened, with times, places and names as soon as you can. Keep copies of any paperwork. Contact legal aid or civil rights groups, which often help for free. Take care of yourself: being detained is stressful even when handled well, so talk to someone you trust.
