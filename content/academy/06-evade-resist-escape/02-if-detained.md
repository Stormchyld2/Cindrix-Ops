If you are detained or held against your will, your job is to **stay alive, stay healthy and keep your mind clear.**

## First moments
- **Stay calm.** Panic helps no one.
- **Do what is needed to avoid violence.** Your life matters more than property.
- **Do not argue, resist physically, or make sudden moves.**
- **Be polite and plain.** Do not provoke.
- **Keep your hands visible.**
- **Follow instructions** unless they put you in immediate mortal danger.

## While held
- **Watch and remember.** Faces, voices, routines, sounds, directions.
- **Stay healthy.** Eat and drink what you can. Keep moving if possible. Rest when you can.
- **Keep your mind busy.** Count, recall songs, plan, remember people who love you.
- **Be careful what you reveal.** Share only what is needed. Do not make promises you cannot keep.
- **Build a calm relationship** with captors where you can. Being seen as a person can make harm less likely, but do not try to manipulate.
- **Keep your dignity.**
- **Do not volunteer** for risky tasks.
- **Medical needs:** state them calmly.
- **Look for signs** of help or change in routine.

## In a crowd or active threat
If there is an active violent threat (such as a shooter), the federal "Run, Hide, Fight" guidance applies:
1. **Run** if there is a safe path. Leave your belongings. Help others if you can. Call 911 when safe.
2. **Hide** if you cannot run. Lock and barricade a door, silence your phone, turn off lights and stay out of sight and quiet.
3. **Fight** only as a last resort, when your life is in imminent danger, with whatever you have, committing fully.
See your local police and the Department of Homeland Security for training.

## Law enforcement stops
If stopped by police, stay calm, keep your hands where they can see them, follow instructions, and know your rights. Do not resist, and handle disagreements later with a lawyer.

## Escape?
Only attempt escape if there is a **clear, safe chance** and you believe staying is more dangerous. Failed attempts can make things worse. **Outside help** is often a better route.

## After release
- **Get medical care** and emotional support.
- **Report** to police and write down what you remember.
- **Talk** to someone you trust. Trauma is real. **988** and the **Disaster Distress Helpline (1-800-985-5990)** can help.

> Survive first. Everything else comes after.

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.
