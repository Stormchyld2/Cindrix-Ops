[video] Reuniting after separation

In a disaster, families are often apart. Having a plan to find each other reduces fear and risk.

## Before an event
- **Choose an out-of-area contact.**
- **Pick two meeting places.**
- **Carry a card** with contact numbers.
- **Teach children** their phone number and the contact.
- **Know** each other's routines: work, school, daycare.
- **Plan** pickup rules for children.
- **Practice.**

## Right after
1. **Get safe.**
2. **Text** or call your out-of-area contact.
3. **Keep calls short.**
4. **Use social media** to post that you are safe.
5. **Register** at **safeandwell.communityos.org** (the Red Cross Safe and Well site) and search for family.
6. **Check** on the nearest meeting place if safe.
7. **Listen** to the radio for official instructions and reunification centers.

## If you cannot reach someone
- **Do not panic.** Networks are jammed. It does not mean they are hurt.
- **Keep trying** at intervals.
- **Leave messages.**
- **Check** with neighbors, the school, the workplace and shelters.
- **Contact** the Red Cross or local authorities if someone is missing.

## Children
- **Schools and daycare** have plans. Know them, and follow instructions. Do not rush to the school. Wait for official guidance.
- **Reunification centers:** bring ID.
- **Teach children** to find a trusted adult (police, firefighter) if separated.

## Pets
- **Microchip** and tag pets.
- **Keep photos.**
- **Check shelters.**

## Radios
- **Two-way radios** let groups communicate when phones fail. See the Radio Licenses course.
- **Set check-in times,** channels and call names.

## Leave notes
If you must leave home, leave a note in a visible place: who, where you have gone, when and contact info. Do not reveal if the home is empty to strangers.

## After reunion
- **Check everyone's health.**
- **Share your story.**
- **Update your plan.**

## Checklist
- [ ] Out-of-area contact and two meeting places
- [ ] Contact cards in every bag
- [ ] Know the school's reunification plan
- [ ] Know Safe and Well

> This is basic awareness, not a substitute for a certified course or hands-on training. Call 911 for life-threatening emergencies when you can.

## Plan the reunion before it is needed
Families are often apart when disaster strikes. Agree on meeting places, contact methods and times in advance, and write them down.

## The reunion plan
1. **Primary meeting place** near home, such as a neighbor's house.
2. **Secondary place** outside the neighborhood.
3. **Out-of-area contact** everyone reports to.
4. **Check-in times** at set intervals.
5. **Notes** left at home and at each stop.
6. **Radios** or other backups for when phones fail.

## For children and pets
Teach children their names, address and parents' numbers, and put a card in their pocket. Tell schools who may pick them up. Keep pet tags and photos current.

## After reunion
Check everyone, share what happened, update the plan and rest.

## Make the plan concrete
A good reunion plan names places, times and methods, not vague intentions. Write them on a card: "If we cannot reach each other, go to Aunt Maria's house at 12 Elm Street. If that is unsafe, go to the library parking lot on Main. Text Uncle Joe in Ohio at 555-0100. Check in at the top of every hour at first."

## Stages
**Right after the event:** make sure you are safe, check on those near you and then try to contact family. Use text first.
**Within an hour:** send a short status message to the out-of-area contact. Move toward the primary meeting point if safe.
**Within the day:** if you cannot meet, go to the secondary place, leave notes at each location and keep trying the contact.
**Within days:** register on official reunification services, such as the American Red Cross Safe and Well site, and check shelters and hospitals through the proper channels.

## Notes that help
A note on the door says who was here, when, where you went and how to reach you. Use a waterproof pen and put it where it will be seen but not seen by everyone. Do not post your address publicly.

## Children and pets
Children should know the plan, carry a card and know a trusted adult to go to. Schools have reunification procedures, so learn them and keep your authorized pick-up list up to date. Microchip pets and keep tags and photos current.

## Radios
Phones may fail, so radios help. A family radio service (FRS) set works over short distances with no license. Agree on a channel and times. Practice before you need it.
