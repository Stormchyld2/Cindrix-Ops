The CDC notes that "a respirator filters out smoke or ash before you can breathe it in" — worth remembering during cleanup, when ash exposure is easy to underestimate. Burned hillsides also carry a real risk of flash flooding and debris flows for a long time afterward, since the vegetation that normally absorbs rainfall is gone.

## Gear you might need

- N95 or P100 respirator
- HEPA air purifier, for indoor recovery

## Checklist
- Monitors local air quality index before resuming outdoor activity
- Uses a respirator when handling ash or debris
- Watches for delayed hazards — flash flooding and debris flows on burned slopes
