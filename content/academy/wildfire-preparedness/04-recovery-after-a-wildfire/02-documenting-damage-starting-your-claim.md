United Policyholders — a national nonprofit that's guided disaster survivors through recovery since 1991 — puts the first priority plainly: "Take care of your family's needs first." Once that's handled, documentation is what protects you financially. Photograph everything before any cleanup begins; once debris is cleared, that evidence is gone.

Understand that there will be lots of well-intentioned people coming in to assist in disaster relief. Make sure that you talk to your insurance company and get your claim going before anyone tries to do work on your property. Unauthorized work can cause problems with your claim. This is a sad fact of life.

Be aware of scams. Get the contact information of anyone legitimate who is offering help. Down the road, you may be able to contact them once the proper claims are made and their assistance would be helpful.

## Gear you might need

- Phone or camera for documentation
- A notebook or note-taking app for a claim diary

## Checklist
- Photographed or filmed all damage before any cleanup begins
- Contacted insurer to open a claim
- Requested an advance on Additional Living Expenses (ALE) if displaced
- Started a claim diary — date, time, who you spoke with, what was said
- Checked FEMA/DisasterAssistance.gov if the disaster received a federal declaration
