Insurance claims after a major wildfire can take months, and the households that fare best tend to be the ones who stay organized and keep a paper trail from day one. This lesson is general information, **not legal or financial advice.**

## What FEMA can and cannot do
If your area is declared a federal disaster, **FEMA assistance** can help bridge gaps that insurance does not cover. It **cannot duplicate** what insurance pays, so a claim has to be filed first. FEMA help is generally limited and is not meant to make you whole.

## The stages of a claim
1. **Report and open the claim.** Get the claim number.
2. **Meet the adjuster.** Show the damage and your photos. Keep the adjuster's name and contact.
3. **Inventory.** List damaged and lost items, with age, value and replacement cost. Use your home inventory.
4. **Estimates.** Get **at least one licensed contractor estimate** to compare against your policy limits and the adjuster's estimate.
5. **Review the offer.** Read the settlement carefully. Compare it with your policy and your estimates. Ask for an explanation of any difference.
6. **Negotiate or appeal.** If you disagree, put your objections in writing with supporting documents. Ask about the appraisal clause or mediation.
7. **Payments.** Know how payments are made: actual cash value first, then replacement cost holdback when you rebuild or replace. Know the **deadlines** for claiming it.
8. **Rebuild or replace.**

## Keep the records
- **Receipts** for repairs, cleanup, temporary housing, meals, clothes and emergency purchases.
- **Estimates** and contracts.
- **Letters and emails.**
- **Photos and videos.**
- **The diary.**

Keep them in a binder or dedicated digital folder, with backups.

## Know your rights
- You have the right to **a prompt response and a fair settlement** under your state's laws.
- You can **ask your insurer to explain** in writing any denial or reduction.
- **State insurance commissioners** can help if a claim stalls or you are treated unfairly. Find your state's contact on the NAIC website.
- You may choose to hire a **public adjuster** or an **attorney** to help, for a fee. Check licenses and fees carefully.
- **Deadlines matter.** Know the time limits to file and to claim ALE and replacement cost.

## Temporary housing
- Use your **ALE** coverage and keep receipts.
- Ask your insurer how long ALE lasts and what extensions exist.
- Look for housing assistance through **FEMA, the Red Cross** and local programs.

## Rebuild with resilience
When you rebuild or repair, use ember-resistant materials and defensible space (see the first module). Ask about **code upgrade** coverage and about discounts.

## Look after yourselves
Claims are tiring. Take breaks. Ask a trusted friend to help with paperwork. See the health lesson for emotional support.

## Gear you might need
- A binder or dedicated digital folder for all claim paperwork

## Checklist
- Obtained a full copy of the policy, including the declarations page
- Got at least one licensed contractor estimate to compare against policy limits
- Kept all receipts for repairs, cleanup and temporary housing
- Knows how to reach the state insurance commissioner's office if the claim stalls
- Knows the deadlines for claiming ALE and replacement cost

> Organization wins claims. Keep every paper and write everything down.
