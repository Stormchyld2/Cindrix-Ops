Not every alert means the same thing, and confusing "be ready" with "leave now" is a genuinely dangerous mistake. Most areas use a tiered system — something like an advisory, then a warning, then a formal evacuation order — and it's worth knowing your local terminology before you're relying on it under stress.

## Gear you might need

- Battery or hand-crank radio, for when cell networks are congested or down
- Shortwave radio or VHF/UHF radio for listening on amateur radio frequencies to gather information. If you are a licensed operator, you can also transmit and ask questions.

## Checklist
- Knows the difference between an evacuation warning and an evacuation order
- Has a way to receive alerts that doesn't depend solely on cell signal
- Knows to monitor local news or radio once fire activity is nearby
