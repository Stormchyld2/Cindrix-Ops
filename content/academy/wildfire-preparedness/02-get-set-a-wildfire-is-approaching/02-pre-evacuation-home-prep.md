If — and only if — time allows, a short list of home actions can meaningfully slow a fire's ability to take hold while you're gone. The USDA Forest Service recommends connecting hoses and filling containers with water in advance, if it's safe to do so. None of these are worth risking your safety for; skip straight to leaving if conditions are already dangerous.

## Gear you might need

- A hose long enough to reach the roofline
- Metal trash can lids (usable as makeshift ember shields)
- Leather work gloves
- Extension ladder

## Checklist
- Gas shut off at the meter, if instructed and time allows
- Windows and doors closed but left unlocked
- Flammable curtains and furniture moved away from windows
- Exterior hoses connected and accessible. Sprinklers can be used on roofs or around property to wet down areas.
- Home address clearly visible from the street for responders
