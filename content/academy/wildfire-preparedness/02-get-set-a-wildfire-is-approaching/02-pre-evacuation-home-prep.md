If **and only if** time allows, a short list of home actions can meaningfully slow a fire's ability to take hold while you are gone. None of these is worth risking your safety. **Skip straight to leaving if conditions are already dangerous.**

## Mitigation: actions that limit damage
Think of these as the fourth principle of emergency management: active steps to limit damage while the emergency unfolds.

## Inside
1. **Close all windows and doors,** but **leave them unlocked** so firefighters can enter.
2. **Remove flammable window coverings** such as curtains and blinds. Close metal shutters or heavy fire-resistant drapes if you have them.
3. **Move flammable furniture** to the center of the room, away from windows and glass doors.
4. **Shut off the air conditioning and close vents,** so you do not pull in smoke and embers.
5. **Turn off gas** at the meter if instructed and time allows. Turn off **propane** tanks.
6. **Leave lights on** (inside and a porch light) so firefighters can see the house in smoke.
7. **Put a note on the door** saying who you are, when you left and where you are going.
8. **Place valuables and documents** you could not take in a fireproof safe or a central interior location.
9. **Unplug the garage door opener** or know the manual release, so the door can be opened without power.

## Outside
1. **Move flammables inside or away:** patio furniture, cushions, doormats, hanging baskets, trash cans, toys and firewood.
2. **Move the propane BBQ** away from the house, and shut off its tank.
3. **Connect hoses** to outdoor taps, and leave them reachable and long enough to reach the roofline. Fill buckets, trash cans, a pool and other containers with water.
4. **Leave sprinklers on** if you have a pre-planned system, **but only if instructed** and if they will not drain your water supply that firefighters need. Check with your fire department. Many agencies advise **not** to run sprinklers on roofs or around the house after an evacuation order because they can drop water pressure for firefighters. Know your local advice.
5. **Wet down the area** only if you have a safe amount of time.
6. **Back your car into the driveway,** facing out, with doors and windows closed. Keep the keys with you.
7. **Close the gate** but leave it unlocked or accessible to responders.
8. **Make sure the address** is clear from the road.
9. **Gather pets and animals** (see the next lesson).

## Never
- Never stay to fight a fire without training, equipment and a safe place.
- Never go up on the roof when embers are falling.
- Never turn the gas on or off if you smell it or if you were not told to. Leave and call from a distance.
- Never delay leaving to save belongings.
- Never leave children or pets behind.

## Time-boxed plan
Use a timer. If you have **30 minutes or more,** do the inside list and the outside list. If you have **10 minutes,** grab people, pets, bags and documents, close doors and windows, and go. If you have **no time,** go.

## Check on neighbors
If time allows, check on neighbors who may need help, and tell your group by radio or text where you are going.

## Gear you might need
- A hose long enough to reach the roofline
- Metal trash can lids (usable as makeshift ember shields)
- Leather work gloves
- An extension ladder

## Checklist
- Gas shut off at the meter, if instructed and time allows
- Windows and doors closed but left unlocked
- Flammable curtains and furniture moved away from windows
- Hoses connected and accessible
- Home address clearly visible from the street for responders
- Car backed into the driveway, facing out

> Do the prep list only if it is safe. Leaving early is the best home protection.
