If evacuation is no longer possible, the interior of a home is safer than being caught outside. This is a **last resort.** Evacuating early is the answer. If you did not, these steps give you your best chance.

## Inside the home
1. **Stay calm** and keep your family together.
2. **Call 911** and give your exact location, and the number of people and pets with you. Tell them you are sheltering in place.
3. **Fill sinks, bathtubs and buckets with cold water.** Soak towels, rugs and blankets to use for sealing gaps and for putting out small fires.
4. **Keep doors and windows closed but unlocked** so firefighters can get in.
5. **Remove drapes and other combustible materials** from windows. Move furniture away from windows.
6. **Stay inside, away from outside walls and windows.** Pick an **interior room** with few windows, near an exit if possible. A bathroom with water is a good choice.
7. **Stay together** in that room.
8. **Wear protective clothing** and have shoes on. Keep the N95 masks and goggles on.
9. **Keep the lights on,** so firefighters see the house.
10. **Turn off the air conditioning** and close the vents and the fireplace damper.
11. **Seal gaps** with wet towels at the bottom of doors.
12. **Keep a fire extinguisher** and a **wool blanket** with you.
13. **Watch for small fires** inside, caused by embers entering through vents or broken windows. Put them out with the extinguisher or water.
14. **Check the attic** and roof area for embers if it is safe to do so, and put out small fires with water.
15. **Stay hydrated** and drink water.
16. **Wait for the fire front to pass.** It may pass quickly, in minutes. Then check the house for fires and smoldering areas, since embers can ignite materials hours later.

## After the front passes
1. **Stay inside until it is safe,** and until the heat and smoke have dropped.
2. **Walk through the house,** including the attic, closets and under the roof, looking for **hot spots** and **smoke.**
3. **Check the roof and the yard** for embers, and wet down smoldering areas.
4. **Watch for hidden fires** for several hours or longer. A fire watch is needed.
5. **Call 911** if you cannot control a fire.
6. **Do not leave** into smoke and flames. Wait for firefighters' instructions.

## What to expect
- It will be **hot,** **smoky,** and **loud.**
- **Power and water** may fail.
- **Windows may crack.**
- The **fire front** passes, but **burning continues.**

## Why this is a last resort
A house that has not been hardened and cleared of fuel may burn down around you. Sheltering in place in a burning house is deadly. Always leave if you can.

## Gear you might need
- A fire extinguisher
- A wool blanket
- A filled bathtub or sinks as an accessible water source
- Buckets and towels

## Checklist
- Knows to stay inside, away from outer walls
- Knows to keep doors closed but unlocked for firefighter access
- Knows to keep the household together in one interior room
- Removes drapes and other combustible materials from windows
- Knows to call 911 with the exact location
- Knows to do a fire watch after the front passes

> Sheltering in a house is a last resort. Leaving early is the plan.

## Check yourself
? Where in the house should you shelter if trapped?
- In the attic
- Near a large window
+ An interior room away from outer walls and windows
- Outside
> Stay away from glass and radiant heat.

? What should you do with doors?
- Lock and barricade
+ Keep them closed but unlocked for firefighters
- Remove them
- Prop them open
> Responders must be able to enter.

? Why fill tubs and sinks with water?
- To drink only
+ To soak towels and put out small fires
- To clean
- To cool the room
> It is an accessible water source.

? What should you do after the fire front passes?
- Leave at once through smoke
- Open all windows
+ Check the house for hidden fires and hot spots
- Go to sleep
> Embers can ignite materials hours later.
