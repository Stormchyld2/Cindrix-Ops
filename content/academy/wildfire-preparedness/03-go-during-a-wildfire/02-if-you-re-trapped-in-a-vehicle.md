If fire overtakes your vehicle before you can get clear, **staying in the car is generally safer than running.** A vehicle gives some shelter from radiant heat, and the main danger is the heat, not the flames themselves. The fire front often passes in minutes.

## Before it gets that bad
- If you see **smoke or flames** on the road ahead, **turn around** if you can do so safely.
- Never drive into thick smoke or flames.
- If visibility drops, **slow down, turn on headlights and hazard lights** and look for a safe spot.

## If you must stop
1. **Stay calm.** Panic kills.
2. **Park in the best place available:** a **clear area away from heavy brush and trees,** preferably a road with bare shoulders, a parking lot or a cleared field. Avoid parking where vegetation can burn next to you.
3. **Park across the road** if it is narrow so others do not hit you, or off the road.
4. **Set the parking brake.**
5. **Turn off the air conditioning** and **close all windows and vents.**
6. **Keep the engine running** if it will run, with **headlights and hazard lights on,** so you can be seen in smoke.
7. **Get down below the window level.** Lie on the floor, **covered** by a **wool blanket** or **cotton jacket,** with your head low, if flames approach. Wool resists burning better than synthetic fibers.
8. **Cover your nose and mouth** with a mask or damp cloth if smoke gets in.
9. **Call 911** on your cell phone and give your exact location and a description of the car and who is with you. If you cannot get a signal, try a text message.
10. **Keep everyone together** and keep children calm.
11. **Stay inside** as the fire front passes. The temperature may rise significantly in the vehicle, and the flames may cause you to panic. Stay calm and **allow the bulk of the fire to pass.**
12. **Wait until the fire has passed and the heat has dropped** before opening the doors. Check for flames or embers under the vehicle and on the tires. Then leave carefully, with caution for downed lines and burning trees.

## What to expect
- The car will get **very hot,** and the **smoke** will be thick.
- The vehicle may be **shaken** by wind.
- **Tires and fuel tanks** may burst or catch fire. Fuel tanks rarely explode in the way movies show, but tires may pop.
- The worst heat usually lasts only a few minutes.
- **Do not get out** unless the vehicle itself is on fire and a safer place is within a very short distance.

## What to carry for this
- A **wool blanket** kept in the trunk
- **N95 masks** for everyone
- A **bottle of water**
- A **flashlight**
- A **phone charger**
- **Gloves**
- A **first aid kit,** with burn supplies

## If the car is on fire
Get out and move away from the car and from the flames, toward the most open, burned or bare ground. Cover your face. Call 911.

## Prevent this
- **Leave early** and know your routes.
- **Avoid dead-end roads** and narrow canyons.
- **Keep your tank half full.**
- **Never try to outrun a fire** uphill or down a narrow road.

## Gear you might need
- A wool blanket kept in the trunk
- N95 masks

## Checklist
- Knows to stay in the vehicle with doors and windows closed
- Knows to get below window level and cover with a blanket if flames approach
- Knows to call 911 and give a location if trapped
- Knows to park away from heavy brush and large trees
- If visibility allows driving, go slowly with headlights on

> Stay calm, stay low and let the fire front pass.
