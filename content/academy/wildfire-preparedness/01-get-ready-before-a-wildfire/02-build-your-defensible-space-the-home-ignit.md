Fire scientist Jack Cohen, a retired USDA Forest Service researcher, developed the "Home Ignition Zone" concept in the late 1990s after research into how homes actually catch fire. It's now the standard framework used nationally through NFPA's Firewise USA program, and it divides the area around a home into three zones:

- **Immediate Zone (0–5 feet):** the highest-priority zone. Nothing flammable touches the structure here — no mulch, no stored firewood, no plants against the wall.
- **Intermediate Zone (5–30 feet):** "lean, clean, and green." Dead vegetation removed, trees spaced and limbed up.
- **Extended Zone (30–100 feet, sometimes to 200 feet):** reduced fuel volume — grass mowed low, shrubs spaced, ladder fuels removed.

Some agencies (including FEMA in places) label these Zone 1, 2, and 3 instead — same concept, different names. Many states and counties legally require some level of defensible space clearance around structures in high-risk areas, but the specifics vary widely, so check with your local fire department or state forestry agency for the rules where you live.

## Gear you might need

- Gravel, pavers, or noncombustible mulch for the Immediate Zone
- Pruning shears / pole saw for tree spacing
- Rake and yard waste bags for seasonal clearing

## Checklist
- Immediate Zone: no mulch, firewood, or plants touching the house
- Immediate Zone: nothing flammable stored under decks or stairs
- Intermediate Zone: dead plants, grass, and weeds removed
- Intermediate Zone: tree branches trimmed 10 ft from chimney and from each other
- Extended Zone: grass mowed low, shrubs spaced out
- Checked local/state rules — requirements vary by jurisdiction

> Expert advice from Brent Weir (25+ years firefighter): Many of my co-workers who have worked on large wildfire deployments tell me stories of patrolling areas doing structure protection.  The houses that had some even BASIC defensible space allowed them to spend a few minutes and a little water to keep the house from igniting.  Those who did no work ended up being too hard to protect and many ended up burning to the ground.
