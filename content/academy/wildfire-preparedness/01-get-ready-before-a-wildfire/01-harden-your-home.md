Most homes lost in a wildfire aren't destroyed by a wall of flame — they're destroyed by embers. A single ember landing in a dry gutter, an open attic vent, or a pile of leaves against the siding can ignite a home long before the fire front ever arrives. **This accounts for 90% of home ignitions.** "Hardening" your home means closing off the small entry points embers use to get in.

Walk the outside of your home and look at it the way an ember would: where would it land, and what would it land on?

## The extra mile gear

- 1/8" or smaller noncombustible metal mesh (for vent retrofits)
- Fire-rated roofing materials (for future roof replacement)
- Ember-resistant gutter guards

## Checklist
- Roof is fire-rated or confirmed fire-resistant material
- All vents covered with 1/8" or smaller noncombustible mesh
- Gutters cleared of leaves and debris
- Eaves and soffits fully enclosed, no gaps
- Windows are dual-pane or tempered glass where feasible
- Deck/porch checked for fire-resistant materials
- Dual-pane or tempered glass window film

> Expert advice from Brent Weir (25+ years firefighter): As a Fire Captain I was on scene of a large apartment fire near a residential neighborhood.  Many fires started nearby from embers landing on wood shake roofs, and on combustible decks and patio furniture. The danger is real.  Be mindful of things around your house that could ignite, and remove them if possible when wildfire danger is high.
