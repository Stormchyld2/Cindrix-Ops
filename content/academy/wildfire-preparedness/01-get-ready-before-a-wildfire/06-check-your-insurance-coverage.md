This is the lesson people skip, and it is the one that costs the most later. Now, before a fire, you have time to read your policy. After a fire, you will be reading it under pressure while managing everything else. This lesson is general information, not legal or financial advice. Ask your insurer, a licensed agent or your state's department of insurance about your own policy.

## Know what you have
Get a **complete copy of your policy,** not only the summary. Read the **declarations page** for coverage limits. Make sure you understand:

- **Dwelling coverage:** the cost to rebuild the structure. Is the limit enough at today's building costs? Rebuilding costs rise, and a policy that was right years ago may now fall short.
- **Other structures:** sheds, fences, garages.
- **Personal property:** your belongings. Is it replacement cost or actual cash value?
- **Additional Living Expense (ALE), also called Loss of Use:** pays for temporary housing, food and other extra costs if you are displaced. It has limits on both amount and time that surprise many people.
- **Deductibles,** including any separate wildfire deductible.
- **Extended replacement cost or guaranteed replacement:** whether you have any extra margin above the limit.
- **Ordinance or law coverage:** the extra cost to rebuild to current codes.
- **Debris removal.**
- **Exclusions and conditions,** such as vacancy and requirements to maintain defensible space.

## Ask your insurer
- Is my dwelling limit enough to rebuild?
- How long does ALE last and what is the limit?
- What happens if my area is under a mandatory evacuation? Does ALE apply without damage?
- What documentation will you need?
- What discounts exist for hardening my home?
- Is my policy at risk of non-renewal?

Every state has an **insurance commissioner or department of insurance** that can answer coverage questions before you are in crisis mode and can help if a claim stalls.

## Make a home inventory
A home inventory speeds your claim and proves what you owned.
1. **Walk through each room** with a phone and record video, speaking aloud: "Kitchen cabinet, 12 plates, a blender..."
2. **Open drawers and closets.**
3. **Photograph** serial numbers, labels and receipts for valuables.
4. **Record** the outside, the garage, the sheds and vehicles.
5. **Save** the video in at least two places: a cloud account and an external drive kept elsewhere.
6. **Update** once a year or after a big purchase.

Use the **Inventory** screen in this program to keep a list with photos and values.

## Keep documents safe
- Keep the full policy in a **fireproof safe** or **safety deposit box,** with a copy in your go bag.
- Keep your agent's phone number and your policy number in your go bag and phone.
- Keep scans of **ID, deeds, mortgage papers, vehicle titles, wills and medical records** in encrypted cloud storage or on a drive in your go bag (see Cindrix Safe for an encrypted vault).

## If you cannot get coverage
Some high-risk areas have limited private coverage. Ask your state about a **FAIR Plan** or other last-resort program, which usually offers basic coverage at higher cost. Reducing risk (hardening, defensible space and joining a Firewise community) can help with insurability.

## Gear you might need
- A fireproof document safe or a safety deposit box for the physical policy
- A cloud backup or external drive for a digital home inventory

## Checklist
- Current full copy of the policy obtained and stored safely
- Declarations page reviewed for coverage limits
- ALE / Loss of Use limits understood
- Home inventory (photos or video of belongings) completed and backed up
- Agent and policy numbers in the go bag
- Questions asked of the insurer and answers written down

> Read the policy now. After the fire is the worst time to learn what it says.

? What does ALE pay for?
- A new car

+ Temporary housing and extra living costs if you are displaced
- Nothing

? Why make a home inventory?

+ It speeds your claim and proves what you owned
- It stops fires
- It is required to evacuate

- Only in the house
- Only on the fridge
- Nowhere
+ In more than one place, such as cloud and an external drive kept elsewhere
