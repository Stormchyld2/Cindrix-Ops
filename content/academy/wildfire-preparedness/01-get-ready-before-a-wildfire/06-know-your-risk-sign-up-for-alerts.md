You can't act on a warning you never receive. The National Weather Service issues Red Flag Warnings nationwide when low humidity, strong wind, and dry fuels combine to create high fire danger — that's a good moment to double-check you're signed up for local alerts, which most counties offer for free.

## Gear you might need

- NOAA weather radio, as a backup if cell towers go down or are overloaded
- In the Communications course, we talk about using two-way radios as a contingency for communication. You can program these to listen to NOAA weather stations.

## Checklist
- Checked whether your home is in a higher-risk wildland-urban interface area
- Signed up for your county or city's emergency alert system
- Saved your local fire department's contact info

## Resources

- USFA's WUI Fire Property Awareness Explorer — check your address's wildfire exposure (https://www.usfa.fema.gov/wui/)
- National Weather Service — Red Flag Warning system
- Your county or city's emergency alert sign-up page
