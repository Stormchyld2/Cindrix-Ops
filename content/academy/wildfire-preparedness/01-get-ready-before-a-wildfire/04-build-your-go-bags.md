A go-bag is what you grab when you have five minutes, not fifty. Pack it now, while you have all the time in the world to think clearly, not later while you're watching the sky. We cover go-bags, and other types of supplies you'd need whether you are "bugging out" or "staying put," in our Baseline Preparedness course.

Pack one Go-Bag per person — kids' kits can be lighter, but everyone should have their own. Store them somewhere genuinely fast to grab. Our Basic Preparedness Tier 1 course goes deep into the details of preparing a Go-Bag.

## Gear you might need

- NIOSH-approved (N95 or better) respirator, one per person
- Headlamp or flashlight with spare batteries
- First aid kit
- Portable phone charger / power bank
- Waterproof pouch for ID, insurance policy, and other documents
- Sleeping bag / backpacker's pillow. You may end up in a shelter that has a cot or carpeted floor. Having items to allow your family to rest comfortably is important!
- Personal hygiene kit
- Jetboil or similar stove/cup setup to allow you to heat water for tea/coffee/freeze-dried meals

## Checklist
- One Go-Bag packed per household member
- Water, non-perishable food, and medications included
- Important documents copied and packed (ID, insurance, deeds)
- NIOSH-approved respirator included
- Pet-specific kit packed (food, leash, carrier, vaccination records)
- Kits stored somewhere fast to grab on the way out

As the fire neared her area and they were preparing to leave, it was very challenging to know what gear to grab and pull it all together. They had the equipment, but it just wasn't set up to leave immediately.
