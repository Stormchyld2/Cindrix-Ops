Fire scientist Jack Cohen, a retired USDA Forest Service researcher, developed the **Home Ignition Zone** concept in the late 1990s after studying how homes actually catch fire. It is now the standard framework used nationally through NFPA's Firewise USA program. It divides the area around a home into three zones:

- **Immediate zone (0 to 5 feet).** The highest priority. Nothing flammable touches the structure here: no mulch, no stored firewood, no plants against the wall. Some agencies call this **Zone 0.**
- **Intermediate zone (5 to 30 feet).** "Lean, clean and green." Dead vegetation removed, trees spaced and limbed up.
- **Extended zone (30 to 100 feet, sometimes to 200 feet).** Reduced fuel volume: grass mowed low, shrubs spaced, ladder fuels removed.

Some agencies label these Zone 1, 2 and 3 instead. It is the same idea with different names. Many states and counties **legally require** a level of defensible space in high-risk areas. The specifics vary widely, so check with your local fire department or state forestry agency.

## Immediate zone (0 to 5 feet)
**Remove**
- Dead weeds, grass and debris. Check roofs, gutters and outdoor areas.
- Combustible items on decks: furniture, doormats and planters.
- Garbage and recycling bins. Move them outside this zone.
- Boats, RVs and vehicles. Park them away from the house.
- Firewood, lumber and stacked materials.

**Replace**
- **Combustible mulch** with gravel, pavers or concrete.
- **Combustible fencing and gates** attached to the house with fire-resistant materials.

**Trim**
- Branches to stay at least **10 feet** from chimneys and stovepipes.

**Keep**
- Irrigated lawn or low, non-woody plants only, if any. Skip junipers, cypress and other resinous shrubs against the house.

## Intermediate zone (5 to 30 feet)
- **Remove dead plants, grass and weeds,** and keep the grass mowed short.
- **Space trees** so crowns do not touch, with roughly 10 feet or more between crowns depending on slope.
- **Prune lower branches** off trees up to 6 to 10 feet from the ground (or a third of the tree's height for short trees) so a ground fire cannot climb.
- **Create islands** of plants separated by gravel or lawn, not a continuous carpet of shrubs.
- **Remove ladder fuels:** vegetation that links the ground to tree crowns.
- Keep **firewood stacks** at least 30 feet from structures, uphill or level. A common rule puts them in the extended zone.
- Keep **propane tanks** with a clear area of 10 feet around them.
- Maintain **irrigated plants** and mow in the morning when it is cool and humid (never in the heat of a Red Flag day).

## Extended zone (30 to 100 feet)
- **Reduce fuel volume.** Thin dense brush and small trees.
- **Mow grass** to 4 inches or lower.
- **Space shrubs** and keep them away from tree trunks.
- **Remove dead trees,** limbs and standing snags that could fall on the house or road.
- **Store firewood and lumber here.**
- On **slopes,** increase the distance, since fire moves faster uphill. Many agencies recommend wider clearing on steeper ground.

## Keep it up
Defensible space is not a one-time job.
- Do the heavy work in spring.
- Rake, mow and clean again in summer and before the peak of fire season.
- Do a quick walk after every windstorm.
- Dispose of cuttings by chipping, hauling or a permitted program. Do not pile them in the zone.

## Watch out for sparks
- Use power tools in the morning, with a charged fire extinguisher or water nearby.
- Never mow or cut when it is dry and windy.
- Avoid hitting rocks with a mower blade.
- Park hot vehicles on pavement.

## Work with your neighbors
Your neighbor's fuel is your risk. Many communities run Firewise USA programs and chipper days. Talk to the neighbors and share the job.

> **Expert advice from Brent Weir (25+ years firefighter):** Many of my co-workers who have worked large wildfire deployments tell me stories of patrolling areas doing structure protection. The houses with even basic defensible space let them spend a few minutes and a little water to keep the house from igniting. Those who did no work were too hard to protect, and many burned to the ground.

## Gear you might need
- Gravel, pavers or noncombustible mulch for the immediate zone
- Pruning shears and a pole saw for tree spacing
- Rake and yard waste bags for seasonal clearing

## Checklist
- Immediate zone: no mulch, firewood or plants touching the house
- Immediate zone: nothing flammable stored under decks or stairs
- Intermediate zone: dead plants, grass and weeds removed
- Intermediate zone: tree branches trimmed 10 feet from the chimney and from each other
- Extended zone: grass mowed low, shrubs spaced out
- Checked local and state rules, which vary by jurisdiction
