A go bag is what you grab when you have five minutes, not fifty. Pack it now, while you have time to think clearly, not later while you are watching the sky. We cover go bags and other supplies for bugging out or staying put in the Basic Preparedness course, and this lesson focuses on what matters for wildfire.

Pack **one go bag per person.** Children's bags can be lighter, but everyone has their own. Store them where they are fast to grab: by the door, in the vehicle or in a closet near the garage. Tell everyone where they are.

## The six Ps (when you have only minutes)
1. **People and pets.**
2. **Prescriptions** and personal needs (glasses, hearing aids, mobility aids).
3. **Papers:** ID, insurance policy, deeds, passports, birth certificates (copies or scans).
4. **Photos and valuables,** including phones, a laptop or drive with backups, cash.
5. **Plan and supplies:** the go bags, chargers and the action plan.
6. **Pets and animals,** with carriers, food, medicines and records.

## What goes in each go bag
**Basics**
- **Water:** at least one gallon per person per day for three days.
- **Food:** non-perishable, ready to eat, for three days.
- **Medicines:** a supply for several days, in labeled containers, with a list. Consult your doctor and pharmacist about how to store and rotate them.
- **Clothing:** a change of clothes, sturdy shoes, a hat and socks.
- **Sleeping:** sleeping bag and a small pillow. You may end up in a shelter with a cot or a carpeted floor. Comfort helps families rest.
- **Hygiene:** soap, toothbrush, wipes, tissue, sanitary items, diapers if needed.
- **Light:** headlamp or flashlight and spare batteries.
- **Power:** phone charger and a power bank.
- **Radio:** battery or hand-crank radio with NOAA weather band.
- **First aid kit** with a tourniquet and bleeding supplies if you are trained.
- **Whistle.**
- **Multi-tool and duct tape.**
- **Cash** in small bills.
- **Waterproof pouch** for documents.
- **Stove and cup:** a small backpacking stove and cup to heat water for tea, coffee or freeze-dried meals. Use it outside only.

**Smoke protection**
- **NIOSH-approved N95 respirator** (or better) for each person. Dust masks, cloth masks and surgical masks do not protect against smoke particles. A respirator must fit tightly to work, so practice putting it on. Children age 2 and older can wear masks, but most standard respirators do not fit small faces. Choose a mask that fits snugly over the nose and under the chin.
- **Goggles or glasses** to protect the eyes.
- **Cotton or wool bandanna.**

**Pets**
- Carrier, leash, food, water, bowls, medicines, vaccination records and a recent photo.

## Store protective clothing nearby
Keep long pants, a long-sleeve shirt, heavy shoes or boots, a cap and gloves in cotton or wool (not synthetic, which can melt) near the door. See the protective clothing lesson.

## Keep it fresh
Check the bags when the clocks change: replace food, water, batteries and medicines, and update clothing for the season and for growing children. Write the date on a tag.

> **Expert advice from Brent Weir (25+ years firefighter):** My daughter lived very close to the catastrophic Spokane, Washington wildfires in the summer of 2026. As the fire neared and they were preparing to leave, it was challenging to know what to grab and pull together. They had the equipment, but it just was not set up to leave immediately.

## Gear you might need
- NIOSH-approved (N95 or better) respirator, one per person
- Headlamp or flashlight with spare batteries
- First aid kit
- Portable phone charger or power bank
- Waterproof pouch for ID, insurance policy and other documents
- Sleeping bag and a small pillow
- Personal hygiene kit
- Backpacking stove and cup

## Checklist
- One go bag packed per household member
- Water, non-perishable food and medicines included
- Important documents copied and packed (ID, insurance, deeds)
- NIOSH-approved respirator included
- Pet-specific kit packed (food, leash, carrier, vaccination records)
- Bags stored somewhere fast to grab on the way out
- Bags checked when the clocks change
