Level 1 (a few hours to a few days: power out, storm, local emergency): phones may work, but use the plan to practise.
Level 2 (a regional disaster: phone towers and internet down or jammed for days): the radio schedule starts, meeting points are used, no one travels alone.
Level 3 (a long emergency: no grid for weeks or more): radios saved for set times, the neighbourhood network starts, runners and signs are used.

## 15. Keeping safe on the air
Anyone can listen. Do not give names, addresses, numbers of people or amounts of supplies on the radio.
Change channels and codes if someone unknown joins.
Be polite. A calm voice carries further.
Do not transmit while someone else is calling for help. Make way and listen.

## 17. Emergency calls: say it like this
1) "Break, break, emergency." 2) Who you are and where you are. 3) What is wrong (fire, injury, danger). 4) What help you need. 5) Say "over" and listen.
For a missing person: the name, age, what they wear, where last seen, the time, and a contact.
Write each call and answer in the log.

## 18. Practice and upkeep
Monthly: radio test on the family channel. Check batteries and the antenna.
Every three months: practise the full plan, including walking to the meeting places and a no-phone day.
Every year: update the phone numbers, call signs, meeting places and the people in the plan.
After any use: write down what worked and what did not, and change the plan.
