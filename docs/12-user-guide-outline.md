# 12. User guide outline

The complete guide ships on the drive as Markdown (`guide/`), with a PDF export. Plain words, short sections, and one page of "if you read nothing else". Sections are written as each feature lands.

1. **If you read nothing else** (one page): how to start, the three most useful screens, and 911.
2. **Quick start**
   - Plugging in and starting on Windows
   - Installing the Android app and the Play Protect notice
   - The Start screen: six steps to get ready
3. **Using Ask (Chat)**
   - What it can and can't do, and why it shows sources
   - Why it won't give medicine doses
   - Choosing a model for your computer's memory
4. **The Library**
   - Archives, manuals, emergency cards
   - Searching, and reading offline
5. **Maps**
   - Setting your position by hand
   - Street, topo, and satellite
   - Finding the nearest pharmacy, hospital, water, shelter
   - Drawing: routes, no-go areas, rendezvous points
   - Saving and exporting (picture, GPX, PDF)
6. **Planning**
   - Family page and the optional passphrase lock
   - Inventory: adding, counting, expiry, rotating
   - What fails next
   - Drills
   - Printing your binder
7. **Diabetes Pack**
   - What's in it and who reviewed it
   - Low and high blood sugar, sick days, insulin storage without power
   - Medicines and doses: follow your doctor's written plan
8. **Settings**
   - Downloading satellite photos for your county
   - Language, devices, updates
9. **Backups and moving your data** (the `user` folder)
10. **Troubleshooting** (Windows warnings, slow answers, low memory, phone battery)
11. **Sources and licenses**
12. **Appendix:** wallet card, one-page checklists, glossary

## Style rules
- Short sentences. No jargon without a one-line explanation.
- Every medical page ends with: reference only, not medical advice, call 911 in an emergency.
- Screenshots come from the finished app, not the mockups.
